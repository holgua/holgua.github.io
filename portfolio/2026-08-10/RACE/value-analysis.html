Ferrari N.V. (RACE) — Value Investor's Analysis

As of 2026-07-21 | Current price: $377.29 | Reporting currency: EUR | All USD figures use implied EUR/USD 1.1440

SECTION 1 — VALUE INVESTOR'S VERDICT

1.1 Circle of Competence Check

Ferrari manufactures and sells ultra-luxury sports cars and grand tourers — approximately 13,500–14,000 units per year — at average selling prices that generate EBITDA margins above 38%, a level associated with luxury-goods companies, not automotive manufacturers. The business model is deliberately simple: permanently constrain supply below demand, charge accordingly, and use the resulting pricing power to expand margins regardless of the economic cycle. The model is stable and comprehensible because it has not changed materially in four decades and does not depend on technology disruption, platform dynamics, or network effects. The primary uncertainty is whether the Elettrica BEV transition, beginning in late 2026, will preserve or compress the margin structure — this is a tractable question that can be monitored through quarterly EBITDA disclosures. Verdict: Within circle. Ferrari is as close to an ideal Buffett business as the automotive sector produces; the business model can be explained in one sentence and its earnings power over ten years can be forecast with above-average confidence relative to most listed companies.

SECTION 2 — ECONOMIC MOAT

2.1 Moat Type and Source

Intangible assets (brand, heritage, IP) — Present and strong. Ferrari's brand is the primary and dominant moat source. A seventy-year racing heritage, Formula 1 association, and the Enzo Ferrari mythology create pricing power that no capital-intensive competitor can replicate. EBITDA margins at 38.8% — 10–15 percentage points above any mass-luxury automotive peer — are the quantitative expression of this brand premium (Financial-Analysis §3.3). The loyalty staircase (access to limited-edition models gated by prior ownership history) creates a closed ecosystem that reinforces the brand advantage structurally.

Switching costs — Present but narrow. The loyalty staircase functions as a switching cost: existing Ferrari owners who leave the ecosystem lose their position in the allocation queue for desirable and appreciating models. This is real but operates only at the margin — it reinforces, rather than independently constitutes, the brand moat.

Network effects — Absent. No meaningful platform dynamics. Value does not increase with the number of participants.

Cost advantages — Absent. Ferrari's supply-restriction model is the opposite of cost leadership; the scarcity is deliberate. No structural cost advantage over peers.

Efficient scale — Present and strong. The ultra-luxury automotive segment is a natural oligopoly of one at Ferrari's margin level. Production at ~14,000 units per year cannot be economically justified at conventional automotive ROICs — the model only works because pricing supports extreme margins at tiny scale. This is efficient scale in Morningstar's technical sense: a market too small for a second competitor to profitably enter.

Primary moat source: Brand heritage and scarcity architecture, operating as a luxury-goods franchise, reinforced by efficient scale in a natural oligopoly. Confidence in this assessment is high — XBRL-verified EBITDA expansion from 34.8% to 38.8% (FY2022–FY2025) is the clearest evidence of economic rent extraction by a moated business.

2.2 Moat Durability — The Ten-Year Test

ROIC is not directly computable from ESEF XBRL (CurrentLiabilities not tagged). Proxy evidence: ROE held at 40–43% for three consecutive years, and EBITDA margins expanded in each of the last three fiscal years despite macroeconomic headwinds and US tariff pressure. These are structural rather than cyclical — the margin expansion is driven by model-mix (Purosangue SUV, limited-edition Icona series) and pricing discipline, not cost reduction or volume leverage (Financial-Brief §5.1).

Key structural threats from the annual filing risk register: Elettrica BEV costs could compress EBITDA toward 34–35% (Exit Condition 2); production cap breach above 16,000 units (Exit Condition 1); US tariff re-escalation to 25% (Exit Condition 5). None of these is structural technological disruption; all are monitored and operationally manageable. No competitor has demonstrated the ability to enter Ferrari's pricing and margin territory. There is no plausible technology that would make Ferrari's heritage replicable.

Verdict: Widening. EBITDA margin has expanded sequentially FY2022–FY2025; the moat is demonstrably wider today than it was three years ago. The Elettrica transition represents a transitional risk (not a structural moat risk) and must be monitored through FY2026–FY2028 margin disclosures.

2.3 Pricing Power Test

Gross margin is not separately tagged in Ferrari's ESEF XBRL (GrossProfit tag absent). Operating margin is the best available proxy: 25.2% (FY2021) → 24.1% (FY2022) → 27.1% (FY2023) → 28.3% (FY2024) → 29.5% (FY2025) — a clear upward trend with only a one-year dip in FY2022. EBITDA margin moved from 35.9% → 34.8% → 38.2% → 38.3% → 38.8% over the same period (Financial-Brief §5.1).

Ferrari imposed up to 10% price surcharges on Purosangue and 12Cilindri in 2025 in response to the 25% US Section 232 tariff threat. FY2025 EBITDA margin of 38.8% exceeded the ≥38.3% guidance, confirming that the surcharge was absorbed by customers without volume or order-book damage. This is direct empirical evidence of exceptional pricing power at the UHNWI clientele level (Political-Exposure §5).

Verdict: Strong pricing power. Ferrari can raise prices without losing volume — the only automotive company for which this statement is demonstrably true.

3.2 Earnings Consistency and Predictability

Net income series FY2021–FY2025 (EUR millions): 830.8 / 932.6 / 1,252.0 / 1,521.9 / 1,596.9. EPS (EUR diluted): 4.50 / 5.09 / 6.90 / 8.46 / 8.96. This is one of the smoothest earnings trajectories in the public markets — no year-on-year decline across the entire observable dataset (Financial-Brief §5.1).

Worst year in dataset: FY2022, where revenue growth decelerated and EBITDA margin compressed 110 bps to 34.8%. EPS still grew 13.1% from EUR 4.50 to EUR 5.09. There was no earnings decline in any year. This is exceptional resilience — the 2022 period included supply-chain disruptions, inflationary cost pressure, and geopolitical shocks.

Accruals ratio: negative in all five observable years (FY2021: −0.066, FY2022: −0.061, FY2023: −0.058, FY2024: −0.043, FY2025: −0.078). Consistently negative accruals mean OCF systematically exceeds reported net income — the opposite of earnings management and a signal of high earnings quality (Financial-Brief §3.3). OCF/NI ratio: 1.54× / 1.51× / 1.37× / 1.27× / 1.47× FY2021–FY2025 — all well above 1.0×.

Verdict: Highly predictable. Ferrari's earnings track record is among the most consistent in the public markets. This supports high DCF confidence and argues for a discount rate at the lower end of the reasonable range.

However, a significant portion of InvCF is growth capital (Elettrica e-building co-funded by the Italian government MOU, R&D for BEV/hybrid platforms). True maintenance CapEx is likely below D&A for a brand-and-scarcity model.

ROE held at 40–43% for three years — exceptional return on book capital. The incremental invested capital is generating returns far above any reasonable cost of capital.

Classification: Capital-intensive but high-return. Ferrari is not a classic asset-light compounder — physical manufacturing requires real capital. But the returns on that capital are among the highest in manufacturing, and the trend (InvCF/Revenue declining) is favourable. The Italian government MOU reducing the effective Elettrica capex burden improves the capital efficiency profile further.

5.2 Earnings Power Value (EPV)

EPV is the zero-growth floor value — what Ferrari is worth assuming it never grows again.

  • Normalised EBIT (3-year average FY2023–FY2025): (EUR 1,617.4M + EUR 1,887.8M + EUR 2,109.7M) ÷ 3 = EUR 1,871.6M
  • Effective tax rate (average FY2023–FY2025): ~22% (FY2023: 22.6%, FY2024: 19.4%, FY2025: 24.3%; 3-year average ~22%)
  • NOPAT = EUR 1,871.6M × (1 − 0.22) = EUR 1,459.8M = USD 1,670.1M
  • Cost of capital: 7.3% (same as DCF)
  • EPV = USD 1,670.1M ÷ 0.073 = USD 22,878M
  • Net debt: approximately zero (net industrial cash position; long-term debt largely offset by cash EUR 1,467.7M)
  • EPV per share = USD 22,878M ÷ 178.2M = $128

The EPV of $128 vs. the current price of $377.29 means investors are paying $249 per share — 66% of the total price — for Ferrari's future growth. This is not unusual for luxury-goods compounders; LVMH and Hermès trade at similar growth premiums. But it means the investment case is almost entirely a growth story: if growth decelerates materially, the stock has significant downside to the EPV floor.

SECTION 7B — POLITICAL & REGULATORY RISK (VALUE INVESTOR'S LENS)

Source: RACE-Political-Exposure.md (2026-07-21) and Financial-Brief §8.1–§8.3.

7B.1 Political Moat or Political Rent?

Classification: Political Moat (with a monitored tail risk).

Ferrari's government relationships represent genuine structural barriers to entry — the EU low-volume manufacturer derogation (≤10,000 units per year exempt from intermediate CO2 targets; 0g/km applies from 2036 vs. 2035 for mass manufacturers) is a regulatory advantage that any large-volume competitor would not enjoy and that Ferrari secured through structural qualification (production cap), not political rent-seeking. The Italian government MOU co-investing >€106M in the Maranello e-building represents a co-investor with aligned industrial-policy interests, not a dependency relationship (Political-Exposure §§1–2).

The single material tail risk is US tariff re-escalation: the July 2026 EU-US trade deal established a 15% all-inclusive tariff ceiling, below the 25% Exit Condition (5) threshold. Ferrari's pricing power was empirically tested at the 25% level in 2025 and held — FY2025 EBITDA margin 38.8% exceeded guidance. This is a political rent exposure in the sense that the 15% rate depends on a deal that could be renegotiated, but Ferrari has demonstrated it can price through at least 15% and partially through 25% (Political-Exposure §5–6).

7B.2 Administration Sensitivity — Permanent Capital Loss Test

US Administration: Ferrari's most material US political exposure — tariff policy — has been partially resolved at 15% under the July 2026 EU-US deal. This does not represent permanent capital loss risk at the 15% level. Re-escalation to 25%+ under a future administration remains a tail risk, with Exit Condition (5) as the monitoring mechanism: "US tariff 25% AND Ferrari guidance citing EBITDA margin below 38%." Exit Condition (5) is not currently triggered (Political-Exposure §4).

EU Administration: EU CO2 regulation direction is a tailwind for the Elettrica strategy, not a headwind. The 2036 derogation deadline is a known constraint that Ferrari's electrification timeline covers. The Italian coalition government's alignment with Ferrari as an industrial champion (co-investment MOU) is structural — it transcends specific government coalitions because Emilia-Romagna employment and Italy's export identity are tied to the Ferrari brand (Political-Exposure §5).

Verdict: Political risk is a cyclical factor but not an existential threat. The most important single piece of evidence is the empirical 2025 tariff test: Ferrari applied surcharges, maintained ≥38.3% EBITDA guidance, and delivered 38.8% — proving that even a 25% US tariff scenario does not constitute a moat breach at Ferrari's pricing levels.

7B.3 Munger's Incentive Test

Ferrari has no US corporate PAC, no identified standalone EU lobbying spend, and operates its political engagement through the ACEA industry association (securing the low-volume CO2 derogation and the 2023 e-fuels carve-out). The Italian government MOU is a co-investment, not a lobbying success; it was structured around Ferrari's own capex commitment and job creation targets (Political-Exposure §2).

PAC contributions: not applicable (Dutch incorporation; no US PAC identified). Executive political donations: not found in public sources. SECTION 8 — KEY TALKING POINTS

  • Ferrari's moat is the widest in the automotive sector and is demonstrably widening: EBITDA margins expanded from 34.8% to 38.8% in three consecutive years, proving economic rent extraction at stable volume — the statistical signature of a true luxury-goods franchise, not an automobile manufacturer. This is the most important single fact about this business. Every other quality metric (ROE 40–43%, accruals persistently negative, OCF/NI above 1.25× every year) is a downstream consequence of this moat.

  • The scarcity architecture is both moat and warning system. Ferrari's production cap (~14,000 units per year) is not a cost constraint — it is a deliberate strategic choice to keep demand permanently above supply. The exit condition for selling is simple: if annual deliveries exceed 16,000 units, the moat is breached. This makes the investment easier to monitor than most — one number, one rule.

  • At $377.29, there is no margin of safety by the Owner Earnings DCF method. The base-case DCF (7.3% discount rate, 9% growth) yields $368/share — essentially at par. Under conservative assumptions (8.3% discount rate), intrinsic value falls to $299. The business deserves ownership; the price requires patience. The 25% margin-of-safety entry price is $276. This strengthens the case for holding but challenges the case for initiating.

  • The Elettrica BEV transition is the single material uncertainty and the most important thesis monitor. Ferrari's entire premium valuation rests on the assumption that the BEV transition is margin-neutral or margin-accretive. If Elettrica requires price concessions or order-book supplements, EBITDA margins could compress toward 34–35% — the institutional threshold for automotive re-rating. FY2025 data shows no sign of this; the watch condition is a single EBITDA margin print below 35% in any fiscal year.

  • Management integrity is exceptional and structurally enforced. Seven consecutive years of clean, cash-backed earnings growth with no restatements, consistently negative accruals, and a controlling Agnelli/Exor family shareholder (36% voting) with multi-generational horizons creates the incentive alignment Buffett requires. CEO Vigna's EPS CAGR of 20.7% from EUR 4.50 to EUR 8.96 under his tenure is XBRL-verified — not adjusted. This strengthens the case for ownership.

  • US tariff risk has been empirically tested and passed. Ferrari imposed up to 10% price surcharges on Purosangue and 12Cilindri in 2025 under 25% tariff threat and delivered FY2025 EBITDA margin of 38.8% — above ≥38.3% guidance. The July 2026 EU-US deal established a 15% ceiling. Ferrari's pricing power at the UHNWI clientele level is now empirically confirmed, not theoretical. This strengthens the case for ownership.

  • The FVB base case ($533) implies 41% upside from current price — but only materialises if the bull scenario (Elettrica margin-accretive, luxury re-classification) partially materialises. The probability-weighted expected return across all three scenarios is approximately 12.6% annualised — which clears a 10% hurdle rate. But 66% of the current price is being paid for future growth that has not yet occurred. This is the correct characterisation of the investment risk.
