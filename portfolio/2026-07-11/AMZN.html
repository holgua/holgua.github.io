Amazon: The Bet Is Wobbling

The Bottom Line

Amazon's investment case rests on a single compounding flywheel: a retail and logistics empire that generates the cash to fund two structurally higher-margin businesses — cloud computing and digital advertising — whose unit economics are still expanding. That flywheel is intact at the engine level, with cloud revenue growing 28% in the most recent quarter and advertising up 24%. The share price sits in the upper half of the hold band, trading at a healthy equity premium to the owner-earnings fair-value anchor and standing well above fair value on a conservative basis. What is wobbling is the capital expenditure trajectory: Amazon spent $43.2 billion on infrastructure in a single quarter — annualising well above the threshold at which a sustained plateau across two full fiscal years would trigger an exit — and management has guided that spending will keep rising.

Recent Development

The most important number from Amazon's first-quarter 2026 earnings report is not a profit figure — it is $43.2 billion. That is what Amazon spent on physical infrastructure in a single three-month period, up 78% from the same quarter of 2025 and annualising at roughly $170 billion a year. The thesis includes a specific exit condition: if total capital spending stays above $120 billion in both 2027 and 2028 without a corresponding cloud revenue acceleration, the assumption that the current cycle will pay off as generously as the last one breaks down. No exit condition has been crossed yet — the gate explicitly requires two full fiscal years — but the slope is firmly upward.

The other numbers are reassuring. Cloud revenue grew 28% year on year to $37.6 billion, well above the 18% thesis minimum. Advertising grew 24% to $17.2 billion. The international segment generated a 3.6% operating margin, up from 3.0% a year earlier, confirming the path to profitability outside North America is structural. The tension between engines confirming and the fuel bill rising is what makes this a watch situation rather than a hold-and-forget one. About Amazon

Amazon makes its money in three distinct ways, which is unusual for a company of its size.

The first and largest is retail. Amazon sells goods directly to consumers and also operates a marketplace through which independent businesses sell to consumers while Amazon handles storage, shipping, and customer service. Retail generated $588 billion of revenue in the twelve months to the end of 2025 — about 82% of the total. Margins on the retail side are thin: North America, the mature market, earned a 6.9% operating margin in 2025, and the international business is only just turning profitable after years of heavy investment.

The second is cloud computing. Amazon Web Services, universally known as AWS, rents computing power, data storage, and software infrastructure to businesses, governments, and other organisations over the internet. AWS generated $129 billion of revenue in 2025 — 18% of Amazon's total — but produced $46 billion of operating profit, which is 57% of Amazon's entire operating income. It is, in other words, a much smaller business by revenue than retail but the engine that funds almost everything else.

The third is digital advertising. When a business wants its products to appear prominently when someone searches Amazon, or wants its brand to appear in Amazon's video streaming service, it pays Amazon for that placement. This advertising business generated $69 billion of revenue in 2025 and is growing at over 20% a year. It has no meaningful disclosed cost of goods, which means its margin is structurally high, though Amazon does not report it separately.

The three businesses reinforce each other. The retail platform generates a large and loyal customer base. That base makes AWS a credible host for AI workloads — companies building AI products want access to Amazon's customers, and the most direct route is to run those products on AWS. Both businesses generate transaction data that powers the advertising engine: Amazon knows what people buy, which makes its advertising unusually effective at predicting purchase intent. Each business makes the others more valuable.

The Case for the Bet

The investment case rests on four conditions that must keep holding for the flywheel to keep turning:

  1. AWS keeps growing fast enough to justify the capital being spent on it. The thesis requires AWS quarterly revenue growth to stay above 18% — the level at which the AI infrastructure investment earns an acceptable return — with an exit floor at 15% sustained for two consecutive quarters.

Latest reading: +28% year on year in Q1 2026 (revenue $37.6 billion), as of 2026-04-30. Cloud backlog of contracted but not yet delivered revenue stood at $364 billion, up materially from the prior year. The AWS and OpenAI partnership expanded by $100 billion over eight years in the same quarter.

  1. AWS operating margins stay above the minimum floor. A sustained compression below 28% for two consecutive quarters — absent a credible one-off explanation — would signal that the economics of reinvestment have broken down. The thesis target is 30% or above.

Latest reading: 37.7% in Q1 2026, as of 2026-04-30. Down approximately 170 basis points from 39.4% a year earlier, driven by depreciation rising 66% as the infrastructure build absorbs into the asset base. Comfortably above the 28% exit floor and the 30% target, though the direction warrants attention over the next two quarters.

  1. Advertising keeps compounding at double-digit rates. The thesis calls advertising "the third compounding engine." An exit would be triggered only if growth slowed to single digits for two consecutive years — a high bar.

Latest reading: +24% year on year in Q1 2026 ($17.2 billion), as of 2026-04-30. Advertising was cited as a primary driver of operating income growth in both the North America and International retail segments.

  1. Capital spending starts to normalise rather than keep rising indefinitely. The thesis requires that consolidated capital expenditure not remain above $120 billion in both 2027 and 2028 without a commensurate acceleration in cloud revenue to justify it.

Latest reading: $43.2 billion cash capital expenditure in Q1 2026 alone, as of 2026-04-30. Annualised, this is approximately $170 billion — well above the threshold. Management guided that spending will keep increasing in 2026. This is the live wobble in the thesis, though the two-year-confirmation rule means no exit condition has yet been crossed.

The Share Price

Valuing Amazon depends on which measure of earnings to use, and the choice changes the picture considerably.

The approach used here is called an earnings yield on the free cash the business generates: take that cash, express it as a percentage of what the whole company costs to buy, and compare the result to a fair-value threshold. When the percentage is high, the shares are cheap; when it is low, they are expensive. The fair-value anchor here is set at 6.0%. On the basis of Amazon's 2025 figures and the current share price of $250, the yield works out at approximately 4.2% — below the fair-value threshold, meaning the shares trade at a premium. The buy level, where the yield would cross 7.5%, implies a share price of around $140. The trim level, where it would fall to 4.0%, implies roughly $263.

The market's view is more optimistic. At $250, the market expects Amazon to grow its earning power at roughly 9% per year in perpetuity — the bottom of the base case but above the bear case. The conservative fair value here is approximately $176 per share. That gap of 42% is not a prediction the shares will fall; it reflects that the market is paying for growth this framework will not credit until it has been earned.

In the base case — cloud sustains 18–22% growth, retail margins keep expanding, advertising compounds at 18%-plus, and capital spending normalises by 2027 — the three-year annualised return works out at approximately +29% per year, using the fair-value midpoint of $533. In the bear case — cloud decelerates to mid-teens and antitrust proceedings produce marketplace conduct restrictions — the annualised return is approximately −15% per year from a bear midpoint of $154. In the bull case — cloud accelerates to 25%-plus, Project Kuiper launches commercially, and advertising re-rates as a standalone franchise — the annualised return is approximately +57% per year.

Why the Market Sees It Differently

The conservative approach used here produces a fair value of approximately $176, based on current earnings capacity capitalised at a 6% yield. A six-method analysis incorporating historical multiples, peer multiples, and enterprise-value approaches produces a base-case midpoint of $533. The current price of $250 sits almost exactly between them.

The market is not pricing current earnings — it is pricing the earnings the current capital-spending cycle will generate in three to five years. AWS followed exactly this template between 2017 and 2022, when comparable concern about stranded assets was equally widespread, and the result was $46 billion of operating income by 2025. A buyer at $250 today is essentially making the same historical wager on the current cycle.

That reasoning is not irrational. But it means the current price offers no buffer if the pattern does not repeat. If AI workloads prove more competitive or more price-sensitive than assumed, the downside is a share price closer to $154 than to $250.

The Standing Orders

Two price alerts are in place:

  • A lower alert at $140: this is the buy-band edge, the level at which the owner earnings yield would cross 7.5% and the thesis, if intact, would justify adding to any existing position. Before acting, confirm that no exit condition has been triggered and that AWS growth is at or above 18%.
  • An upper alert at $263: this is the trim-band edge, the level at which the owner earnings yield would fall below 4.0%, signalling that the position is being priced at a premium requiring action.

Three standing orders follow from those alerts:

At or above $263 (trim territory): reduce the position by 25–33% to harvest the premium while keeping the core holding. Re-evaluate the remaining size after trimming.

At or below $140 (buy territory): add to the position in tranches over one to two quarters, provided no exit condition has been triggered and AWS growth remains above 18%.

At or above $421 (exit territory): exit the full position. At that price, roughly eleven years of base-case compounding would be priced in immediately — the market is extrapolating far beyond what the evidence supports. Redeploy proceeds at the next cheap-edge crossing or into a cheaper alternative. No re-entry within 90 days to defuse the psychological pull of a familiar name.

What's Wobbling

The capital expenditure trajectory is the load-bearing wobble. Amazon spent $43.2 billion on physical infrastructure in Q1 2026. AWS-specific equipment additions more than doubled year on year to $41.5 billion in the quarter alone. Management has been explicit — both in the quarterly filing and in public statements — that this spending will keep rising in 2026. The thesis's exit condition for capital expenditure requires the spending to remain above $120 billion in 2027 and 2028 simultaneously, without a commensurate acceleration in cloud revenue. That gate is deliberately set two full years out, and AWS revenue just accelerated to 28%. But the slope of spending is now firmly upward, and the gap between what is being spent and what is being earned will need to close visibly before the wobble resolves.

The AWS operating margin compression is a secondary wobble, and a more benign one. The 170-basis-point decline from 39.4% to 37.7% reflects depreciation charges rising as the infrastructure build completes and enters service — a mechanical effect that every prior capex cycle has produced. The margin remains well above both the 30% thesis target and the 28% exit floor. Whether utilisation catches up to capacity in the next two quarters is the question to watch.

Capital allocation is a chronic wobble rather than an acute one. Amazon has had $6.1 billion of share buyback authorisation sitting untouched since March 2022, when the shares were trading at $100–$130. Simultaneously, it committed $15 billion to OpenAI's Series C funding round in Q1 2026 and entered an agreement to commit a further $35 billion, while also investing $5 billion in Anthropic preferred stock and arranging an additional $20 billion financing facility. These are large concentrations of capital into a single AI ecosystem, made by a company whose own shares were demonstrably cheaper three years ago. The thesis description of capital allocation as "acceptable but not excellent" remains accurate.

What Could Still Go Wrong

  • AWS growth decelerates to mid-teens as Microsoft Azure and Google Cloud close the competitive gap on AI workload performance, and the depreciation from the current capex wave compresses operating margins simultaneously — producing both an earnings miss and a multiple de-rating.
  • The Federal Trade Commission's monopoly case against Amazon's marketplace produces a conduct remedy — not a structural breakup, but mandatory algorithmic neutrality on seller rankings — that structurally limits operating leverage in North American retail.
  • Capital expenditure remains above $120 billion in 2027 and again in 2028 without AWS revenue acceleration commensurately confirming the investment economics, triggering exit condition (4).
  • The Anthropic investment stake reverses in value in a subsequent funding round, producing a non-cash reported earnings headwind in 2026 that clouds the operating picture.
  • A change of political administration restores Federal Trade Commission aggressiveness toward Amazon's marketplace, and the regulatory tailwinds produced by the current administration's posture unwind over 12–24 months.

Gaps in the Picture

The valuation framework here uses a conservative measure of owner earnings that treats all depreciation as a proxy for maintenance capital requirements. Amazon does not separately disclose how much of its capital spending is required to maintain existing earning power versus how much is discretionary growth investment. If a meaningful portion of the current $128 billion-plus in annual spending is truly growth capital — as management argues and the prior cycle's returns suggest — then the true owner earnings figure is materially higher than the conservative estimate used here. That would bring the fair-value anchor closer to the market price. The gap between the two approaches ($176 versus a range closer to $490 on a more generous definition) is not a data error; it is an unresolved judgement about the nature of Amazon's capital intensity.

The fair-value bands analysis produces a wide dispersion across methods in the base case, ranging from $162 to $904 per share. This is not unusual for a company with Amazon's combination of size, segment diversity, and capital-cycle position, but it means the midpoint of $533 should be understood as the centre of a very wide range, not as a precision estimate.

The Rules

The pre-committed rules for this holding follow directly from the conditions above.

If the thesis is intact — all drivers confirming or mixed, no exit condition triggered — hold and do not add. The current wobble in capital expenditure does not change this: the exit condition requires two full fiscal years of excess spending, and that test cannot be run until at least the end of 2027.

If AWS quarterly revenue growth falls below 15% for two consecutive quarters, exit the full position within 30 days. No averaging down. Note the triggered exit condition for future reference. An FTC structural remedy or unexpected auditor change also triggers immediate exit rather than the two-quarter confirmation rule.

If AWS segment operating margin falls below 28% for two consecutive quarters without an explainable one-off charge, exit the full position within 30 days.

If the share price reaches $140 and the thesis is intact with AWS growth at or above 18%, add to the position in tranches over one to two quarters.

If the share price reaches $263, trim 25–33% of the position to harvest the premium. Re-evaluate the remaining size after trimming.

If the share price reaches $421, exit the full position. At roughly eleven years of base-case compounding priced in immediately, redeploy proceeds elsewhere. No re-entry within 90 days.

If capital expenditure remains above $120 billion in both 2027 and 2028 without commensurate AWS revenue acceleration in those years, exit the full position within 30 days. This condition cannot be evaluated until after the 2028 annual results.
