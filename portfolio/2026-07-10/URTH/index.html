About the Fund

All World is the iShares fund that tracks the MSCI World Index — a float-adjusted, market-cap-weighted measure covering large- and mid-cap equities across 23 developed markets. Roughly 70% of the basket is in US companies; the remaining 30% spans Japan, the United Kingdom, France, Switzerland, Germany, Canada, and sixteen other developed economies. In total the fund holds around 1,300 positions. The practical function of this breadth is to dilute the concentration risk of an all-US allocation: if US technology valuations compress sharply, the non-US portion of the basket offers some insulation.

The fund is a Delaware trust, so no K-1 tax form is issued. Annual cost is 0.24% — competitive for a global large-cap fund, though four basis points above the functionally equivalent IWDA vehicle listed in Europe (0.20%), a gap worth tracking given that a fee rise above 0.35% is a written exit condition. Securities-lending income is not disclosed and cannot be set against the cost. Tracking difference and tracking error for the most recent year are not available from the current data; those figures would need to be verified from the iShares factsheet directly.

The Case for the Bet

The holding exists because a broad basket of the world's highest-quality listed companies, bought at a fair price, should compound purchasing power over a long holding period better than a risk-free alternative. The bet is not that the basket is always cheap — the written thesis acknowledged from the outset that it was not — but that over the full holding period the basket's aggregate earnings yield will exceed the risk-free rate plus a reasonable equity risk premium of at least 2 percentage points. Six conditions set the floor:

  1. The basket's earnings yield must exceed the 10-year government bond yield over rolling two-quarter periods. If it falls short for two consecutive quarters, the equity risk premium has failed and the written exit condition (1) fires — the bet's core premise has not held.

The equity risk premium is −0.43%, negative across every one of the ten daily readings since 2026-06-16. The exit condition requires two consecutive quarters of negative readings, not consecutive daily readings. All ten entries fall within the third calendar quarter of 2026 (June–July). Q2 2026 data would be needed to confirm the quarterly persistence leg. The exit condition has not yet fired, but it is accumulating.

  1. The fund's annual expense ratio must stay below 0.35%. A fee increase above that level, with no fee response from iShares, is exit condition (2). The 0.24% current rate already runs four basis points above the equivalent IWDA vehicle at 0.20%, so a further hike would signal fee drift that the cheaper structure does not require.

The Share Price

The basket currently earns $1 for every $24.29 of share price — an earnings yield of 4.12%. The written fair-value anchor for this fund is an earnings yield of 5%, which implies a share price of $167. The trim threshold is an earnings yield of 4%, equivalent to a share price of $209. Today's price of $203 sits 12 basis points above the trim threshold — in practical terms, this is as close to the trim edge as the share price can get without crossing it. The basket is meaningfully above the $167 fair-value anchor.

The buy edge — where the basket would be genuinely cheap — corresponds to an earnings yield of 6.25%, or a share price of $134. That is 34% below today's price. The exit edge, where the basket would be fully priced relative to the risk-free rate, corresponds to an earnings yield of 3%, or a price of $279.

What's Wobbling

The only wobble is the equity risk premium, and it has been wobbling continuously since the first data point was recorded on 2026-06-16. Ten consecutive daily readings have shown the basket earning less than the 10-year Treasury on a trailing-earnings basis. The gap is not large — currently −0.43% — but the direction of travel matters: Treasury yields have risen while the basket's earnings yield has been flat, so the gap has been widening rather than closing.

The wobble does not cross the exit line yet. Exit condition (1) requires two consecutive calendar quarters of negative equity risk premium, not two consecutive daily observations. All ten negative readings fall within Q3 2026. The test cannot formally fail until Q2 2026 data is available to confirm a prior-quarter negative reading — which it is not, because the equity risk premium history only begins at 2026-06-16. In the absence of Q2 data, the exit condition has not fired.

What this means in practice is that the position is under active watch. A re-validation at the end of Q3 2026 will determine whether a full negative quarter has accumulated. If it has, and Q2 data also confirms a negative reading, the exit condition fires. If earnings yields recover as Treasury yields pull back, the wobble may resolve without triggering an exit.

What Could Still Go Wrong

  • The equity risk premium stays negative for two consecutive quarters. This fires exit condition (1) and the Rules call for closing the position. At current trajectories — rising Treasury yields, flat basket earnings — the risk of this outcome is not trivial.
  • A sharp multiple contraction in the absence of earnings growth. The basket trades at a trailing multiple of 24.29 times earnings, well above the long-run developed-market average of roughly 16–18 times. The bear-case five-year forward return, which assumes a compression back toward 14 times combined with 3% annual earnings growth, lands at roughly −6% per year. The basket can lose capital even while underlying businesses continue to grow.
  • Technology sector concentration amplifies drawdowns. Technology companies account for 31% of the basket — a structural property of float-adjusted cap weighting in an era of US mega-cap dominance, not an active tilt. If the technology sector reprices materially, the basket reprices with it.
  • Fee drift. URTH already runs four basis points above the equivalent IWDA vehicle listed in Europe. A fee increase above 0.35% would trigger exit condition (2) and make the cheaper UCITS structure the rational alternative.
  • Vehicle-integrity failure. A basket-integrity exit can be triggered by any of: an expense ratio breach, the asset base falling below $2 billion, top-1 concentration above 10%, or an MSCI World index methodology change. Each is independent of the valuation position. For this fund, exit fires when the share price reaches the exit edge, when a vehicle-integrity condition breaches, or when the named exit-condition trigger fires — any of three paths.

The Rules

These are the pre-committed rules that govern the position. They read as states of the evaluation, not as instructions from outside.

  • If the thesis is confirmed (all drivers healthy, basket fairly priced): hold — basket is reasonably priced and the vehicle is sound.
  • If the thesis is weakened (at least one driver challenges without an exit condition firing): do not add. Re-validate after the next quarter. If the equity risk premium stays negative or the median look-through return on equity keeps falling, swap to the IWDA vehicle (0.20% annual cost) or move to cash per the position-sizing rule.
  • If the thesis is broken (a vehicle-integrity condition breaches or a written exit condition fires): exit. Move proceeds to risk-free or to a holding that meets the value test.
  • If the share price falls below the buy edge ($134): add to the position. The basket is meaningfully cheap relative to the risk-free rate.
  • If the share price rises above the trim edge ($209): trim 25–33%. The basket is meaningfully expensive relative to the risk-free rate; harvest the premium.
  • If the share price rises above the exit edge ($279): exit. The basket earnings yield falls to 3.0% at that price — fully priced relative to the risk-free rate. Redeploy proceeds to risk-free or to a holding that meets the value test.
