About Bund Linker 2033

The Federal Republic of Germany issued this inflation-linked bond (ISIN DE0001030583, WKN 103058) in February 2021 with a 0.10% annual real coupon, maturing on 2033-04-15. The bond's principal and coupon payments are linked to the German Harmonised Index of Consumer Prices excluding tobacco (HICP ex-tobacco), as compiled by Eurostat. This means that if consumer prices rise, both the repayment amount and the coupon payment rise with them — the purchasing power of the capital is mechanically protected, rather than merely targeted.

Germany has never defaulted on a euro-denominated obligation and carries the highest available sovereign credit rating from all three major agencies. The issuance volume is €10.65 billion, making this a highly liquid instrument within the inflation-linked government bond universe. The next coupon payment falls on 2027-04-15 and interest accrues on an actual/actual basis following ICMA convention.

A buyer pays the dirty price of €119.99 per €100 original face value at settlement, not €94.92. The €94.92 is the quoted clean price in real terms (same purchasing-power units as the issuance date). To get the actual euros that change hands, multiply by the index ratio (1.26369), then add accrued interest of €25.07.

The Case for the Bet

The bet is structural: Germany's inflation-linked bond is held as a portfolio hedge against persistent euro-area inflation, sized as an allocation rather than a rates trade. Three conditions must continue to hold for the position to serve its purpose:

  1. Real yield does not fall into persistently negative territory on a sustained basis. The compound exit trigger fires if the real yield at current market price stays below −1.0% for at least 90 consecutive calendar days and EUR cash or nominal Bunds simultaneously offer a better real after-tax return. A brief dip below −1.0% does not alone trigger exit — both legs of the compound condition must be met continuously.

What Could Still Go Wrong

The three exit conditions in The Case for the Bet define the scenarios in which the position should be closed. Each has a distinct pathway:

  • Sustained low real yield combined with a superior EUR cash alternative. If the European Central Bank cuts its deposit facility rate sharply — returning to sub-zero territory as seen in 2014–2022 — and nominal Bund yields simultaneously fall, the linker's real yield could push below −1.0%. If EUR cash simultaneously offered a better real after-tax return (possible if a cash rate cut is delayed relative to market moves), the compound trigger would activate after 90 days. The historical precedent of extended negative real yields in the eurozone makes this the most realistic of the three exit paths.
  • German sovereign downgrade. Two-agency downgrade of Germany below AA− is a low-probability but non-zero risk, particularly in a scenario of sustained fiscal deterioration or a structural fracture within the eurozone that forces Germany into implicit liability for peripheral sovereign debt. A downgrade would not mechanically affect the bond's cash flows, but the exit rule exists because the credit assumption underpinning the position as a near-zero-risk inflation hedge would no longer hold.
  • HICP index methodology restructuring. If Eurostat were to change the reference price index — for example, by incorporating owner-occupied housing costs in a way that fundamentally alters the index's relationship to everyday consumer prices — the inflation-protection function of the linker could be compromised. This risk is remote but structural: it would require a regulatory decision at European level, and no such decision is pending.

The Rules

If the thesis is confirmed — all exit conditions intact and real yield within the hold band — hold the position. Real yield within band, German credit intact, and HICP linkage providing inflation protection as intended.

If the thesis is weakened — real yield has moved below 0% but has not yet activated the compound exit trigger — do not add. Re-assess by comparing the real after-tax return against EUR cash and nominal Bunds before any increase in position size.

If the thesis is broken — one or more of the three written exit conditions has fired — exit the entire position. Move proceeds to nominal short-duration EUR Bunds or EUR cash.

If the real yield rises above 1.5% (clean price falls to approximately €91 or below) — add to the position. A real yield above 1.50% on a seven-year AAA EUR sovereign linker is highly attractive; scale in proportionally to the inflation-hedge allocation target.

If the real yield falls below −0.50% (clean price rises to approximately €104 or above) — trim 25% on the first crossing. If the real yield remains below −0.50% at the next quarterly review at least 90 calendar days later, trim a further 25%. Park proceeds in EUR cash or short conventional Bunds. The 25 + 25 ladder is bounded — further crossings do not trigger additional trims until exit condition (1) fires.

If the compound exit trigger has fired — real yield below −1.0% for at least 90 days and EUR cash offering a superior real return simultaneously — exit the position. Switch proceeds to EUR cash or short-duration nominal Bunds; the inflation-hedge function is no longer additive relative to the available alternative.

Scenario Definitions

A scenario is a joint assumption about two independent variables: operating trajectory (how the holding's underlying cash flows grow relative to their long-run trend) and exit multiple (what the market pays for those cash flows at the horizon, relative to a fair-value anchor).

Scenario Operating trajectory Exit multiple
Bear Below trend Below fair value
Base At trend At fair value
Bull Above trend At or above fair value

Non-negotiable rule: In the bear case, both dimensions must be adverse simultaneously — a scenario where only the multiple moves is a re-rating scenario, not a bear.

Bear cases are comparable in structure across all holding types (company, ETF, bond), but not in magnitude. A company bear reflects idiosyncratic execution failure; an ETF bear reflects a broad market drawdown. Different mitigations, different diversification implications.
