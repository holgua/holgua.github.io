About the Fund

US Treasury 0-1y is a passively managed fund that tracks a basket of United States government bonds maturing within the next twelve months — instruments known as Treasury bills. These are the shortest-dated obligations of the federal government, typically used as a near-cash resting place by large institutions. Income from the bonds is not paid out to holders; it is reinvested inside the fund, so the fund's net asset value (the price of one unit) drifts upward over time at roughly the current yield on the basket. The fund is domiciled in Ireland, listed on the London Stock Exchange, and managed by Vanguard Group (Ireland) Limited.

The annual cost — the ongoing charges figure — is 0.07 per cent of assets. No constituent-level holdings data is available through the standard data feed for this Irish-domiciled fund; the fund classifies approximately 56 per cent of the portfolio as cash-equivalent instruments and 44 per cent as short bonds, consistent with an ultra-short-duration Treasury-bill mandate. Assets under management stand at approximately $2.4 billion (Trackinsight, 2026-06-19), and average daily trading volume is roughly $5.5 million — adequate for routine rebalancing at typical portfolio allocation sizes.

The Case for the Bet

The holding is a USD cash equivalent held inside a euro-denominated portfolio. The logic is not that Treasury bills offer an attractive long-run investment return — it is that they provide optionality capital: money that can be deployed quickly into equities or bonds when those assets reach attractive prices, at a cost (the 0.07 per cent annual fee) that is close to zero. The relevant question is therefore not whether the yield is attractive in absolute terms, but whether the vehicle remains sound and whether the cost of holding cash is justified.

For the bet to keep working, three conditions must hold:

  1. Fund cost stays below the exit ceiling. The ongoing charges figure must remain at or below 0.10 per cent on a two-consecutive-quarter basis. Above that level, comparable ultra-short-duration Treasury funds are available at lower cost, and the fund's cost advantage is lost.

  2. Fund size stays above the liquidity floor. Assets under management must remain above $1 billion. Below that level, the redemption and liquidity profile of the fund changes in ways that undermine its usefulness as instant-access cash.

  3. United States sovereign credit remains sound. If any two of the three major rating agencies (Fitch, Moody's, Standard & Poor's) downgrade US sovereign debt below AA–, the underlying obligor's creditworthiness has structurally shifted, and the cash-equivalent premise requires reassessment from scratch.

The Rules

If vehicle integrity remains intact and all three conditions above are met, hold the position. The rules do not call for adding or trimming on the basis of vehicle integrity alone.

If the basket yield falls to the bear-case scenario (3.5 per cent, share price approximately $63), trim at the margin. The USD T-bill carry versus EUR cash alternatives has weakened materially at that yield level. Vehicle-integrity rules still govern any large-scale exit.

If the basket yield rises to the bull-case scenario (5.5 per cent, share price approximately $40), add at the margin if cash is available. T-bill rates at that level are unusually attractive relative to EUR alternatives. Vehicle-integrity rules still govern any large-scale addition.

If any of the three exit conditions fires — ongoing charges figure above 0.10 per cent for two consecutive quarters, assets under management below $1 billion, or US sovereign credit downgraded below AA– by two agencies — exit the full position and reinvest in EUR cash or short nominal Bunds.

If the basket yield falls to the exit-band level (3.0 per cent, share price approximately $74), exit per the applicable exit condition.

If thesis conditions weaken without a full exit trigger — for example, the ongoing charges figure trends upward toward but does not breach the ceiling, or assets under management decline but remain above the floor — do not add.
