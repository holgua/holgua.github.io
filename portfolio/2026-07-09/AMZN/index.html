Recent Development

Amazon's latest quarterly report, for the three months ending in March 2026, produced the two headline numbers the thesis needed: the cloud business (Amazon Web Services) posted 28% revenue growth compared to the same period a year earlier, and advertising services grew by nearly 24%. Both figures sit comfortably above the written exit-condition thresholds. The committed backlog of long-term customer contracts reached $364 billion — roughly five and a half years of current revenue — and the company disclosed an expansion of its arrangement with OpenAI by an additional $100 billion over eight years, to be delivered on Amazon's own computing infrastructure.

The one shadow in the quarterly numbers is capital spending, which continues to accelerate. Cash capital expenditures reached $43.2 billion in that single quarter, more than double the $24.3 billion recorded in the same period a year earlier. The trailing twelve-month total sits at approximately $147 billion. This has not yet triggered a written exit condition — the condition targets 2027 and 2028 spending without commensurate cloud acceleration, and the cloud acceleration is plainly present — but the trajectory is widening the gap between investment and current cash generation with each quarter that passes.

For the implications of today's share price against these readings, see the Share Price.

About Amazon

Amazon operates three distinct businesses, each large enough to stand on its own. The retail and logistics operation — spanning a marketplace where roughly 60% of items sold are listed by independent merchants — generated combined North American and international sales of $588 billion in the year ending December 2025, turning the inventory, shipping, and fulfilment infrastructure into a platform that third-party sellers pay to access. That platform is inseparable from Prime membership, a subscription bundle of fast delivery, video streaming, and music that counted tens of millions of paying households as of 2025.

Running under the same roof is Amazon Web Services, which rents computing capacity, databases, and software tools to businesses and governments that prefer not to own their own servers. AWS generated $128.7 billion in revenue in 2025, with a 35.4% operating profit margin — meaning more than a third of every cloud-services dollar became profit. On top of the retail and cloud businesses sits an advertising layer: sponsored listings and display advertising on the retail platform produced $68.6 billion in 2025 revenue, growing at 22% year over year. The advertising business is capital-light relative to the other two, because it runs on the transaction-intent data that the marketplace and Prime subscription already generate. The retail operation funds the infrastructure; the infrastructure funds the cloud business; and the whole flywheel generates the audience that makes the advertising business valuable.

The Case for the Bet

The central idea is that Amazon runs two compounding profit pools — cloud computing and digital advertising — whose economics are still maturing, and that the retail and logistics base beneath them is large and durable enough to fund the investment those pools require. The specific conditions that must keep holding for the bet to continue working are:

  1. AWS quarterly revenue growth remains above 15% for any two consecutive quarters. The 15% level is the exit threshold; the thesis target is 18–20% or higher. The gap between the current reading and the exit threshold matters as much as the threshold itself.

Latest reading: Three months ending in March 2026: Amazon Web Services revenue $37.6 billion, up 28% from the same period a year earlier. The committed backlog of long-term contracts stands at $364 billion, weighted toward a five-and-a-half-year remaining life. The 15% exit threshold is not in sight.

  1. AWS segment operating margin stays above 28% for any two consecutive quarters. A compression below this level, absent a one-off charge, would signal that the infrastructure investment cycle is not converting into profitable capacity — that the economics of the artificial-intelligence capital build have broken rather than deferred.

Latest reading: Three months ending in March 2026: AWS operating margin 37.7%, down roughly 170 basis points from 39.4% in the same period a year earlier. The compression is attributed to infrastructure investment for growth. The 28% exit threshold sits nearly ten percentage points below the current reading.

  1. Advertising revenue growth stays above single digits in any two consecutive full years. Single-digit growth in the advertising business would signal the third compounding engine has saturated — that the marketplace audience is no longer generating incremental monetisation above what legacy digital platforms offer.

Latest reading: Advertising services revenue grew 23.9% in the three months ending in March 2026 compared to the year-earlier quarter. Single-digit annual growth is not approached.

  1. The International segment continues its trajectory toward sustained profitability. The segment was loss-making until recently; continued margin expansion validates the long-duration growth runway embedded in the thesis.

Latest reading: International operating profit was $1.424 billion in the three months ending in March 2026 (3.58% margin), up from $1.017 billion (3.03%) in the same period a year earlier.

  1. Consolidated capital expenditure in 2027 and 2028 begins to normalise, with commensurate AWS revenue acceleration already established. Exit condition four targets specifically the combination of elevated spending and absent demand evidence — not elevated spending alone. The current reading is elevated spending with strong demand evidence.

Latest reading: Trailing twelve-month capital expenditures at approximately $147 billion, with the AWS-related component more than doubling year over year in the most recent quarter. Exit condition four's demand-evidence leg is satisfied (28% AWS growth, $364 billion backlog), so the condition has not fired — but the trajectory is accelerating toward the 2027 test window.

  1. No structural remedy is imposed in the competition case against the retail business. A court-ordered separation of marketplace operations from the logistics and retail infrastructure would mechanically impair the cross-subsidy that makes Prime economics work. A settlement with behavioural constraints would not fire this condition.

Latest reading: The latest quarterly filing references competition-related litigation in general risk factors but discloses no new timeline and no structural remedy.

The Share Price

The valuation method used here expresses the company's earnings power as a yield — what fraction of the market's total price tag would be received each year if the business ran at its current cash-generation rate and paid it all out. A higher yield means the price is cheaper relative to earnings. A fair-value yield of 6.0% implies a price of approximately $176 per share at current earnings levels; Amazon's shares are priced at $250, which implies an owner-earnings yield of 4.2%. In other words, the market is willing to pay enough to leave the yield below the fair-value anchor.

By the valuation bands, shares sit in the upper half of the hold band. The trim line is $263 — the price that implies a yield of 4.0%, the point at which the Rules call for harvesting part of the premium — and the current $250 sits below that line, within the hold zone but not far from its upper edge. The fair-value anchor at $176 is 29% lower than today's price. Put plainly, the shares are not cheap — they are meaningfully above this valuation's estimate of fair value, though not yet at the point where the Rules call for reducing the position.

Why the Market Sees It Differently

The price says the business will earn more over time than our valuation assumes. Our view is more cautious.

The market's implied premium is 42.4% above our fair-value estimate of $176 per share. That gap works out to the market pricing in approximately 8.9% long-run earnings growth against our 8.0% assumption — a difference of less than one percentage point that compounds into a large price difference because the valuation method is sensitive to small changes in the assumed long-run rate.

Three coherent hypotheses explain the premium. The first is structural demand acceleration: AWS growing at 28% per year, with a $364 billion committed backlog and a $100 billion arrangement with OpenAI anchored to Amazon's cloud infrastructure, is direct evidence that artificial-intelligence demand is absorbing the capital spending at a rate above the 8% base assumption. The latest quarterly results support this hypothesis strongly.

The second hypothesis is operating leverage materialising across the business: consolidated operating income grew 30% year over year in the quarter ending in March 2026, while revenue grew 16.6%, and the international business turned in a 3.6% margin. The multi-segment profit expansion partially corroborates the idea that the business earns more per dollar of revenue than our base-case growth estimate captures — but AWS margin was compressing even as the consolidated numbers improved, so the support here is partial.

The third hypothesis is optionality embedded in the balance sheet that our model does not capture: Amazon carries roughly $48 billion in private artificial-intelligence equity (Anthropic and OpenAI combined), which generated $12.3 billion in mark-to-market gains in a single quarter in early 2026. These positions are not included in the earnings-yield calculation. Their value is real, but depends on private-market sentiment that can reverse.

For our view to catch up to the market price, either the long-run growth assumption would need to rise to near 9%, or the valuation model would need to be adjusted to capture the AI equity positions on the balance sheet — both of which the most recent calibration review has flagged as warranting attention before the next add decision.

What's Wobbling

Two signals register as neither clean confirmation nor an exit trigger. The first is AWS segment operating margin, which compressed by roughly 170 basis points year over year to 37.7% in the three months ending in March 2026. The compression is attributed to infrastructure investment rather than pricing erosion — the exit threshold is 28%, and the current margin sits almost ten percentage points above it — but the direction is worth watching as the capital cycle continues and depreciation charges on the expanded asset base begin to flow through. The written condition requires two consecutive quarters below 28%; a healthy but declining margin does not fire it.

The second is capital allocation. There have been no share repurchases in the most recent two reported quarters. The $6.1 billion authorisation sits untouched. The company has committed more than $50 billion to OpenAI and announced a $10.9 billion acquisition of a satellite-broadband business. These decisions are consistent with the thesis characterisation of capital allocation as acceptable but not excellent — the investments are strategically linked to the AWS business, not diversifying departures — but the failure to repurchase shares when they were materially cheaper a few years ago remains an omission worth noting.

Neither wobble changes the call. No driver registers as a challenge to the thesis, and no exit condition has fired.

What Could Still Go Wrong

  • AWS growth decelerates below 18% for two consecutive quarters — the most direct path to a weakened thesis. Microsoft's Azure and Google Cloud are both growing faster than AWS in artificial-intelligence workloads, and the competitive gap could widen further before the current capital wave has fully converted to revenue.
  • Capital expenditure continues above $120 billion into both 2027 and 2028 without the matching AWS revenue acceleration, triggering the written exit condition. The current trajectory — the AWS-related capital additions more than doubling in a single quarter — means the test window arrives relatively soon.
  • The competition case produces a structural remedy rather than a behavioural settlement. Any court order requiring marketplace separation or algorithmic restrictions would impair the cross-subsidy between Prime membership, fulfilment, and advertising that powers the retail flywheel.
  • The Anthropic position marks to market at a loss. The roughly $48 billion in private artificial-intelligence equity on the balance sheet is a Level 3 accounting asset — its value depends on private-market sentiment, which can reverse if frontier model development disappoints or competition erodes Anthropic's position.
  • Advertising revenue slows to single digits across two consecutive full years. The digital advertising market is competitive, and a sustained macro pull-back in marketing budgets could expose how dependent the advertising layer is on continuing marketplace growth.

Gaps in the Picture

The valuation used here computes owner earnings as the difference between operating cash flow and capital expenditure as defined in the official financial statements. Amazon's total infrastructure spending — including finance leases for data-centre equipment — runs at roughly $128 billion per year on management's own definition, approximately nineteen times the narrow figure. The narrow figure captures direct property purchases; the broader figure captures the full economic investment cycle. Both are valid analytical lenses; the reader should understand that the yield of 4.2% reflects the narrow definition.

A separate model calibrated to the company's own historical earnings multiples and scenario-based cash flow projections implies a fair-value range of roughly $530 per share in the base case, versus the $176 derived from the method above. The two approaches diverge because the scenario model uses normalised future cash flow as the input, while the earnings-yield method uses current cash flow as its numerator. The divergence is material, and a formal re-calibration of the valuation anchors is overdue — the most recent calibration review has flagged this.

The Rules

If the thesis is confirmed — all drivers confirming or at neutral watch, no exit condition triggered — hold without action and continue compounding the two profit pools.

If the thesis is weakened — any driver deteriorating but no exit condition yet fired — do not add to the position. Run a fresh thesis check on the specific deteriorating driver, most commonly AWS revenue growth or AWS margin. Wait for the next two quarterly filings before deciding; one quarter of data is not enough to distinguish a cycle from a break.

If the thesis is broken — any exit condition fires — exit the full position within thirty days. No averaging down. AWS revenue and margin conditions require confirmation across two consecutive quarters before triggering; a court order imposing structural separation or an unexpected change of auditor is an immediate exit regardless of the two-quarter rule.

If the share price falls below the buy edge of $140 — confirm first that the thesis is intact and AWS growth remains above 18%. If confirmed, add in tranches over one to two quarters.

If the share price rises above the trim line of $263 — trim 25–33% of the position to harvest the premium while retaining the core holding.

If the share price rises above the exit line of $421 — exit the full position. The market is pricing in roughly 11 years of base-case compounding instantly; redeploy proceeds at the next cheap-edge crossing or into a cheaper alternative. No re-entry within 90 days.
