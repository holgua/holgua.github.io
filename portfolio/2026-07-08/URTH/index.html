All World: The Bet Is Wobbling

The Bottom Line

Data as of 2026-07-07 · Latest filing 2026-07-07.

The bet is wobbling. The fund's underlying basket of roughly 1,300 shares across 23 developed markets is priced to deliver owner earnings (the profits that flow through to shareholders expressed as a percentage of what a buyer pays today) at 4.1% a year — below the 4.5% yield on a ten-year US Treasury bond. That gap means equities are, arithmetically, the worse deal at today's price. The share price sits at the trim edge, meaningfully above fair value. The one condition to watch is the equity risk premium — the margin by which the basket's earnings yield exceeds the risk-free rate — and it has been negative at every single weekly snapshot since the first reading was taken.

Recent Development

The equity risk premium — the gap between the basket's earnings yield and the yield on a risk-free ten-year US Treasury — turned negative from the first measurement taken on 2026-06-05 and has remained negative through every subsequent reading. As of 2026-07-07, the basket's earnings yield stands at 4.12%, while the ten-year Treasury yields 4.53%, leaving the risk premium at −0.41 percentage points — the widest negative reading in the recorded history to date. In other words, at current prices, a buyer of this global equity basket is accepting lower annual earnings per dollar invested than they would receive from a US government bond carrying no business risk whatsoever.

The underlying businesses in the basket remain in excellent shape: the median return on equity among the fund's nine largest holdings sits at 37.3%, comfortably above the 15% floor that is an explicit exit condition. Vehicle integrity — costs, fund size, and single-name concentration — is similarly clean. The problem is the price, not the quality.

What matters for the next gate is calendar-based. The written exit condition requires two consecutive quarterly negative readings, not daily or weekly ones. The current streak, covering approximately three calendar weeks, does not yet satisfy that test. The next quarterly snapshot falls in the three months ending September 2026; if the premium remains negative then, the exit trigger fires. About the Fund

All World is the iShares MSCI World exchange-traded fund (URTH), which tracks the MSCI World Index — a float-adjusted, market-capitalisation-weighted index of large- and mid-cap equities across 23 developed countries. Approximately 70% of the basket sits in US-listed shares, with the remaining 30% spread across developed markets in Europe, Japan, Canada, Australia, and a handful of others. The index holds roughly 1,300 positions, making it meaningfully broader than a US-only index fund.

The fund is managed by BlackRock's iShares division — the world's largest exchange-traded fund issuer by assets. Its legal form is a Delaware statutory trust, the standard US structure for exchange-traded funds; it does not issue the K-1 tax forms associated with partnerships. The annual cost to hold the fund is 0.24% of the value invested each year, charged as an ongoing fee deducted from the net asset value. That is competitive for a fund of this scope, though it runs four basis points (hundredths of a percentage point) above the functionally near-equivalent IWDA fund listed in London, which charges 0.20% and tracks the same index under a UCITS regulatory wrapper. The gap is small but it forms the basis of one of the written exit conditions: if the cost rises above 0.35% without a corresponding fee reduction elsewhere, the mandate as held here becomes hard to justify against the cheaper alternative.

No tracking difference or tracking error figures were available in the latest data snapshot; those metrics — which measure how closely the fund replicates its index — would normally come from the iShares factsheet and are not accessible through the automated pipeline used here.

The Case for the Bet

The bet is not that global equities are always attractively priced. It is that over a multi-year holding period, the basket's owner earnings yield — the fraction of the fund's total market value that the underlying companies generate in annual profits — will exceed the yield on a risk-free ten-year government bond by at least 2 percentage points, providing a genuine equity risk premium for the capital at risk. A second purpose is diversification: holding this fund alongside a US-only fund dilutes the concentration risk of an all-US allocation by adding roughly 30% developed-market exposure.

Six conditions must hold for the bet to remain intact:

  1. The equity risk premium stays positive — specifically, the basket's earnings yield must exceed the ten-year US Treasury yield. If the premium is negative for two consecutive quarterly snapshots, the exit condition fires regardless of the absolute level of the yield.

Latest reading: −0.41 percentage points (basket earnings yield 4.12% versus Treasury yield 4.53%). Negative across all nine recorded snapshots from 2026-06-16 to 2026-07-07. Exit condition requires two consecutive quarterly negative readings; the streak so far is daily/weekly and the quarterly test has not yet been triggered.

  1. The annual fee stays below 0.35% — BlackRock may not raise the expense ratio above 0.35% without a fee response that keeps this fund competitive with the cheaper UCITS alternative.

Latest reading: 0.24% — well within the limit. Exit condition inactive.

  1. Fund size stays above $2.0 billion — below this level, the bid-ask spread on transactions typically widens enough to erode the cost advantage of a passive fund. Current assets under management are approximately $8.1 billion, requiring a roughly 75% drawdown from today before the floor is reached.

Latest reading: approximately $8.1 billion — 4.0 times the floor. Exit condition inactive.

  1. No single holding exceeds 10% of the basket — at approximately 10% in a float-adjusted global index, a single company's dominance signals a structural regime change in the market, not normal market-capitalisation drift. The current top holding is NVIDIA Corporation at 5.17%.

Latest reading: 5.17% — well below the 10% ceiling. Exit condition inactive.

  1. The MSCI World index methodology remains stable — any material restructuring by MSCI Inc. that changes the developed-markets mandate, reclassifies countries, removes major constituents, or shifts away from float-adjusted market capitalisation weighting would be an exit trigger. This condition is monitored manually; the automated pipeline carries no signal for methodology drift.

Latest reading: No automated signal. No known changes to the methodology as of the latest refresh.

  1. The median return on equity among the basket's largest holdings stays above 15% — this is a quality floor. A collapse in the median to below 15% would indicate that the basket has lost the character of a diversified holding of capital-efficient compounders.

Latest reading: 37.3% median return on equity across the nine largest holdings (the pipeline returned nine rather than the target ten). Well above the 15% floor. Exit condition inactive.

The Share Price

The fund's earnings-yield valuation works in the opposite direction to a conventional share price: a higher yield means a cheaper fund (buyers get more earnings per dollar), and a lower yield means a more expensive one.

The fair-value anchor is an earnings yield of 5.0%, equivalent to a price-to-earnings multiple of 20 times — the long-run developed-market average. At the current basket earnings yield of 4.12%, the fund trades at a trailing multiple of roughly 24 times, a premium of about 21% above the fair-value anchor price of $167. The cheap-edge yield is 6.25% (a price of $134), and the trim-edge yield is 4.0% (a price of $209). Today's price of $203 sits within the hold band — above fair value and below the trim edge — but just $6 below the line at which the trim order arms.

The fund is therefore not cheap, not at fair value, and not at the trim edge — but it is close to the trim edge. The basket has been in this zone since the earliest measurements, and the spread between current price and the trim line has been narrowing as the price drifts higher.

Standing orders (actionable).
- A standing buy order at $134. The rules call for adding to the position when the share price closes at or below this line and the conditions are still working — specifically, when the basket's earnings yield has recovered above the risk-free rate by a meaningful margin.
- A standing trim order at $209. The rules call for trimming 25 to 33 percent of the position when the share price closes at or above this line, to harvest the premium while keeping the core holding.
- A standing exit order at $279. The rules call for closing the full position when the share price closes at or above this line, at which point the basket's earnings yield would have fallen to approximately 3.0%, offering less annual income per dollar than a risk-free Treasury at any reasonable spread.

Vehicle-integrity breaches and the named exit conditions (two consecutive quarterly negative risk-premium readings, fee rise above 0.35%, fund size below $2 billion, single-name concentration above 10%, index methodology change, or median look-through return on equity below 15%) are independent additional paths to exit — any one of them fires the exit rule without requiring the basket price to reach $279.

What's Wobbling

The equity risk premium is the only condition that is not holding. Every other driver — fund costs, fund size, single-name concentration, and the quality of the underlying holdings — is reading as expected.

The premium has been negative from the first measurement taken in early June 2026 and has widened to its most negative point yet at −0.41 percentage points as of 2026-07-07. At this level, the argument for holding the fund over a risk-free bond rests entirely on a belief that the premium will eventually recover — that earnings growth will raise the yield, or that prices will fall to more attractive levels. Neither is guaranteed.

The graduated response in the rules reflects this: the thesis is rated as wobbling (rather than broken) because the exit condition specifically requires quarterly persistence to avoid false triggers from short-term market noise. The regime is real and the trend is worsening, but the quarterly clock has not yet run long enough to trigger a mandatory exit. The next gate falls in the three months ending September 2026.

What Could Still Go Wrong

  • The equity risk premium stays negative through the three months ending September 2026 and then through a second consecutive quarterly snapshot, satisfying the written exit condition and requiring a full exit from the position regardless of price.
  • The fund's annual cost rises above 0.35%, with no compensating fee reduction from BlackRock, making the UCITS-listed alternative the more rational vehicle for developed-market exposure at the same economic exposure.
  • Fund assets fall materially from the current $8.1 billion level — through redemptions, a severe market correction, or both — toward the $2 billion floor, at which point trading spreads typically widen enough to erode the passive cost advantage.
  • A single-name within the basket — most plausibly one of the US technology mega-caps that together represent roughly 25% of the basket — rises to exceed 10% of total weight, signalling index-level concentration risk that a broadly-diversified mandate is not supposed to carry.
  • MSCI restructures the World Index in a way that materially changes the developed-markets mandate: country reclassification, removal of large constituents, or a shift away from float-adjusted market-capitalisation weighting would call into question whether the fund still serves the purpose for which it is held.

(For this fund, exit fires when the share price reaches $279, when a vehicle-integrity condition breaches, or when the named exit-condition trigger fires — any of three paths.)

The Rules

If the equity risk premium recovers and the basket is reasonably priced and the vehicle is sound: hold the position.

If the equity risk premium remains challenged or the median look-through return on equity keeps falling: do not add to the position. Re-validate after the next quarter. If either condition persists, swap to the IWDA UCITS fund (charging 0.20% a year) or move proceeds to risk-free assets per the position-sizing rule.

If a vehicle-integrity condition is breached or an exit condition fires: exit the position. Move proceeds to risk-free assets or to a holding that meets the value test.

If the basket's earnings yield rises above 6.25% (the buy edge, corresponding to a price around $134): add to the position. The basket is meaningfully cheap relative to risk-free.

If the basket's earnings yield falls below 4.0% (the trim edge, corresponding to a price around $209): trim 25 to 33 percent of the position. The basket is meaningfully expensive relative to risk-free; harvest the premium.

If the basket's earnings yield falls to or below 3.0% (the exit edge, corresponding to a price around $279): exit the full position. The basket is fully priced relative to risk-free; redeploy proceeds to risk-free assets or to a holding that meets the value test.
