PART I — INVESTMENT THESIS

1.1 One-Paragraph Thesis

PayPal is a global two-sided digital payments network with 439 million active accounts and $1.79 trillion in total payment volume, generating $33.2B in FY2025 revenue across transaction fees and a fast-growing value-added services segment (BNPL, lending, crypto, partnership revenue). The central return mechanism is network-effect leverage: a 21% share-count reduction since FY2021 combined with operating margin expansion (13.9% → 18.3%) drove a 37% EPS CAGR over three years and ROIC expansion to 15.0%. The central debate is whether branded checkout can re-accelerate after losing share to Apple Pay and Google Pay, while a new CEO (Enrique Lores, appointed February 2026) conducts a strategic review that has already produced a Q1 2026 operating income decline and FY2026 guidance calling for GAAP EPS contraction. EDGAR data through FY2025 confirms high earnings quality (Beneish M-Score −3.185, accruals ratios consistently negative) and $14B net cash, but Q1 2026 signals the re-investment year introduces near-term uncertainty.

PART III — COMPETITIVE POSITION (DISTILLED)

3.3 Moat — Type, Evidence, Trajectory

  • Moat type: Narrow — primary moat is two-sided network effect (439M active accounts); switching costs and brand are secondary (§3.3)
  • Network evidence: PayPal maintained dominant digital wallet position for 10+ years despite sustained competitive pressure; cited in §3.3
  • Durability threat: Apple Pay and Google Pay use device-native integration to bypass PayPal branded checkout; transaction count fell 4% while TPV rose 7% in FY2025, confirming mix shift (§3.4)
  • ROIC trend confirms residual moat: 8.2% (FY2022) → 11.7% (FY2023) → 12.5% (FY2024) → 15.0% (FY2025) — recovering and above any 7–9% WACC benchmark (§3.3)
  • Trajectory: Stable to Eroding — moat exists but open-ecosystem multi-wallet behaviour limits durability vs. closed-ecosystem competitors (§3.3)

PART IV — MANAGEMENT (DISTILLED)

4.1 Leadership, Capital Allocation, Insider Activity

  • CEO: Enrique Lores, appointed February 2026 (ex-HP Inc. CEO); prior CEO Alex Chriss departed; CFO: Jamie Miller (since Feb 2024, also served as interim CEO Nov 2025–Feb 2026) (§4.1)
  • Capital return cadence: $6.0B buybacks in FY2025 (86M shares); $1.5B in Q1 2026 (34M shares); $15B authorisation active; new $0.14/quarter dividend initiated Q3 2025 (§4.2)
  • Share count reduction: 1,168M (FY2021) → 920M (FY2025) — 21% reduction; buybacks are the dominant capital allocation lever (§6.3)
  • Insider direction: Neutral — all Form 4 activity in 2025–2026 is RSU/PBRSU grant and vesting; no open-market discretionary purchases or sales detected (§4.1)
  • Integrity: No restatements; PwC unqualified opinion; two-round CORRESP on revenue disaggregation methodology (May/June 2025) — no accounting concern; matter closed (§4.2)
