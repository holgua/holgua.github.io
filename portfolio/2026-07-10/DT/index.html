About Dynatrace

Dynatrace sells software that tells large organisations what is happening inside their technology. A modern enterprise runs on dozens or hundreds of interconnected software systems — applications processing customer orders, databases storing account records, cloud servers running the computation — and when something breaks or slows down, finding the cause across that web is enormously difficult. Dynatrace's product, OneAgent, deploys across every component of an enterprise's technology environment and correlates everything it observes into a single causal picture. Rather than flooding operations teams with raw alerts, it points to the root cause.

The business model is purely subscription-based: companies sign annual contracts and pay more as they add modules or increase the volume of data they monitor. Dynatrace Platform Subscription — the company's flagship licensing format, now covering 70% of the recurring-revenue base — commits customers to a platform spend upfront and lets them consume specific modules on flexible terms afterward. Revenue has grown from $930 million in fiscal 2022 to just over $2 billion in fiscal 2026 at roughly 20% a year, while the cash left over after running the business (free cash flow, meaning operating cash less the modest capital expenditure the company requires) has grown from $233 million to $530 million. The company holds $823 million in net cash and pays no dividend, deploying surplus capital through share buybacks.

The Case for the Bet

The bet rests on a specific claim: that Dynatrace has built an enterprise franchise so deeply embedded in its customers' technology stacks that replacement is too costly and disruptive to contemplate lightly, and that this moat will hold long enough for the cash-flow compounding to deliver substantial returns even from today's price.

For that bet to keep working, the following conditions must remain true:

  1. ARR growth (annual recurring revenue, in constant currency) stays above 13% for any rolling two-quarter window. Dynatrace's revenue is almost entirely subscription-based and renews automatically — the growth rate on this subscription base is the primary signal of whether customers are expanding their use of the platform or thinning out. The 13% floor is the level below which growth would imply the moat is losing its grip on the customer base.

Latest reading: The annual report for fiscal 2026 discloses total recurring revenue of $2,054 million, up 18% from the prior year (which itself grew at 15%). The filing does not separately break out constant-currency growth for the full year, but the reported 18% is well above the 13% threshold, and net revenue retention of 110% makes it implausible that constant-currency growth would fall materially below the reported figure.

  1. Net revenue retention stays at or above 107% — measured over two consecutive quarters before any exit is considered. Net revenue retention is the share of last year's subscription base still being paid by the same cohort of customers one year later. When it is above 100%, customers are expanding their spend; when it falls, they are trimming or leaving. The 107% floor is a two-stage rule: a single quarter below 107% is a watch signal, not an exit; two consecutive quarters below 107% would confirm moat erosion.

Latest reading: Dollar-based net revenue retention held at 110% constant-currency for fiscal 2026, matching fiscal 2025 and just above fiscal 2024 (111%). Three consecutive years at or above 110% is the strongest available signal that the switching-cost moat is intact. The two-stage 107% watch threshold has not been approached.

  1. Free cash flow margin stays above 20% for any full fiscal year. Free cash flow margin — the share of every sales dollar that becomes cash after running the business — is the measure of whether Dynatrace's moat translates into durable economic returns or is being competed away through rising costs. The 20% floor is the level below which cash generation would no longer justify the growth investment.

Latest reading: Free cash flow was $530 million in fiscal 2026, a margin of 26.2% on $2.0 billion of revenue. The margin is 6 percentage points above the exit floor and expanded from 25.3% in fiscal 2025 — the opposite of compression.

  1. DPS platform penetration does not stall below 72% of recurring revenue for three consecutive quarters. The Dynatrace Platform Subscription model (the flexible consumption format that currently covers 70% of recurring revenue) is the structural mechanism that locks customers in at the platform level before they select individual modules. If adoption stalls or management stops reporting the metric, the key expansion engine has lost momentum.

Latest reading: The annual filing describes the DPS model extensively but does not disclose a numeric penetration percentage. The prior public figure — 70% of recurring revenue — was stated in the November 2025 earnings commentary. Exit condition 5 (stalling below 72% for three consecutive quarters) cannot be evaluated from this filing alone. Watch the quarterly earnings releases for this figure.

  1. No CEO or CFO open-market equity sales exceed $5 million within any 90-day window. Open-market sales by the chief executive or chief financial officer represent a direct vote against intrinsic value by the people with the most complete picture of the business. The $5 million threshold per 90-day window is calibrated to distinguish ordinary pre-planned selling programmes from an executive acting on a pessimistic private assessment.

Latest reading: All 62 Form 4 dispositions filed in the last 12 months are tax-withholding events on restricted stock unit vesting — automatic transactions required by the tax code, not discretionary selling. Zero open-market sales are recorded. The company simultaneously repurchased $479 million of stock at an average of $37.71 per share.

  1. Palo Alto Networks or Cisco does not publicly announce named Dynatrace customer wins confirming bundled-suite displacement is occurring. The risk is not that a well-funded rival enters the market — they are already there — but that the bundled delivery of security and observability tools displaces Dynatrace at enterprise renewal time. A named customer win announcement is the specified evidence.

Latest reading: The filing confirms the Chronosphere acquisition by Palo Alto Networks (the preparatory signal described in the written conditions) but discloses no named Dynatrace customer displacement. The condition has not been triggered.

  1. Net cash does not fall below $600 million without a disclosed acquisition rationale. The cash balance is both a financial floor (preventing the company from needing to raise equity in a downturn) and a sanity check on capital allocation. An unexplained fall below $600 million would suggest either heavy share repurchases at prices implying poor capital discipline, or a hidden cash drag.

Latest reading: Net cash stood at $823 million at 2026-03-31, well above the $600 million floor. Long-term debt was $274 million. The $1 billion buyback programme active as of the year end ($849 million remaining) is the disclosed use of capital — no undisclosed drain is present.

The Share Price

At $45, the share price sits in the middle of the hold band. The valuation method used here is owner earnings yield — the annual cash generated by the business divided by the market's price for the whole company. Dynatrace generated $530 million of free cash flow in fiscal 2026; at $45 per share and roughly 304 million diluted shares, the market cap is approximately $13.7 billion, implying an owner earnings yield of about 4.0%.

The fair-value anchor for this holding is a yield of 4.4%, which translates to approximately $41 per share given current earnings. At $45, the stock trades at a 9% premium to that fair-value anchor — modestly above fair value. The buy edge (5.9% yield, around $31) is 31% below the current price; the trim edge (3.3% yield, around $55) is 22% above. The exit edge (not a valuation band but a yield level at which the implied long-run return falls below what any growth rate can justify) sits around $101.

Put plainly: the business is neither obviously cheap nor close to expensive. The share price is in the part of the band where holding without action is the neutral position.

Why the Market Sees It Differently

The share price is about 9% above the fair-value anchor — a mild divergence rather than a dramatic disconnect. The market currently implies that Dynatrace's free cash flow will grow at roughly 7–8% a year in the long run, which is approximately what the company's own bear-case scenario assumes. The base case, by contrast, assumes 16–17% sustained growth.

The market is not betting that Dynatrace is a bad business; it is pricing in the bear scenario while the business is executing the base case. Three possible explanations for this: first, that the AI commoditisation risk is more immediate than current net revenue retention data suggests and the market is pre-pricing erosion that has not yet appeared in the numbers; second, that the market is applying a higher discount rate to software businesses generally following the repricing of long-duration assets since 2022; third, that the large annual stock-based compensation charge ($300 million in fiscal 2026, equal to 60% of GAAP operating income) is obscuring the real cash-generation profile for investors who price on earnings multiples rather than cash yield. None of these explains why the gap persists given three years of confirmed 110% net revenue retention — but one of them eventually will.

What's Wobbling

Two things are less than fully settled in the current picture.

The first is the Palo Alto Networks acquisition of Chronosphere. That acquisition is the preparatory signal for exit condition 6 — the one that requires a named Dynatrace customer displacement to be confirmed before an exit decision is required. Preparatory signal is not exit signal. The filing discloses no customer losses to the combined Palo Alto / Chronosphere entity, and net revenue retention of 110% is the most direct available refutation of the displacement story. But the acquisition has happened, the product integration will follow, and the risk level has risen. This is the wobble to watch at every quarterly release.

The second is the gross margin nuance. Overall gross profit margin expanded from 81% to 82% in fiscal 2026, which sounds positive — and is, in aggregate. But within that, the subscription gross margin compressed one percentage point to 85%, driven by higher cloud hosting costs as customers adopt more platform features and consume more data. This is a predictable pattern when a subscription software business scales rapidly on third-party cloud infrastructure, and it does not signal pricing-power erosion. The revenue growth rate (19%) is absorbing the hosting cost increase while expanding overall margins. The trend worth monitoring is whether subscription gross margin stabilises at 85% or continues to compress — the written exit conditions do not include a gross margin clause, but the value analysis has identified 78% as the level that would suggest structural pricing pressure.

What Could Still Go Wrong

  • The net revenue retention rate, which has held at 110% for three consecutive years, could compress. A move below 107% for two consecutive quarters would trigger the primary exit condition. At that point, the signal is not "caution" but "exit the full position."
  • Datadog is growing faster in absolute terms and has more resources to invest in product development. If Datadog's AI-native monitoring product (Bits AI) or the general-purpose large-language-model tools that management has newly named as competitors begin winning at Dynatrace's renewal conversations, the net revenue retention rate is where that would first appear.
  • The company carries $300 million a year of stock-based compensation — equity grants to employees that dilute shareholders rather than flowing through the cash accounts. Dynatrace's buyback programme partially offsets this dilution, but if cash generation decelerates, the buyback pace would have to slow. The full economic cost of the business is higher than the cash flow statement alone reveals.
  • The Palo Alto Networks / Chronosphere combined platform could prove more competitive than the current data suggests. The integration of endpoint security, network security, and observability into a single vendor relationship creates procurement pressure that operates independently of any individual product comparison.
  • A sustained rise in interest rates or a compression of software-sector valuations broadly could reduce the market multiple applied to Dynatrace's cash flow even if the business itself continues to perform. The bear-case scenario assumes a market multiple of 20 times free cash flow — below where the stock has traded in recent years, but not an extreme assumption.

The Rules

The rules that govern action on this holding are as follows:

  • If the thesis is confirmed — all conditions working, no exit condition triggered — maintain full position, continue monitoring quarterly metrics (net revenue retention, constant-currency annual recurring revenue growth, free cash flow margin, platform penetration), and consider adding modestly on dips below $38 if retention holds above 107%.
  • If the thesis is weakening — net revenue retention declining toward 107% or annual recurring revenue growth decelerating toward 14% for one quarter — reduce to half position within 30 days of the trigger and monitor for two additional quarters before deciding to exit fully or rebuild.
  • If the thesis is broken — net revenue retention below 107% for two consecutive quarters, or annual recurring revenue growth below 13% for two consecutive quarters, or free cash flow margin below 20% for a full fiscal year, or documented Dynatrace enterprise customer displacement by Cisco or Palo Alto — exit fully within one quarter of confirmation. Do not average down on a broken thesis.
  • If the price falls below the buy edge — owner earnings yield above 5.9%, implying a price around $31 — accumulate toward a full position. The base-case discounted cash-flow estimate of $58–$86 per share at a 10–9% discount rate provides ample margin of safety at that entry.
  • If the price rises above the trim edge — owner earnings yield below 3.3%, implying a price around $55 or above — trim toward a half-to-two-thirds position. Exit fully only if the thesis is simultaneously breaking.
