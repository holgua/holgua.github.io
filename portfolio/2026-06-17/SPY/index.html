SP500: The Bet Is Broken

The Bottom Line

The S&P 500 basket — the 500 largest publicly listed American companies, held through a single fund — is currently flagging an integrity breach that the pre-committed rules treat as an automatic exit signal. Separately, the basket's earnings are producing less, in yield terms, than a ten-year US Treasury bond, which means equities are not compensating for their extra risk today. At $752 per share, the fund is priced meaningfully above the fair-value anchor of $558, placing it in expensive territory. The primary condition to watch is the source-data quality behind the NAV deviation reading; if that figure turns out to be a stale-data artefact rather than a genuine market gap, the exit trigger dissolves — but the valuation picture still warrants caution.

About the Fund

The SPDR S&P 500 ETF Trust — traded under the ticker SPY — tracks the S&P 500 index, a rules-governed list of the 500 largest companies listed on US stock exchanges. Membership is determined by an independent index committee at S&P Dow Jones Indices, which meets regularly to add and remove companies as their market values shift. SPY was launched in 1993 and is the oldest exchange-traded fund in the United States. It is structured as a unit investment trust, an older wrapper that differs slightly from the open-end fund structure used by more recent competing products, but which has proved entirely stable over three decades of operation.

The fund holds all 500 index constituents in proportion to their market capitalisation — larger companies occupy larger slices. As of the latest snapshot, technology companies make up roughly 39% of the basket, a concentration that reflects the extraordinary market valuations of a handful of mega-cap semiconductor and software names. That single-sector weight is high by historical standards and means that SPY moves more closely with the technology industry than a fully diversified broad-market fund would. Financial services (11%), communication services (11%), and consumer discretionary (10%) round out the major weights.

Annual cost: 0.09% per year.

SPY is structured as a unit investment trust — the oldest form of exchange-traded fund in the US market. No K-1 tax form is issued to holders; the fund uses in-kind creation and redemption, which typically results in no capital gain distributions being passed through to shareholders, though the precise history has not been confirmed from the fund's official annual report. No tracking difference or tracking error figures were available at this refresh.

The Case for the Bet

The bet is not that the S&P 500 is always worth owning. It is that, over the holding period, the basket's collective earnings power — measured as a percentage of the price paid — will exceed what a ten-year US Treasury bond pays, by at least two percentage points. That margin is the compensation demanded for accepting the volatility, execution risk, and drawdown exposure that equities carry but Treasuries do not. When that margin exists, the basket is worth holding. When it disappears — or when the vehicle itself becomes structurally unsound — it is not.

Six numbered conditions must stay satisfied for the bet to hold:

  1. Equity risk premium: The basket's earnings yield (total corporate earnings divided by the price paid) must remain above the ten-year Treasury yield by at least two percentage points. A negative spread for two consecutive quarters triggers an exit.
  2. Expense ratio: State Street must not raise the annual fund fee above 0.15% without a commensurate fee response. The current fee is 0.0945%.
  3. Asset floor: Total assets under management must stay above $100 billion. A fall below that level would indicate an extraordinary liquidity event.
  4. Single-name concentration: The largest single holding must not exceed 13% of the basket. Above that threshold, the fund has effectively become a concentrated bet on one company.
  5. Index methodology: The S&P 500's governing methodology must remain a broad-market mandate. Any material restructuring by the index committee that undermines that mandate triggers an exit.
  6. Look-through quality: The median return on equity of the basket's ten largest holdings — a proxy for how efficiently the underlying businesses use shareholders' capital — must stay above 15%.

Early-warning levels are configured below the hard thresholds: the expense ratio flags at 0.12% (before hitting 0.15%), assets flag at $300 billion (before hitting $100 billion), and single-name concentration flags at 10% (before hitting 13%).

The Rules

The pre-committed action plan covers five situations:

  • If all six conditions are passing and the basket is reasonably priced: hold. No action required.
  • If a condition is weakening but has not broken: do not add to the position. Re-validate at the next quarter. If the equity risk premium remains negative or quality is deteriorating, switch to an equivalent low-cost fund (IVV or VOO) or move to cash under the position-sizing rule.
  • If any vehicle-integrity condition breaches its hard limit, or if any exit condition triggers: exit the position entirely. Move the proceeds to a risk-free instrument or to a different holding that passes the value test.
  • If the basket's earnings yield rises above 6.25% (price falls below around $447): add to the position. At that yield the basket is meaningfully cheap relative to the risk-free rate.
  • If the basket's earnings yield falls below 4.0% (price rises above around $698): trim 25–33% of the position. At that yield the basket is meaningfully expensive; the pre-committed response is to harvest the premium while keeping the core holding.

The Conditions Today

Equity risk premium. The basket's earnings yield on 2026-06-16 stood at 3.71%. The ten-year US Treasury yield was 4.43%. The gap between the two — the premium the basket pays over a government bond — was therefore negative 0.72 percentage points. Equities are paying less, in yield terms, than Treasuries. That crosses the threshold in condition (1), but the exit rule requires the negative reading to persist for two consecutive quarters; this is the first such reading on record. The condition is challenged but the exit trigger has not yet fired from this source alone.

Look-through quality. The median return on equity across the basket's nine reported top-ten holdings (after deduplicating Alphabet's two share classes) was 37.3% — more than twice the 15% floor. Eight of the nine reported holdings earn above 20%. Only Tesla, at 4.9%, is well below the threshold; it is a single constituent within a 500-company basket. Quality condition passing comfortably.

Expense ratio. State Street's current annual fee is 0.0945%, with 0.056 percentage points of headroom before the 0.15% hard cap. The fee has not drifted. Condition passing.

Single-name concentration. NVIDIA — currently the largest holding — represents 7.89% of the basket. The early-warning flag sits at 10%; the hard cap at 13%. Both are clear. Condition passing.

Asset floor. Total assets at the most recent snapshot were $783.8 billion, against a $300 billion early-warning level and a $100 billion hard floor. Both are clear by a wide margin. Condition passing.

Index methodology stability. No automated signal is available for changes to the S&P 500 index methodology or the State Street trust structure. No news of any such change has been flagged. The condition is watched but silent.

NAV deviation — vehicle integrity. The fund's share price on 2026-06-16 was $752.48. The reported net asset value — the value of the underlying holdings — was $754.31. The resulting gap was 0.243%, against a 0.02% hard maximum. That crosses the threshold and automatically triggers the exit signal under the pre-committed rules. The caveat: SPY is the most liquid equity fund in existence, with $784 billion in assets and more than $48 billion in average daily trading volume. A persistent 24-basis-point deviation from net asset value is inconsistent with the market's continuous arbitrage mechanism at that scale. The most likely explanation is that yfinance used a stale prior-close NAV figure against an intraday price. Verification directly from State Street's end-of-day published net asset value, or from a financial data terminal, is the recommended first step before acting.

That is 1.29 percentage points below the 5.0% fair-value anchor, and 0.29 points below the 4.0% trim threshold. What's Wobbling

Two things are not right, and they are at different levels of concern.

The more urgent item is the NAV deviation. The 0.243% gap between share price and net asset value is the reading that fires the exit signal under the rules, and it almost certainly reflects a stale-data problem in the market-data feed rather than a genuine arbitrage gap. SPY's liquidity is such that a real deviation of this size would be closed by institutional arbitrageurs within seconds of appearing. The right course is to verify the net asset value directly from State Street or a terminal before treating this as an operational breach. If confirmed as a data artefact, the exit trigger dissolves and the position reverts to a trim signal — see below.

The second item is the equity risk premium. The basket's earnings yield at 3.71% is below the ten-year Treasury yield at 4.43%, for a negative spread of 0.72 points. Equities are yielding less than the risk-free rate. The exit condition requires this to persist for two consecutive quarters; this is the first such reading on record. But the direction is clear: if Treasury yields stay elevated and the basket's earnings do not grow faster than its price, the spread will stay negative. The second consecutive negative reading would trigger the exit on its own, independently of the NAV question.

If the NAV breach is confirmed as a data artefact, the current position would carry a trim signal — basket earnings yield below 4.0% — rather than an exit. That is still a meaningful caution: the basket is expensive on the rules' own measure, and the equity risk premium is moving in the wrong direction.

What Could Break the Fund

  • Prolonged negative equity risk premium. A second consecutive quarter of negative premium fires exit condition (1) on its own. Treasury yields staying at current levels while market prices continue rising would deliver that outcome.
  • Technology-sector correction. At 39% technology weighting, a sharp repricing of semiconductor and software names — NVIDIA, Apple, Microsoft, Alphabet, and Broadcom together make up roughly 29% of the basket — would translate into a significant basket drawdown even if the broader economy holds up.
  • Multiple compression from current levels. The bear-case five-year return of negative 2.8% a year requires only modest 3% earnings growth and a reversion of the price-to-earnings multiple from 27× to 18×. That terminal multiple is still above the long-run historical average of 16–17×. There is no scenario modelled that requires an extreme multiple to produce a material loss.
  • Fee drift at State Street. SPY's trust structure is the oldest in the market and operates on thin margins. If State Street raised fees materially, the pre-committed response is to rotate into a structurally identical but lower-cost alternative such as IVV or VOO. The early-warning flag sits at 0.12%.
  • Index methodology change. S&P Dow Jones Indices operates independently of State Street and can restructure the index without notice. A meaningful change to constituent eligibility or weighting methodology could alter the character of what SPY actually holds.
