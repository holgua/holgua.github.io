Steering and Alignment · 54-05-03

The ball nut assembly and the sector gear must be adjusted properly to maintain minimum steering shaft end play (a factor of preload adjustment) and minimum backlash between sector gear and ball nut. There are only two adjustments within the recirculating ball-type steering gear, and these should be made in the following order to avoid damage or gear failure.

  1. Raise the front of the vehicle and install safety stands under the front axle. Do not use a bumper jack to raise the vehicle.

  2. On a Montego, Cougar, Fairlane, Falcon, Maverick or Mustang, use Tool T64P-3590-F and disconnect the Pitman arm from the sector shaft. (Fig. 1).

    • On a Ford or Mercury, disconnect the Pitman arm from the drag link with Tool 3290.

  3. Remove the crash pad and hardware necessary to expose the steering shaft nut.

  4. Check the alignment of the shaft to the bearing. If the shaft is not centered in the bearing, make the check described in steps 5, 6 and 7. If the shaft is centered, proceed with step 9.

  5. Attach a 30 to 50 lb. capacity spring scale to the end of the shaft.

  6. Center the shaft by pulling on the spring scale. Note the reading.

  7. If more than 20 lbs pull is required to center the shaft, the steering column should be aligned. Refer to Group 13 of the appropriate Shop Manual for the proper procedure. If less than 20 lbs pull will center the shaft, it need not be adjusted. Proceed to step 8.

  8. Loosen the nut which locks the sector adjusting screw (Fig. 2), and turn the adjusting screw counterclockwise two complete turns.

  9. Measure the worm bearing preload by attaching an in-lb torque wrench to the steering wheel nut (Fig. 3). With the steering wheel off center, read the pull required to rotate the input shaft approximately 1 1/2 turns either side of center. If the torque or preload is not within specification (refer to Group 13), adjust as explained in the next step.

  10. Loosen the steering shaft bearing adjuster lock nut, and tighten or back off the bearing adjuster (Fig. 2) to bring the preload within the speci- fied limits.

  11. Tighten the steering shaft bearing adjuster lock nut, and recheck the preload.

  12. Turn the steering wheel slowly to either stop. Turn gently against the stop to avoid possible damage to the ball return guides. Then rotate the wheel 2 1/4 turns (2 3/4 turns for Ford and Mercury cars) to center the ball nut.

  13. Turn the sector adjusting screw clockwise until the specified pull is necessary to rotate the worm past its center (high spot).

  14. Check the steering wheel mesh back lash. No perceptible backlash is permissible at 30 degrees on either side of center.

    Check the steering wheel center mesh backlash using the following procedure:

    With the steering gear positioned on center and the sector shaft retained to prevent rotation, apply a 15-in-lb torque on the steering gear input shaft, in both right and left turn directions. The total angular travel of the input shaft cannot exceed 8 1/2 degrees with 8-in-lb net meshload.

  15. While holding the sector adjusting screw, tighten the lock nut to specification and recheck the backlash adjustment.

  16. Connect the Pitman arm and verify secureness of associated steering linkage.

  17. Check, and tighten where required, the instrument panel-to-steering column bracket bolts.

  18. Check the steering shaft for correct alignment (steps 5, 6 and 7).

  19. Install the crash pad and hardware removed in step 3.

Check Over-Center Mesh Load — Power Steering

  1. Disconnect the Pitman arm from the sector shaft.

  2. Disconnect the fluid return line at the reservoir. At the same time, cap the reservoir return line pipe.

  3. Place the end of the return line in a clean container and cycle the steering wheel in both directions as required to discharge the fluid from the gear.

  4. Remove the crash pad from the steering wheel and turn the steering wheel to 45 degrees from the left stop.

  5. Using an inch-pound torque wrench on the steering wheel nut, determine the torque required to rotate the shaft slowly through an approximate 1/8 turn from the 45 degrees position.

  6. Turn the steering gear back to center, then determine the torque required to rotate the shaft back and forth across the center position. Loosen the adjuster nut, and turn the adjuster screw (Fig. 4) until the reading is 8-9 in-lb greater than the torque 45 degrees from the stop (max. 14 in-lb).

    Tighten the lock nut while holding the screw in place.

  7. Recheck the readings and replace the Pitman arm and steering wheel crash pad.

  8. Connect the fluid return line to the reservoir and fill the reservoir to the proper level with the specified type of lubricant.
