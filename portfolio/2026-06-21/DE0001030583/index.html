The Verdict

The Bund Linker 2033 is a German government bond whose principal and coupon payments
rise with consumer-price inflation (as measured by the HICP ex-tobacco index, a
Eurostat benchmark). Every condition written into the holding's thesis is working as
intended: the real yield is positive and within its target range, Germany's AAA credit
rating is undisturbed at all three major agencies, the HICP index methodology is
unchanged, and the compound exit mechanism — which fires only when the real yield has
been below −1.0% for ninety consecutive days and EUR cash simultaneously offers a
better real return — is inactive. At today's clean price of €94.96, the bond provides
positive inflation-adjusted income and has not approached any of the exit
thresholds written into the thesis. The one thing worth watching
is the breakeven inflation rate, which ticked down two basis points
since the prior reading and is approaching a level where it would move from confirming
to neutral — though it remains comfortably within the acceptable range today.

About the Bond

The Federal Republic of Germany issues two broad categories of bonds: conventional
(fixed-coupon) Bunds and inflation-linked Bunds, known as Bundesobligationen
inflationsindexiert or simply linkers. The Bund Linker 2033 (ISIN DE0001030583)
belongs to the second category. It was issued in February 2021, carries a stated coupon
of 0.10% per year, and matures on 2033-04-15. At issuance the German Finance Agency
sold €10.65 billion of it to the market.

What makes this bond different from a conventional Bund is the index ratio. Every
payment — coupon and principal — is multiplied by a number that grows in line with
the HICP ex-tobacco index (a measure of consumer-price inflation published by
Eurostat for the euro area). When inflation rises, the index ratio rises, and every
euro of promised payment is worth proportionally more in nominal terms. Put plainly:
a holder who buys the bond today and holds to maturity will receive 25% more in
nominal principal if consumer prices are 25% higher at maturity than on the reference
date — the real purchasing power of the principal is preserved.

The price that appears on market screens — €94.96 today — is the clean price: the
price in real terms, using the same purchasing-power units as the original issuance
date. The actual euros that change hands at settlement (the dirty price) are
calculated differently. A buyer pays the dirty price of €119.65 per €100 original
face value at settlement, not €94.96. The €94.96 is the quoted clean price in real
terms (same purchasing-power units as the issuance date). To get the actual euros
that change hands, multiply by the index ratio (1.25891), then add accrued interest.
The index ratio of 1.25891 reflects the cumulative rise in the HICP ex-tobacco index
since the bond's base reference date — roughly 26% of consumer-price inflation since
issuance in 2021.

The Case for the Bet

The bet is structural, not tactical. The position is sized as a portfolio
inflation-hedge allocation — a slice of the portfolio that is designed to hold its
real purchasing power regardless of what consumer prices do over the next several
years. It is not a rates trade; the bet does not depend on interest rates falling.

Four conditions must continue holding for the bet to work as intended:

  1. Real yield within band. The real yield at the current clean price must remain
    above −1.0%. At any yield below that level, the bond embeds a guaranteed real
    loss that is large enough to outweigh the inflation-hedge function, especially once
    the comparison against EUR cash is taken into account. The full exit trigger for
    condition (1) is compound: the yield must stay below −1.0% for at least ninety
    days and EUR cash must simultaneously offer a superior after-tax real return.
    The buy band is set at 1.50% — a real yield that high on a seven-year AAA
    sovereign linker is considered highly attractive and warrants adding to the
    position.

  2. Breakeven inflation in range. The breakeven inflation rate (the gap between
    the conventional Bund yield and the linker's real yield for similar maturities)
    must remain broadly consistent with the European Central Bank's 2% inflation
    target. A breakeven rate far below 1.5% would suggest the market is pricing in
    persistent deflation, which would reduce the linker's actual payments and make
    a conventional Bund a better choice. A breakeven rate above 3% would suggest
    speculative excess, which would inflate the linker's market price beyond
    fundamental value. The acceptable range is 1.5%–3.0%.

  3. Germany sovereign credit intact. Exit condition (2) fires if any two of the
    three major rating agencies — Fitch, Moody's, and S&P — downgrade Germany
    below AA-. At the current AAA/Aaa ratings across all three agencies, condition
    (2) is far from active.

  4. HICP index methodology stable. Exit condition (3) fires if Eurostat
    materially restructures the HICP ex-tobacco index in a way that severs the link
    between the index and actual consumer-price inflation — for example by
    substituting a different reference basket that systematically understates
    experienced inflation. There is no current indication of any such change.

The Rules

Five pre-committed rules govern the position. No new judgement is required until one
of the five states applies.

  • If the thesis is confirmed (all conditions intact, real yield within band): hold.
    The inflation-hedge function is working as intended.

  • If the thesis is weakened (one condition is challenged but no exit trigger has
    fired): do not add. Re-assess the real yield against EUR cash and nominal Bunds
    before any further action.

  • If the thesis is broken (one or more exit conditions have fired): close the full
    position and move proceeds to nominal short-duration Bunds or EUR cash.

  • If the real yield rises above 1.50% (the buy band): add to the position in
    proportion to the inflation-hedge allocation target. A seven-year AAA sovereign
    linker yielding 1.5% in real terms is highly attractive.

  • If the real yield falls below −0.50% (the trim band): trim 25% of the position
    on first crossing. If the yield remains below −0.50% at the next quarterly review
    (at least ninety calendar days later), trim an additional 25%. This 25-plus-25
    ladder is bounded — further crossings do not trigger further trims until the
    compound exit trigger fires.

  • If the compound exit trigger fires (real yield below −1.0% for ninety-plus days
    and EUR cash offers a better real return): close the full position and move
    proceeds to EUR cash or short-duration nominal Bunds.

The Real Yield Today

The real yield of 0.87% is the return above consumer-price inflation that the bond
delivers to a buyer at today's clean price, held to the 2033-04-15 maturity. For a
seven-year AAA sovereign bond denominated in EUR, this is a modest positive real
yield — not particularly attractive relative to the buy threshold of 1.50%, but
clearly positive and consistent with holding the position as a portfolio hedge rather
than a source of high real income.

The relevant comparison is EUR cash. The European Central Bank's deposit facility
rate stands at 2.25%. With break-even inflation at 1.93%, EUR cash earns a real
yield of approximately +0.32%. The linker's 0.87% real yield is therefore +55 basis
points ahead. This comparison is pre-tax — German income tax applies to both legs
(Abgeltungssteuer on cash interest and on the linker's coupon plus index accrual);
the after-tax direction is not modelled here.

The linker is ahead, but only modestly. The 55-basis-point advantage is meaningful
enough to confirm the thesis, but not so large that the position looks obviously
cheap relative to the alternative. The position earns its keep as a hedge, not as
a yield instrument.

What's Wobbling

No driver is currently challenged or mixed. The only item worth flagging is the
breakeven inflation rate, which has drifted down by two basis points since the
prior reading — from 1.95% on 2026-06-17 to 1.93% today. This is immaterial in
isolation: 1.93% is solidly within the 1.5%–3.0% acceptable range and consistent
with the ECB's 2% target. If the rate continued drifting lower and approached
1.5%, it would begin to suggest that the market is pricing in below-target
inflation, which would reduce the linker's effective income and make a conventional
Bund relatively more attractive. At the current rate, no reclassification is
warranted.

One operational note: the Bundesbank's public data service is anticipated to shut
down around 2026-06-30. After that date, the nominal Bund yields used to compute
the breakeven may become unavailable, which would cause the breakeven inflation
driver to revert to a silent state (no data, no confirmation, no challenge). This
would not change the recommendation, but it would reduce the information available
at each quarterly review. The ratings and index-methodology sidecars are unaffected.

What Could Still Go Wrong

  • Real yields fall sharply. A significant decline in real yields — driven by ECB
    rate cuts, a flight-to-safety bid, or falling inflation expectations — would push
    the clean price well above €104 and start activating the trim ladder. A sustained
    move below −1.0% accompanied by EUR cash offering better real returns would fire
    the exit trigger.

  • Inflation expectations collapse. If the breakeven inflation rate fell below
    1.5%, the market would be pricing in persistent below-target inflation. In that
    environment, the linker's indexed payments would be smaller than projected, and a
    conventional Bund would offer a better nominal return without the complexity of
    indexation.

  • ECB rate decisions diverge. The comparison against EUR cash depends on the
    ECB deposit rate. A large reduction in the policy rate would reduce the cash real
    yield and widen the linker's advantage — but it would also likely push nominal
    yields down and the linker's price up toward the trim band. The relationship is
    non-linear.

  • Bundesbank data loss. If the nominal Bund yield data becomes unavailable
    after the anticipated service shutdown, the breakeven driver becomes silent. A
    silent driver does not trigger any action, but it reduces the monitoring quality
    of the position.

  • Index methodology change. A restructuring of the HICP ex-tobacco index by
    Eurostat that systematically understates consumer-price inflation — for example, a
    change in the substitution methodology or the reference basket — would sever the
    link between the bond's indexed payments and experienced inflation. Exit condition
    (3) is designed for this event.
