2. Equity Risk Premium

Metric Value

Equity Risk Premium 0.15%

Judgment: MIXED — ERP of 0.15% falls in the 0.0–2.0% MIXED band (confirm_min_pct: 2.0%; mixed_min_pct: 0.0%).

The ERP of just 0.15 percentage points is the single most important number in this snapshot. At current US 10-year yields of ~4.70%, the equal-weight basket is offering almost no premium over a risk-free government bond. Historically a healthy equity risk premium runs 3–5%; anything below 2% is a caution flag, and below 1% it is hard to justify equity risk on a pure yield basis. The equal-weight tilt should structurally deliver a higher basket EY than cap-weighted MSCI World — and it does, compared to URTH — but high bond yields have eroded that advantage. The ERP does not yet reach zero (which would trigger a CHALLENGES verdict per the thesis), but the margin is thin enough that one quarter of further yield compression could push it negative.

5. Owner Perspective (Buffett Frame)

"Would Berkshire deploy cash into this basket today?"

At a 0.15% equity risk premium and a basket earnings yield of 4.85%, Berkshire would almost certainly not deploy significant capital into this basket today. The core Berkshire test is simple: can we earn more than the risk-free rate, with reasonable certainty, by owning this set of businesses? At current 10-year yields near 4.70%, the answer is barely yes — and "barely yes" is not Buffett's standard. The median ROE of the visible top-10 drift leaders (30.93%) is attractive at face value, but Berkshire invests in businesses it understands and controls, not sampling-replicated equal-weight baskets of 1,500 names. The structural virtue of MWEQ — deliberate dilution of mega-cap concentration, higher EY than URTH, developed-market breadth — is a diversification argument, not a value argument. Buffett would frame this as "paying fair price for average businesses, diversified beyond the point of edge." That is a reasonable holding posture for a portfolio allocator who accepts index-like returns; it is not a compelling deployment of opportunistic capital.
