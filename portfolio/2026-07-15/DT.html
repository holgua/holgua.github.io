Recent Development

The annual filing for the fiscal year ending 2026-03-31 confirmed all three load-bearing pillars of the thesis at once: net customer expansion held at 110% for a third consecutive year, free cash flow grew 23% to $530 million at a 26% margin, and management executed a $479 million share buyback and announced a fresh $1 billion repurchase programme. On the numbers alone it is a clean filing.

The wobble is competitive. Palo Alto Networks acquired Chronosphere in 2026, as confirmed in the risk factors section of the annual filing — the preparatory signal for one of the written exit conditions. The condition itself requires publicly announced named Dynatrace enterprise customer wins by the acquirer. No such disclosure exists in this filing. The 110% net customer expansion rate is the primary counter-signal: if large enterprises were actively switching to a bundled Palo Alto suite, expansion from the existing base would not hold at that level.

The filing also introduces, for the first time in management's own language, the risk of tools built using natural-language prompts ("vibe coding") and generic large-language-model systems as a competitive vector against specialised observability platforms. This is an incremental headwind, not yet quantified. For the full valuation picture see the Share Price; for the competitive detail see What's Wobbling.

About Dynatrace

Dynatrace sells software that monitors software. Its platform is installed as an automated agent across the full technology stack of large enterprises — banks, airlines, retailers, government agencies — collecting performance data from every component of every application running in the cloud or on-premise. That data flows into a proprietary data repository called Grail, and an artificial-intelligence engine called Smartscape maps the causal relationships between components in real time, so that when something goes wrong, the platform can identify the root cause rather than simply alert that a problem exists. Removing Dynatrace from a large enterprise requires years of re-instrumentation work across the entire infrastructure — which is why its customers tend to stay.

Revenue for the year ended 2026-03-31 reached $2,018 million, up 19% from the prior year, almost entirely from subscriptions (gross margin 81 cents on every dollar of subscription revenue). Free cash flow was $530 million — 26 cents of every revenue dollar — and has held between 25% and 29% of revenue for five consecutive fiscal years. The company carries $823 million of net cash (cash minus debt) and does not pay a dividend; it returns surplus capital through buybacks. The co-founder and chief technology officer, Bernd Greifeneder, remains in the business.

The Case for the Bet

The central argument is that Dynatrace's embedded monitoring agent and proprietary data architecture create switching costs strong enough to sustain above-market customer retention and growth rates for at least the next several years, and that the current share price does not fully reflect that durability. Eight conditions must remain intact for the bet to hold:

  1. Annual recurring revenue growth above 13% in constant-currency terms for any two consecutive quarters. Growth below this floor for two consecutive quarters signals that competitive or macro pressure is materially eroding the platform's new-business momentum.

Latest reading: Total annual recurring revenue reached $2,054 million at 2026-03-31, up 18% year-on-year — reversing a deceleration from 21% (2024) to 15% (2025). The annual filing does not separately disclose a constant-currency growth rate; the 18% reported growth is used as a proxy and remains comfortably above the 13% floor.

  1. Net revenue retention at or above 107%. The single most important moat-health signal: what proportion of the prior year's contracted revenue is still flowing from the same customers one year later, including their expansions and net of any cancellations. One quarter below 107% triggers a watch; two consecutive quarters below 107% triggers the exit.

Latest reading: Dollar-based net retention held at 110% in constant-currency terms for the year ended 2026-03-31, matching the prior year and within one percentage point of the year before that (111%). Three consecutive years at or above 110% is the most credible available confirmation that the switching-cost moat remains intact.

  1. Platform subscription penetration above 72% of annual recurring revenue for any sustained period. The Dynatrace Platform Subscription lets customers make a platform-level commitment and allocate spend across modules as they use them — a model that drives natural cross-sell without a new procurement cycle. Penetration stalling below 72% for three consecutive quarters, especially if management stops reporting the metric, would signal a stall in the platform transition.

Latest reading: The annual filing describes the platform subscription model extensively but discloses no numeric penetration percentage. The last reported figure — approximately 70% of annual recurring revenue — came from prior quarterly commentary. This condition cannot be evaluated from the annual filing alone; the metric to watch is the quarterly press release.

  1. Free cash flow margin above 20% for any full fiscal year. A sustained margin below this floor would indicate that cost discipline is breaking down — through higher infrastructure costs, share-based compensation growing faster than revenue, or deteriorating pricing power.

Latest reading: Free cash flow was $529.5 million for the year ended 2026-03-31, a 26.2% margin on $2,018 million of revenue, up from $430.6 million (25.3% margin) the prior year. Operating leverage is confirmed: free cash flow grew 23% against 19% revenue growth.

  1. No CEO or chief financial officer open-market equity sales of five million dollars or more in any 90-day window. Large open-market sales by the two most senior executives — distinct from routine tax-withholding dispositions on equity grants — would signal that insiders view shares as fairly or expensively priced relative to their own assessment of the business.

Latest reading: No CEO or chief financial officer open-market sales were disclosed in the filing. The company repurchased $478.7 million of its own shares during the year (11.4 million shares) and launched a new $1 billion authorisation with $848.6 million remaining at 2026-03-31. This is the opposite of insider selling.

  1. No public announcement of named Dynatrace enterprise customer wins by Cisco/Splunk or Palo Alto/Chronosphere. The primary competitive exit condition. The trigger fires when the displacing vendor publicly announces that named Dynatrace enterprise customers have switched to their bundle — confirmation that displacement is occurring at scale, not merely threatened.

Latest reading: The annual filing confirms Palo Alto Networks acquired Chronosphere in 2026 — the preparatory step. No announcement of named Dynatrace customer wins has been made by either Palo Alto or Cisco. Net retention of 110% is the primary counter-signal.

  1. Net cash above $600 million without a disclosed acquisition rationale. Protects against an undisclosed deterioration in the balance sheet or a capital allocation decision inconsistent with the thesis.

Latest reading: Net cash was $823 million at 2026-03-31, well above the $600 million floor. No acquisitions were disclosed.

  1. Artificial intelligence commoditisation risk remains containable. A broader watch condition: if large-language-model-based monitoring tools prove capable of replicating Dynatrace's causal topology engine at a fraction of the switching cost, the pricing power underpinning the thesis compresses over a multi-year horizon. This is a directional risk without a binary trigger.

Latest reading: The annual filing introduces, for the first time in formal disclosure, the risk of "vibe coding" and generic large-language-model tools as a competitive vector. No quantified customer impact is evident in the retention figures. Net retention of 110% is the most credible available counter-signal.

The Share Price

At $45, the share price sits in the middle of the hold band. The underlying valuation metric is free cash flow as a share of the company's market value: $530 million in free cash flow divided by a market value of roughly $13.2 billion gives a yield of approximately 4%. Fair value is set at a yield of 4.4%, which translates to a share price of roughly $41. The buy edge (a yield of 5.9%) implies a price around $31; the trim edge (a yield of 3.3%) implies a price around $55.

Put plainly: the business is generating cash at a rate slightly below the fair-value yield, and the price reflects that — it sits roughly 9% above the fair-value anchor. That is modestly above fair value, but within the band. Both order lines remain several percentage points away from today's price, and per the Rules no action is warranted on price alone while conditions are intact and the price stays within band.

What's Wobbling

Two wobbles are visible in the current filing. Neither changes the verdict, but both require monitoring.

Competitive consolidation. Palo Alto Networks' acquisition of Chronosphere is now confirmed in the annual filing's own risk factor section. In prior filings, this scenario was described as a risk; it is now a fact. The exit condition still requires a public announcement of named Dynatrace customer wins by the acquirer — that announcement has not come. But the 12 to 18 months ahead carry elevated risk: the combined Palo Alto/Chronosphere platform will be actively marketed to enterprise accounts, some of which are also Dynatrace customers. The 110% net retention figure for 2026-03-31 precedes that marketing effort's full impact. Additionally, the annual filing names generic large-language-model tools and software built through natural-language prompts as a new competitive category — language absent from prior filings — adding a second, longer-horizon threat vector.

Subscription gross margin. Subscription gross margin compressed one percentage point to 85%, from 86% the prior year, as higher cloud infrastructure hosting costs followed increased customer usage of the platform. Total gross margin improved one point to 82% over the same period. This is a predictable scale-up pattern at this volume of cloud-hosted data, and the compression is modest. The metric warrants monitoring in subsequent filings — if subscription gross margin falls further as the AI platform features (Grail, Dynatrace Intelligence) consume more infrastructure, it could indicate that the platform's cost structure is becoming less favourable.

What Could Still Go Wrong

  • Net retention falls below 107%. If existing customer expansion stalls to the point where net retention falls below 107% for two consecutive quarters, the moat-health signal has broken and a full exit is called for — not a reduction, but a full exit. Three years at 110% is encouraging, but the competitive pressure from Cisco/Splunk and Palo Alto/Chronosphere is still building.
  • Bundled rivals announce named customer wins. If Palo Alto Networks or Cisco publicly announces that named Dynatrace enterprise customers have switched to their integrated observability-and-security bundle, exit condition six fires regardless of how other metrics look at that moment.
  • Generic artificial-intelligence tools commoditise observability. If tools built from general-purpose large language models prove capable of matching Dynatrace's causal topology engine for enterprise monitoring without the instrumentation overhead, the switching-cost advantage erodes over a multi-year horizon. This is not a 12-month signal; it is a structural risk to the thesis's durability beyond the three-year window.
  • Revenue growth decelerates sharply. Annual recurring revenue growing below 13% in constant-currency terms for two consecutive quarters would signal a meaningful deterioration in the competitive or macro environment. The business has grown at or above 18% for several consecutive years; a sudden deceleration to this floor would be a qualitative as well as quantitative signal.
  • Share-based compensation stays elevated. At roughly 15% of revenue ($300 million in the fiscal year just ended), share-based compensation means that the cash available to shareholders after paying for staff equity is considerably less than the headline free-cash-flow figure. If this ratio does not compress as the business scales, the economics are less attractive than they appear from free cash flow alone.

The Rules

If the thesis is confirmed — all conditions intact, no exit condition crossed — maintain the full position and continue monitoring quarterly net retention, annual recurring revenue growth in constant currency, free cash flow margin, and platform subscription penetration.

If the thesis is weakening — net retention declining toward 107%, annual recurring revenue growth decelerating toward 14%, or platform subscription penetration stalling below 72% — reduce to a half position within 30 days of the trigger. Monitor for two further quarters before deciding to exit fully or rebuild.

If the thesis is broken — net retention below 107% for two consecutive quarters, annual recurring revenue growth below 13% for two consecutive quarters, free cash flow margin below 20% for a full fiscal year, CEO or chief financial officer open-market equity sales of five million dollars or more in any 90-day window, or confirmed enterprise customer displacement by Cisco/Splunk or Palo Alto/Chronosphere — exit fully within one quarter. Do not average down on a broken thesis.

If the share price falls below the buy edge (a free-cash-flow yield above 5.9%, implying a price around $31) and the thesis remains intact — accumulate toward a full position size.

If the share price rises above the trim edge (a free-cash-flow yield below 3.3%, implying a price above $55) — trim toward a half-to-two-thirds position to harvest the premium. Do not exit fully unless the thesis is simultaneously breaking.

Scenario Definitions

A scenario is a joint assumption about two independent variables: operating trajectory (how the holding's underlying cash flows grow relative to their long-run trend) and exit multiple (what the market pays for those cash flows at the horizon, relative to a fair-value anchor).

Scenario Operating trajectory Exit multiple
Bear Below trend Below fair value
Base At trend At fair value
Bull Above trend At or above fair value

Non-negotiable rule: In the bear case, both dimensions must be adverse simultaneously — a scenario where only the multiple moves is a re-rating scenario, not a bear.

Bear cases are comparable in structure across all holding types (company, ETF, bond), but not in magnitude. A company bear reflects idiosyncratic execution failure; an ETF bear reflects a broad market drawdown. Different mitigations, different diversification implications.
