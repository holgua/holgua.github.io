About OMV

OMV AG is Austria's largest integrated energy company, with operations spanning three distinct segments. The upstream arm — exploration and production — pumps oil and gas from fields in Central and Eastern Europe, Norway, and the UAE. The downstream arm refines crude oil into fuels and converts hydrocarbons into plastics at three European refineries, with the 46.94% stake in Borouge Group International as the principal chemicals vehicle: BGI, formed in 2026 by combining Borealis, Borouge, and NOVA Chemicals, is the world's fourth-largest producer of polyolefins, the plastics used in packaging, pipes, and automotive components. The retail and commercial arm sells fuels through a network spanning Austria, Romania, and other Central and Eastern European markets.

The business is commodity-linked: when oil prices hit their 2022 peak, OMV earned €5.2bn in net profit on €62bn of revenue; by 2025, revenue had contracted to €24.3bn and net profit to €1.5bn. Two structural features define the investment context above all others. First, the Austrian state — through its holding company, ÖBAG — controls 31.5% of the shares, while the UAE state energy group XRG controls a further 24.9%; combined, state actors hold 56.4% of the company. Second, the BGI combination is the primary value-creation bet: if polyolefin margins recover and the merger delivers synergies, the equity book value held through the stake should grow; if not, a figure that has already declined €2.0bn since 2022 may eventually breach the exit floor.

The Case for the Bet

The thesis rests on the proposition that OMV's operating cash engine is durable enough across the commodity cycle to sustain the investment programme and the dividend, while the BGI chemicals combination delivers earnings growth that justifies holding through the structural headwinds of rising EU carbon-compliance costs and an elevated tax burden. Six conditions must hold:

  1. Annual operating cash flow stays above €4.5bn at prevailing oil prices. The cash engine is the load-bearing pillar: it funds the dividend, services debt, and finances the capital-expenditure programme. At oil above $60 a barrel, a decline below €3.5bn would be a bear signal.

Latest reading: €5,215M in 2025 (2024: €5,456M). Fifth consecutive year above the €4.5bn level. Confirms.

  1. Capital expenditure relative to operating cash flow stays below 90% for any two consecutive years. Free cash flow generation depends on this ratio staying manageable. A two-year breach above 90% would signal that the investment programme is consuming all operating cash, leaving no room for the dividend or debt repayment.

Latest reading: 73.8% in 2025 (capital expenditure €3,849M / operating cash flow €5,215M), rising from 35.6% in 2021. Free cash flow was €1,366M — positive but compressed. The 90% two-year exit threshold has not been approached. Mixed.

  1. The BGI and Borealis equity-method book value stays above €4.0bn for any two consecutive years. The chemicals stake is the primary value-creation vehicle. Sustained decline below €4.0bn over two years would signal the chemicals pivot is destroying rather than creating value.

Latest reading: €5,255M at the end of 2025 (2024: €6,661M; 2022 peak: €7,294M). The buffer above the €4.0bn floor is 24%, but the trend has been consistently downward since 2022. The 2026 annual filing will be the first to reflect the full BGI combination; the exit threshold may need recalibrating. Mixed.

  1. The effective tax rate stays below 55% in any year after 2026. A rate above 55% after 2026 would confirm that EU windfall levies have become recurring rather than exceptional, structurally impairing after-tax earning power beyond the one-off 2023 solidarity charge of €552M.

Latest reading: 54.7% in 2025 (2024: 51.7%; 2023: 58.4%). Below the 55% line; the 2025 filing cannot trigger this exit condition. The first test comes with the 2026 annual filing. A credible EU windfall-tax proposal for 2026 remains on the table. Mixed.

  1. Interest coverage stays above 4.0× for any two consecutive years. Coverage below 4.0× over two years signals leverage stress threatening the investment programme.

Latest reading: 8.0× in 2025 (operating income €3,110M / interest expense €388M), down from 10.8× in 2024. Accruals ratio of −0.08 confirms high cash earnings quality. Confirms at double the exit floor.

  1. Fuel sales volume in Austria and Romania does not decline more than 10% over any two-year period. Polish energy group PKN Orlen's expansion into Central and Eastern Europe is the primary named competitive threat to OMV's downstream retail moat.

Latest reading: Not assessable from structured filing data. The narrative sections of the annual filing are not extractable by automated tools in the current format. Manual review of the annual report document is required.

The Share Price

At €57.05 (the price recorded in the latest re-validation, expressed in the listing currency on the Vienna Stock Exchange), the share price sits below the buy edge, deeply below fair value. The valuation method — owner earnings yield, the ratio of normalised annual owner earnings to the company's market value — reads 9.35% at this price. The fair-value yield is 5.60%, which corresponds to a share price of approximately $95. The buy-edge yield of 7.50% corresponds to approximately $71.

A reading of 9.35% means the market is offering more earnings per euro of purchase price than the fair-value level — or, put plainly, the market is pricing OMV as though its earning power will never recover to the level the thesis considers normal. The gap between $57 today and the $95 fair-value anchor is roughly 40%. That gap closes only if oil prices recover, chemicals margins stabilise, and the effective tax burden does not escalate. None of those conditions is implausible in the base case; none is guaranteed.

What's Wobbling

The most material monitoring item is the capital-expenditure trajectory. At 73.8% of operating cash flow, capital expenditure has consumed roughly three-quarters of every euro generated from operations in 2025, and the trend has moved consistently upward since 2021. Free cash flow of €1,366M is positive and covers the dividend, but the margin is thin: a single year at oil prices near $55 a barrel would push the ratio close to the 90% exit threshold. Management is making a capital-allocation bet that the investment programme will generate adequate returns when commodity prices recover; the thesis holds so long as the 90% line is not breached twice in a row.

The effective tax rate approaching 55% is the asymmetric tail risk. At 54.7% in 2025, it is within 0.3 percentage points of the exit line. The €552M EU solidarity contribution paid in 2023 established legislative precedent; a 2026 repeat would almost certainly push the rate above 55%, and the exit condition would then be testable at the 2026 annual filing. This is not a distant hypothetical.

The BGI book value trend deserves attention. The chemicals platform is the primary source of prospective value creation, but the figure has declined by €2.0bn since its 2022 peak. The 2026 annual filing — the first to reflect the full BGI combination — will be the first genuine read on whether the merger is building or eroding value in the combined entity.

Two drivers remain entirely silent due to data limitations: CEE fuel-volume trends and governance quality cannot be read from the structured data. Anyone making a decision on this holding should read the annual report manually for both.

What Could Still Go Wrong

  • Oil price collapse. If Brent crude falls to around $55 a barrel and holds, free cash flow turns negative, the dividend comes under pressure, and the equity value in the bear scenario falls to approximately $32 a share — 46% below today's price. This scenario carries 25% probability.
  • Chemicals impairment. The BGI and Borealis equity-method stake has already lost €2.0bn in book value since 2022. If the BGI merger fails to deliver synergies or polyolefin feedstock economics deteriorate, further impairments could eventually push the book value below the €4.0bn exit floor.
  • EU windfall tax recurrence. The 2023 solidarity levy pushed the effective tax rate to 58.4%. A 2026 equivalent would most likely push the 2026 tax rate above 55% and fire exit condition (3). The EU Commission has signalled this is politically viable.
  • CapEx overrun meeting low oil prices simultaneously. If oil stays compressed while capital expenditure remains elevated, the CapEx-to-OCF ratio approaches the 90% threshold within a single year — one more year at those levels would trigger exit condition (1).
  • CEE retail fuel erosion by PKN Orlen. The Polish energy group is expanding into OMV's core markets in Austria and Romania. If it captures more than 10% of OMV's CEE fuel sales over two years, exit condition (6) fires. This cannot be monitored from structured data alone.

Scenario Definitions

A scenario is a joint assumption about two independent variables: operating trajectory (how the holding's underlying cash flows grow relative to their long-run trend) and exit multiple (what the market pays for those cash flows at the horizon, relative to a fair-value anchor).

Scenario Operating trajectory Exit multiple
Bear Below trend Below fair value
Base At trend At fair value
Bull Above trend At or above fair value

Non-negotiable rule: In the bear case, both dimensions must be adverse simultaneously — a scenario where only the multiple moves is a re-rating scenario, not a bear.

Bear cases are comparable in structure across all holding types (company, ETF, bond), but not in magnitude. A company bear reflects idiosyncratic execution failure; an ETF bear reflects a broad market drawdown. Different mitigations, different diversification implications.
