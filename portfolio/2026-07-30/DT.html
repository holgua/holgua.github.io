About Dynatrace

Dynatrace is an enterprise software company that sells a single, unified platform for monitoring and managing the health of complex technology environments. In practical terms: large companies run thousands of interdependent software systems; when one component slows down or fails, finding the root cause in real time is the difference between a five-minute outage and a five-hour one. Dynatrace's platform — built on a proprietary data engine called Grail and a topology-mapping system called Smartscape — does that automatically. The company's software agent, OneAgent, installs once at the operating-system level and instruments every application, container, and network connection across an enterprise's stack. It does not need to be told what to watch; it discovers and maps the environment on its own.

The business model is a subscription. Customers sign annual or multi-year contracts and pay per unit of capacity consumed. As their technology environments grow more complex, they tend to consume more — which is why existing customers on average spend 10% more each year than they did the prior year without any new sales cycle required. Revenue is therefore highly predictable: the company's annual recurring revenue of $2.1 billion represents nearly all of the subscription revenue for the next twelve months before a single new contract is signed. The company serves roughly 4,100 enterprise customers globally, concentrated in financial services, technology, telecommunications, and retail, with approximately half of revenue generated outside the United States.

The Case for the Bet

The bet rests on Dynatrace's ability to hold its customers inside a switching-cost structure that makes replacement genuinely painful, while the market for enterprise observability continues to grow:

  1. Net revenue retention stays above 110%. This metric captures whether existing customers renew, expand, contract, or leave — all in one number. A reading above 100% means the average customer spends more year-over-year; sustained readings above 110% mean the business is growing even if it never acquired another new customer. Three consecutive years at 110% is the most direct evidence available that the switching cost is real and compounding. The exit rules require action if the reading falls below 107% for two consecutive quarters — the watch level is the first warning, and two quarters below it is the exit.

Latest reading: 110% constant-currency as of fiscal year ending 2026-03-31 — the third consecutive year at or above this level. The metric matches the prior year exactly; no deceleration is visible in the annual filing.

  1. Annual recurring revenue grows at 13% or faster in constant currency. Below that rate, the business would be losing pricing power or customer reach at a speed that undermines the long-term cash-generation case. The bear case threshold of 13% constant-currency growth for two consecutive quarters triggers the exit rule. The base case expects 16–17%.

Latest reading: 18% growth year-over-year as of 2026-03-31 ($2,054 million from $1,734 million). The filing does not separately disclose a constant-currency breakdown for the full-year figure, but the net-retention data is explicitly stated on a constant-currency basis, confirming the comparison basis. The base-case confirming signal of 18% or higher is met.

  1. Free cash flow margin holds above 20% for any full fiscal year. Cash generation below that level would suggest either a meaningful deterioration in pricing power or an acceleration of costs that undermines the capital-light thesis. The 20% floor is the annual exit trigger.

Latest reading: 26.2% free cash flow margin for fiscal year 2026 (free cash flow $529 million on revenue of $2,018 million), up from 25.5% the prior year. The margin expanded rather than compressed — the opposite of what the exit condition is watching for.

  1. No open-market equity sales by the chief executive or chief financial officer above $5 million in any 90-day window. Such sales would represent management flagging that the price is above their own view of fair value, directly undermining the conviction behind the business. Pre-planned routine sales under a 10b5-1 plan by other executives do not trigger this condition.

Latest reading: No chief executive or chief financial officer open-market equity sales disclosed as of the 2026-05-20 filing. The company repurchased 11.4 million shares for $479 million in fiscal year 2026 — the opposite of insider selling. A new $1 billion repurchase authorisation with $849 million remaining was announced in February 2026.

  1. No confirmed named Dynatrace customer wins by Cisco/Splunk or Palo Alto/Chronosphere. Competitive acquisitions that bundle observability into broader enterprise security suites are the most credible structural threat to Dynatrace's installed base. The condition fires only on confirmed displacement — named customers won away — not on acquisition announcements or competitive positioning.

Latest reading: Not triggered as of 2026-05-20. The annual filing explicitly names Palo Alto Networks' acquisition of Chronosphere as a new competitive event. No named Dynatrace customer wins by the combined entity are disclosed or independently evidenced. The condition is the active watch item for the next two to three quarterly reports.

The Share Price

At $44 today, the share price sits in the middle of the hold band — above the level at which the Rules call for adding ($31) and below the level at which they call for trimming ($55). The fair-value anchor is $41, which means the current price is roughly 6% above that anchor. In the framework's language, the share is modestly above fair value but well short of expensive.

The fair-value figure is derived from the cash the business generates — specifically, the yield that free cash flow represents as a proportion of the total market value. At $41, that yield equals 4.4%, which is the level judged to fully price in the business's current earnings power. At $44, the yield is 4.1% — slightly lower, meaning the market is paying a slight premium. That premium is within normal tolerance; the share price has not moved into the zone where the Rules call for action on either side.

The 52-week range runs from $32 to $58, so today's price is near the middle of the historical range. The reverse-engineering exercise — asking what long-term growth rate the market is implicitly pricing in at $44 — produces approximately 9% annually, which is meaningfully below the base case expectation of 14%. If the base case holds, today's price should prove conservative in retrospect.

What's Wobbling

The only genuine wobble is competitive — and it is pre-conditions, not post-conditions. Cisco's 2024 acquisition of Splunk and Palo Alto Networks' 2026 acquisition of Chronosphere are now both named in the annual filing as competitive threats. These are exactly the combinations identified in the fifth exit condition: a bundle of network security plus application performance monitoring plus log management could, in theory, give large enterprise customers a reason to consolidate away from a standalone observability vendor. The condition fires only when named Dynatrace customer losses are confirmed. As of the most recent filing, no such losses appear. The Palo Alto/Chronosphere combination is newly assembled and has not yet had time to generate enterprise wins at Dynatrace's expense.

What does cross a line, in spirit if not in the formal trigger, is the ambiguous evidence on AI commoditisation risk. Dynatrace Intelligence — the company's own agentic AI product — reached general availability in fiscal year 2026, which is a direct counter-investment to the threat. But the filing also explicitly acknowledges that general-purpose large language models and AI agents represent a growing risk to observability vendors that cannot build proprietary context. Both truths sit side by side: the company is investing in the right direction, and the direction of travel in the competitive landscape is creating genuine risk. The moat is intact; the environment around it is changing.

The DPS penetration metric — the share of annual recurring revenue on the new consumption-based subscription model — was not disclosed in the annual filing, because the company reports that figure quarterly rather than annually. The prior quarterly disclosure was approximately 70% of annual recurring revenue. No deceleration was flagged; the metric is simply absent from the annual report. Watch the next quarterly release for this figure and see The Case for the Bet for the threshold that matters.

What Could Still Go Wrong

  • Moat erosion from AI-native competitors. If OpenTelemetry-based or large-language-model-native monitoring tools achieve sufficient depth at the instrumentation layer, Dynatrace's switching-cost thesis weakens. Net retention is the first metric to show stress — a sustained decline toward 104–105% would be the signal, and the exit rule at 107% for two consecutive quarters would follow.

  • Cisco/Splunk and Palo Alto/Chronosphere execute on bundle displacement. Large enterprise customers in financial services and telecommunications are exactly the buyers most receptive to consolidated security and observability contracts. If the combined suites generate named wins from Dynatrace's installed base, the fifth exit condition fires regardless of the financial metrics.

  • Free cash flow growth stalls if share-based compensation stays elevated. Annual share-based compensation of $300 million represents 14.8% of revenue — a rate that partially offsets the cash generated for shareholders even as the headline free cash flow figure grows. If this ratio does not decline as revenue scales, the per-share economics improve more slowly than the aggregate figures suggest.

  • A no-growth outcome destroys the thesis entirely. The share price at $44 contains almost no value from current earnings power alone — the entire case depends on continued compounding. If growth stalls for any reason — macro-driven enterprise budget freezes, a technology shift away from the OneAgent model, or management execution failure — the fair value falls toward levels far below the current price.

  • Tax structure extends the earnings distortion. The 45.7% effective tax rate, a product of the company's intellectual property transfer to Switzerland, is expected to persist through 2035 to 2044. Investors relying on standard earnings multiples will systematically underprice the cash generation, which is an opportunity; investors who need to exit quickly may struggle to find buyers comfortable looking through the accounting noise.

The post-compensation figure of $230 million represents a materially different picture of per-share value. The divergence does not affect the current holding verdict but is flagged so any reader can weigh the methodological choice.

The Rules

The rules govern what action the pre-committed framework calls for under each combination of thesis state and share-price position. There are five branches:

  • If the thesis is confirmed (all key metrics are on track and no exit condition has fired): maintain the full position. Continue monitoring quarterly disclosures for net retention, ARR growth, free cash flow margin, and DPS penetration. Consider adding modestly on dips below $38 while net retention holds above 107%.

  • If the thesis is weakening (net retention declining toward 107% or ARR growth decelerating toward 14%, but no exit condition has yet fired): reduce to half position within 30 days of the trigger event. Monitor for two additional quarters before deciding whether to exit fully or rebuild. Weakening signals include: net retention 107–109% for one quarter, ARR growth 13–15% for one quarter, or DPS penetration stalling below 72%.

  • If the thesis is broken (any written exit condition is confirmed): exit fully within one quarter. Broken conditions include net retention below 107% for two consecutive quarters; ARR growth below 13% for two consecutive quarters; free cash flow margin below 20% for a full fiscal year; chief executive or chief financial officer open-market equity sales above $5 million; or confirmed named Dynatrace enterprise customer displacement by Cisco/Splunk or Palo Alto/Chronosphere. Do not average down on a broken thesis.

  • If the price falls below the buy edge (share price at or below $31, cash yield above 5.9%): accumulate toward full position size. At that level the share price implies a deeply undervalued entry relative to the discounted cash flow fair value. The management buyback programme sets a practical floor; below $35 the yield exceeds 6.2%, which represents a compelling accumulation zone if the thesis remains intact.

  • If the price rises above the trim edge (share price at or above $55, cash yield below 3.3%): trim toward a half to two-thirds position. At that level the share price approaches the base-case discounted cash flow value and the remaining upside requires bull-case execution. Do not exit fully unless the thesis is simultaneously breaking — a compounder with 110% net retention and more than $800 million in net cash deserves continued partial ownership through multiple expansions.

Scenario Definitions

A scenario is a joint assumption about two independent variables: operating trajectory (how the holding's underlying cash flows grow relative to their long-run trend) and exit multiple (what the market pays for those cash flows at the horizon, relative to a fair-value anchor).

Scenario Operating trajectory Exit multiple
Bear Below trend Below fair value
Base At trend At fair value
Bull Above trend At or above fair value

Non-negotiable rule: In the bear case, both dimensions must be adverse simultaneously — a scenario where only the multiple moves is a re-rating scenario, not a bear.

Bear cases are comparable in structure across all holding types (company, ETF, bond), but not in magnitude. A company bear reflects idiosyncratic execution failure; an ETF bear reflects a broad market drawdown. Different mitigations, different diversification implications.
