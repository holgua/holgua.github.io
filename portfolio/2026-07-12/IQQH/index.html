About the Fund

Clean Energy is the colloquial name for the iShares Global Clean Energy Transition UCITS ETF, an Irish-domiciled exchange-traded fund managed by BlackRock's iShares division. The fund tracks the S&P Global Clean Energy Transition Index, a basket of roughly one hundred companies selected by S&P Dow Jones Indices for their exposure to the global shift away from fossil-fuel energy: solar panel makers, wind turbine manufacturers, grid-infrastructure equipment companies, electrification-technology businesses, and clean-energy enabling technologies. The fund is listed in euros on the Frankfurt Stock Exchange (Xetra) under the ticker IQQH.

The basket is deliberately concentrated. Technology, Utilities, and Industrials together account for over 98% of the portfolio — each sector above 30% — because the decarbonisation opportunity sits at the intersection of energy generation (Utilities), physical infrastructure (Industrials), and the hardware and software needed to operate it (Technology). This is a sector fund, not a broad-market vehicle. The ten largest positions collectively represent about 55% of the basket, with Bloom Energy, a US hydrogen and fuel-cell company, holding the top slot at 14.76%. The annual cost is 0.65% of assets.

No tracking-difference or tracking-error figures are available for this fund in the current data set — a gap documented in Gaps in the Picture. Net cost (after any securities-lending rebate) is also unconfirmed.

The Case for the Bet

The position rests on a single structural idea: that global decarbonisation is a capital-expenditure supercycle lasting a decade or longer, and that the companies best positioned to capture that spend — solar, wind, grid, electrification — are concentrated in the S&P Global Clean Energy Transition Index. Regulatory mandates (the EU Green Deal, the US Inflation Reduction Act), falling renewable energy costs, and sustained industrial investment are the three load-bearing columns of this thesis. For the position to remain valid, all three must continue to hold.

Six conditions define the boundaries.

  1. Earnings-yield trap absent. The basket's earnings yield (the share of earnings delivered per euro of market price) must not remain above 8.0% for four consecutive quarters while the index also suffers persistent capital losses. A basket earnings yield above 8.0% held over four quarters with simultaneous capital losses signals constituent-fundamentals collapse — the basket is becoming cheap because the underlying businesses are deteriorating, not because the market is being irrational. That pattern invalidates the capex-cycle thesis.

Latest reading: Basket earnings yield 4.59% as of 2026-07-12. This is 341 basis points (3.41 percentage points) below the 8.0% trap threshold. Exit condition (1) is not approached. The five-day rolling earnings-yield series has been stable and positive; capital losses are not persistent at index level.

  1. Vehicle integrity intact. The fund's annual cost must remain below 0.80%, assets under management must stay above €200 million, and no single constituent may exceed 20.0% of the basket. A breach of any of these limits, or a formal wind-down notice from iShares, would make the fund itself unsuitable as the vehicle for this bet.

What's Wobbling

Only one driver is not fully confirming: the equity risk premium. At +0.02 percentage points (basket earnings yield of 4.59% minus the ten-year US Treasury yield of 4.57%), the basket is offering virtually no extra return for taking on clean-energy equity risk versus a risk-free government bond. This is the weakest reading in recent history — the five-day series has ranged from +0.05% to +0.02% — and it reflects the ten-year Treasury yield rising to 4.57% rather than any deterioration in the basket's earnings.

This reading does not change the verdict. The exit-condition (1) persistence test requires two consecutive readings of negative equity risk premium — both recent readings are positive. Exit condition (1) also requires the basket earnings yield to be above 8.0% simultaneously with persistent capital losses, a threshold that is 341 basis points away from today's 4.59%.

The practical implication is clear enough: this position is held on thematic conviction, not on cheap valuation relative to Treasuries. Anyone who requires the basket to look inexpensive versus a risk-free alternative before holding it should note that it does not clear that bar at current prices — the case rests entirely on the structural decarbonisation thesis.

Worth noting: if the correct risk-free benchmark for this euro-denominated fund is the German Bund rather than the US Treasury (the Bund yielded approximately 2.5–2.6% as of mid-2026), the equity risk premium would widen to approximately +2.0 percentage points, which would clear the 2.0% confirmation threshold. That calibration question is unresolved and is flagged in Gaps in the Picture.

What Could Still Go Wrong

  • Policy reversal. The US and EU clean-energy policy frameworks — the Inflation Reduction Act and the EU Green Deal — are the primary regulatory tailwind. A legislated rollback of either would remove the policy anchor underwriting the capex-cycle bet, potentially before the market prices it in.
  • Basket constituent deterioration. Two top-ten holdings, Plug Power and SolarEdge Technologies, are burning equity capital (return on equity of −128.8% and −72.5% respectively). If more basket constituents migrate into this territory, the earnings-yield signal loses its meaning as a valuation anchor.
  • Multiple contraction. The bear-case scenario in the value analysis assumes the market re-rates clean-energy companies from their current trailing price-to-earnings multiple of 21.8× to 15×. Even with 8% earnings growth, that multiple contraction produces a negative annual return of approximately −1.4% over five years. The thematic bet requires the market to sustain or expand multiples, not contract them.
  • Interest-rate pressure. The basket is heavily weighted in capital-intensive Utilities and Industrials. Higher real interest rates compress the valuation of long-duration assets — utilities in particular are rate-sensitive. A sustained rise in real rates would pressure both the discount rate applied to future earnings and the basket's multiple.
  • Earnings-yield trap. If clean-energy basket earnings deteriorate sharply while capital losses persist at index level, the earnings yield could rise above 8.0% — not because the basket is cheap, but because earnings are collapsing. Four consecutive quarters in that state trigger exit condition (1).

The Rules

The position is governed by six pre-committed rules, one for each state the holding can occupy:

  • If the thesis is confirmed (all drivers pass or are silent): hold. The thematic thesis is intact and the vehicle is sound. Do not size up on the earnings-yield signal alone; add only if the decarbonisation capital-expenditure case is actively strengthening.
  • If the thesis is weakened (any driver is in the mixed or challenging range, but no exit condition is triggered): do not add. Re-validate after the next quarter and after any material policy development affecting the Inflation Reduction Act or the EU Green Deal. If exit conditions (3) through (5) are approaching, accelerate the review timeline.
  • If the thesis is broken (a vehicle-integrity breach or a thematic exit condition is triggered): exit. Move proceeds to a risk-free instrument or into a broad-market replacement fund.
  • If the share price falls below the buy edge (basket earnings yield above 6.25%): add to the position, provided the basket earnings yield is below 8.0% and the thematic thesis is intact. A basket earnings yield above 8.0% with persistent capital losses is exit condition (1) — verify the distinction before adding.
  • If the share price rises above the trim edge (basket earnings yield below 4.00%): trim 25–33%. The basket is meaningfully expensive relative to the risk-free rate. Move proceeds to a risk-free instrument or to a broad-market fund rather than holding cash within the thematic position.
  • If the share price rises above the exit edge (basket earnings yield at or below 3.00%): exit. The basket is fully priced versus the risk-free rate. Redeploy proceeds to a risk-free instrument or to a holding that meets the value test.
