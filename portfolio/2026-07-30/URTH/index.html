All World: The Bet Is Wobbling

The Bottom Line

Data as of 2026-07-30 · Latest filing 2026-07-30.

The bet is wobbling. The basket of around 1,300 large and mid-sized companies from 23 developed markets no longer earns enough in aggregate to compensate for the risk of holding shares rather than government bonds — the earnings yield on the basket has been below the 10-year Treasury yield for fifteen consecutive pipeline snapshots. The calendar exit gate has not yet elapsed, so no sell signal has fired yet. The condition to watch is whether the equity risk premium — the gap between what the basket earns and what a government bond pays — turns positive again before the end of September 2026.

The most consequential recent reading is where the basket earnings yield stands relative to the US 10-year Treasury. As of 2026-07-30, the basket's earnings yield — what the 1,300-odd businesses collectively earn per dollar of share price — is 4.16%, while the Treasury yields 4.60%. In plain terms: a dollar placed in a government bond today earns more in interest than the same dollar placed in this basket earns in look-through corporate earnings. About All World

All World is a passively managed exchange-traded fund that tracks the MSCI World Index — a float-adjusted, market-capitalisation-weighted index of large and mid-sized companies from 23 developed countries. Around 70% of the basket sits in US-listed companies; the remaining 30% spans Japan, the United Kingdom, France, Switzerland, Germany, Canada, and a dozen other developed markets. The fund holds approximately 1,300 positions, compared with 500 for a US-only index fund, giving broader geographic exposure while still concentrating on large businesses that are traded in liquid markets.

The fund is managed by BlackRock under the iShares brand, which is the world's largest exchange-traded fund issuer by assets. The vehicle is a Delaware statutory trust — the standard US legal wrapper for exchange-traded funds. It does not issue a Schedule K-1 tax form. The annual charge is 0.24% of assets, which compares with 0.20% for the functionally equivalent UCITS-wrapper version listed in Europe; the 4 basis-point gap is the reason the thesis singles out a fee hike above 0.35% as an exit condition. No tracking-difference or tracking-error figures are available for the fund from publicly available sources; no securities-lending revenue offset is on record. The annual cost therefore stands at the gross 0.24%.

The Case for the Bet

The bet is that, over a long holding period, the basket's earnings yield will exceed the risk-free rate by at least 2 percentage points — and that the fund remains a sound vehicle to express that bet:

  1. Equity risk premium above zero for at least two consecutive quarters. The basket's earnings yield must stay above the 10-year Treasury yield for the risk-premium case to hold. The exit condition fires when the premium is negative for two full consecutive calendar quarters.

Latest reading: −0.44% as of 2026-07-30. The equity risk premium has been negative for fifteen consecutive pipeline snapshots since 2026-06-16. The two-quarter calendar gate requires confirmation at the Q3 2026 quarter-end (2026-09-30); the exit condition has not yet triggered.

  1. Annual fee at or below 0.35%. A hike above this ceiling with no fee response signals that the cheaper UCITS-domiciled equivalent is the better vehicle.

Latest reading: 0.24% as of 2026-07-20. Eleven basis points below the ceiling; exit condition (2) is not triggered.

  1. Assets under management above $2.0 billion. Below this floor, bid-ask spreads on a global blend fund typically widen to a point where the cost of trading in and out erodes returns.

Latest reading: approximately $8.06 billion as of 2026-07-20. 4.03 times the floor; exit condition (3) is not triggered.

  1. Top single holding below 10.0% of the fund. A single name reaching 10% of a 1,300-company global index is a signal of regime-change concentration — not normal market-capitalisation drift.

Latest reading: Nvidia at 5.17% as of 2026-07-20. 4.83 percentage points below the ceiling; exit condition (4) is not triggered.

  1. MSCI World index methodology unchanged. The developed-markets mandate — 23 countries, float-adjusted market capitalisation, large and mid-cap scope — must remain materially intact.

Latest reading: No automated signal of methodology change as of 2026-07-30. Exit condition (5) requires manual monitoring; no trigger.

  1. Median return on equity of the ten largest holdings above 15%. When the basket's top holdings lose their compounding character, the quality case for the equity-risk-premium bet weakens at the root.

Latest reading: Median top-ten return on equity at 37.28% as of 2026-07-20. More than double the 15% threshold; exit condition (6) is not triggered.

The Share Price

The fund is currently priced at $202 per share. The basket's earnings yield of 4.16% is within the hold band — above the trim edge of 4.0% (equivalent to a price-to-earnings ratio of 25) and below the fair-value anchor of 5.0% (equivalent to a ratio of 20). In plain terms, the basket is priced above what the rules consider fair value, but not so far above it that the trim order has fired.

The margin is narrow. The 16 basis-point gap between the current earnings yield (4.16%) and the trim edge (4.00%) corresponds to a share price of roughly $211 — about $9 higher than today's level. A modest rally of 4–5% from here would push the basket into trim territory without any change in the underlying companies' earnings.

The buy edge sits at 6.25% earnings yield, which would imply a price of around $135. That is roughly a third below today's level — a gap the market rarely closes quickly in a developed-markets basket without a broad recession or a sharp repricing of risk-free rates.

What's Wobbling

The one wobble is the equity risk premium. At −0.44 percentage points, the basket's look-through earnings yield falls below the 10-year US Treasury yield for the fifteenth consecutive reading since 2026-06-16. The exit condition (1) in the Case for the Bet requires two complete calendar quarters of negative premium — the first would end at 2026-09-30. That checkpoint has not passed. Until it does, the rules classify this as a weakening thesis, not a broken one: the basket remains within the hold band, all vehicle-integrity conditions are clear, and the quality of the underlying businesses remains intact.

The buffer above the trim band is thin — roughly 16 basis points of earnings yield, or about 4–5% of share-price movement. A further rise in the share price without a corresponding rise in underlying earnings would push the position into trim territory and trigger the trim order independently of the premium question.

What Could Still Go Wrong

  • The equity risk premium remains negative through the end of September 2026, at which point exit condition (1) fires and the rules call for closing the position.
  • The share price rallies another 4–5% without a commensurate increase in basket earnings, pushing the earnings yield below the 4.0% trim edge and triggering the trim order.
  • The US Federal Reserve cuts interest rates sharply, which typically lifts equity prices and compresses earnings yields simultaneously — the basket could move into trim or even exit territory faster than the quarterly review cycle catches it.
  • BlackRock raises the expense ratio above 0.35% in response to rising operational costs without a corresponding fee reduction in the UCITS-wrapper equivalent, triggering exit condition (2).
  • A single mega-cap company grows to more than 10% of the fund's weight — currently Nvidia at 5.17%, the nearest candidate — triggering exit condition (4). At current pace this requires a roughly doubling of Nvidia's relative weight.
  • A broad market downturn compresses earnings across the basket's 1,300+ companies, pushing the median return on equity of the ten largest holdings toward the 15% exit threshold of condition (6). The current reading of 37.28% provides substantial cushion.

For this fund, exit can also fire through vehicle-integrity breaches (conditions 2–4) or through the earnings-yield exit threshold of condition (6) — any of these paths is independent of the risk-premium exit condition.

The Rules

  • If the thesis is confirmed — the equity risk premium is positive and all vehicle-integrity conditions are clear — hold the position. The basket is reasonably priced and the vehicle is sound.
  • If the thesis is weakened — any thesis driver is in a challenging state — do not add. Re-validate after the next quarter. If the equity risk premium stays negative or the median look-through return on equity keeps falling, swap to the UCITS-wrapper equivalent at 0.20% expense ratio or move to cash per the position-sizing rule.
  • If the thesis is broken — a vehicle-integrity condition breaches or an exit condition is triggered — exit. Move proceeds to risk-free instruments or to a holding that meets the value test.
  • If the price falls below the buy band (basket earnings yield at or above 6.25%) — add to the position. A global equity basket at that earnings yield against the current risk-free rate is rare and decisive.
  • If the price rises above the trim band (basket earnings yield at or below 4.0%) — trim 25–33% of the position. The basket is meaningfully expensive relative to the risk-free rate; harvest the premium.
  • If the price rises above the exit band (basket earnings yield at or below 3.0%) — exit the full position. At that earnings yield the basket is fully priced against the risk-free rate. Redeploy proceeds to risk-free instruments or to a holding that meets the value test.

Scenario Definitions

A scenario is a joint assumption about two independent variables: operating trajectory (how the holding's underlying cash flows grow relative to their long-run trend) and exit multiple (what the market pays for those cash flows at the horizon, relative to a fair-value anchor).

Scenario Operating trajectory Exit multiple
Bear Below trend Below fair value
Base At trend At fair value
Bull Above trend At or above fair value

Non-negotiable rule: In the bear case, both dimensions must be adverse simultaneously — a scenario where only the multiple moves is a re-rating scenario, not a bear.

Bear cases are comparable in structure across all holding types (company, ETF, bond), but not in magnitude. A company bear reflects idiosyncratic execution failure; an ETF bear reflects a broad market drawdown. Different mitigations, different diversification implications.
