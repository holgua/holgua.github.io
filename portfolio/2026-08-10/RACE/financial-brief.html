PART I — INVESTMENT THESIS

1.1 One-Paragraph Thesis

Ferrari N.V. is the only publicly listed automotive company operating a deliberate production-cap model (~13,500–14,000 units annually) while expanding EBITDA margins from 34.8% (FY2022) to 38.8% (FY2025) — a luxury-goods franchise masquerading as an automobile manufacturer. The scarcity architecture (permanent excess demand via supply restriction) and the loyalty staircase (access to limited-edition models gated by ownership history) are structural barriers that no capital-intensive competitor can replicate. The central debate is whether Ferrari can absorb the Elettrica BEV transition without compressing the margin structure or breaching the production-cap moat; XBRL data through FY2025 shows no evidence of margin compression — EBITDA expanded in each of the last three fiscal years, OCF/NI exceeded 1.25× every year, and all six exit conditions remain untriggered. The current price, however, is assessed to be at or above the base-case DCF fair value (~$337), leaving limited margin of safety.

PART III — COMPETITIVE POSITION (DISTILLED)

3.3 Moat — Type, Evidence, Trajectory

  • Moat type: Wide — brand heritage + scarcity architecture; no competitor replicates a seventy-year heritage at comparable margin (§3.3)
  • Strongest evidence: EBITDA margin 34.8% → 38.8% (FY2022–FY2025) = economic rent expansion at stable volume (§3.3)
  • ROE sustained above 40% for three consecutive years (40.9% / 43.1% / 40.9% FY2025/FY2024/FY2023) — rare in manufacturing (§3.3)
  • Accruals ratio negative in all five observable years (FY2025: −0.078) — cash generation systematically exceeds reported earnings (§3.3)
  • Trajectory: Widening — EBITDA margin expanded each year FY2022–FY2025; buyer bargaining power score 1/5 (demand > supply by design) (§3.4)
