About Datadog

Datadog sells software that lets engineering and operations teams see what is happening inside their cloud-based applications in real time — measuring performance, collecting logs, catching errors, and, increasingly, monitoring artificial-intelligence workloads. A small piece of software called an agent is installed on every server, container, and serverless function a customer runs; that agent sends a constant stream of measurements back to Datadog's platform. The company earns revenue based on how much of this stream a customer sends and how many of Datadog's thirty-plus products they have switched on.

The business model is built around a dynamic called land and expand: customers typically begin with one or two products, then adopt additional modules as their cloud deployments grow and their engineering teams discover new uses for the data. This creates a natural compounding of revenue with relatively low incremental selling effort — the installed base of roughly 32,700 customers was collectively spending around 20% more per year as of 2025 without any new customer acquisition. Fifty-five per cent of customers use four or more products, and 9% use ten or more, up from 5% in the previous year. Each additional product deepens the cost of switching to a competing platform, which is why the retention statistics are the primary indicator of whether the business is working as intended.

The Case for the Bet

The bet is that Datadog's agent-based instrumentation is so deeply embedded in customer infrastructure that displacement requires months of engineering effort — and that, as cloud deployments grow and AI workloads multiply, customers will keep adding Datadog products rather than switching away. Six conditions must hold for the bet to remain intact:

  1. Net revenue retention above 112% for at least two consecutive quarters. Below this floor, the installed base is shrinking in net revenue terms, which would indicate that existing customers are reducing spend faster than they are expanding — a structural signal that the switching-cost moat is losing its hold, most likely from hyperscaler bundling or AI-native cohort spend optimisation.

Latest reading: Low-120s per cent range as of March 2026 (up from high-110s per cent range a year earlier). The trend is moving in the right direction, comfortably above the 112% exit floor.

  1. Gross margin above 77% for at least two consecutive quarters. Below this line, the cost of serving customers through third-party cloud infrastructure is being absorbed without the ability to pass it through — a structural impairment to per-customer unit economics.

Latest reading: 79.2% gross margin in the three months ending March 2026, flat year on year and 4 percentage points above the exit floor. Pricing power is intact.

  1. Stock-based compensation below 25% of revenue, with a credible glide-path toward normalisation within two years. Above this threshold, the gap between cash flow from operations and what a real owner earns after the cost of employee equity widens to the point where per-share value creation is illusory.

Latest reading: 20.5% of revenue in the three months ending March 2026 — improved by 1.8 percentage points year on year. Below the 25% exit threshold, with normalisation trajectory intact.

  1. Revenue growth above 20% for at least two consecutive quarters. Below this rate, the deceleration goes beyond what the law of large numbers explains, and competitive displacement or macro contraction becomes the more likely cause.

Latest reading: 32% year-on-year revenue growth in the three months ending March 2026. Well above the 20% floor.

  1. No single acquisition above $2 billion at premium multiples. A departure from the disciplined bolt-on track record — where individual deals have ranged from $10 million to $220 million — would introduce integration risk inconsistent with the current earnings base.

Latest reading: No acquisitions above $2 billion have been disclosed. Capital allocation remains disciplined.

  1. No regulatory enforcement action or mandatory market exit related to cross-border data telemetry or AI-model training data privacy affecting more than 5% of revenue. A material fine under European data-protection law or a compliance shutdown affecting a significant revenue geography would impair both the addressable market and the cost structure.

Latest reading: No new enforcement action in the quarter ending March 2026. Standard risk-factor language on data privacy reiterated but unchanged.

The Share Price

At $261, Datadog's share price sits well above the trim line and is well above fair value. The owner earnings yield — the cash the business generates per year as a fraction of what the market is asking to own it — is approximately 0.27%. The trim threshold is 1.5%: below that yield level, the price is considered stretched. The current reading of 0.27% is not close to 1.5%; it is less than a fifth of it. The fair-value yield anchor is 2.5%, which implies a fair-value price of approximately $28 per share against current earnings. The current price of $261 is nearly ten times that.

This is not a close call. The business is performing well — revenue is growing at 32%, the retention metrics are recovering, and the cash generation is strong. The price simply reflects expectations that are years of compounding ahead of where the earnings are today. A separate bottom-up scenario analysis places the base-case intrinsic value in the range of $114–$115 per share. Both estimates — the yield-method anchor of $28 and the scenario-based anchor of $115 — are far below the current price, though they diverge substantially from each other (see Gaps in the Picture).

What's Wobbling

Free cash flow margin compression. The three months ending March 2026 produced a free cash flow margin of 28.7% — positive and well above the 25% floor that would trigger concern, but down around three and a half percentage points from the same period a year earlier. The cause is that stock-based compensation grew in absolute terms: $206 million in the quarter versus $170 million a year prior. All six conditions in the Case for the Bet remain unbreached, and the stock-based compensation ratio of 20.5% remains below the 25% exit threshold. But the direction — absolute compensation dollars rising as headcount scales — is worth tracking across the next two quarterly reports to confirm that normalisation is on track rather than stalled.

Fair-value anchor divergence. The yield-method fair value of approximately $28 and the bottom-up scenario analysis central estimate of approximately $114–$115 diverge substantially. Both confirm the current price is materially above any base-case estimate, but a reader trying to judge how far above fair value the stock is sitting will reach different conclusions depending on which anchor they use. The divergence is a known methodological gap; see Gaps in the Picture for more detail.

What Could Still Go Wrong

  • Hyperscaler bundling accelerates. Amazon Web Services, Microsoft Azure, and Google Cloud all offer native monitoring tools embedded in enterprise discount programmes at near-zero incremental cost. If large enterprises consolidate on single-cloud deployments, Datadog's cross-cloud advantage disappears, and net revenue retention could compress toward 110% — the level at which the bear case activates. This is the primary structural risk and it will intensify, not abate, over time.
  • AI observability monetisation disappoints. The AI-native customer cohort contributed several percentage points of growth in recent quarters. If hyperscalers simultaneously launch GPU monitoring and large-language-model tracing at zero marginal cost before Datadog's AI observability products reach critical adoption, the wedge narrows before it has time to compound.
  • Stock-based compensation fails to normalise. At 20–22% of revenue, employee equity expense transforms a business generating over $1 billion in operating cash flow into one generating a small fraction of that in per-share value after dilution. If the glide-path toward the 15% target stalls, the gap between reported cash flow and true owner earnings widens permanently.
  • Valuation contraction without a business error. Even in the base case — the business executing exactly as planned — the current price requires a multi-year journey back toward fair value that the three-year scenario analysis prices at roughly −24% per year annualised. A re-rating driven by rising discount rates rather than business deterioration would produce the same outcome.
  • Security and regulatory incidents. A material data-privacy enforcement action in the European Union or a significant data breach affecting customer telemetry would impair trust in the platform at precisely the moment Datadog is seeking to expand into regulated federal and enterprise verticals.

Gaps in the Picture

Three data-quality notes apply to this analysis.

The relationship between the two fair-value anchors used in this analysis — the yield-method anchor of approximately $28 per share and the bottom-up scenario central estimate of approximately $114–$115 per share — is a known methodological gap. The yield-method number is derived from the current free-cash-flow yield at the fair-value threshold configured at thesis time (2.5% yield, corresponding to a price anchored to today's earnings power). The scenario-based number is derived from a forward projection of earnings three years out, discounted back at a risk-adjusted rate. Both figures confirm the current price is materially above any base-case estimate. A future schema update will align the two approaches so they reference the same underlying earnings forecast.

The maintenance capital expenditure is not separately disclosed in official filings — the analysis uses depreciation and amortisation as a proxy, which is conservative for an asset-light software business where true maintenance spending is likely well below that level. This overstates the deduction from owner earnings relative to a precise calculation.

The risk-free rate used in the hurdle calculation was not available from Datadog's own market data file or from the portfolio's United States benchmark file at the time of this run. The Dimension B return comparison has been omitted from the Bottom Line as a result. The hurdle rate itself was computed correctly from the German inflation-linked bond rate; the omission affects only the premium-over-risk-free comparison.

The Rules

There are six pre-committed rules governing what action follows from each combination of conditions and share price. The rules are stated as facts about the evaluation.

  1. If all conditions are intact and the price is within the hold band: hold any position; no new entry. Net revenue retention, revenue growth, and AI observability traction are the quarterly checkpoints. The current free-cash-flow yield of around 1% is well below the trim threshold; the stock is in full avoid territory for new money.

  2. If the thesis is showing signs of softening but no exit condition has triggered: do not initiate. If net revenue retention is drifting toward 115%, or revenue growth is approaching 20%, the rules call for reassessing within one earnings cycle before any sizing decision. A softening-but-not-broken thesis at an elevated valuation has negative risk and reward on both dimensions.

  3. If an exit condition has triggered: do not enter. If already holding, exit the full position within 30 days. The three triggering conditions are: net revenue retention at or below 112% for two consecutive quarters; gross margin below 77% for two consecutive quarters; or stock-based compensation above 25% with no credible normalisation path. No averaging down.

  4. If the share price falls to the buy edge (free-cash-flow yield reaches 4%, approximately $18 per share at current earnings): confirm first that all conditions remain intact — net revenue retention above 115%, free cash flow margin above 25%, no exit condition triggered — then initiate in two tranches over one quarter.

  5. If the share price rises above the trim line (free-cash-flow yield falls below 1.5%, approximately $47 per share at current earnings): do not initiate. If holding, trim 25–33% of the position. The current price of $261 is already well above this threshold.

  6. If the share price rises above the exit line (the level at which the market is pricing approximately a fixed number of years of base-case compounding at no discount): exit the full position, or remain out. Redeploy the proceeds to a holding with a positive risk-and-reward profile. No re-entry within 90 days, to avoid anchoring to the prior price.

Scenario Definitions

A scenario is a joint assumption about two independent variables: operating trajectory (how the holding's underlying cash flows grow relative to their long-run trend) and exit multiple (what the market pays for those cash flows at the horizon, relative to a fair-value anchor).

Scenario Operating trajectory Exit multiple
Bear Below trend Below fair value
Base At trend At fair value
Bull Above trend At or above fair value

Non-negotiable rule: In the bear case, both dimensions must be adverse simultaneously — a scenario where only the multiple moves is a re-rating scenario, not a bear.

Bear cases are comparable in structure across all holding types (company, ETF, bond), but not in magnitude. A company bear reflects idiosyncratic execution failure; an ETF bear reflects a broad market drawdown. Different mitigations, different diversification implications.
