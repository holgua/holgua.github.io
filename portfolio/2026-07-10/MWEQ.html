About the Fund

World Equal Weight tracks the MSCI World Equal Weighted Index, a version of the standard developed-world equity index that assigns roughly the same weight — about 0.07% — to each of the approximately 1,500 constituent companies at each quarterly rebalance. The index covers companies across 23 developed markets: the United States, Japan, Germany, the United Kingdom, France, and others. The fund is domiciled in Ireland and listed on the Frankfurt Xetra exchange as an accumulating share class, meaning income received from dividends is reinvested within the fund rather than distributed to shareholders.

The practical effect of equal weighting is to reduce the influence of the largest companies. In a standard cap-weighted version of the same index, the ten biggest stocks — currently dominated by large American technology companies — account for roughly a quarter to a third of the total. In an equal-weight version, each of those ten companies receives the same allocation as each of the smaller 1,490. This makes the basket comparatively heavier in mid-size companies, European and Japanese names, and sectors such as industrials and financial services, which are underrepresented in cap-weighted global indices. The trade-off is a higher turnover cost: rebalancing roughly 1,500 positions quarterly back to their target weight generates approximately 30% annual portfolio churn, creating a modest but permanent drag on net returns.

The annual cost is approximately 0.20%, from the fund's factsheet (this figure is not returned by the automated data source and must be verified at each refresh). No tracking difference or tracking error data is available in the current snapshot, so the comparison between headline costs and realised performance gap is deferred. The fund uses an accumulating UCITS structure; capital gains accumulate within the fund.

The Case for the Bet

The structural bet is that equal weighting across the developed world delivers a higher basket earnings yield than a cap-weighted index over a full cycle — because it persistently dilutes the mega-cap premium embedded in cap-weighted benchmarks — while remaining diversified enough that no single business failure can materially impair the basket. Six conditions must keep holding:

  1. The basket's earnings yield must exceed the risk-free 10-year government bond yield, maintaining a positive equity risk premium. The risk premium is the core economic justification for holding a broad equity basket rather than government bonds. If the basket's earnings yield falls below the risk-free rate for two consecutive quarters, the risk premium case has failed.

What Could Still Go Wrong

  • Rising government bond yields compress the equity risk premium to zero or below. If the 10-year Treasury yield rises by a further 0.31 percentage points while basket earnings hold flat, the equity risk premium turns negative. Two consecutive negative readings would trigger exit condition (1).
  • Basket earnings contract across the developed world. A global earnings slowdown — triggered by a recession, a credit event, or a tightening of financial conditions — would reduce basket earnings yields at current prices, removing what little cushion the risk premium currently provides.
  • Multiple contraction from 20× to historical norms. The five-year bear scenario assumes the trailing price-to-earnings ratio compresses from 20.54 to 12 — a level consistent with prolonged deflationary periods in developed markets. That path produces an annualised total return of approximately −4.9%, a meaningful loss against the risk-free alternative.
  • Fund-scale risk. The fund is approximately 22 months old. While assets of €1.64 billion are well above the €500 million floor, a sustained market downturn could accelerate outflows from a relatively young fund. Invesco has closed sub-scale UCITS products before.
  • Methodology or sponsor change. A decision by MSCI to alter the equal-weight methodology, or a corporate event at Invesco affecting its UCITS franchise, would trigger exit condition (5). This risk is not monitored automatically and depends on external vigilance.

(For this fund, exit fires when the share price reaches the exit edge, when a vehicle-integrity condition breaches, or when the equity risk premium turns negative for two consecutive quarters — any of three paths.)

The Rules

The pre-committed action plan maps each state of the evaluation to a specific instruction:

  • If the thesis is intact (all structural conditions holding, no exit condition fired, basket within the hold band): hold — the equal-weight basket is reasonably priced, the vehicle is sound, and the equal-weight earnings yield premium over the cap-weighted alternative is intact.
  • If the thesis is weakened (for example, the equal-weight earnings yield premium over the cap-weighted benchmark compresses below 0.5 percentage points for two consecutive quarters): do not add; re-validate after the next quarter. If the compression persists, the diversification benefit no longer justifies the higher cost versus a cap-weighted alternative — consider consolidating into a cap-weighted global fund.
  • If the thesis is broken (vehicle integrity breached, or the risk-premium exit condition has fired): exit — move proceeds to a cap-weighted global fund or to risk-free.
  • If the share price falls to or below the buy edge of €4.6: add to the position — the equal-weight basket is meaningfully cheap relative to the risk-free rate; entries at this level should be rare and decisive.
  • If the share price rises to or above the trim edge of €7.2: trim 25–33% of the position — the basket is meaningfully expensive relative to the risk-free rate; harvest the premium and redeploy on the next rebalance.
  • If the share price rises to or above the exit edge of €9.6: exit — the basket is fully priced relative to risk-free; redeploy proceeds to risk-free or to a holding that meets the value test.
