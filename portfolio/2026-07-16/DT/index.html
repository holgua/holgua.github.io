About Dynatrace

Dynatrace sells software that watches software. Large organisations — banks, retailers, telecoms, government agencies — run dozens of interdependent applications across cloud infrastructure they do not entirely control, and when something breaks or slows down, finding the cause quickly is commercially important. Dynatrace's platform does this automatically. A single proprietary agent embeds itself throughout an enterprise's technology stack and builds a real-time causal map of how every component depends on every other. The agent observes; the Grail data lakehouse stores the observations; the Smartscape topology engine traces which application or infrastructure change caused any given problem. The answer arrives in seconds rather than hours of manual log-trawling.

Revenue is almost entirely recurring. Customers sign annual or multi-year contracts and pay to keep the agent running, with spending rising naturally as they add new applications, new cloud regions, and new Dynatrace modules. The Dynatrace Platform Subscription model — consolidating that expansion into a single flexible commitment rather than a patchwork of separate product licences — had reached roughly 70% of total recurring revenue by the most recent quarterly disclosure. The business compounds through expansion of existing accounts rather than constant new-logo acquisition, which is why net revenue retention sits at the centre of the investment case.

The Case for the Bet

The position rests on eight conditions that must keep holding for the thesis to work:

  1. Annual recurring revenue grows at or above 13% on a constant-currency basis for two consecutive quarters. Below 13% constant-currency for two quarters straight is a written exit condition.

Latest reading: 18% year-on-year to $2,054M 2026-05-20. The annual filing does not separately disclose constant-currency growth, but the 18% reported figure is comfortably above the 13% floor and reversed the prior year's deceleration from 21% to 15%.

  1. Net revenue retention stays at or above 107%. A single quarter below 107% triggers a watch; two consecutive quarters below it triggers an exit.

Latest reading: 110% constant-currency 2026-05-20. Third consecutive year at 110% or above, with no quarter approaching the 107% watch threshold.

  1. Platform subscription penetration continues rising and management keeps disclosing it. Stalling below 72% of total recurring revenue for three consecutive quarters, especially if management stops reporting the figure, is a written exit condition.

Latest reading: not disclosed in the annual filing 2026-05-20. The last known figure was approximately 70% from a prior quarterly release. The annual filing describes the model extensively but provides no numeric penetration percentage; watch quarterly earnings releases for this metric.

  1. Free cash flow margin stays above 20% for any full fiscal year. Falling below that floor for a complete fiscal year is a written exit condition.

Latest reading: 26.2% ($529.5M on $2,018M revenue) FY2026 2026-05-20. Up from 25.3% the prior year, confirming operating leverage.

  1. Revenue keeps growing. The qualitative test: is the platform attracting and retaining paying customers?

Latest reading: $2,018M (+18.8% year-on-year) FY2026 2026-05-20. Driven by existing customers expanding usage and new customer adoption; subscription gross profit grew 18% at an 85% margin.

  1. Subscription gross margin holds broadly stable. Sustained compression would signal that the platform's cost structure is deteriorating faster than pricing power can compensate.

Latest reading: no displacement disclosed 2026-05-20. The filing confirms Palo Alto Networks acquired Chronosphere in 2026 — the preparatory signal. No named displacement appears in this filing. Net revenue retention at 110% is the primary counter-signal.

Latest reading: no open-market sales FY2026 2026-05-20. The company repurchased $478.7M of stock and launched a new $1B authorisation with $848.6M remaining; exit condition 4 is not triggered.

The Share Price

Owner earnings yield is the metric the standing rules use to locate the share price in the valuation band. "Owner earnings yield" means: take the cash the business generates for its owners each year, divide by the market value of the company, and express as a percentage — the same idea as a savings account's interest rate, applied to a business. At $45 and with free cash flow of $529.7 million across 303.7 million diluted shares, the yield works out to 4.0%. The fair-value anchor is 4.4% — the yield level at which the shares are priced at exactly what the standing rules regard as fair value. A yield above 4.4% (price below $41) means the shares are trading below fair value; a yield below 3.3% (price above $55) means they have crossed into trim territory.

Today's 4.0% yield sits inside the 3.3%–5.9% band — about $4 above the $41 fair-value reference price. That is a 9% premium to fair value, a narrow gap by the standards of a business growing revenue at 19% a year. The trim line at $55 is 22% above today's price; the exit line at $101, where the yield would fall to 1%, is more than double that.

The Standing Orders

Four price points govern this position. Two are alerts; two are standing orders.

At $41 (fair value): The share price touches the standing rules' own estimate of intrinsic value. This is not a trigger — the thesis would still be intact — but it is the natural reloading point if the price softens while conditions hold.

At $31 (buy edge): The free cash flow yield reaches 5.9%, the level the rules treat as a clear buying opportunity. The company repurchased shares at an average of approximately $42 a share across the full fiscal year (Q4 FY2026 average: $37.71 — TODO(verify): confirm full-year vs Q4 distinction), providing a reference for how management values the business.

Buy at $31. Below the buy edge, the yield exceeds 5.9% and the rules call for accumulation toward a full position — provided the thesis remains intact. At that price the base-case valuation of roughly $58 a share (at a 10% discount rate) implies a margin of safety of roughly 47% (at the $41 fair-value anchor, the margin of safety is approximately 29%).

Trim at $55. Above the trim edge, the free cash flow yield falls below 3.3% and the rules call for reducing the position toward a half-to-two-thirds size. A business with net revenue retention above 110% and over $800 million in net cash does not warrant a full exit at the trim line.

No exit standing order was pre-committed. The $101 level — where the yield would fall to 1% — appears in the scenario chart as a structural reference point, not an actionable instruction. A full exit is reserved for the written exit conditions in The Case for the Bet.

What's Wobbling

Palo Alto Networks' acquisition of Chronosphere. The annual filing confirms the deal, making it the first on-record confirmation of the preparatory signal the thesis treats as a precursor to an exit trigger — public announcement of named Dynatrace customer wins by a bundled-suite competitor. To be precise about what this does and does not mean: the trigger itself requires named customer displacement, confirming that bundled-suite substitution is actually occurring at scale. No such disclosure appears in this filing. Net revenue retention at 110% is the strongest available counter-signal: if displacement were occurring at scale, it would show up in customer cohort spending before it showed up in press releases. The practical watch task is monitoring Palo Alto's quarterly commentary for any named-customer displacement announcements.

Generic AI as a new risk vector. The same filing introduces software built through natural language prompts ("vibe coding") and generic large-language-model tools as competitive threats that could substitute for Dynatrace's specialised observability platform. This language did not appear in prior filings. It does not cross any written exit condition. The switching-cost logic of the thesis — that replacing OneAgent requires years of re-instrumentation regardless of how the monitored software was originally built — remains intact. This is nevertheless a new competitive vector to watch as AI-native monitoring tools mature and enterprise buying criteria shift.

Subscription gross margin. At 85% in the most recent fiscal year, down one percentage point from the prior year due to higher cloud hosting costs as customer utilisation grew, the subscription gross margin introduced a minor note of caution. The overall gross margin expanded by one percentage point to 82%, so pricing power is not in question. One year's compression at this scale is a normal SaaS infrastructure pattern, and no exit floor in The Case for the Bet addresses gross margin directly.

What Could Still Go Wrong

  • Net revenue retention falls below 107% for two consecutive quarters. At the bear-case level of 104%, expansion from existing customers stalls, the platform subscription grows more slowly than anticipated, and the market re-rates the shares sharply lower.
  • Annual recurring revenue growth slips below 13% on a constant-currency basis for two consecutive quarters. Datadog's faster growth rate, combined with bundled-suite positioning from Cisco, Splunk, and Palo Alto/Chronosphere, compresses new-logo win rates below the level the thesis requires.
  • Palo Alto Networks or Cisco publicly announces named Dynatrace customer wins confirming bundled-suite displacement. A single high-profile named displacement in a vertical where Dynatrace has a strong installed base would strike directly at the switching-cost narrative.
  • Generic large-language-model tools erode the perceived complexity of enterprise observability. If future application environments are simpler and more transparent by construction, the premium on deep auto-instrumentation diminishes over time.
  • Free cash flow margin falls below 20% for a complete fiscal year. The business spent $479 million on share repurchases in the most recent fiscal year; any reversal of operating leverage pushing the margin toward the 20% floor would breach a written exit condition.

The Rules

Five pre-committed rules govern what happens from each combination of conditions and share price:

If the thesis is confirmed (all conditions holding): maintain the full position. Continue monitoring net revenue retention, annual recurring revenue growth, free cash flow margin, and platform subscription penetration each quarter. Consider adding modestly if the price dips below $38 — the bear-case midpoint — while retention holds at or above 107%. The confirming signal for the base case is recurring revenue growth at 18% or above, or Dynatrace Intelligence revenue above $50 million.

If the thesis weakens (one or more conditions deteriorating but not broken): reduce to half the position within 30 days of the trigger. The watch signals are: net revenue retention between 107% and 109% for one quarter; annual recurring revenue growth between 13% and 15% for one quarter; platform subscription penetration stalling below 72%. Monitor for two additional quarters before deciding whether to exit fully or rebuild.

If the thesis breaks (a written exit condition confirmed): exit the position fully within one quarter. Do not add to the position on a broken thesis. The conditions that trigger a full exit are enumerated in The Case for the Bet: retention below 107% for two consecutive quarters; recurring revenue growth below 13% for two consecutive quarters; free cash flow margin below 20% for a full fiscal year; CEO or CFO open-market sales above $5 million; documented named-customer displacement by Cisco, Splunk, or Palo Alto/Chronosphere.

If the price falls below $31 (the buy edge): accumulate toward a full position size. At that price the free cash flow yield exceeds 5.9% and the base-case valuation of roughly $58 implies a margin of safety of roughly 47%. The company's own buyback at $37.71 average (Q4 FY2026) sets a practical lower reference; below $35 the yield exceeds 6.2%.

If the price rises above $55 (the trim edge): trim toward a half-to-two-thirds position size as the free cash flow yield falls below 3.3%. Do not exit fully unless the thesis is simultaneously breaking — a business with this level of cash generation and customer retention justifies continued partial ownership through multiple expansion.

Scenario Definitions

A scenario is a joint assumption about two independent variables: operating trajectory (how the holding's underlying cash flows grow relative to their long-run trend) and exit multiple (what the market pays for those cash flows at the horizon, relative to a fair-value anchor).

Scenario Operating trajectory Exit multiple
Bear Below trend Below fair value
Base At trend At fair value
Bull Above trend At or above fair value

Non-negotiable rule: In the bear case, both dimensions must be adverse simultaneously — a scenario where only the multiple moves is a re-rating scenario, not a bear.

Bear cases are comparable in structure across all holding types (company, ETF, bond), but not in magnitude. A company bear reflects idiosyncratic execution failure; an ETF bear reflects a broad market drawdown. Different mitigations, different diversification implications.
