About Bund Linker 2033

The Bundesrepublik Deutschland Inflationsindexierte Anleihe 2021/2033 (ISIN: DE0001030583) is a German sovereign inflation-linked bond issued by the Federal Republic of Germany and maturing on 15 April 2033. The bond's principal and coupon payments are adjusted by the German Harmonised Index of Consumer Prices excluding tobacco (HICP ex-tobacco), published by Eurostat, meaning the real purchasing power of the position is preserved regardless of realised inflation over the holding period.

The Federal Republic of Germany carries the highest sovereign credit rating at all three major agencies: AAA from Fitch and S&P and Aaa from Moody's. The bond is issued and administered by the Deutsche Finanzagentur. The current index ratio stands at 1.26021, reflecting approximately 26% cumulative inflation since the bond's index base date. The Macaulay duration is approximately 6.8 years; a one-basis-point move in yield changes the clean price by approximately €0.064 per €100 face value.

Clean price: €94.93 as of 2026-06-24. Real yield: 0.87%. Maturity: 15 April 2033.

The Case for the Bet

Bund Linker 2033 is held as EUR inflation protection. The bond's principal and coupon payments are linked to the German HICP ex-tobacco index, meaning the real purchasing power of the position is preserved regardless of realised inflation. Credit risk is effectively zero: the Federal Republic of Germany has never defaulted on a EUR-denominated obligation. The bet is structural, not tactical — this holding is sized as a portfolio inflation-hedge allocation, not as a rates trade.

Three conditions govern the bet:

  1. The real yield at the current market price must not fall below −1.0% for two consecutive quarterly reviews while EUR cash or nominal Bunds offer a superior after-tax real return. Below that level, the inflation-hedge benefit is outweighed by the guaranteed real loss embedded in the price paid; the compound trigger requires both legs to be active simultaneously.
  2. German sovereign credit must not be downgraded below AA− by any two of the three major rating agencies. A double downgrade would signal a structural shift in issuer creditworthiness and require full reassessment of the holding.
  3. The HICP ex-tobacco index methodology must not be materially restructured in a way that severs the link between the index and realised consumer-price inflation. A substitution of the reference index by Eurostat would change what the bond actually hedges.

Condition (3) — HICP methodology carries no automated signal. Eurostat has not announced any pending restructuring of the HICP ex-tobacco index methodology. Exit condition (3) is assessed manually at each quarterly review.

What Could Still Go Wrong

  • Real yield falls toward the exit threshold. A sustained rally in the bond price — driven by ECB rate cuts, a flight to quality, or low inflation expectations — could push the clean price above €104 (trim edge) and eventually above €108 (exit edge). At €108 the compound trigger's first leg fires; exit requires the second leg (EUR cash offering better real return) to be active simultaneously.
  • Break-even inflation rises sharply above the ECB target. A break-even above 3.0% would signal the market pricing in sustained above-target inflation. At that level the bond continues to protect purchasing power, but the linker's advantage over nominal Bunds narrows, which could make the nominal alternative more attractive from a carry perspective.
  • Germany downgrade. A double downgrade below AA− remains a remote tail risk. Germany carries AAA/Aaa at all three agencies; any downgrade would need to cover two agencies before exit condition (2) fires.
  • ECB rate cuts compress the linker advantage. If the ECB cuts its deposit rate to 1.0% while break-even inflation stays near 1.9%, implied EUR cash real yield turns negative (−0.9%) and the linker's 0.87% real yield looks even more attractive. This would be a reason to hold or add, not exit — but it changes the opportunity-cost calculation materially.

Gaps in the Picture

  • Accrued interest is not included in the €94.93 clean price; the dirty price (clean + accrued) determines actual settlement costs. The accrued amount is small at current coupon levels.
  • The pull-to-par schedule assumes the real yield holds at exactly 0.5% (fair value). If yields move materially, the schedule's price estimates will diverge from realised outcomes.
  • Tax treatment of the inflation uplift on the principal — which is taxable in some jurisdictions in the year the index ratio rises — is not modelled in the forward return figures.

The Rules

If the conditions remain intact and the real yield stays within the band: hold — the bond is within its intended range and the inflation-hedge thesis is working.

If any condition weakens without reaching its exit threshold: do not add. Re-assess at the next quarterly review. Monitor the ECB deposit rate and break-even inflation before increasing the allocation.

If a named exit condition triggers — compound trigger fires, Germany is downgraded by two agencies, or HICP methodology changes: exit. Move proceeds to nominal short-duration EUR Bunds or EUR cash.

If the bond's real yield rises to or above 1.5% (price falls to or below €91): add to the position. A 7-year AAA EUR sovereign linker at 1.5% real is highly attractive; scale in proportionally to the inflation-hedge allocation target.

If the bond's real yield falls to or below −0.5% (price rises to or above €104): trim 25% on the first crossing. If the yield remains below −0.5% at the next quarterly review, trim an additional 25%. Park proceeds in EUR cash or short nominal Bunds.

If the compound trigger fires — real yield below −1.0% for 90 days and EUR cash offering a superior real return: exit the full position. Switch proceeds to EUR cash or short-duration nominal Bunds; the inflation-hedge function is no longer additive versus the alternative.
