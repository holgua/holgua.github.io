About Clean Energy

Clean Energy — the iShares Global Clean Energy Transition UCITS ETF (listed in Frankfurt as IQQH) — holds roughly 100 companies that stand to benefit from the global shift away from fossil fuels. The fund tracks the S&P Global Clean Energy Transition Index, an index that spans solar panel manufacturers, wind-turbine makers, electricity-grid infrastructure builders, electrification enablers, and companies providing the technology backbone for the energy transition. The mix sits heavily in utilities (42% of the basket), technology (33%), and industrials (23%), which explains why the fund's earnings per share are structurally lower than those of the broad market — regulated utilities and industrial equipment companies simply earn less per dollar of assets than software or financial businesses do.

The fund is managed by iShares, the exchange-traded-fund arm of BlackRock, one of the world's largest asset managers. It is structured as a UCITS fund (Undertakings for Collective Investment in Transferable Securities), an Irish-domiciled legal wrapper that offers investor protections standard across European Union markets. The annual cost is 0.65%, or 65 cents for every €100 invested, within the configured ceiling of 0.80%.

The Case for the Bet

The bet rests on a single structural proposition — that the global shift away from fossil fuels will drive sustained investment in clean-energy infrastructure over the next decade, creating durable earnings growth for the companies that supply the equipment, generate the power, and build the grid. Six specific conditions must hold for that proposition to remain valid:

  1. Basket earnings yield stays below the earnings-yield trap threshold. Exit condition 1 fires when the basket earnings yield rises above 8.0% for four consecutive quarters while the fund is simultaneously sustaining capital losses at the index level — a pattern that signals constituent-company earnings are collapsing, not that the fund is simply cheap.

Latest reading: Basket earnings yield 4.59% as of 2026-08-17. The exit leg requiring yield above 8.0% is not met. The persistence leg (five consecutive negative equity risk premium snapshots) is satisfied, but since both legs must fire simultaneously, exit condition 1 is not triggered.

  1. Vehicle integrity: no breach on expense ratio, assets under management, or single-holding concentration. The fund should not become too expensive (annual cost above 0.80%), too small (assets below €200 million), or too concentrated in a single name (any holding above 20% of the basket). A product wind-down notice from iShares would also constitute a breach.

Latest reading: Expense ratio 0.65% (ceiling 0.80%); assets under management €3.56 billion (floor €200 million); top holding First Solar at 8.07% (ceiling 20.0%). All three measures clear their thresholds; no wind-down notice. No breach.

  1. Index methodology intact. S&P Global should not materially alter the Clean Energy Transition Index — changing which sectors or companies qualify, or how they are weighted — in a way that changes the economic exposure this position was intended to own.

Latest reading: No automated signal for a methodology change is available in the current pipeline. The fund sponsor is BlackRock/iShares and the legal type is confirmed Exchange Traded Fund. Monitoring relies on external S&P index-governance alerts and iShares product-page announcements.

  1. No legislated repeal of the key regulatory tailwinds. The US Inflation Reduction Act's clean-energy tax credits and the EU Green Deal's binding emissions targets are the primary policy underwriters of the capex-cycle thesis. A durable, legislated repeal — not a temporary pause — of either programme would remove the regulatory foundation.

Latest reading: No automated signal. The thesis marks this as a condition to monitor through external policy sources rather than financial data.

  1. Solar and wind costs continue to undercut fossil incumbents. The structural cost advantage of renewables — their levelised cost of energy falling below fossil-fuel alternatives in most geographies — is the economic engine behind the capex cycle. If that cost advantage disappears and capacity additions flatline for two consecutive years, the core driver is gone.

What's Wobbling

The only condition generating a caution signal is the equity risk premium against the US 10-year Treasury benchmark. With the basket earnings yield at 4.59% and the Treasury rate at 4.72%, the premium is −0.13% — the basket is yielding fractionally less than the risk-free alternative on a trailing earnings basis. That negative reading has now persisted for five consecutive snapshots, a streak running from 2026-07-30, which satisfies the persistence leg of exit condition 1 as described in The Case for the Bet.

That signal is weaker than it looks. The comparison uses a US dollar benchmark (the 10-year Treasury) for a fund denominated in euros, which structurally understates the basket's premium against a euro investor's natural risk-free rate. Using the German 10-year government bond at roughly 2.5% instead, the premium would be approximately +2.1% — a reading that would be in the confirm range. The thesis itself acknowledges this limitation: the earnings-yield comparison against a US benchmark carries less decision-making weight for IQQH than it does for US-dollar funds. That said, the signal is real, it has persisted, and it stays in the wobble category until it turns positive.

The second leg of exit condition 1 — requiring the basket earnings yield to rise above 8.0% simultaneously with the negative premium — remains far from being met at 4.59%. No exit condition has fired.

What Could Still Go Wrong

  • Rising fund costs. If iShares raises the annual expense ratio above 0.80%, vehicle-integrity condition 2 breaches and the case for holding this specific fund weakens regardless of the thematic outlook.
  • Fund shrinkage. If assets under management fall below €200 million, product-closure risk rises sharply. iShares has a history of closing thematic UCITS funds that fall below commercial viability; at €3.56 billion the fund is well above this floor today, but a prolonged bear case in clean energy could erode it.
  • Index restructure. S&P Global could alter the S&P Global Clean Energy Transition Index's methodology in ways that shift the fund's economic exposure toward different sectors or geographies, invalidating the original thesis without any change in the fund's price or name.
  • Concentration creep. The basket's top-10 names hold roughly half the fund, and a rapid run-up in a single constituent could push it past the 20% ceiling. The quarterly rebalance manages this, but it is a structural feature of a ~100-stock thematic basket.
  • Policy reversal and the earnings-yield trap. A sustained withdrawal of IRA or EU Green Deal support could simultaneously shrink earnings and push the basket earnings yield above 8.0% — the combination that triggers exit condition 1.

(For this fund, exit fires when the share price reaches the exit edge, when a vehicle-integrity condition breaches, or when the named exit-condition trigger fires — any of three paths.)

The Rules

If the thesis is confirmed — all drivers in the confirm or mixed range and no exit condition met — hold the position. The thematic thesis is intact and the vehicle is sound. Do not add on the earnings-yield signal alone; add only when the decarbonisation capex case is actively strengthening.

If the thesis is weakening — any driver showing a challenge signal but no exit condition triggered — do not add. Re-assess after the next quarter and after any material policy development affecting the US Inflation Reduction Act or the EU Green Deal. If exit conditions 3 through 5 are approaching, accelerate the assessment timeline.

If the thesis is broken — vehicle integrity breached or a thematic exit condition triggered — exit the position. Move proceeds to a risk-free holding or into a broad-market fund as a replacement.

If the price falls below the buy edge — the basket earnings yield rises above 6.25%, implying a price at or below €6.8 — add in tranches, provided the basket earnings yield is below 8.0% and the thematic thesis is intact. A basket earnings yield above 8.0% alongside capital losses signals exit condition 1: verify before adding.

If the price rises above the trim edge — the basket earnings yield falls below 4.00%, implying a price at or above €11 — trim 25–33% of the position. The basket is meaningfully expensive against the risk-free rate at that level; move proceeds to a risk-free holding or to a broad-market fund rather than holding cash within the thematic position.

If the price rises above the exit edge — the basket earnings yield falls to or below 3.00%, implying a price at or above €14 — exit the full position. The basket is fully priced against the risk-free rate; redeploy proceeds to a risk-free holding or to a position that meets the value test.

Scenario Definitions

A scenario is a joint assumption about two independent variables: operating trajectory (how the holding's underlying cash flows grow relative to their long-run trend) and exit multiple (what the market pays for those cash flows at the horizon, relative to a fair-value anchor).

Scenario Operating trajectory Exit multiple
Bear Below trend Below fair value
Base At trend At fair value
Bull Above trend At or above fair value

Non-negotiable rule: In the bear case, both dimensions must be adverse simultaneously — a scenario where only the multiple moves is a re-rating scenario, not a bear.

Bear cases are comparable in structure across all holding types (company, ETF, bond), but not in magnitude. A company bear reflects idiosyncratic execution failure; an ETF bear reflects a broad market drawdown. Different mitigations, different diversification implications.
