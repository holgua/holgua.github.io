About Ferrari

Ferrari makes its money by selling ultra-luxury sports cars and grand tourers — approximately 13,500 to 14,000 per year — at prices that generate operating margins above 38%, a level associated with the world's finest luxury-goods houses rather than with any automotive peer. The business model is deliberately simple: permanently keep supply below demand, set prices accordingly, and use the resulting pricing power to expand margins through any economic cycle. Ferrari has held annual production essentially flat for a decade while revenues have nearly tripled, proof that the equation works.

The structural barriers are three. First, the brand — seventy years of Formula 1 racing heritage and the mythology of Enzo Ferrari create a desirability that no amount of capital spending can replicate. Second, the scarcity architecture — production caps enforced by management choice, not by capacity constraint, ensure that demand permanently exceeds supply and that buyers compete for allocation rather than negotiating prices. Third, the loyalty staircase — access to the most desirable limited-edition models is gated by documented prior ownership history, creating a closed ecosystem that rewards existing owners and effectively taxes any attempt to defect to a competitor. Taken together, these three barriers explain why Ferrari can charge a premium that results in one of the highest operating margins in listed manufacturing.

The Case for the Bet

The bet is that Ferrari's scarcity-franchise model remains intact and compounds earnings at 9–11% a year for the next several years, with the Elettrica electric vehicle expanding rather than compressing the margin structure. Six specific conditions must hold for the thesis to remain valid:

  1. Annual production volume stays at or below 16,000 units. Production discipline is the moat. Any management decision to grow volumes materially — beyond the 14,500-unit thesis threshold and certainly above the 16,000-unit exit trigger — would signal that the scarcity architecture has been abandoned and that the brand premium would erode toward conventional automotive levels over time.

Latest reading: FY2025 deliveries were 13,640 units (confirmed from the Ferrari Annual Report 2025), below the 14,500-unit thesis discipline threshold and well below the 16,000-unit exit trigger. Volume was broadly flat from FY2024's 13,752 units. Exit condition (1) is not triggered.

  1. Operating margin stays above 35% in any reported fiscal year. The 38% level is the ongoing moat-intact signal; the 35% threshold is the exit floor. A single fiscal year below 35% would indicate either that the scarcity premium has been compromised or that Elettrica development costs have permanently altered the cost structure.

Latest reading: FY2025 operating margin was 38.8%, expanding from 38.3% in FY2024. The four-year trajectory (FY2022–FY2025) shows sequential expansion from 34.8%. Management guidance for 2026 is at or above 39%. Exit condition (2) is not triggered.

  1. Cash generated from operations stays above net income in any fiscal year. A ratio below 1.0 times would be an accounting-quality red flag inconsistent with every year of the observed track record and could indicate earnings manipulation or working-capital deterioration.

Latest reading: Cash from operations was EUR 2,349 million against net income of EUR 1,597 million in FY2025 — a ratio of 1.47 times, the highest in two years. The five-year range has been 1.27 to 1.54 times. Exit condition (3) is not triggered.

  1. The implied average selling price per unit does not decline for two consecutive fiscal years. A sustained pricing decline on non-limited models would signal demand softening enough to affect the standard lineup — a different signal from the year-to-year timing of limited-series deliveries.

Latest reading: Revenue grew 7.03% while volume fell slightly from 13,752 to 13,640 units, implying an average selling price of roughly EUR 524,000 per unit — higher than the prior year. Exit condition (4) is not triggered.

  1. The US tariff rate stays below 25%, OR Ferrari does not guide for operating margin below 38% attributable to tariff absorption. This is a two-leg trigger: both conditions must be simultaneously met to fire the exit. The point is to test whether pricing power is sufficient to absorb a material tariff — Ferrari's surcharge response in 2025 was the empirical test.

Latest reading: The EU-US trade agreement effective July 2026 sets the Ferrari US tariff at 15%. Ferrari's 2026 guidance is at or above 39% operating margin. A threat of 25% re-escalation emerged in May 2026 but remains unconfirmed. Neither leg of exit condition (5) is currently met.

  1. The Elettrica order book oversubscribes within 12 months of opening. The Elettrica electric vehicle, revealed at the October 2025 Capital Markets Day, is the most important strategic test of the franchise over the next few years. If the order book requires price concessions or delivery deferrals, it would signal that the electric-vehicle transition requires compromising the pricing model.

Latest reading: The Elettrica has not yet opened its commercial order book as of the analysis date. The car was technically revealed in late 2025 — four electric motors, over 1,000 horsepower, expected pricing above EUR 500,000, and deliveries planned from late 2026 — but customer allocation has not begun. Exit condition (6)'s 12-month clock has not started.

The Share Price

At today's price of $377 per share, Ferrari sits at fair value — an earnings yield (net income as a proportion of market value) of 2.75% matches the fair-value anchor of 2.75% almost exactly. In other words, $377 is the price at which the business earns exactly what the valuation model says it should at baseline. There is no margin of safety at this entry point.

The buy edge is at $244, where that earnings yield would rise to 4.25% — the level at which the Rules call for adding to the position, because the price is discounting the business's earnings power enough to offer a meaningful return above the hurdle rate. The trim edge is at $829, where the yield would compress to 1.25% — a level at which the market would be pricing in decades of compounding in advance, and where reducing the position to harvest that premium makes sense.

Between those two edges lies a wide middle band — $244 to $829 represents a range of roughly 240% from bottom to top — which is intentionally designed to avoid activity during normal price fluctuations. The current price at $377 sits comfortably in the middle of that band. See the TL;DR above for the exact price levels.

What's Wobbling

The US tariff situation. The current 15% tariff rate has been absorbed without margin concession — Ferrari's 2025 surcharge test confirmed pricing power empirically. But the threat of re-escalation to 25% is real and sits on the agenda of the next US-EU trade review. The wobble is classified as mixed rather than a direct challenge to the thesis: the two-leg exit condition (25% tariff rate AND a margin guidance cut below 38%) is not currently met, and Ferrari's 2026 guidance of at or above 39% operates as a buffer on the second leg. The wobble does not change the call, but it should be monitored at each Ferrari earnings release and at any US trade policy announcement. See the Case for the Bet for what would tip this into a trigger.

The Elettrica order book is not yet open. Exit condition (6) — oversubscription within 12 months of opening — cannot yet be assessed. The clock has not started. This is not a wobble in the negative sense; Ferrari's revealed specifications (over 1,000 horsepower, four electric motors, expected price above EUR 500,000) suggest the intent is to apply the same scarcity-and-premium model that works for the combustion-engine lineup. The watch condition becomes active when the commercial order book opens, expected in the second half of 2026. A 12-month window from that date is the measurement period.

What Could Still Go Wrong

  • A luxury-market downturn could reduce the order-book depth that sustains Ferrari's pricing discipline. At today's price, the bear case — where Elettrica development costs simultaneously compress margins toward 34–35% and institutional investors reprice the stock toward automotive rather than luxury-goods benchmarks — still generates a positive annualised return of approximately +1.8% over three years, but leaves essentially no buffer. A harsher version of the bear case (deeper multiple compression) would produce losses.
  • Elettrica order-book failure is the single highest-severity risk. If demand for the electric model requires price concessions or production schedule changes, the thesis is undermined at its forward growth driver. This has not happened, but the verdict will not be available until well into 2027.
  • A return to 25% US tariffs, combined with evidence that Ferrari cannot fully pass them through (visible in an operating margin guidance cut), would trigger exit condition (5). This scenario was partially tested in 2025 and Ferrari passed — but at 15%, not 25%.
  • Production cap breach above 16,000 annual units is a structural moat risk. Volume expansion to chase revenue growth would dilute the scarcity premium and compress the pricing power that the entire valuation model rests on. This is the irreversible risk — once the cap is broken, the model cannot be reassembled.
  • Capital allocation failure at elevated multiples: Ferrari has been repurchasing shares while the stock trades at around 36 times trailing earnings. From a strict value standpoint, buybacks at this multiple only create value if the intrinsic worth per share exceeds the repurchase price — which the current owner-earnings model suggests is approximately at par, not below it. The risk is that continued buybacks at or above fair value gradually erode the per-share compounding trajectory.

Gaps in the Picture

Ferrari files its annual report in a structured electronic format (ESEF) that does not separately tag several items a full analysis would require: capital expenditure, gross profit, long-term debt, and shares outstanding are all absent from the structured data and must be estimated or drawn from narrative disclosures. Four of the seven thesis drivers — scarcity architecture, Elettrica execution, US tariff exposure, and governance alignment — rely on auxiliary analyses (competition research, political exposure review, and the financial brief) rather than structured electronic extraction, because the relevant data does not appear as machine-readable tags in the filing. This is a limitation of the mandate covering companies at Ferrari's filing scope, not a disclosure deficiency on Ferrari's part.

The earnings figure used in the valuation ($1.82 billion, derived from FY2025 net income of EUR 1,597 million converted at EUR/USD 1.1406) is an approximation: it uses net income directly as a proxy for true earnings power, because Ferrari's annual filing does not separately tag capital expenditure. The true earnings available to shareholders — net income plus depreciation less true maintenance capital spending — are likely higher than this figure, since significant portions of Ferrari's investment cash outflows are growth capital for the Elettrica programme rather than maintenance. The valuation is therefore conservative on this dimension.

A discrepancy exists in the delivery-count data: the competition analysis cites 13,640 FY2025 deliveries from the Annual Report in one section, while a summary table elsewhere references approximately 14,633 units. Both figures are below the 14,500-unit thesis discipline threshold and well below the 16,000-unit exit trigger, so neither version would change the exit-condition verdict — but the discrepancy should be reconciled at the next competition-analysis refresh.

The Rules

If the thesis is confirmed — all measurable drivers in line and no exit condition triggered — the rule is to hold with no action. Ferrari's moat is intact; scarcity architecture, operating margins at or above 38%, and cash conversion above 1.20 times are all confirmed. Continue compounding.

If the thesis is weakened — one or more drivers move toward challenge without triggering a written exit condition — the rule is to not add to the position. Monitor the specific weakening driver (most likely: operating margin trend or Elettrica uptake) through one full fiscal year before reassessing position size.

If the thesis is broken — any single exit condition fires — the rule is to exit the full position within 30 days. In particular, exit condition (1) (production above 16,000 units) destroys the moat irreversibly and requires no further deliberation. No averaging down. Document the triggered condition for future learning.

If the price falls below the buy band — owner-earnings yield above 4.25%, implying a price near or below $244 — the rule is to confirm the thesis is intact first, then add in tranches over one to two quarters. A price at that level represents a rare buy-band entry into an exceptional franchise and should be acted on decisively once conditions are confirmed.

If the price rises above the trim band — owner-earnings yield below 1.25%, implying a price near or above $829 — the rule is to trim 25–33% of the position to harvest the premium. At that price level, Ferrari's market value would be north of $145 billion on current earnings — the market would be discounting decades of compounding. Disciplined reduction preserves the option to re-enter at a better yield.

If the price rises above the exit band — owner-earnings yield below 1.0%, implying a price well above $829 — the rule is to exit the full position. The market would be pricing in years of base-case compounding instantly; the implied forward return would fall below the long-term equity premium. Redeploy proceeds at the next buy-band crossing or into a cheaper compounder. No re-entry within 90 days to defuse anchoring.

Scenario Definitions

A scenario is a joint assumption about two independent variables: operating trajectory (how the holding's underlying cash flows grow relative to their long-run trend) and exit multiple (what the market pays for those cash flows at the horizon, relative to a fair-value anchor).

Scenario Operating trajectory Exit multiple
Bear Below trend Below fair value
Base At trend At fair value
Bull Above trend At or above fair value

Non-negotiable rule: In the bear case, both dimensions must be adverse simultaneously — a scenario where only the multiple moves is a re-rating scenario, not a bear.

Bear cases are comparable in structure across all holding types (company, ETF, bond), but not in magnitude. A company bear reflects idiosyncratic execution failure; an ETF bear reflects a broad market drawdown. Different mitigations, different diversification implications.
