About US Treasury 0-1y

The Vanguard U.S. Treasury 0-1 Year Bond UCITS ETF (Acc) holds a basket of US government bills maturing within one year. Every holding in the basket is a short-dated obligation of the US federal government — the closest thing to risk-free lending that exists in the dollar system. The fund is domiciled in Ireland, listed on the London Stock Exchange, and carries the accumulating share class label: rather than paying out interest as cash dividends, the fund reinvests income back into the net asset value, so the fund's quoted price drifts upward over time as coupons accrue.

The weighted-average maturity of the basket is approximately 0.38 years — a little under five months. That ultra-short duration means the fund reprices to whatever short-term rate prevails within months, not years. It behaves like a rolling deposit in US Treasury bills rather than a bond with meaningful interest-rate sensitivity. Annual cost is 0.07% (gross annual charge; no securities-lending revenue is configured).

The Case for the Bet

VDST is held as a USD cash-equivalent placeholder — not as a return-seeking investment in the conventional sense. The bet is that this vehicle offers the best available combination of cost, liquidity, and mandate stability for parking dollars while waiting for equity opportunities.   1. Annual charge stays below 0.10% for every consecutive pair of quarterly reviews. The fund currently charges 0.07% a year — well below the 0.10% ceiling. If it breached that ceiling in two consecutive quarterly checks, comparable ultra-short-duration US Treasury vehicles would be available at lower cost and the fee advantage of this particular fund would be lost.

What Could Still Go Wrong

  • Fee drift. Vanguard raises the annual charge above 0.10% for two consecutive quarterly periods. Comparable ultra-short-duration US Treasury products exist at lower cost; the fund's fee advantage would disappear and the rationale for this specific vehicle over a cheaper alternative would weaken.
  • Size erosion. Assets under management fall below the one-billion-dollar floor. That scale is the threshold below which secondary-market liquidity and redemption capacity become materially uncertain for a fund whose core value is instant liquidity.
  • Index or mandate change. Vanguard alters the index methodology — extending the maturity band, switching index providers, or converting to a distributing share class. Any structural change that alters the T-bill-basket character of the fund would require reassessment from scratch.
  • US sovereign downgrade. Two of the three major rating agencies (Fitch, Moody's, S&P) downgrade US sovereign credit below AA−. This would call into question whether the fund still serves as a genuine cash equivalent or whether it now carries meaningful credit risk.

(For this fund, the named exit triggers are: annual charge above 0.10% for two quarters, assets below one billion dollars, or a US sovereign double-downgrade — any of these fires the exit regardless of where the share price or yield stands at the time.)

The Rules

If the vehicle-integrity conditions all hold and the basket yield remains between 3.5% and 5.5%, hold the position without adding or trimming.

If any vehicle-integrity condition shows early-warning signs — the annual charge approaching 0.10%, assets declining toward the one-billion-dollar floor, or external reports of a sovereign rating watch — do not add. Re-assess at the next quarterly review and monitor trends before increasing allocation.

If one or more of the numbered exit conditions fires — annual charge above 0.10% for two consecutive quarterly reviews, assets falling below one billion dollars, or US sovereign credit downgraded below AA− by at least two of the three major rating agencies — exit the position and reinvest in EUR cash or short-dated nominal German government bonds.

If the basket yield reaches 5.5% or higher (share price at or below $41) and vehicle-integrity conditions are clear, add at the margin when cash is available — that yield level represents an unusually attractive T-bill rate relative to EUR alternatives.

If the basket yield compresses to 3.5% or lower (share price at or above $65) and no integrity condition has fired, trim at the margin — at that yield the carry advantage of USD T-bills over EUR cash is materially weaker.

If the basket yield falls to 3.0% or below (share price at or above $76), exit the position per whichever of the three numbered exit conditions is most applicable.

Scenario Definitions

A scenario is a joint assumption about two independent variables: operating trajectory (how the holding's underlying cash flows grow relative to their long-run trend) and exit multiple (what the market pays for those cash flows at the horizon, relative to a fair-value anchor).

Scenario Operating trajectory Exit multiple
Bear Below trend Below fair value
Base At trend At fair value
Bull Above trend At or above fair value

Non-negotiable rule: In the bear case, both dimensions must be adverse simultaneously — a scenario where only the multiple moves is a re-rating scenario, not a bear.

Bear cases are comparable in structure across all holding types (company, ETF, bond), but not in magnitude. A company bear reflects idiosyncratic execution failure; an ETF bear reflects a broad market drawdown. Different mitigations, different diversification implications.
