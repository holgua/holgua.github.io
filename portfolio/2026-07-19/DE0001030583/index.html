About Bund Linker 2033

The Federal Republic of Germany issues a class of government bonds whose principal and interest payments are linked to a consumer-price index — specifically the German harmonised index of consumer prices excluding tobacco, published by Eurostat. When consumer prices rise, the face value on which interest is paid rises with them, preserving the purchasing power of the investment in real terms. These instruments are known as inflation-linked government bonds, or linkers for short.

This particular bond was issued in February 2021 and matures on 2033-04-15, giving it roughly twelve years of original life. The stated coupon rate is 0.10% per year — deliberately low, since the real return comes primarily from the indexation of principal rather than from the cash coupon itself. At settlement, a buyer pays the dirty price of approximately €119.82 per €100 original face value, not €94.78. The €94.78 is the quoted clean price in real terms (the same purchasing-power units as the issuance date). To arrive at the actual euros that change hands, one multiplies by the current index ratio of 1.26381 and adds accrued interest of approximately €25.04.

The Case for the Bet

The bond is held as structural EUR inflation protection, sized as a portfolio allocation rather than a directional rates trade. The real yield versus the risk-free EUR alternative is the primary signal; three numbered exit conditions define the circumstances under which the bet would need to be abandoned:

  1. Real yield falling below −1.00% for two consecutive quarters, with EUR cash offering a superior after-tax real return. A sustained real yield that deeply negative means the bond is priced to deliver a guaranteed real loss — the inflation-hedge benefit is outweighed by the cost embedded in the purchase price. The two-quarter persistence requirement filters out brief rate dislocations. Both legs must fire simultaneously: yield persistently below −1.00% and cash offering more in real terms.

Latest reading: Real yield 0.90% as of 2026-07-17. Zero days below the exit threshold have been recorded. Cash real yield is approximately +0.20%, meaning the linker leads cash by about 70 basis points. Compound trigger not active.

  1. Germany sovereign credit being downgraded below AA− by any two of the three major rating agencies. The investment case assumes near-zero credit risk — a structural property of the issuer, not a market estimate. A downgrade below AA− by at least two agencies would signal a fundamental shift in issuer creditworthiness that the original thesis did not price in.

Latest reading: Germany is rated AAA by Fitch, Aaa by Moody's, and AAA by S&P as of 2026-06-15. No downgrade signals observed. Exit condition (2) not triggered.

  1. The HICP ex-tobacco index methodology being materially restructured in a way that severs the link between the index and realised consumer-price inflation. The inflation-protection thesis depends on the integrity of the reference index. If Eurostat were to change the index structure such that it no longer tracks actual consumer-price experience, the hedging function would be lost even if the bond's contractual terms remained unchanged.

Latest reading: No pending methodology restructuring recorded as of 2026-06-15. Exit condition (3) not triggered.

What Could Still Go Wrong

  • Sustained inflation overshoot compresses the real yield. If inflation expectations rise sharply above the current break-even of 2.05%, the nominal bond market could re-price, compressing the linker's real yield. Should that compression persist below −1.00% while EUR cash simultaneously rose to offer a better real return, the compound exit trigger would fire.
  • European Central Bank cuts rates aggressively. A sharp reduction in the ECB deposit rate would lower the hurdle for EUR cash real returns and could, depending on break-even movements, narrow or eliminate the linker's real-yield advantage. At −0.50% the trim ladder begins; at −1.00% the full exit fires.
  • German sovereign credit deterioration. An extraordinary fiscal event — sustained debt-to-gross-domestic-product deterioration combined with a European sovereign stress episode — could prompt rating agencies to lower Germany's top-tier credit standing. Exit condition (2) fires at a two-notch downgrade by at least two agencies; the current AAA/Aaa/AAA standing leaves substantial buffer.
  • HICP methodology restructuring. Eurostat holds authority over the reference index. A structural change to the basket or calculation methodology that severed the link between the index and realised consumer-price experience would break the inflation-hedge thesis regardless of the bond's price level.
  • Liquidity at a forced-exit moment. Inflation-linked sovereign bonds trade in relatively thin markets compared with conventional government bonds of similar maturity. In a stress scenario where EUR cash is urgently needed, the bid-ask spread could be wider than normal, making an exit more costly than the clean-price chart implies.

The Rules

If the thesis shows signs of weakening — specifically, if the real yield moves below 0% but has not yet triggered exit condition (1) — then do not add. Reassess by comparing the real after-tax return against EUR cash and nominal government bonds before any sizing decision.

If any of the three numbered exit conditions fires — real yield below −1.00% persistently with cash offering a superior real return, a German sovereign downgrade below AA− by two or more agencies, or a material HICP index restructuring — then exit. Move the proceeds to nominal short-duration EUR government bonds or EUR cash.

The inflation-hedge function is no longer additive versus the alternative at that point; switch proceeds to EUR cash or short-duration nominal government bonds.

Scenario Definitions

A scenario is a joint assumption about two independent variables: operating trajectory (how the holding's underlying cash flows grow relative to their long-run trend) and exit multiple (what the market pays for those cash flows at the horizon, relative to a fair-value anchor).

Scenario Operating trajectory Exit multiple
Bear Below trend Below fair value
Base At trend At fair value
Bull Above trend At or above fair value

Non-negotiable rule: In the bear case, both dimensions must be adverse simultaneously — a scenario where only the multiple moves is a re-rating scenario, not a bear.

Bear cases are comparable in structure across all holding types (company, ETF, bond), but not in magnitude. A company bear reflects idiosyncratic execution failure; an ETF bear reflects a broad market drawdown. Different mitigations, different diversification implications.
