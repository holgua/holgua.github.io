About the Bond

The Federal Republic of Germany issued this bond in February 2021, with maturity on 2033-04-15 — roughly seven years from now. Unlike a conventional German government bond, which pays a fixed nominal coupon and returns a fixed nominal principal at maturity, this bond ties both its coupon and its redemption amount to the German consumer-price index — specifically, the Harmonised Index of Consumer Prices excluding tobacco (HICP ex-tobacco), published by Eurostat. When inflation rises, the bond's cash flows rise in step. The stated coupon is 0.10% per year, but that 0.10% applies to a growing inflation-adjusted principal, so the actual cash paid out increases with the index over time. Issue volume was €10.65 billion, making it a liquid benchmark linker. The next coupon falls on 2027-04-15.

Dirty price, clean price, and the index ratio. The market quotes the bond in clean price terms — stripping out accrued interest so that prices are comparable across settlement dates. Today's clean price is €94.93 per €100 of original face value. But the amount that actually changes hands is the clean price multiplied by the index ratio (the cumulative inflation since issuance), plus accrued interest.

A buyer pays the dirty price of €119.74 per €100 original face value at settlement, not €94.93. The €94.93 is the quoted clean price in real terms (same purchasing-power units as the issuance date). To get the actual euros that change hands, multiply by the index ratio (1.26021), then add accrued interest.

The Case for the Bet

The holding exists as a euro-denominated inflation hedge. The bond's principal and coupon payments are tied to the German consumer-price index, so the real purchasing power of the position is preserved regardless of realised inflation. Credit risk is negligible — Germany (rated AAA by Fitch, Aaa by Moody's, AAA by S&P) has never defaulted on a euro-denominated obligation. The position is sized as a structural portfolio allocation to inflation protection, not as a tactical interest-rate trade.

The primary exit signal is the real yield relative to the risk-free euro alternative. Three specific conditions, written at the time the position was established, govern when to leave:

  1. Persistent negative real yield with superior cash alternative. The real yield at the current market price falls below −1.00% for two consecutive quarters, AND EUR cash or conventional Bunds offer a better after-tax real return. Both legs must fire together — the inflation-hedge benefit is considered lost only when the bond simultaneously delivers a guaranteed real loss and an alternative is available.

  2. Germany sovereign downgrade. Two of the three major rating agencies (Fitch, Moody's, S&P) downgrade Germany below AA−, signalling a material shift in issuer creditworthiness.

  3. HICP index restructuring. Eurostat materially restructures the HICP ex-tobacco index in a way that severs the link between the index and realised consumer-price inflation — for example, by substituting an entirely different reference series.

The Rules

The pre-committed rules translate the four thesis conditions and the real-yield band into five clear instructions:

  • If all conditions confirm: hold the position. Real yield within band, German credit intact, HICP linkage functioning as designed.
  • If the thesis weakens but no exit condition fires: do not add. Re-assess if the real yield moves below 0%, but compare the real after-tax return against EUR cash and nominal Bunds before adding further.
  • If one or more exit conditions fire: exit in full. Move proceeds to nominal short-duration EUR Bunds or EUR cash.
  • If the real yield rises above 1.50% (price falls below €91): add to the position. A real yield above 1.50% on a seven-year AAA EUR sovereign linker is considered highly attractive; scale in proportionally to the inflation-hedge allocation target.
  • If the real yield falls below −0.50% (price rises above €104): trim 25% on the first crossing. If the real yield remains below −0.50% at the next quarterly review (at least 90 calendar days later), trim an additional 25%. The ladder is bounded — no further trims trigger until exit condition (1) fires.
  • If the compound exit trigger fires (real yield below −1.00% for at least 90 days AND EUR cash offers a superior real return): exit in full. Switch to EUR cash or short-duration nominal Bunds; the inflation-hedge function is no longer additive versus the alternative.

What Could Still Go Wrong

  • The ECB cuts rates aggressively. If the deposit facility rate falls sharply, cash real yields compress and the linker's advantage over EUR cash narrows. At some point, conventional short-dated Bunds or EUR cash may offer comparable inflation protection with less duration risk. The compound exit trigger is designed precisely for this scenario — it fires only when both legs are simultaneously true.
  • Break-even inflation collapses. A sharp fall in break-even below 1.00% would signal that markets expect deflation or near-zero inflation. In that environment, the linker's inflation-linking feature provides little additional return relative to a nominal Bund; the holding would persist but its inflation-hedge value would be temporarily diminished.
  • Germany credit deterioration. A downgrade of Germany by two major agencies below AA− is the second written exit condition. While Germany is one of the world's strongest sovereign credits, a sustained fiscal deterioration — for example, from a prolonged economic downturn combined with large defence and transition spending — could, over a long horizon, put pressure on ratings. This remains a remote scenario.
  • HICP methodology change. If Eurostat restructures the HICP ex-tobacco basket in a way that decouples it from household inflation experience, the bond's linkage loses its economic purpose. This is the most theoretical of the three exit conditions and has no current signal.
  • Rising real yields compress the holding's value. If global real rates rise substantially — driven by structural fiscal deficits in major economies or a shift in central bank policy frameworks — the market price of the linker falls, even if no exit condition fires. The holding absorbs mark-to-market losses until maturity or until the price triggers the buy threshold (at €91) and additional tranches are purchased at the more attractive yield.
