About Bund Linker 2033

Bund Linker 2033 is a bond issued by the Federal Republic of Germany that matures on 2033-04-15. It carries a nominal coupon of 0.10% — deliberately near zero — but both the principal and the coupon payments are scaled upward by the German Harmonised Index of Consumer Prices excluding tobacco, the inflation gauge maintained by Eurostat for Germany. The practical effect is that every payment is expressed in real terms: if consumer prices rise 20% from the issuance date to maturity, the bondholder receives 20% more in cash than a conventional bond with the same face value would pay.

The index ratio — which captures cumulative price-level growth since the bond was issued in February 2021 — stands at 1.2635 as of 2026-07-08. A buyer at today's clean price of €94.69 per €100 of original face value does not pay €94.69 at settlement: the actual cash that changes hands is the dirty price of €119.67 per €100 original face value, which equals the clean price multiplied by the index ratio, plus accrued interest. The distinction matters because the quoted market price (€94.69) is in constant purchasing-power units as of the issuance date; the settlement amount is in today's euros.

The Case for the Bet

The case for holding this bond is that it provides portfolio insurance against sustained inflation, sourced from the safest possible counterparty in the European government-bond market, at a positive real yield. Three conditions must keep holding for the case to remain sound.

  1. Real yield above the exit floor, and preferably above the fair-value anchor. A negative real yield means the price paid guarantees a real loss over the life of the bond — a premium for the inflation protection rather than a reward for providing capital. Written conditions set a buy band above 1.50%, a fair-value anchor at 0.50%, a trim band below −0.50%, and an exit floor at −1.00%. The compound exit trigger (see the Standing Orders) also requires EUR cash to offer a superior real return simultaneously — it is not the yield floor alone.

Latest reading: Real yield 0.91% as of 2026-07-08. The bond is within the hold band, above the fair-value anchor but below the buy edge. The compound trigger for exit condition (1) has zero days of persistence below the exit threshold, and the bond leads EUR cash by 65 basis points on a real-yield basis.

  1. German sovereign credit at or above AA- across at least two of three major agencies. The inflation hedge is only as strong as the issuer's ability to repay. Exit condition (2) fires if Germany is downgraded below AA- by two of the three major rating agencies.

Latest reading: Germany is rated AAA by Fitch, Aaa by Moody's, and AAA by S&P as of the 2026-06-15 credit sidecar. Exit condition (2) is not triggered.

  1. HICP ex-tobacco index methodology intact. The bond's inflation link runs through the Eurostat index. If Eurostat materially restructures the index in a way that severs its connection to real consumer-price experience, exit condition (3) fires.

Latest reading: No pending HICP methodology restructuring recorded as of the 2026-06-15 sidecar. Exit condition (3) is not triggered.

What Could Still Go Wrong

  • Sustained real-yield collapse. A prolonged fall in the linker's real yield toward or below −1.00% — driven by a surge in demand for inflation protection, a significant ECB rate cut, or a combination of both — would eventually trigger the compound exit condition. The 90-day persistence requirement is a deliberate buffer, but a multi-quarter yield decline could cross it.
  • German sovereign downgrade. A credit-quality deterioration at the federal level — unlikely given Germany's current fiscal position, but not structurally impossible across a seven-year horizon — would trigger exit condition (2) if two agencies downgrade to below AA-.
  • HICP index methodology restructuring. Eurostat periodically reviews its inflation measurement framework. A revision that materially severs the bond's link to real consumer-price experience would trigger exit condition (3) and remove the core rationale for the holding.

The Rules

If the thesis is confirmed — real yield within band, German credit intact, HICP linkage intact — hold. Real yield is within band, German credit is intact, and HICP linkage provides inflation protection as intended.

If the thesis is weakened — real yield approaches but has not crossed −0.50% — do not add. Reassess if real yield moves below 0% but has not yet triggered exit condition (1); compare real after-tax return against EUR cash and nominal Bunds before adding.

If the thesis is broken — one or more of the three numbered exit conditions has triggered — exit the position. Move proceeds to nominal short-duration EUR Bunds or EUR cash.

If the real yield crosses above 1.50% (clean price at or below €91) — add to the position. A 7-year AAA EUR sovereign linker at 1.50% real yield is highly attractive; scale in proportionally to the inflation-hedge allocation target.

If the real yield falls below −0.50% (clean price at or above €104) — trim. The 25 + 25 rule applies, bounded by the ladder described in the Standing Orders.

If the compound trigger fires (real yield below −1.00% for at least 90 days AND EUR cash offers a superior real return) — exit the full position. The inflation-hedge function is no longer additive versus the alternative; switch proceeds to EUR cash or short-duration nominal Bunds.
