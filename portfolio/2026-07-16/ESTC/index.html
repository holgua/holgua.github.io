About Elastic

Elastic makes the Elasticsearch platform — the world's most-downloaded open-source search and vector database — available to enterprises as a managed cloud service and self-hosted subscription. The business has three product families: a search-and-AI suite that lets organisations build natural-language and vector-search applications on their own data; an observability platform that ingests and analyses logs, metrics, and traces to monitor application performance; and a security information and event management product that detects threats by correlating signals across an organisation's IT environment. All three run on the same underlying data layer, which means a customer running one product has already installed the infrastructure for the other two.

The economics are classic recurring-revenue software: customers pay an annual subscription, expand usage as their data needs grow, and rarely leave because the platform has been embedded too deeply into their data operations to remove without significant cost. In the year ending 2026-04-30, revenue reached $1.74 billion, of which $837 million came through Elastic Cloud — the fully managed hosted version. That segment is growing faster than the overall business and now accounts for 48% of total revenue. Cash generated from operations after minimal capital expenditure (less than 0.3% of revenue) was $322 million, a margin of 18.5%.

The Case for the Bet

The central bet is that Elastic's three-product platform creates switching costs durable enough to sustain double-digit revenue growth and expanding cash margins over many years, while the transition from self-managed software to cloud subscriptions compounds revenue per customer over time. Six conditions must continue to hold:

  1. Customer expansion rate above 105% for any rolling two-quarter window. The net expansion rate measures whether existing customers are spending more or less than they did a year ago. A rate above 100% means the installed base is growing through existing customers alone, with new wins on top. Below 105% for two consecutive quarters would signal the switching-cost moat is no longer holding.

Latest reading: 112% as of 2026-04-30. The rate has been at or above 112% for multiple consecutive periods. The high-value customer tier (those spending above $100,000 annually) grew to over 1,720, from over 1,510 a year earlier.

  1. Elastic Cloud revenue growth above 10% in any full fiscal year. The cloud segment's growth rate is the observable proxy for whether the AWS OpenSearch fork is displacing the self-managed base and preventing migrations to the managed service. A full-year rate below 10% would confirm the fork is winning.

Latest reading: Total Elastic Cloud grew 22% in the year ending 2026-04-30, to $837 million. Higher-tier annual cloud contracts grew 28% to $641 million. Both are well above the 10% exit floor.

  1. Cash generation margin above 14% on a full-year basis. A sustained drop below 14% without a clearly identified one-off cause would signal that the capital-light model is eroding — through increased infrastructure investment, competitive pressure on gross margins, or a reversal of the operating leverage the business has been generating.

Latest reading: 18.5% in the year ending 2026-04-30. Operating cash flow of $327 million less capital expenditure of $5 million gives $322 million on revenue of $1.74 billion. The 14% floor has 450 basis points of headroom.

  1. Share-based pay below 18% of revenue in any two consecutive fiscal years. The company pays a large fraction of employee costs in shares rather than cash — common for growth software companies but dilutive unless offset by buybacks. Above 18% for two consecutive years would signal compensation discipline has reversed.

Latest reading: 17.16% in the year ending 2026-04-30, down from 17.38% a year earlier. The 18% threshold has not been breached in two consecutive years. A related figure in the company's own financial statements shows 18% when employer taxes on share awards are included; the threshold applies to the pure share-compensation figure only.

  1. No acquisition above $500 million without a credible cash-flow accretion case within three years. Large acquisitions at high revenue multiples without a clear path to improving the economics of the combined business are a common route to permanent capital loss in software companies.

Latest reading: Two acquisitions in the year ending 2026-04-30 — Jina AI at $43 million total consideration and Keep at $11 million. Both are small capability additions, well below the threshold.

  1. GAAP operating margin positive by the year ending 2028-04-30. The company has been in operating loss since its 2018 listing, primarily because share-based compensation exceeds the operating income the business generates. Convergence to positive GAAP margins by 2028 is a specific, time-bound test of whether that trajectory is genuine.

Latest reading: GAAP operating loss of $33.5 million, or minus 1.9% of revenue, in the year ending 2026-04-30 — down from minus $54.9 million (minus 3.7%) a year earlier, a 39% improvement. The trajectory is consistent with crossing zero by 2028.

The Share Price

At $59, the share price produces a cash yield (the annual cash generated by the business divided by what the market says the whole business is worth) of 5.17%. The fair-value anchor — derived from a discounted cash flow model assuming 15% annual cash flow growth and a 10% required return — implies a fair price of $101. The current price is therefore 42% below that estimate.

The band structure translates this into a straightforward positioning: the buy signal fires when the yield rises above 5.50%, corresponding to roughly $56 at current earnings. At $59, the stock sits in the lower half of the hold band — past the midpoint, close to the buy edge, but not yet at it. The trim signal would fire at $140, where the yield falls below 2.20%.

The $101 fair value used here is the more conservative of two available estimates. A peer-multiples model produces a midpoint well above $200. The two use different methodologies and disagree by roughly a factor of two. The discounted cash flow anchor is the deliberate choice — it is the appropriate anchor when the thesis rests on a specific growth assumption rather than a comparison to what the market pays for similar businesses.

Why the Market Sees It Differently

The price says the business will earn less over time. Our view says the opposite.

The gap is 41.1%: the market puts the current value at $59, the discounted cash flow model at $101. To justify $59, the market would have to believe the business's cash generation will grow at roughly 6% a year over the long run — well below the 15% assumed in the base case and materially below the guidance management has given for the year ahead.

The filing evidence allows three hypotheses to be tested. The first is that demand for the platform has structurally stalled: the 2026-04-30 annual filing shows cloud revenue up 22%, the customer expansion rate at 112%, and the high-value customer tier growing by 14%. This hypothesis is not supported by the current data. The second is that margins will erode rather than expand: the filing shows the cash margin at 18.5%, up from 17.7% a year earlier, operating losses narrowing, and the company guiding to a 21.5% cash margin for the year ahead. This hypothesis is also not supported by the current data.

The third hypothesis has partial support: the buyback programme deployed $340 million at an average price of $76.91, above both the current market price and the conservative fair-value anchor of $101. Capital deployed above intrinsic value is a legitimate concern about capital allocation judgement, even if it does not affect the underlying business. The market is right to weigh some scepticism about whether the board will stop repurchasing shares above fair value.

For the current price to converge toward $101, one of two things needs to happen: the business delivers revenue and margin numbers that force the market to revise its implied growth rate upward, or the buyback programme is suspended and capital is redeployed at prices below $101. The forward revenue pipeline — contracted obligations of $1.98 billion, up 28% — provides a credible signal that the first route is more likely.

What's Wobbling

Two fair-value drift warnings accompanied the latest filing review, and neither has been resolved.

The first is structural: the fair-value anchor used in the Share Price section (3.04% cash yield, corresponding to $101 per share) was derived assuming 15% annual cash flow growth. The current yield at $59 is 5.17% — 70% above that anchor. This divergence is expected when the market prices the stock for a slower growth scenario than the base case assumes. But it means the anchor needs revisiting at each annual filing to confirm the growth assumption is still defensible. If the market is right about long-run growth being closer to 6%, the fair-value anchor should be much lower than $101.

The second wobble concerns the gap between two valuation methods. A peer-multiples model produces an implied fair value above $200 per share; the conservative discounted cash flow model used as the primary anchor produces $101. Both coexist without contradiction — they answer different questions — but the gap matters for anyone relying on either number in isolation. Both figures are recorded in Gaps in the Picture.

On the competitive side, the latest filing could not provide evidence on the Datadog growth gap and the Gartner Observability position — these are external signals not disclosed in an annual filing. The last available data showed Datadog growing at 28% against Elastic's 17%, a gap of 11 percentage points. The exit threshold is 15 percentage points for two consecutive years combined with a loss of Gartner Leader status; neither leg has fired, but the growth-rate gap is approaching the threshold and requires monitoring through Datadog's own quarterly filings and Elastic's investor relations communications.

What Could Still Go Wrong

  • The AWS OpenSearch fork closes the functionality gap with Elasticsearch over the next two to three years, sufficiently to arrest new enterprise wins and accelerate churn. At $59 the bear-case discounted cash flow fair value is $38 — there is no bear-case protection at the current price.
  • Revenue recognition shifts further toward consumption-based cloud billing, which is more variable than fixed subscriptions and can create quarter-to-quarter volatility even when annual contract values are growing.
  • Share-based pay remains above 16% of revenue, which means the net economic earnings after accounting for the real cost of share dilution are much lower than the headline cash figure suggests — approximately $24 million in adjusted terms in the last full year, versus the $322 million headline.
  • Management continues to repurchase shares at prices above the conservative $101 fair-value anchor, compounding the capital-allocation blemish already documented in the thesis.
  • A hyperscaler bundles native AI search at zero marginal cost to cloud customers, removing the economic rationale for a standalone Elastic subscription in the search-and-AI segment.

The Rules

The following rules are in force. They describe what happens at each combination of thesis state and share price, and require no fresh judgement to apply.

  • If the thesis is confirmed and the share price is in the hold band: hold and monitor. The current reading — all conditions intact, price near the buy edge at $59 — calls for holding, monitoring the customer expansion rate and cloud growth quarterly, and confirming the share-based pay trajectory at each annual filing. Adding is not called for unless the price drops to the buy band.
  • If the thesis is weakened (one or more conditions deteriorating but no exit trigger fired): do not add. If the customer expansion rate drops toward 107% or cloud growth decelerates below 15% for two consecutive quarters, re-run the validation and assess whether to reduce. Two consecutive quarters of deterioration are required before acting; one quarter is insufficient to confirm a structural break.
  • If the thesis is broken (any exit condition fires): exit the full position within 30 days. The exit conditions are: customer expansion rate below 105% for two consecutive quarters; Elastic Cloud growth below 10% for a full year; cash generation margin below 14% on a full-year basis; share-based pay above 18% for two consecutive years; any acquisition above $500 million at more than eight times trailing revenue without a disclosed cash-flow accretion case within three years; GAAP operating margin not positive by the year ending 2028-04-30; or Datadog's growth rate exceeding Elastic's by more than 15 percentage points for two consecutive years combined with a loss of Gartner Observability Leader status. No averaging down on a thesis break.
  • If the share price drops below the buy band (cash yield above 5.50%, approximately $56): confirm the thesis is still intact, then add in tranches. No more than half the intended position size in a single move — the bear-case discounted cash flow fair value of $38 is destructive enough to require staged entry with thesis confirmation between tranches.
  • If the share price rises above the trim band (cash yield below 2.20%, approximately $140): trim approximately a quarter of the position to harvest the premium while keeping the core holding for continued compounding if the base case plays out.
  • If the share price rises above the exit level (cash yield below 1.50%, approximately $200): exit the entire remaining position. At that price the market is pricing in the bull-case tail and the forward asymmetry has inverted. No re-entry within 90 days to avoid anchoring to the original cost basis.

Scenario Definitions

A scenario is a joint assumption about two independent variables: operating trajectory (how the holding's underlying cash flows grow relative to their long-run trend) and exit multiple (what the market pays for those cash flows at the horizon, relative to a fair-value anchor).

Scenario Operating trajectory Exit multiple
Bear Below trend Below fair value
Base At trend At fair value
Bull Above trend At or above fair value

Non-negotiable rule: In the bear case, both dimensions must be adverse simultaneously — a scenario where only the multiple moves is a re-rating scenario, not a bear.

Bear cases are comparable in structure across all holding types (company, ETF, bond), but not in magnitude. A company bear reflects idiosyncratic execution failure; an ETF bear reflects a broad market drawdown. Different mitigations, different diversification implications.
