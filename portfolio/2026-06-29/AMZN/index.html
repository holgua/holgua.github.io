About Amazon

Amazon (ticker: AMZN) is a US-listed technology and retail conglomerate with three principal operating segments: a North American retail and marketplace operation that generated approximately $426 billion in revenue in fiscal 2025 at a 6.9% operating margin; an international retail operation that generated approximately $162 billion at a 2.9% margin; and Amazon Web Services, the cloud-infrastructure platform, which generated approximately $129 billion at a 35.4% operating margin.

Amazon Web Services provides most of the group's operating profit despite accounting for approximately 18% of total revenue. The group also operates one of the largest digital advertising networks in the world; advertising revenue is reported within the North American segment rather than separately. Amazon's logistics network — warehouses, sortation centres, and delivery fleets — is the largest private parcel-delivery operation in the United States by volume.

Group revenue: approximately $717 billion in fiscal 2025 (+12.4% from fiscal 2024). Group operating income: approximately $80 billion. Trailing earnings yield: approximately 4.2% at the current share price of $250. Trailing earnings multiple: approximately 24 times.

The Case for the Bet

Amazon is held as US large-cap technology and cloud-infrastructure exposure. The bet is not that Amazon is always a buy. The bet is narrower: over the holding period, the business's earnings yield — the aggregate profit earned per dollar of share price — must exceed the return on a risk-free 10-year government bond plus a reasonable equity risk premium. When that condition fails for two consecutive quarterly reviews, the economic rationale has broken down.

Eight conditions govern the bet:

  1. The earnings yield must not fall below 3.0% for two consecutive quarterly reviews. At that level, the business is fully priced relative to the risk-free rate and the equity risk premium case has failed.
  2. The equity risk premium — the gap between the earnings yield and the 10-year Treasury yield — must not be negative for two consecutive quarterly reviews. If bonds yield more on an earnings basis than the business does, the case for holding the business over bonds has failed.
  3. Amazon Web Services' annual revenue growth must not fall below 12% for two consecutive quarterly reviews. Below that level, the cloud segment can no longer be valued as a high-growth business and the earnings trajectory implied by today's multiple would require reassessment.
  4. The capital expenditure-to-operating-cash-flow ratio must not exceed 1.3 for two consecutive quarterly reviews. Above that level, the business is committing more capital than it generates before reinvestment, which undermines the owner-earnings premise.
  5. No single business segment may account for more than 65% of total group revenue for two consecutive quarterly reviews. Concentration above that level signals a structural shift in the business model.
  6. Amazon Web Services' operating margin must remain above 25% for two consecutive quarterly reviews. A collapse below that level signals either pricing pressure from competitors or a structural cost increase in cloud infrastructure.
  7. No regulatory remedy may materially restructure the group's ability to operate its retail marketplace and logistics network jointly. A forced separation or significant operating restriction would change the competitive position underlying the bet.
  8. The trailing earnings multiple must not exceed 40 times for two consecutive quarterly reviews. Above that level, the price implies a growth trajectory the business cannot sustain over a multi-year horizon.

The Share Price

The band edges in dollar terms: the cheap edge sits at $140, where the business would yield 7.5%; the fair-value anchor sits at $176; the trim edge sits at $263. At today's $250, the business is approximately 5% below the trim line and approximately 42% above the fair-value anchor.

To put the fair-value gap in plain terms: the business would need to fall roughly 30% from today's price to reach the level at which the rules regard it as fairly valued. That is not a prediction of when or whether the price falls — it is the distance between today's price and the bet's neutral reading.

What's Wobbling

One condition is at the boundary.

The trailing ratio of capital expenditure to operating cash flow stands at approximately 1.3 — the ceiling at which exit condition (4) would begin counting if sustained for a second consecutive quarter. The ratio has risen steadily over the past year as the business has expanded its data-centre capacity ahead of expected demand for artificial intelligence infrastructure. An improvement in operating cash generation or a moderation in the build-out pace could pull the ratio back below the boundary; neither is certain for the next reporting period.

Two additional signals merit watching. The share price at $250 sits 42% above the fair-value anchor at $176, and the gap has widened as the price has risen. The fair-value distance is not a trigger in itself — the trim order at $263 is the first price-based action signal — but it means the business must sustain its current earnings trajectory for several years to justify today's price. The buyback programme has also been absent in the current period; without capital return, the earnings per share trajectory depends entirely on operating growth.

No condition has reached its exit threshold. The bet is working, not broken.

What Could Still Go Wrong

  • Capital expenditure intensity breaches the threshold for a second consecutive quarter. The trailing ratio stands at the boundary. An acceleration in the data-centre build-out or a slowdown in operating cash generation would push it over, triggering exit condition (4).
  • Cloud growth decelerates below 12%. First-quarter 2026 showed 28.4% growth in Amazon Web Services. A deceleration below 12% for two consecutive quarters — driven by enterprise spending pressure, competitive displacement by Microsoft Azure or Google Cloud, or a reduction in artificial intelligence infrastructure investment — would trigger exit condition (3).
  • Tariff exposure compresses retail margin. A material portion of Amazon's North American retail catalogue is sourced from manufacturers in China. Sustained tariff increases at current levels could compress retail margins below the point where the segment's operating profit covers its allocated costs.
  • Multiple contraction without an earnings recovery. The business currently trades at approximately 24 times trailing earnings. A re-rating toward the long-run market average of roughly 16 times would produce significant losses even with steady underlying earnings growth.
  • Regulatory separation of marketplace and logistics. The Federal Trade Commission and Department of Justice have pursued enforcement actions against Amazon. A remedy requiring the marketplace to operate at arms-length from the logistics and fulfilment network would change the competitive position underlying the bet.

Gaps in the Picture

  • The fair-value anchor derived from the earnings-yield method ($176) differs materially from the midpoint of the business's historical trading range (approximately $569). The two estimates use different methodologies — the historical-bands approach uses observed price ranges, the earnings-yield approach anchors to current profitability at a long-run earnings multiple. The divergence is discussed in the TL;DR section.
  • The forward earnings multiple is not available from the data feed; only the trailing figure (approximately 24 times) is used. A forward figure would show whether analysts expect earnings growth to narrow the valuation gap over the next twelve months.
  • Tariff exposure by product category and country of origin is not broken down in the public filing. The quantitative impact of sustained tariff increases on retail margin cannot be modelled from available data.
  • Amazon's advertising revenue is reported within the North American segment, not as a standalone division. The advertising business's margin profile — likely materially higher than the 6.9% retail margin — cannot be isolated from public disclosures alone.

The Rules

If the conditions remain intact and the price stays within the band: hold the position — the business is reasonably priced and the conditions are working.

If any condition weakens without reaching its exit threshold: do not add. Re-validate after the next quarter. If the capital expenditure ratio stays at or above the ceiling for a second consecutive quarter, or if cloud revenue growth falls below 12%, move to a lower-beta holding or to cash per the position-sizing rule.

If a vehicle-integrity breach fires or a named exit condition triggers: exit. Move proceeds to the risk-free alternative or to a holding that meets the value test.

If the share price falls to or below $140: add to the position in tranches. The business is meaningfully cheap versus the risk-free alternative and the conditions are working.

If the share price rises to or above $263: trim 25–33%. The business is meaningfully expensive; harvest the premium while keeping the core holding.

If the share price rises to or above $421: exit the full position. The business is fully priced versus the risk-free alternative; redeploy proceeds to the risk-free alternative or to a holding that meets the value test.
