About the Fund

SP500 is the SPDR S&P 500 ETF Trust, launched in January 1993 as the first US-listed
exchange-traded fund. It tracks the S&P 500 index on a cap-weighted basis — meaning each of the
500 largest publicly traded US companies is represented in proportion to its total market value.
Bigger companies get a bigger slice. The largest single position is Nvidia at 7.89%, followed
by Apple at 7.05% and Alphabet at 6.11%; the top nine positions together account for about 39%
of the fund.

The fund's annual cost is 0.0945% of assets — less than one tenth of one percent each year. It
operates as a Unit Investment Trust (the oldest US exchange-traded fund wrapper), which means it
cannot reinvest dividends between quarterly distribution dates; dividends accumulate as cash and
are paid out quarterly. No tracking difference or tracking error figures are available from the
current data; both would need to be verified from State Street's factsheet. The technology
sector accounts for roughly 39% of the basket — a structural feature of cap-weighting when the
largest companies by market value happen to be technology businesses — with financial services at
11% and communications at 11% making up the next two largest slices.

The Case for the Bet

The bet is that the S&P 500 basket's look-through earnings yield will exceed the risk-free rate
by at least two percentage points over the holding period. That premium is the reward for holding
equities rather than government bonds; when it disappears, the basic case for the holding
disappears with it.

Six conditions must keep holding for the bet to work:

  1. Equity risk premium stays positive. The basket earnings yield (100 divided by the
    price-to-earnings multiple) must remain above the 10-year US Treasury yield. If it turns
    negative for two consecutive quarterly readings, exit condition one fires.

  2. Expense ratio stays below 0.15%. State Street must not raise the annual cost above this
    line without a comparable fee reduction in response. Current: 0.0945%; early-warning flag
    below 0.12%.

  3. Assets under management stay above $100 billion. A fall below this floor would signal
    an extraordinary liquidity event — the kind that impairs the fund's ability to track the
    index. Current: roughly $784 billion; early-warning flag below $300 billion.

  4. Top-1 holding weight stays below 13%. Single-name concentration above that line signals
    a regime change in the index — not normal drift but a structural shift. Current: Nvidia at
    7.89%; early-warning flag above 10%.

  5. S&P 500 index methodology stays intact. S&P Dow Jones Indices must not materially
    restructure the index rules in a way that undermines the broad-market mandate.

  6. Median look-through quality stays above 15% return on equity. The median return on
    equity of the top ten holdings (excluding duplicates) must not collapse below this floor.
    A fall below it would mean the basket has lost the quality underpinning the earnings-yield
    calculation. Current: 37.28%.

The Standing Orders

The rules translate the share-price bands into three standing orders and two price alerts.

Price alerts (informational).
- A first alert at $558, the fair-value anchor. Crossing below this level means the share price
has returned to a neutral reading; no action follows automatically, but a fresh check of the
conditions is warranted.
- A second alert at $447, the buy edge. Crossing below this level arms the buy order; before
adding, the latest re-validation should confirm the conditions are still intact.

Standing orders (actionable).
- A standing buy order at $447. The rules call for adding once the share price closes at or
below this line and the conditions are still intact. At $447, the basket earnings yield
reaches 6.25% — the long-run historical average for the S&P 500 — and the equity risk
premium would be comfortably positive.
- A standing trim order at $698. The rules call for trimming 25–33% of the position when the
share price closes at or above this line. The basket is meaningfully expensive relative to
risk-free; the trim harvests that premium while keeping the core holding.
- A standing exit order at $931. The rules call for closing the full position when the share
price closes at or above this line — basket earnings yield at or below 3.0%, fully priced
relative to risk-free. Proceeds should be redeployed to risk-free or to a holding that clears
the value test.

Vehicle-integrity breaches (conditions 2–6 above) and the named exit-condition trigger
(condition 1, two consecutive quarterly negative equity risk premium readings) are independent
additional paths to exit that operate regardless of where the share price sits relative to
these three order levels.

What's Wobbling

The equity risk premium is the one condition currently under pressure. The basket earnings yield
of 3.749% sits below the 10-year Treasury yield of 4.485%, producing a negative premium of
−0.736%. This is not a hairline breach — the premium has been negative across all three
snapshots recorded this month, spanning 10 days.

The exit condition requires two consecutive quarterly readings to be negative. On that test,
the current data does not yet trigger an exit: the snapshots span one month, not two calendar
quarters. But the direction is unambiguous, and the next quarterly snapshot is the critical
gate. If the equity risk premium is again negative at that point, exit condition one is
formally met.

The valuation-band trim signal and the challenging equity risk premium both point in the same
direction: the basket is priced beyond what the value case supports. That said, the trim signal
is a price-based rule, while exit condition one is a fundamental rule; they are conceptually
separate, and one triggering does not imply the other.

What Could Still Go Wrong

  • Exit condition one fires on the next quarterly reading. If the 10-year Treasury yield
    remains above the basket earnings yield at the end of the next quarter, the equity risk
    premium will again be negative — and the two-consecutive-quarterly persistence test will be
    met. The rules then call for a full exit.
  • Multiple compression deepens the bear case. The five-year bear scenario assumes the
    price-to-earnings multiple contracts to 15×, implying an annualised return of roughly −6.6%.
    At today's 26.67×, even the base case (20× terminal multiple) requires meaningful compression
    before the return turns positive.
  • Technology concentration amplifies drawdowns. Technology businesses account for roughly
    39% of the basket by weight. A sector-specific downturn — regulatory, cyclical, or
    valuation-driven — would hit the basket harder than a broadly diversified multi-sector index.
  • Expense ratio creep. State Street's UIT structure provides less flexibility to manage costs
    than a 1940 Act open-end fund. A fee increase without a compensating reduction would cross the
    early-warning band at 0.12% and eventually the 0.15% hard ceiling.
  • A sustained asset-base contraction. An extraordinary market dislocation that drove a
    sustained redemption wave could in theory compress total assets below the $300 billion
    early-warning level. At $784 billion, this would require a drop of more than 60% — plausible
    only in a crisis that affected every equity fund simultaneously.

The Rules

If the basket is reasonably priced and the vehicle is sound, hold.

If the thesis is weakened — the equity risk premium challenges the value case but the
quarterly-persistence gate has not fired — do not add. Re-validate after the next quarter.
If the equity risk premium stays negative or the median look-through quality keeps falling,
move to an alternative fund or to cash.

If the thesis is broken — vehicle integrity breached or an exit condition formally triggered —
exit. Move proceeds to risk-free or to a holding that meets the value test.

If the price falls below the buy band, add to the position. The basket is meaningfully cheap
relative to risk-free.

If the price rises above the trim band, trim 25–33%. The basket is meaningfully expensive
relative to risk-free; harvest the premium.

If the price rises above the exit band, exit. The basket earnings yield is at or below 3.0% —
fully priced relative to risk-free. Redeploy proceeds to risk-free or to a holding that meets
the value test.
