About Dynatrace

Dynatrace sells software that tells enterprise technology teams what is happening inside their cloud applications in real time. The product — called Dynatrace Platform Subscription — acts as a continuous observer across a company's entire technology stack: it monitors cloud infrastructure, applications, user experience, and security posture from a single platform, automatically identifying the root cause when something goes wrong rather than just surfacing an alert. Customers pay a recurring annual subscription. The typical deal begins with one part of the enterprise and expands as more teams adopt the platform, which is why the net revenue retention rate (what existing customers spend in year two compared with year one) is the primary moat-health signal.

Revenue in the fiscal year ended 2026-03-31 was $2,018 million, up 18.8% from the prior year. Virtually all of it was subscription. The company generated $530 million in free cash flow (operating cash minus capital spending), a margin of 26.2% on revenue — consistent with the prior four years and rising. The balance sheet carries $1,097 million in cash and only $274 million in long-term debt, giving net cash of $823 million. There are no dividends; capital is returned through buybacks. The company is headquartered in Waltham, Massachusetts, and was taken private in 2014 before relisting in 2019.

The Case for the Bet

The bet rests on the proposition that Dynatrace's combination of deep switching costs and proprietary artificial-intelligence capabilities will allow it to sustain above-average revenue growth and expand cash flow margins over a multi-year horizon, and that the market is currently pricing that growth too conservatively. The conditions that must keep holding for this bet to work are:

  1. Net revenue retention remains at or above 110%. The primary empirical test of whether existing customers are staying and expanding their usage. Below 107% for two consecutive quarters triggers an exit. Below 107% for one quarter triggers a watch posture.

Latest reading: 110% constant currency as of 2026-06-30. The reading is 3 percentage points above the watch threshold and unchanged from the prior full year's reported figure.

  1. Annual recurring revenue grows at or above 13% on a constant-currency basis for two consecutive quarters. Sustained deceleration below this level would signal that competitive pressure is eroding growth at a pace that changes the long-run return calculation.

Latest reading: 17% constant currency as of 2026-06-30. The deceleration of 1 percentage point from 18% in the same quarter a year earlier is within the normal maturation pattern for a software-as-a-service leader of this size.

  1. Free cash flow margin stays at or above 20% for any full fiscal year. A sustained drop below 20% would indicate that operating leverage is reversing or that competitive pressure is forcing elevated spending.

Latest reading: 55.8% margin in Q1 FY2027 (2026-06-30), which is seasonally elevated by annual billing. The full-year FY2026 figure was 26.2%. The full-year exit floor is not at risk based on current trajectory.

  1. No CEO or CFO open-market equity sales of $5 million or more within any 90-day window. Such sales would signal a change in management's private assessment of the business's prospects.

Latest reading: Zero open-market sales as of 2026-06-30. The quarter's repurchase programme — 7.1 million shares for $275.5 million at approximately $38.88 per share — is the opposite signal.

  1. Dynatrace Platform Subscription penetration stays above 72% of annual recurring revenue for three consecutive quarters. Stalling below this level, particularly if accompanied by management ceasing to disclose the metric, would indicate that the transition to the all-in-one licensing model is reversing.

Latest reading: Not disclosed in the Q1 FY2027 filing. The last known figure was approximately 70% of annual recurring revenue from the FY2026 annual report. Condition 5 cannot be assessed from this filing.

  1. No named Dynatrace customer displacement by Cisco or Palo Alto Networks is publicly documented. Such a disclosure would confirm that bundled-suite competition is displacing Dynatrace inside its installed base rather than merely slowing new-logo acquisition.

Latest reading: Palo Alto Networks' acquisition of Chronosphere is now named in the filing's risk factors as of 2026-06-30. No named Dynatrace customer displacement is disclosed. Exit condition 6 is not triggered.

  1. Net cash stays above $600 million without a disclosed acquisition rationale. A sustained decline below this level without a named deal would suggest capital is being consumed in ways not visible in the reporting.

Latest reading: Net cash $823 million as of 2026-03-31 (FY2026 year-end). The Q1 FY2027 filing indicates $573 million remained under the buyback programme as of 2026-06-30. Net cash condition is comfortably clear.

That said, the picture here requires one clarifying note. The gap between $41 and $118 is acknowledged and not a surprise: the first method anchors to current cash earnings, while the second looks through to where revenues and margins might be in three to five years. For the purpose of buy and trim decisions, the more conservative cash-earnings yield anchor is used. The peer-multiple range is treated as a description of the upside scenario, not a competing baseline.

The 52-week range for the share price runs from $31.64 to $57.55. The current price of $43.84 sits in the lower half of that range, consistent with the hold-band reading.

What's Wobbling

Two readings in the latest filing carry a mixed signal rather than a clean confirmation.

Subscription gross margin compression. In the three months ending 2026-06-30, subscription gross margin was approximately 85%, down about 1 percentage point from 86% in the same period a year earlier. The compression came from cloud hosting costs growing at 23% — faster than the 16% revenue growth rate. As noted in The Case for the Bet, no exit condition governs gross margin directly, so this does not cross any written threshold. The direction is worth monitoring over the next two quarters: if cloud hosting cost growth continues to outrun revenue growth, it would eventually become a free cash flow margin issue, and the exit floor there is 20% on a full fiscal year.

The Palo Alto/Chronosphere risk is now named. Exit condition 6 requires a public announcement of named Dynatrace customer wins by Cisco or Palo Alto — not merely the existence of a competing product. The filing moves the risk from hypothetical to formally named, but the trigger has not been crossed. The distinction matters: a risk factor disclosure is standard practice for competitive threats; a named customer displacement is an observable business event. The thesis requires the latter to exit, not the former.

Neither wobble crosses any written line. Together they represent the competitive and cost dynamics that would need to worsen considerably before the thesis position changes.

What Could Still Go Wrong

  • Growth deceleration faster than the base case. If annual recurring revenue growth slows below 13% on a constant-currency basis for two consecutive quarters — driven by Datadog or other cloud-native rivals winning new enterprise contracts at Dynatrace's expense — the thesis is broken and a full exit follows. At the current trajectory of 17%, this would require a meaningful change in competitive dynamics.
  • Net revenue retention compresses below 107%. A decline here would signal that existing customers are spending less each year, which would undermine the core logic of the bet: that switching costs keep customers and cause them to expand. One quarter at 107% or below triggers a watch; two consecutive quarters triggers an exit.
  • Operating leverage fails to materialise. If cloud hosting costs continue to grow faster than revenue for multiple quarters, free cash flow margins could compress toward the 20% full-year floor. Below that, the bet's mathematics change materially.
  • Management turnover at the top. The chief financial officer's departure by 2027-03-31 is disclosed and being managed with a nine-month lead time, which is transparent handling. A rapid or unexpected change at chief executive level would be a different matter — the current chief executive's compensation is structured around annual recurring revenue growth, and succession risk is real in a business where product roadmap direction matters as much as it does here.
  • A large acquisition at a high price. The balance sheet carries $823 million in net cash. An acquisition above $2 billion, particularly one that moved the company into adjacent markets where its switching-cost moat does not extend, would consume that buffer and introduce integration risk not currently priced in.

The Rules

The following rules govern what action follows from each combination of thesis state and share-price position. They are fixed in advance and applied mechanically when the conditions are met.

  • If all thesis drivers confirm or give a mixed or silent reading — meaning the thesis is intact — the rules call for maintaining the full position and continuing to monitor quarterly metrics (net revenue retention, annual recurring revenue growth on a constant-currency basis, free cash flow margin, and platform subscription penetration). The rules also permit adding modestly on dips below $38 while net revenue retention holds at or above 107%.
  • If thesis drivers weaken — net revenue retention declining toward 107%, or annual recurring revenue growth decelerating toward 14% — the rules call for reducing to half the position within 30 days of the trigger. The holding is then monitored for two further quarters before deciding whether to exit fully or rebuild.
  • If the thesis is broken — net revenue retention below 107% for two consecutive quarters, annual recurring revenue growth below 13% for two consecutive quarters, free cash flow margin below 20% for a full fiscal year, CEO or CFO open-market equity sales of $5 million or more in any 90-day window, or a documented named customer displacement by Cisco or Palo Alto Networks — the rules call for a full exit within one quarter. No averaging down on a broken thesis.
  • If the share price falls below $31 (the buy edge, where the free cash flow yield reaches 5.9%), the rules call for accumulating toward a full position size, provided the thesis remains intact.
  • If the share price rises above $55 (the trim edge, where the free cash flow yield falls to 3.3%), the rules call for trimming toward a half-to-two-thirds position, harvesting the premium while retaining a core holding in a business that continues to compound. A full exit at the trim edge is not called for unless the thesis is simultaneously breaking.

Scenario Definitions

A scenario is a joint assumption about two independent variables: operating trajectory (how the holding's underlying cash flows grow relative to their long-run trend) and exit multiple (what the market pays for those cash flows at the horizon, relative to a fair-value anchor).

Scenario Operating trajectory Exit multiple
Bear Below trend Below fair value
Base At trend At fair value
Bull Above trend At or above fair value

Non-negotiable rule: In the bear case, both dimensions must be adverse simultaneously — a scenario where only the multiple moves is a re-rating scenario, not a bear.

Bear cases are comparable in structure across all holding types (company, ETF, bond), but not in magnitude. A company bear reflects idiosyncratic execution failure; an ETF bear reflects a broad market drawdown. Different mitigations, different diversification implications.
