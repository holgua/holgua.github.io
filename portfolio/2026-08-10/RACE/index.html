Recent Development

The latest annual filing, covering the year ending 2025-12-31, confirmed the scarcity-franchise thesis across every numerically measurable dimension. Operating income reached EUR 2,110 million — up 11.76% from EUR 1,888 million the year before — while revenue grew 7.03% to EUR 7,146 million on essentially flat volume. Ferrari delivered 13,640 cars in the full year, barely changed from 13,752 in the prior year, which means the revenue growth was driven almost entirely by pricing and model-mix enrichment. The implied average price per car — derived by dividing revenue by deliveries — came to approximately EUR 524,000, confirming that buyers are paying more, not that more buyers are being served.

Cash generation remained exceptional. Operating cash flow reached EUR 2,349 million against net income of EUR 1,597 million, a ratio of 1.47×. That ratio — cash from operations divided by reported profit — has exceeded 1.25× in every year of the five-year observable record, which is a strong signal that reported earnings are not being inflated by accounting adjustments.

The one area that remains a mixed reading is US tariff exposure. A July 2026 trade agreement between the European Union and the United States set the all-inclusive tariff rate for Ferrari imports at 15%. That is below the 25% level that would trigger exit condition (5), and Ferrari's guidance for the current year targets an operating margin above 39% — above the 38% floor embedded in the exit condition's second leg. The threat of re-escalation to 25% surfaced in May 2026 from press reports but remains unconfirmed. The two-leg condition is not currently met. For the full valuation picture, see the Share Price.

About Ferrari

Ferrari makes its money selling hand-assembled sports cars, grand tourers, and — since 2023 — a four-door sport-utility model called the Purosangue, all produced in Maranello, Italy. Revenue also includes spare parts, licensed merchandise, and an in-house financial-services arm that helps buyers fund purchases. But the economics are driven overwhelmingly by the cars themselves, and by one structural peculiarity that sets Ferrari apart from every other car maker: the company deliberately produces fewer cars than the market wants to buy.

Annual production has been held at roughly 13,500–14,000 vehicles for years, even as order backlogs extend well beyond 18 months for most models. This is not a capacity constraint — it is a strategic choice. By keeping supply permanently below demand, Ferrari preserves the scarcity premium that justifies prices from EUR 250,000 for an entry model to EUR 4 million or more for limited-edition hypercars. Access to the most exclusive models is gated by a documented history of prior purchases — the loyalty staircase — which means the most desirable products are effectively closed to newcomers. The result is an operating margin above 38%, a return on equity above 40%, and cash generation that exceeds reported profit in every year on record. A luxury franchise, not a car maker.

The Case for the Bet

Ferrari is held on the proposition that its scarcity architecture — permanent excess demand through deliberate production caps, reinforced by brand heritage and the loyalty staircase — constitutes a durable competitive advantage that will sustain high margins and compound earnings for years. Six specific conditions must keep holding for that proposition to remain valid:

  1. Annual production held below 16,000 vehicles. Any management commentary framing volume expansion as strategic would signal a willingness to trade the scarcity premium for short-term revenue, permanently destroying the moat's foundation.

Latest reading: 13,640 units delivered in the year ending 2025-12-31 (confirmed from the annual report). Volume was essentially flat year-on-year — down less than 1% from 13,752 the prior year. Well below both the 14,500-unit internal thesis threshold and the 16,000-unit exit level. Production-cap discipline intact.

  1. Operating margin above 35% in every full fiscal year. A sustained drop below this level would signal that either the scarcity premium has been eroded or that investment costs for the Elettrica electric model are permanently compressing the margin structure.

Latest reading: Operating margin (earnings before interest and tax as a share of revenue) was 29.5% in the year ending 2025-12-31; the broader profitability measure that includes depreciation — sometimes called the earnings-before-interest-tax-depreciation-and-amortisation margin — stood at 38.8%, 380 percentage points above the 35% exit floor. The company's guidance for the current year targets this broader margin above 39%. Exit condition not triggered.

  1. Cash from operations at or above reported profit in every full fiscal year. A ratio below 1.0× would trigger an immediate accounting quality review; it would be inconsistent with every year of the five-year track record and could indicate earnings manipulation or working-capital deterioration.

Latest reading: Operating cash flow EUR 2,349 million against net income EUR 1,597 million = ratio of 1.47× for the year ending 2025-12-31 (confirmed from filed structured data). Well above both the 1.0× exit floor and the 1.25× observed historical floor. Exit condition not triggered.

  1. Average price per car not declining for two consecutive years on standard models. A sustained fall in average selling prices on non-limited models would signal demand softening beyond the normal timing effects of limited-series delivery cycles.

Latest reading: Implied average price per car in the year ending 2025-12-31 was approximately EUR 524,000 (derived: EUR 7,146 million revenue divided by 13,640 deliveries), rising from prior years. Revenue grew 7.03% on flat volume — confirming pricing-led growth. Exit condition not triggered.

  1. US import tariff rate below 25%, or — if at 25% — Ferrari guidance holding operating margin above 38%. The exit condition requires both legs simultaneously: a 25% tariff rate AND a guidance cut below 38% attributable to tariff absorption. Either leg alone is not sufficient to trigger the exit.

Latest reading: The current US tariff rate is 15% under the EU-US trade agreement effective 2026-07-01 — below the 25% exit-condition threshold. Ferrari absorbed a 25% tariff episode in 2025 with a selective price surcharge of up to 10% and still delivered the 38.8% operating margin. Current-year guidance targets above 39%. A threat to re-escalate to 25% was reported in May 2026 but remains unconfirmed. Exit condition not triggered.

  1. Elettrica electric-vehicle order book oversubscribed within 12 months of opening. Slow uptake, required price concessions, or announced delivery deferrals would signal that electrification requires pricing compromise, converting the Elettrica from a margin tailwind into a headwind.

Latest reading: The Elettrica was publicly revealed at the October 2025 Capital Markets Day — four doors, four electric motors, above 1,000 horsepower, expected price above EUR 500,000, deliveries from late 2026. The commercial order book has not yet opened as of the 2026-07-30 analysis date. Exit condition (6) clock has not started; this condition remains pending until the order book opens.

The Share Price

The share price at the time of the latest analysis was $377, against a calculated fair value of $377. In other words, the shares are priced at fair value — the market is paying precisely what the analysis says the business is worth at today's earnings and growth trajectory, with no discount available and no premium that would warrant reducing a position.

An earnings yield on owner earnings (annual net income as a fraction of the company's total market value) of 2.75% currently matches the 2.75% level designated as fair value exactly. The buy edge — the price level at which a margin of safety becomes available — is reached when that yield rises to 4.25%, corresponding to a share price of approximately $244. The trim edge, at which the market's pricing implies decades of compounding already captured, fires when the yield falls to 1.25%, corresponding to approximately $829 per share. At $377, the position is in the middle of the hold band.

What's Wobbling

The only driver reading below a clean confirmation is US tariff exposure, rated as a mixed signal rather than a confirmation. The current regime — a 15% all-inclusive rate under the July 2026 EU-US trade agreement — has been absorbed without any impact on Ferrari's margin guidance, and the two legs of exit condition (5) are not simultaneously met. But a threat to re-escalate to 25% surfaced in press coverage in May 2026. If that re-escalation were to materialise AND Ferrari's guidance included a margin cut below 38% attributable to tariff absorption, the exit condition would trigger. At present, neither leg is active.

Two drivers — the Elettrica electric-vehicle order book and governance alignment — are recorded as silent rather than confirming: not because anything adverse has appeared, but because the order book has not yet opened and governance is evaluated from narrative disclosures that could not be extracted from the structured filing data. Neither reading changes the overall verdict.

The scarcity architecture — specifically, delivery volume — showed a minor internal discrepancy between two figures cited in the competition analysis (13,640 deliveries in one section, approximately 14,633 in a summary table). Both figures are well below the 14,500-unit thesis threshold and the 16,000-unit exit level, so neither reading triggers a concern. The discrepancy should be resolved at the next competition-data refresh.

What Could Still Go Wrong

  • Volume discipline breaks down. Any signal that management is willing to grow production materially beyond 14,000 units — whether from revised guidance, a strategic announcement, or a completed acquisition — invalidates the entire thesis immediately and irreversibly. This is exit condition (1), and it is the only one with no recovery path.
  • Electric-vehicle development costs compress margins permanently. If the Elettrica requires more investment than planned, or achieves lower pricing than expected, the operating margin could decline toward 34–35% and stay there. Exit condition (2) fires if any full fiscal year falls below 35%.
  • Luxury macro contraction. A sustained contraction in ultra-high-net-worth spending would put pressure on order backlogs, potentially forcing either price concessions on standard models or a relaxation of production discipline. The scarcity model insulates against mild downturns; a prolonged luxury recession is a different order of stress.
  • US tariff re-escalation at 25% with insufficient pricing power. Ferrari absorbed a prior 25% tariff episode with selective price surcharges, but the demonstration was partial and brief. If a sustained 25% tariff were to require margin absorption, exit condition (5) could fire — and with it, a fundamental revision of the pricing-power thesis.
  • Elettrica order-book disappointment. If the electric model fails to oversubscribe within 12 months of the order book opening — requiring price concessions, accepting deferrals, or seeing slow uptake — exit condition (6) fires and the Elettrica becomes a thesis headwind rather than a tailwind.

Gaps in the Picture

Owner earnings — the measure of cash profit used to compute the valuation yield — are set by a manual override in the analysis settings rather than extracted automatically from filed data. The figure used is EUR 1,597 million (FY2025 net income, converted to US dollars at EUR/USD 1.1406), on the basis that depreciation and maintenance capital expenditure are approximately equal, making net income a reasonable proxy. This override must be refreshed whenever the value analysis is refreshed; if it falls out of date, the valuation yield will drift. The capital-expenditure figure is not separately tagged in Ferrari's structured filing data, which is why the proxy is necessary.

The fair-value band cross-check — which normally compares a yield-based fair value against an independent range derived from historical and peer price multiples — could not be run this cycle. Four of the self-multiple bands (enterprise-value-to-earnings-before-interest-tax-depreciation-and-amortisation, enterprise-value-to-revenue, price-to-book, and price-to-cash-flow) have insufficient history in the current window; the peer-based band is still being populated. This leaves the yield-based fair value figure as the sole anchor.

The Rules

Six rules govern the bet, each tied to the six conditions and the current share price:

  • If the thesis is confirmed — all six conditions still working and no exit condition triggered — hold the position and continue compounding. No action is required when everything is working.
  • If the thesis weakens — one or more drivers moves to an adverse reading but no exit condition fires — do not add to the position. Monitor the specific weakening driver (most likely the operating margin trend or Elettrica uptake) for one full fiscal year before reassessing position size.
  • If the thesis breaks — any single exit condition is triggered — exit the full position within 30 days. Exit condition (1), production above 16,000 units, destroys the moat irreversibly; no averaging down and no waiting for recovery apply in that scenario. Document the triggered condition for future learning.
  • If the share price falls to the cheap edge — at around $244, the earnings yield on owner earnings exceeds 4.25% — confirm the thesis is intact, then add in tranches over one to two quarters. An entry at that yield level into this franchise is rare and should be acted on decisively.
  • If the share price rises to the trim edge — at around $829, the earnings yield on owner earnings falls below 1.25% — trim 25–33% of the position to harvest the premium. At that price, the market is pricing in decades of compounding already captured; disciplined reduction preserves the option to re-enter at a better yield.
  • If the share price rises above the exit level — at that price, the earnings yield on owner earnings falls below 1.0%, implying a market capitalisation north of $182 billion on current earnings — exit the full position. The implied forward return falls below the long-term equity premium, and redeploying into a cheaper compounding franchise is the better use of the capital. No re-entry within 90 days, to defuse the psychological pull of the original purchase price.

Scenario Definitions

A scenario is a joint assumption about two independent variables: operating trajectory (how the holding's underlying cash flows grow relative to their long-run trend) and exit multiple (what the market pays for those cash flows at the horizon, relative to a fair-value anchor).

Scenario Operating trajectory Exit multiple
Bear Below trend Below fair value
Base At trend At fair value
Bull Above trend At or above fair value

Non-negotiable rule: In the bear case, both dimensions must be adverse simultaneously — a scenario where only the multiple moves is a re-rating scenario, not a bear.

Bear cases are comparable in structure across all holding types (company, ETF, bond), but not in magnitude. A company bear reflects idiosyncratic execution failure; an ETF bear reflects a broad market drawdown. Different mitigations, different diversification implications.
