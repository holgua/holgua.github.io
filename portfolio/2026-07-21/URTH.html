About All World

All World is the iShares MSCI World exchange-traded fund, which tracks the MSCI World Index — a float-adjusted, market-capitalisation-weighted index covering roughly 1,300 large- and mid-cap companies across 23 developed countries. The geographic split is approximately 70% United States and 30% the rest of the developed world (Japan, the United Kingdom, France, Switzerland, Germany, Canada, and others). The fund holds no exposure to emerging markets. Its role in a portfolio is to dilute the concentration risk of an all-US allocation: while an S&P 500 fund by definition holds only US-listed companies, URTH distributes about a third of its weight across developed markets outside the United States — reducing the regime risk that comes from being wholly reliant on any single country's political and economic cycle.

The largest sector tilt is technology, which accounts for 30.85% of the basket — a structural property of US mega-cap dominance within a float-adjusted global index rather than an active overweight. Financial services is the second-largest sector at 15.66%, followed by industrials at 11.39%. The fund is a Delaware statutory trust under the iShares/BlackRock umbrella; it does not issue K-1 tax forms. The annual cost is 0.24%, which compares to 0.20% for the functionally equivalent IWDA UCITS listed in London — a gap of four basis points that the thesis notes explicitly as a monitoring item.

The Case for the Bet

The bet is that a globally diversified basket of high-quality developed-market businesses, held patiently, will deliver a look-through earnings yield that exceeds the risk-free rate of return by a meaningful margin over the holding period. The conditions that must keep holding for this to work are:

  1. The basket's earnings yield must exceed the risk-free 10-year bond yield — the equity risk premium must remain positive. An earnings yield below the government bond yield means the basket is priced to deliver less than a risk-free alternative — the core value case has failed. The exit condition fires when this negative reading persists across two consecutive calendar quarters, not just one.

Latest reading: As of 2026-07-20, the basket earnings yield is 4.16% and the 10-year US Treasury yields 4.60%, giving an equity risk premium of −0.44 percentage points. This is the 15th consecutive negative daily reading since 2026-06-16. The first affected calendar quarter (April–June 2026) closed negative; the second (July–September 2026) remains open. The two-quarter exit gate has not yet elapsed.

  1. BlackRock/iShares must not raise the expense ratio above 0.35%. A fee hike beyond this level, without a corresponding fee reduction, signals cost drift that a cheaper equivalent fund (the UCITS structure at 0.20%) does not require.

Latest reading: As of 2026-07-20, the expense ratio is 0.24% — 11 basis points below the 0.35% ceiling. Exit condition not triggered.

  1. Assets under management must remain above $2.0 billion. Below roughly $2 billion, bid-ask spreads on a global blend fund typically widen materially, impairing the ability to exit efficiently. Current assets are $8.06 billion, approximately four times the floor.

What's Wobbling

The only wobble is the equity risk premium, and it has been wobbling for 15 consecutive daily snapshots. The basket's earnings yield of 4.16% trails the 10-year US Treasury yield of 4.60% by 44 basis points as of 2026-07-20. The same negative reading has persisted since 2026-06-16, with the daily shortfall ranging from −0.11 percentage points (on 2026-06-29, when the gap was at its narrowest) to −0.48 percentage points (on 2026-07-11, when it was widest).

This does not cross any of the lines in The Case for the Bet yet. The exit condition explicitly requires two consecutive calendar quarters of negative equity risk premium, not snapshot-persistence alone. The first full quarter (April through June 2026) closed negative. The second (July through September 2026) is open. If the third month of the quarter also closes with the equity risk premium negative, exit condition (1) will have fired and the rules will call for exiting the position.

The thesis acknowledged this situation at inception: the basket's earnings yield was below the risk-free rate on the very first measurement in June 2026 — the bet was never that the entry price was cheap but that the basket's earnings yield would recover above the risk-free rate over the holding period. What has changed is the accumulation of consecutive negative readings, which is now a calendar-clock matter rather than a single-snapshot concern.

What Could Still Go Wrong

  • The equity risk premium may remain negative for the full second quarter (July–September 2026), completing the two-quarter calendar gate and triggering exit condition (1). At that point the rules call for a full exit regardless of where the share price sits.
  • The basket's price-to-earnings multiple could compress sharply if global growth expectations deteriorate — the bear scenario assumes earnings fall nearly 11% and the multiple re-rates from 24 times to 14 times simultaneously, producing a five-year annualised return of roughly −20%.
  • BlackRock could raise the annual expense ratio, pushing it above the 0.35% ceiling. Given that the functionally equivalent UCITS structure charges 0.20%, any fee increase without a corresponding reduction would trigger exit condition (2).
  • Nvidia's weight in the basket could drift above the 10.0% ceiling if its share price continues to outperform. At 5.17% today it has roughly 4.83 percentage points of headroom, but concentrated outperformance in a single name compresses that cushion quickly.
  • MSCI Inc. could revise the World Index methodology in a way that undermines the developed-markets mandate — for example, by reclassifying a major country, dropping significant constituents, or abandoning float-adjusted market capitalisation weighting. This condition cannot be monitored automatically.
  • For this fund, exit fires when the share price reaches the exit edge at $281, when any vehicle-integrity condition breaches, or when the named exit-condition trigger completes its two-quarter calendar gate — any of three independent paths.

The Rules

If the basket's earnings yield is in the hold band (between 4.0% and 6.25%), the vehicle is sound, and all thesis drivers confirm: hold. The basket is reasonably priced and the vehicle is sound.

If the basket is in the hold band but at least one driver is in a challenged state — as it is today with the equity risk premium negative: do not add. Re-validate after the next quarter. If the equity risk premium stays negative or the median look-through return on equity keeps falling, swap to the equivalent UCITS fund at 0.20% annual cost, or move to cash in line with the position-sizing rule.

If any vehicle-integrity condition breaches or the equity risk premium exit condition triggers: exit. Move the proceeds to a risk-free instrument or to a holding that meets the value test.

If the basket's earnings yield rises above 6.25% (share price at or below $135): add to the position. The basket is meaningfully cheap versus risk-free alternatives.

If the basket's earnings yield falls below 4.0% (share price at or above $210): trim 25–33%. The basket is meaningfully expensive versus risk-free alternatives; harvest the premium.

If the basket's earnings yield falls to or below 3.0% (share price at or above $281): exit. The basket is fully priced versus risk-free alternatives. Redeploy proceeds to a risk-free instrument or to a holding that meets the value test.

Scenario Definitions

A scenario is a joint assumption about two independent variables: operating trajectory (how the holding's underlying cash flows grow relative to their long-run trend) and exit multiple (what the market pays for those cash flows at the horizon, relative to a fair-value anchor).

Scenario Operating trajectory Exit multiple
Bear Below trend Below fair value
Base At trend At fair value
Bull Above trend At or above fair value

Non-negotiable rule: In the bear case, both dimensions must be adverse simultaneously — a scenario where only the multiple moves is a re-rating scenario, not a bear.

Bear cases are comparable in structure across all holding types (company, ETF, bond), but not in magnitude. A company bear reflects idiosyncratic execution failure; an ETF bear reflects a broad market drawdown. Different mitigations, different diversification implications.
