About US Treasury 0-1y

The fund holds a basket of United States government Treasury bills with remaining maturities of less than one year. There are no corporate bonds, no equities, and no foreign sovereign debt in the portfolio — the mandate is narrow by design. Income earned on the bills is not distributed as cash; instead, it accumulates inside the fund, lifting the net asset value over time. A position in the fund therefore compounds silently, without requiring active reinvestment of coupon payments.

The weighted-average maturity of the basket at the latest snapshot is approximately 0.375 years, or around four and a half months. That ultra-short duration means the basket reprices to the prevailing rate within months rather than years — interest-rate sensitivity is minimal. Annual cost is 0.07%, sourced from Vanguard's published ongoing charges figure; yfinance does not return this number for this fund's London Stock Exchange listing, so the figure is carried from the Vanguard factsheet. No tracking-difference or tracking-error data is available from the automated pipeline for this fund.

The Case for the Bet

The bet rests on three conditions that must keep holding for this vehicle to remain the right placeholder for USD cash:

  1. Charges remain below 0.10% per year for every quarterly review. The fund earns its keep by converting the prevailing T-bill yield into portfolio growth at negligible cost. If the annual charges figure rises above 0.10% for two consecutive quarterly reviews, comparable funds — including ultra-short-duration Treasury funds listed in the United States — are available at lower cost, and the fee advantage disappears.

Latest reading: 0.07% as of 2026-07-15 (carry-forward from Vanguard-published figure; the automated pipeline returned no value for this listing this cycle). The figure is 3 basis points below the 0.10% ceiling. Exit condition inactive. Manual re-verification against the Vanguard factsheet is required at each quarterly review.

  1. Total assets remain above USD 1 billion. The fund's value proposition is instant access to T-bill-like liquidity — the ability to sell a meaningful position on any trading day without moving the price. That quality depends on the fund being large enough to operate efficiently. Below USD 1 billion, liquidity and redemption risk could impair the instant-access premise.

Latest reading: Approximately $2.4 billion as of the last confirmed figure (carry-forward; yfinance does not return total assets for this fund). Well above the floor. Exit condition inactive. Manual re-verification against the Vanguard product page is required quarterly.

  1. The index mandate remains unchanged and US sovereign credit stays rated AA− or above by at least two of three major agencies. The fund holds bills issued by the United States federal government. If the mandate were widened — for example, to include corporate paper — or if two of the three major credit-rating agencies (Fitch, Moody's, S&P) downgraded the US below AA−, the underlying credit quality of the basket would shift materially and the cash-equivalent premise would require reassessment.

Latest reading: No automated signal of mandate change as of 2026-07-15. US credit ratings as of snapshot remain above the trigger; monitoring falls outside the automated pipeline and is carried out externally.

That fair-value anchor of $46 deserves a brief explanation. Because the fund accumulates income internally, the share price drifts upward as T-bills roll and reinvest. The price bands are therefore not fixed lines; they are recomputed at each quarterly refresh to reflect the current net asset value.

What Could Still Go Wrong

Several scenarios could force a reassessment or an exit:

  • The Federal Reserve cuts rates aggressively — for example, in response to a sharp slowdown — pushing the basket yield below 3.5%. At that level, the trim edge fires and the carry versus EUR cash narrows to a point where the argument for holding USD T-bills weakens. A yield of 2.5% is the bear-case scenario modelled for this basket.
  • Charges rise above the 0.10% ceiling. Vanguard has kept this fund's charges at 0.07% since inception, but any fee revision that sustains above the ceiling for two consecutive quarterly reviews triggers the exit rule under condition one.
  • Total assets fall below USD 1 billion. The fund currently holds approximately $2.4 billion, so reaching the floor would require a very large redemption wave. However, because the automated pipeline cannot verify total assets directly, a quiet erosion could go undetected without manual quarterly checks.
  • The index mandate widens or Vanguard restructures the vehicle. A change to include longer maturities or corporate paper would alter the risk profile materially and require a full review.
  • US sovereign credit is downgraded below AA− by two of the three major credit-rating agencies. This would require a reassessment of the cash-equivalent premise at its foundation.

For this fund, exit fires when the share price reaches the exit edge ($74), when a vehicle-integrity condition breaches, or when the named exit-condition trigger fires — any of three paths.

The Rules

If vehicle integrity is intact, all conditions confirm, and the yield remains within the 3.5%–5.5% band, hold. The running thesis is sound and the fund is doing its job as a near-cash USD placeholder.

If one or more conditions show early-warning signs but no hard threshold has been breached — for example, if charges are trending upward or total assets are declining — do not add. Reassess at the next quarterly review and monitor the relevant trend before increasing the allocation.

If one or more numbered exit conditions fires — charges above 0.10% for two consecutive quarters, total assets falling below USD 1 billion, or the US government downgraded below AA− by two of the three major agencies — exit the position and reinvest in EUR cash or short nominal German government bonds.

If the share price falls to $40 or below (meaning the basket yield has reached 5.5%), add at the margin when cash is available. T-bill rates at that level are unusually attractive relative to EUR alternatives. Vehicle-integrity checks still gate any large addition.

If the share price rises to $63 or above (meaning the basket yield has compressed to 3.5%), trim at the margin. The USD T-bill carry versus EUR cash has weakened materially at that yield level. Vehicle-integrity checks still gate any full exit on price grounds alone.

If the share price reaches $74 or above (meaning the basket yield has fallen to 3.0%), exit per the applicable exit condition. The basket is fully priced versus the risk-free alternative at that level.

Scenario Definitions

A scenario is a joint assumption about two independent variables: operating trajectory (how the holding's underlying cash flows grow relative to their long-run trend) and exit multiple (what the market pays for those cash flows at the horizon, relative to a fair-value anchor).

Scenario Operating trajectory Exit multiple
Bear Below trend Below fair value
Base At trend At fair value
Bull Above trend At or above fair value

Non-negotiable rule: In the bear case, both dimensions must be adverse simultaneously — a scenario where only the multiple moves is a re-rating scenario, not a bear.

Bear cases are comparable in structure across all holding types (company, ETF, bond), but not in magnitude. A company bear reflects idiosyncratic execution failure; an ETF bear reflects a broad market drawdown. Different mitigations, different diversification implications.
