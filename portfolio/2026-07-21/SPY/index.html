About SP500

SP500 — formally the SPDR S&P 500 ETF Trust — is a passive fund that tracks the S&P 500 index on a cap-weighted basis. It holds shares in approximately 500 of the largest publicly listed US companies, weighted by their total market value. The heaviest weights go to whichever companies the market currently values most highly: today that means a 38.55% allocation to technology companies, led by Nvidia (7.50%), Apple (6.58%), Alphabet (5.83%), and Microsoft (4.29%). The remaining weight is spread across financial services, healthcare, consumer, industrial, and energy companies, with no other single sector above 12%.

The fund launched in January 1993 as the first US-listed exchange-traded fund and has operated under a unit investment trust structure since then — the oldest ETF legal wrapper in the country, maintained by State Street Investment Management. Tracking difference and tracking error figures are not available from the current sidecar; State Street's SPDR factsheet is the primary source for verifying these. Securities lending is not available in a unit investment trust structure. The annual cost is 0.0945%. The fund pays a quarterly distribution; the trailing annual yield is 1.01%. No K-1 is issued.

The Case for the Bet

SP500 is held as core US large-cap equity exposure on one specific condition: that the basket's earnings yield will exceed the risk-free rate plus at least a 2 percentage-point equity risk premium over the holding period. Six numbered conditions define when that bet is still alive and when it is not:

  1. Equity risk premium above zero for two consecutive quarter-ends. The basket's earnings yield (the inverse of the trailing price-to-earnings ratio, expressed as a percentage) must remain above the ten-year US Treasury yield. Two consecutive negative readings at quarter-end trigger exit condition (1) regardless of the absolute earnings yield level.

Latest reading: Basket earnings yield 3.74%, ten-year Treasury 4.60%, equity risk premium −0.86% as of 2026-07-20. Fourteen consecutive negative daily snapshots recorded since 2026-06-16. Q2 2026 quarter-end registered negative; Q3 2026 quarter-end (2026-09-30) is the pending confirmation gate. Exit condition not yet triggered, but the clock is running.

  1. Annual cost below 0.15%. State Street must not raise the expense ratio above 0.15% without a fee response. Exit condition (2) fires on an unchallenged fee increase above that line.

Latest reading: Annual cost 0.0945% as of 2026-07-20. Headroom of 5.55 basis points to the 0.15% ceiling. No fee change announced. Exit condition (2) not triggered.

  1. Assets under management above $100 billion. An extraordinary liquidity event that drives assets below this floor would indicate structural fund distress.

Latest reading: Assets under management $781.2 billion as of 2026-07-20 — 7.81 times the $100 billion floor, with $681 billion of headroom. Average daily traded volume $39.0 billion. Exit condition (3) not triggered.

  1. Largest single holding below 13% of the basket. Single-name concentration above this level would signal a regime change in the index's composition, not normal market drift.

Latest reading: Nvidia at 7.50% of the basket as of 2026-07-20 — 5.50 percentage points below the 13.0% ceiling. Exit condition (4) not triggered.

  1. S&P 500 index methodology remains intact. A material restructuring of the index's constituent rules or float-weighting approach by S&P Dow Jones Indices would undermine the broad-market mandate.

Latest reading: No automated signal available; the fund family is State Street Investment Management and no methodology change has been announced. Condition (5) monitored externally on a quarterly basis. Exit condition (5) not triggered.

  1. Median return on equity of the deduped top-10 holdings above 15%. A collapse in underlying earnings power across the basket's leading companies would signal that the quality case has broken.

Latest reading: Median return on equity of the top-nine deduped holdings is 37.28% as of 2026-07-20, clearing the 15% floor by 22 percentage points. The equity risk premium is the active wobble. At −0.86% it represents the gap between the basket's 3.74% earnings yield and the 4.60% ten-year Treasury yield — equities are paying less than risk-free paper. This has persisted across fourteen consecutive daily snapshots stretching back to 2026-06-16. The Q2 2026 quarter-end reading was negative. If the same is true at the Q3 2026 quarter-end (2026-09-30), the two-consecutive-quarter exit gate in the Case for the Bet will be met.

The wobble has not yet crossed the exit line. What Could Still Go Wrong

  • Cost shock. State Street raises the expense ratio above 0.15% with no fee response from a competing fund. The fund's unit investment trust structure gives State Street limited operational flexibility but does not contractually cap the fee.
  • Liquidity collapse. Assets under management fall below $100 billion in an extraordinary market event. At $781 billion today the headroom is vast, but the 2020 experience showed how quickly liquidity can move in a coordinated shock.
  • Index-methodology change. S&P Dow Jones Indices materially restructures the S&P 500's constituent rules, float-weighting, or sector classification system in a way that undermines the broad-market mandate. This is externally monitored and not signalled today.
  • Concentration drift. A single company's weight in the basket rises above 13% through sustained market-cap outperformance. Nvidia is the most likely candidate at 7.50%; reaching 13% would require roughly 73% further relative outperformance.
  • Exit condition (1) fires. The equity risk premium is negative at both Q2 2026 quarter-end and Q3 2026 quarter-end. Under the action plan this triggers a full exit, not a trim. (For this fund, exit fires when the share price reaches the exit edge at $931, when a vehicle-integrity condition breaches, or when exit condition (1) fires — any of three paths.)

The Rules

  • If the basket earnings yield is at or above 6.25% and the vehicle is sound, hold — the basket is reasonably priced and the vehicle is sound.
  • If the equity risk premium is negative or the median look-through return on equity is falling, do not add; re-validate after the next quarter. If the trend persists, move to a fund that meets the value test or to cash.
  • If a vehicle-integrity condition is breached or an exit condition has been triggered, exit and move proceeds to the risk-free rate or to a holding that meets the value test.
  • If the basket earnings yield reaches 6.25% or above, add to the position — the basket is meaningfully cheap versus the risk-free rate.
  • If the basket earnings yield falls below 4.0%, trim 25 to 33% of the position — the basket is meaningfully expensive versus the risk-free rate; harvest the premium.
  • If the basket earnings yield falls below 3.0%, exit — the basket is fully priced versus the risk-free rate; redeploy proceeds to the risk-free rate or to a holding that meets the value test.

Scenario Definitions

A scenario is a joint assumption about two independent variables: operating trajectory (how the holding's underlying cash flows grow relative to their long-run trend) and exit multiple (what the market pays for those cash flows at the horizon, relative to a fair-value anchor).

Scenario Operating trajectory Exit multiple
Bear Below trend Below fair value
Base At trend At fair value
Bull Above trend At or above fair value

Non-negotiable rule: In the bear case, both dimensions must be adverse simultaneously — a scenario where only the multiple moves is a re-rating scenario, not a bear.

Bear cases are comparable in structure across all holding types (company, ETF, bond), but not in magnitude. A company bear reflects idiosyncratic execution failure; an ETF bear reflects a broad market drawdown. Different mitigations, different diversification implications.
