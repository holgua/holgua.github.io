About World Equal Weight

World Equal Weight tracks the MSCI World Equal Weighted index, a variant of the standard MSCI World that holds roughly 1,500 developed-market companies at approximately equal weight — near 0.07% each on the quarterly rebalance date — rather than weighting by market capitalisation. The practical effect is deliberate: the fund reduces the mega-cap concentration that dominates the standard cap-weighted MSCI World (where the top ten names account for around 25% of the index), and tilts the basket toward smaller names, ex-US developed markets including Japan, the United Kingdom, and continental Europe, and lower-multiple constituents whose earnings yields are structurally higher than those of the largest growth companies. The trade-offs are real: the quarterly rebalance creates roughly 30% annual turnover, sampling replication (rather than holding every constituent) adds a modest tracking-error cost, and the median return on equity across 1,500 equal-weighted holdings is structurally lower than the top ten of a cap-weighted index.

The fund is managed by Invesco Investment Management Limited as an Irish-domiciled accumulating UCITS exchange-traded fund (no capital-gain distributions; gains are retained within the fund). The expense ratio per the Invesco factsheet is 0.20% annually — below the 0.30% ceiling set in the written exit conditions — though this figure does not appear in the automated data feed and must be verified from the factsheet directly. No tracking-difference or tracking-error data is available for this fund from the current data source.

The Case for the Bet

The structural argument is that developed-market equity exposure at near-equal weighting offers a higher basket earnings yield than cap-weighted alternatives, with sufficient diversification that no single company's failure is catastrophic. Six conditions must hold for the bet to remain sound:

  1. The basket's earnings yield must exceed the ten-year risk-free rate by at least two percentage points. When the equity risk premium — earnings yield minus the ten-year government bond yield — falls and stays negative for two consecutive measurement periods, the fundamental rationale for holding equity over a risk-free instrument fails.

Latest reading: Equity risk premium +0.121% as of 2026-08-17 (basket earnings yield 4.845% minus 10-year Treasury yield 4.724%). Positive, but 179 basis points below the two-percentage-point confirmation threshold. Fourteen consecutive positive readings; exit condition not triggered.

  1. The fund's annual expense ratio must remain at or below 0.30%. Equal-weight funds incur higher trading costs than cap-weighted funds because of the quarterly rebalance, so 0.30% is the ceiling beyond which the cost drag would outweigh the diversification benefit compared with cheaper alternatives.

Latest reading: Known Invesco factsheet expense ratio is 0.20% — 10 basis points below the ceiling. The automated data feed does not surface this figure; the guardrail cannot be enforced until the value is added to the fund's settings. No breach is expected given the known factsheet rate.

  1. Assets under management must remain above $500 million. Invesco has closed sub-scale UCITS products before; a fund below roughly €300–500 million faces elevated risk of closure regardless of market-price behaviour. The floor is a vehicle-survival signal, not a valuation signal.

Latest reading: Assets under management $1.824 billion as of 2026-08-17, 3.65 times the $500 million floor. Trend is positive: up from $1.48 billion at 2026-06-11 and $1.64 billion at 2026-07-30.

  1. The single largest holding must not exceed 1.0% of the fund. Equal weighting pins every constituent near 0.07% on rebalance day; any drift above 1.0% — roughly fourteen times target weight — signals either a methodology failure, a rebalance miss, or extreme intra-quarter price movement that the sampling strategy has not corrected.

Latest reading: Top-1 holding is Dell Technologies at 0.1303% as of 2026-08-17, giving 87 basis points of headroom below the 1.0% ceiling. Equal-weight integrity confirmed.

  1. The MSCI Equal Weighted index methodology must remain materially unchanged. A shift to factor-tilted weighting, removal of the quarterly rebalance, or a change of the parent universe from MSCI World to a narrower index would alter the product's fundamental character.

Latest reading: No automated signal is available for MSCI methodology changes; the sidecar confirms the fund family as Invesco Investment Management Limited and legal type as Exchange Traded Fund. No restructuring on record.

  1. The median return on equity of the fund's top ten visible holdings must not fall below 12%. Below 12%, even the basket's highest-appreciated names have lost the quality that the equal-weight thesis requires. The threshold is set lower than for cap-weighted funds because equal weighting structurally includes more lower-return-on-equity mid-cap names.

Latest reading: Median return on equity of the deduped top ten (nine names; Dell excluded due to missing data) was 27.53% as of 2026-08-17, clearing the 12% exit floor by 15.53 percentage points and the 15% confirmation threshold by 12.53 percentage points.

What's Wobbling

The single structural tension is the equity risk premium compression. The basket earnings yield has held steady at 4.845% while the ten-year US Treasury yield has risen from 4.541% in mid-July to 4.724% as of 2026-08-17, narrowing the premium to just 0.121 percentage points. The written exit condition requires two consecutive snapshots with a negative premium — neither of the last two readings (0.149% at 2026-08-10 and 0.121% at 2026-08-17) has crossed zero. The trend is worth monitoring: if Treasury yields rise by a further 13 basis points without any corresponding increase in the basket earnings yield, the premium would turn negative for the first time.

This is not a breach of any condition in the Case for the Bet. It is, however, the one monitoring item that could escalate quickly. The compression is being driven by rising bond yields, not by deterioration of the basket's underlying earnings — a mechanically different dynamic than a fundamental deterioration of the investment case.

What Could Still Go Wrong

  • Annual cost increase. Invesco could raise the fund's expense ratio beyond the 0.30% ceiling, at which point the cost of the equal-weight rebalance drag would no longer be justified versus switching to a cheaper cap-weighted alternative. Currently 0.20%; the ceiling gives 10 basis points of headroom.
  • Fund size falls below the survival floor. A sustained market drawdown could reduce assets under management toward the $500 million floor. At $1.824 billion today, the fund is 3.65 times the floor, but a 73% decline — plausible in a severe bear market — would breach it. Invesco has closed sub-scale UCITS products before.
  • Index methodology change. MSCI Inc. could restructure the equal-weighted methodology — shifting to factor-tilted weighting, removing the quarterly rebalance, or narrowing the parent universe. No automated signal monitors this; it requires manual review.
  • Single-name concentration drift. An extreme intra-quarter price surge in one or more holdings could push top-1 concentration beyond the 1.0% ceiling before the next rebalance corrects it.
  • Equity risk premium turns persistently negative. If basket earnings yield falls below the ten-year government bond yield for two consecutive quarters, exit condition (1) fires. At 0.121 percentage points today, this is the closest any exit condition has come to triggering.

(For this fund, exit fires when the share price reaches the exit edge, when a vehicle-integrity condition breaches, or when the named exit-condition trigger fires — any of three paths.)

The Rules

If the thesis is confirmed — all vehicle-integrity checks pass, the basket is within the earnings-yield band, and no driver is in the challenged state — then hold. Equal-weight basket is reasonably priced, vehicle is sound, equal-weight earnings-yield premium over the cap-weighted alternative is intact.

If the thesis is weakened — a driver is challenged but no exit condition has fired — then do not add. Re-validate after the next quarter. If the equal-weight earnings-yield premium over the cap-weighted alternative compresses below 0.5 percentage points for two consecutive quarters, the diversification benefit no longer justifies the higher annual cost versus switching to the cap-weighted alternative — consider consolidating.

If the thesis is broken — vehicle integrity has breached or the value test has failed — then exit. Move proceeds to a cap-weighted global fund or to risk-free.

If the price falls below the buy band (basket earnings yield above 6.25%) — then add to the position. The equal-weight basket at that price is meaningfully cheap versus the risk-free rate; the structural earnings-yield premium over cap-weighted alternatives makes this a decisive entry point.

If the price rises above the trim band (basket earnings yield below 4.0%) — then trim 25–33%. The basket is meaningfully expensive versus the risk-free rate; harvest the premium and redeploy on the next rebalance.

If the price rises above the exit band (basket earnings yield at or below 3.0%) — then exit. Redeploy proceeds to risk-free or to a holding that still meets the value test.

Scenario Definitions

A scenario is a joint assumption about two independent variables: operating trajectory (how the holding's underlying cash flows grow relative to their long-run trend) and exit multiple (what the market pays for those cash flows at the horizon, relative to a fair-value anchor).

Scenario Operating trajectory Exit multiple
Bear Below trend Below fair value
Base At trend At fair value
Bull Above trend At or above fair value

Non-negotiable rule: In the bear case, both dimensions must be adverse simultaneously — a scenario where only the multiple moves is a re-rating scenario, not a bear.

Bear cases are comparable in structure across all holding types (company, ETF, bond), but not in magnitude. A company bear reflects idiosyncratic execution failure; an ETF bear reflects a broad market drawdown. Different mitigations, different diversification implications.
