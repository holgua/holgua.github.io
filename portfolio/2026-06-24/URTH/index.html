About the Fund

The iShares MSCI World ETF tracks the MSCI World Index — a float-adjusted, market-capitalisation-weighted index covering approximately 1,300 large- and mid-cap companies across 23 developed markets. The United States accounts for roughly 70% of the index; the remaining 30% spans Japan, the United Kingdom, France, Switzerland, Germany, Canada, and other developed economies. The fund holds shares in proportion to each company's market value within the index, rebalanced periodically as prices and index eligibility change.

At 31.25%, technology is the largest single sector by weight, reflecting the current dominance of US mega-cap technology companies within the developed-markets universe. Financial services (15.04%), industrials (10.90%), and consumer-facing sectors make up the bulk of the remainder. The fund is essentially fully invested in equities: 99.66% of assets are held in shares, with a 0.31% routine cash residual.

The annual cost of holding the fund is 0.24% of assets, charged as an ongoing management fee. For comparison, the comparable UCITS-structured version available outside the United States — the iShares Core MSCI World UCITS ETF — charges 0.20%, four basis points less. Both are competitive for global large-cap blend exposure. Tracking difference and tracking error data are not available in the current snapshot.

The fund is structured as a Delaware statutory trust and does not issue K-1 tax forms. Capital-gain distribution history has not been verified against the iShares annual report; the in-kind redemption mechanics typical of exchange-traded funds often result in no capital-gain distributions in a given year.

Annual cost: 0.24% expense ratio.

The Case for the Bet

The bet is that over the holding period, the look-through earnings yield of this basket of developed-market companies will exceed the risk-free rate by at least two percentage points — delivering a genuine equity-risk premium (the extra return that compensates for holding equities over government bonds) — while the fund itself remains operationally sound and cost-efficient.

Six conditions must keep holding for the bet to work:

  1. Equity-risk premium stays positive and grows toward 2%. The basket's earnings yield (what the underlying companies collectively earn, divided by the market value of the basket) must exceed the ten-year US Treasury yield by at least 2% to confirm the equity case. Current reading: earnings yield 4.23% vs. Treasury 4.40% — premium is −0.17%, negative for five consecutive snapshots in the current quarter. Exit condition (1) fires if the premium stays negative for two consecutive quarters, regardless of the absolute level of the earnings yield.

  2. Annual expense ratio stays below 0.35%. Current 0.24%; early-warning flag below 0.30% (current: 6 basis points of cushion). A rise above 0.35% with no fee response signals fee drift the lower-cost UCITS structure does not require.

  3. Assets under management (the total value of all fund units outstanding) stay above $2.0 billion. Current: $8.1 billion — above the $4.0 billion early-warning flag. Below approximately $2 billion, bid-ask spreads for global blend funds typically widen enough to impair exit liquidity.

  4. No single holding exceeds 10% of the basket. The largest current holding is NVIDIA at 5.42% — below the 8.0% early-warning level and well below the 10.0% ceiling. Above 10%, a single company's fortunes dominate the index return in a way inconsistent with a diversified developed-markets mandate.

  5. MSCI does not materially restructure the World index methodology. A significant country re-classification, the removal of major developed-market constituents, or a fundamental shift away from float-adjusted market capitalisation would alter what the bet actually is. This condition is monitored manually; no automated signal is available.

  6. The median return-on-equity of the basket's ten largest holdings stays above 15%. Return-on-equity (the percentage of shareholders' net assets that the business converts to profit each year) measures quality. Current reading: 37.28% — well above the exit floor. A collapse below 15% would indicate that the basket's underlying businesses have lost the compounding quality that justifies holding equities over bonds.

The Rules

Five states drive the response. The action in each follows pre-committed logic, not judgement made at the moment of a price move:

  • If the thesis is confirmed (all conditions passing, premium positive and rising): hold — the basket is reasonably priced and the vehicle is sound.
  • If the thesis is weakening (as it is today — the premium is negative but exit condition one has not yet fired): do not add. Re-validate after the next quarter. If the premium stays negative or the look-through quality starts falling, swap to the UCITS equivalent at a lower cost or move to cash under the position-sizing rule.
  • If the thesis is broken (vehicle-integrity breach or exit condition triggered): exit. Move proceeds to a risk-free instrument or to a holding that meets the value test.
  • If the price falls to the cheap edge (basket earnings yield reaching 6.25%, equivalent to a trailing price-to-earnings ratio of 16): add to the position. The basket is meaningfully cheap against the risk-free rate.
  • If the price rises to the trim edge (basket earnings yield falling to 4.00%, equivalent to a trailing price-to-earnings ratio of 25): trim 25–33% of the position. The basket is meaningfully expensive; harvest the premium.

What's Wobbling

The equity-risk premium is the single wobble. The basket currently earns less on a look-through basis than a ten-year government bond. A holder of this fund accepts the volatility of global equities, the risks of individual business cycles across 23 developed markets, and the uncertainty of a drawdown for a return that, at today's prices, does not beat risk-free paper even before those risks are counted.

The five negative readings — on 2026-06-16, 2026-06-17, 2026-06-21, 2026-06-23, and 2026-06-24 — all fall within the second quarter of 2026. The written rule requires the signal to persist across two consecutive quarters. That means the earliest the exit condition can fire is at the first re-validation following the start of the third quarter of 2026. If the ten-year yield eases or the basket's earnings recover, the signal may correct before that date — the current reading at −0.17% is the least negative in the series. If it does not, the call shifts to exit.

Everything else is passing. The basket's underlying businesses are high-quality compounders. The vehicle is sound. The cost is competitive. The only thing wobbling is the price relative to the risk-free alternative, and the rules draw a clear line: watch it, do not act on it yet.

What Could Break the Fund

  • The equity-risk premium stays negative through a second consecutive quarter. At the next quarterly re-validation, if the premium is still negative, exit condition one fires and the call moves to exit. The current reading is improving, but the starting point (−0.37% when first measured in June 2026) is deep enough that a single quarterly snapshot could lock in the trigger.
  • A sharp rise in the ten-year Treasury yield. The basket's earnings yield is a slow-moving number tied to corporate earnings across 23 markets. The risk-free rate is not. A sustained rise in the ten-year yield — driven by fiscal pressure, persistent inflation, or a policy shift — would widen the negative premium without any change in the basket's underlying earnings, accelerating the exit-condition clock.
  • A significant re-rating of technology stocks. Technology accounts for 31.25% of the basket, and the top holdings — NVIDIA, Apple, Alphabet, Microsoft — carry earnings multiples well above the historical average. A sector-wide de-rating would compress the basket's earnings yield from the price side rather than the earnings side, pushing the premium further negative and moving the price closer to the trim and fair-value lines simultaneously.
  • An expense-ratio increase by BlackRock without a corresponding fee reduction. The fund already costs four basis points more than the equivalent UCITS structure. A further increase without a fee response would represent structural drift. The 0.30% early-warning threshold is only six basis points away from the current 0.24%.
  • An MSCI World index methodology change that materially alters the developed-markets mandate — country re-classifications, removal of major constituents, or a shift away from float-adjusted market capitalisation. This is a low-probability, high-impact risk that cannot be monitored automatically.
