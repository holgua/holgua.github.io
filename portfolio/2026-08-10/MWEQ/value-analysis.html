5. Owner Perspective (Buffett Frame)

"Would Berkshire deploy cash into this basket today?"

The basket ERP of 0.25% clears the bar for "not negative" but is well short of the 2.0% threshold at which the equity case is comfortably ahead of risk-free. The underlying basket quality is genuinely strong — a median top-10 ROE of 29.27% is well above the 15% CONFIRM floor, meaning the businesses in the drift-winner cohort are compounding capital efficiently. The verdict is: adequate yield premium, supported by strong underlying quality from the visible drift-winners, but the full basket's ~1,500-name equal-weight median ROE is structurally lower. Berkshire would not deploy cash aggressively at a 0.25% ERP, but the positive ERP combined with a healthy equal-weight EY premium over cap-weighted (URTH trailing PE ~21–22×) provides a narrow but intact rationale for holding. The case is "hold, do not add" — the thesis is working but the risk-premium cushion is thin.

Read: The spread is wide — −19% to +14% — driven almost entirely by the bear multiple compression from 20.54× to 12×. The base case of 7.7% is a reasonable real return above inflation; the bull at 14.3% requires both above-trend EPS growth and a meaningful P/E expansion, which is plausible but not certain. The bear case is painful: a sector-beta-driven EPS contraction (−11%) combined with a 12× exit multiple would produce a ~−19% 5-year cumulative return. Crucially, the base and bull cases are both comfortably positive, meaning the basket is not priced for disaster — but the bear scenario is the tail risk that equal-weight diversification is meant to mitigate compared to concentrated cap-weighted alternatives.

Note: config.forward_return.terminal_pe_bear=12.0 overrides ERP-anchored value. The ERP-anchored bear P/E formula (1 / max(risk_free_10y + 0.04, 0.02)) at current rates would produce ~15×; the user-configured 12× is the more conservative assumption.
