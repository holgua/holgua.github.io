Overall Competitive Position Strong

Overall Competitive Position: Strong. Frito-Lay's 60% US salty snacks share with DSD distribution infrastructure constitutes the clearest moat advantage in this analysis cohort. No competitor has the combination of brand depth, DSD-enabled shelf control, and dual-category (beverages + snacks) scale that PepsiCo operates. The beverage segment is competitively weaker (second-place to KO in US LRB; structural Ireland concentrate tariff disadvantage), but the snacks dominance more than compensates in aggregate. The Mars-Kellanova combination is the most significant structural change in the competitive map, but integration risk and PEP's DSD lead mean this does not threaten PEP's position in the 3-year monitoring horizon.

Narrative Assessment

PepsiCo's moat is verifiably wider than the next-best competitor on the two dimensions that matter most: brand portfolio density and DSD infrastructure. No named competitor — not Coca-Cola (which lacks snacks scale), not Mars/Kellanova (which lacks a DSD system), not Mondelez (which lacks beverages) — replicates PepsiCo's dual-category structure with owned distribution. The most significant vulnerability is structural rather than competitive: GLP-1 drug adoption is a demand-side shock to the PFNA snack franchise that does not arise from a competitor's action and cannot be defended against through pricing or distribution moat. With 21% US household adoption in May 2026 and acceleration ongoing, this represents a multi-year volume headwind whose magnitude is still uncertain. The second most important threat is the Ireland concentrate tariff, which creates a cost disadvantage in PepsiCo's carbonated beverage business vs. Coca-Cola — a structural disadvantage that no amount of marketing spending can fully offset. PEP's strategic response (poppi acquisition for functional beverages, Siete for premium snacks, Celsius partnership for energy) is appropriately defensive and portfolio-oriented, but these are offset mechanisms against a GLP-1 headwind that operates at a different scale. The single most important competitive threat over the next 3 years is sustained GLP-1 adoption reducing the addressable salty snack TAM, combined with private label gaining share in a volume-constrained category — together, these could compress both volume and pricing power in PFNA simultaneously.
