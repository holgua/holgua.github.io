The Bottom Line

Data as of 2026-07-15 · Latest filing 2026-04-30.

The bet is working — Amazon's cloud and advertising flywheels are delivering precisely the kind of compounding the thesis requires, and no written exit condition has triggered. The one condition to watch is whether Amazon's cloud infrastructure spending — running at over $150 billion a year — converts to revenue fast enough to keep the central thesis from switching from confirmation to caution. Recent Development

Amazon's three-month period ending 31 March 2026 was the most confirmatory quarter the thesis has seen in years. Amazon Web Services (Amazon's cloud computing platform, the primary source of the company's profits) grew 28% compared with the same period a year earlier, reaching $37.6 billion in quarterly sales — comfortably above the 18–20% growth the thesis requires and nowhere near the 15% floor at which the exit conditions would begin to count quarters. At the same time, advertising revenue reached $17.2 billion, up 24%, making it the primary engine of operating-profit growth in both the North American and International businesses.

The only meaningful counterweight is the scale of investment spending. Cash capital expenditure reached $43.2 billion in the quarter alone — more than Amazon spent in the entire year of 2021 — and management has stated it expects spending to continue rising through 2026. The trailing twelve-month cash figure now runs at roughly $147 billion, compressing free cash flow (cash from operations after deducting all capital spending) to just $1.2 billion for the twelve months ended March 2026, down from $25.9 billion a year earlier. The thesis requires this spending to convert into cloud revenue. Through the first quarter of 2026, the conversion is on track: Amazon's contracted cloud backlog stands at $364 billion, and in the same quarter the company announced a $100 billion expansion of its existing commercial arrangement with OpenAI. The data supports the thesis; the risk is that two or three more quarters of evidence are required before the capital-return pattern resolves clearly.

The overall verdict, and what it means for the Share Price, is unchanged: the conditions are intact, the entry price is not cheap.

About Amazon

Amazon earns money across three interlocking operations. The first is retail — selling goods directly and providing the marketplace through which third-party merchants reach customers, with $49.6 billion in Prime subscription revenue threading the whole together into a bundled loyalty programme. The second is Amazon Web Services, the company's cloud-computing infrastructure business, which supplies server capacity, databases, machine-learning tools, and storage to businesses around the world on a pay-per-use model. The third is digital advertising, which monetises the first-party purchase data Amazon holds by selling sponsored placements to merchants and brands. In the financial year ended December 2025, total revenue reached $717 billion, of which cloud computing produced 18% of revenue but 57% of operating profit — the asymmetry that makes the cloud business the thesis's true engine.

The commercial logic of all three engines reinforces itself. Prime memberships attract buyers; buyers attract third-party sellers; seller volume funds logistics scale that reduces delivery costs for Prime members; lower delivery costs improve Prime renewal rates; Prime data feeds the advertising audience; advertising revenue subsidises the retail economics. Amazon Web Services operates somewhat separately but is connected: the cloud's profitability funds the retail infrastructure investment cycle, which in turn generates the transaction data that differentiates Amazon's advertising from purely contextual alternatives.

The Case for the Bet

The bet rests on three compounding engines — cloud, advertising, and international retail — each still well short of what their economics could eventually produce. The conditions that must keep holding are:

  1. Amazon Web Services revenue growth stays above 15% on a trailing two-quarter basis. The cloud platform needs to keep absorbing the company's enormous infrastructure spending at commercially viable utilisation rates. A sustained fall below 15% growth for two consecutive quarters would signal that the AI workload thesis has not materialised on schedule and that the capital cycle is at risk of producing stranded assets.

Latest reading: +28% year-on-year in the three months ending 31 March 2026 ($37.6 billion in sales). The $364 billion contracted backlog, with a weighted-average remaining life of 5.5 years, and the $100 billion expanded OpenAI commitment confirm structural demand well above the threshold. Exit condition (1) is not in view.

  1. AWS operating margin holds above 28% on a trailing two-quarter basis. The cloud business's unit economics are what justify the capital intensity. A sustained fall to 28% or below (from the current 37.7%) would signal that the reinvestment cycle has broken the business model, not just temporarily compressed it.

Latest reading: 37.7% in the three months ending 31 March 2026, down approximately 170 basis points year-on-year from 39.4% — the compression driven by deliberate infrastructure investment spending, not pricing erosion. The current level is nearly 10 percentage points above the exit threshold, and the decline requires two consecutive quarters below 28% before the condition triggers. One quarter of investment-driven compression does not constitute a signal.

  1. No court-ordered structural remedy is imposed in the Federal Trade Commission monopoly case. Amazon faces ongoing antitrust litigation. A structural remedy requiring business separation — the most severe outcome — would be an immediate exit trigger regardless of financial performance.

Latest reading: The most recent quarterly filing references antitrust and monopolisation claims in generic risk-factor language. No structural remedy, consent decree, or new trial timeline has been disclosed. The exit condition is not triggered.

  1. Consolidated capital expenditure does not remain above $120 billion in both 2027 and 2028 without commensurate cloud revenue acceleration. The current spending level is a deliberate investment cycle with a defined conversion thesis. If the spending rate stays this high through 2027 and 2028 without cloud revenue growing commensurately, the economics of the investment case deteriorate materially.

Latest reading: The company is currently in financial year 2026. The exit condition monitors 2027 and 2028. At this point, cloud revenue is accelerating at +28%, which is exactly the commensurate growth the condition requires. The clock is running but the condition is not yet triggered.

  1. Advertising revenue growth remains above single digits over any two consecutive years. Advertising is the third engine, and a sustained deceleration to single-digit growth would signal that the core commercial advantage — first-party purchase-intent data — has lost its premium over third-party alternatives.

Latest reading: +23.9% year-on-year in the three months ending 31 March 2026 ($17.2 billion). Exit condition (5) is not in view.

  1. No material weakness in internal controls is disclosed, and the auditor is not replaced unexpectedly. These are integrity and operational-control signals. A material weakness or an unexpected auditor change would trigger an immediate exit.

Latest reading: No material weakness disclosed; Ernst & Young has audited Amazon since 1996 and continues in that role. Exit condition (6) is not triggered.

  1. Jeff Bezos does not file a Form 144 covering more than 40 million shares in a single filing. Bezos holds approximately 8.8% of the company's shares, worth roughly $237 billion at current prices — the largest alignment signal in the holding. Abnormal insider selling beyond his established pre-planned cadence would be a meaningful deterioration in that alignment.

Latest reading: The latest quarterly filing discloses that there were no open-market sales and that item 5 (trading arrangements) reports "None" for any new plans. The previously disclosed pre-planned sales have proceeded in line with established cadences. Exit condition (7) is not triggered.

  1. The owner-earnings yield does not fall below 3.0%. When the yield on cash earnings relative to market capitalisation falls below 3.0%, the valuation has priced in optimistic assumptions about capital normalisation and leaves no margin for error.

Latest reading: 4.21% as of 2025 year-end (based on $113.3 billion of owner earnings after stock-based compensation against a $2.69 trillion market capitalisation). The threshold is 120 basis points below the current reading. Exit condition (8) is not triggered.

The Share Price

The share price of $250 sits in the upper half of the hold band — comfortably above the buy edge at $140 but still below the trim edge at $263. The fair-value reference price used in this framework is $176, implying the current price is 42% above what the business's current earnings would justify at a neutral rate of return.

In other words: a buyer at today's price is paying not just for what Amazon earns now, but for approximately 4–5 additional years of base-case compounding, already baked into the price. The market's implied view is that Amazon's owner-earnings growth will run at a slightly faster pace than the 8% base assumption in the framework — a difference of about 0.9 percentage points. That gap is narrow enough to be within normal forecast uncertainty.

The share price does not yet require action under the Rules — no trim condition has triggered — but it leaves essentially no room for disappointment in the near term.

Why the Market Sees It Differently

The market prices Amazon at $250 against our framework's fair-value estimate of $176 — a premium of 42%. That gap is large enough that it deserves a direct explanation rather than a shrug.

The market's implied owner-earnings growth rate is 8.9% annually, against our base assumption of 8%. On the surface, that looks like a small disagreement. But the divergence also reflects three specific things the market appears to be pricing more generously than our baseline.

The first is structural cloud demand. Amazon Web Services is growing at 28% and holds $364 billion in contracted future revenue with a remaining life of over five years. The OpenAI arrangement alone — $100 billion committed over eight years — locks in a level of compute demand that our base model, which uses steady-state owner earnings, does not fully capture. There is credible evidence that the addressable demand for cloud computing at scale is larger than the 8% growth assumption implies.

The second is margin trajectory. Amazon Web Services currently operates at a 37.7% margin, temporarily depressed by infrastructure investment. The market appears to believe — and the filing's management commentary supports this — that the depreciation surge is a front-loaded cost that will be followed by margin re-expansion as AI capacity fills. On that view, the current compressed free cash flow is transitory and the fair-value calculation should use a normalised, post-investment margin rather than the current one.

The third is optionality in Amazon's positions in Anthropic and OpenAI. These are not ordinary financial investments — both come with commercial cloud-service arrangements that lock in AWS compute revenue regardless of which AI model wins. In the first three months of 2026 alone, the Anthropic position appreciated by $12.3 billion. A pure cash-flow model does not capture this kind of embedded option value.

Two of the three hypotheses have direct support from the current filing. The 0.9 percentage-point growth gap between the market's implied rate and our base assumption may be as simple as the market weighting hypothesis one and three slightly more than our framework does. What would need to happen for our view to close the gap: either cloud growth decelerates to confirm the 8% base, or the valuation inputs are refreshed to incorporate a higher base growth rate consistent with the evidence — a question flagged for the Standing Orders review.

The Standing Orders

The Rules translate the share-price bands into three standing orders and two price alerts.

Price alerts (informational).

- A second alert at $140, the cheap edge. The crossing arms the buy order; before adding, the latest re-validation should confirm that the conditions are still intact — specifically that cloud revenue growth remains above 18%.

Standing orders (actionable).
- A standing buy order at $140. The Rules call for adding in tranches over one to two quarters once the share price closes at or below this line and the conditions remain intact, with cloud growth confirmed above 18%.
- A standing trim order at $263. The Rules call for trimming 25–33% of the position when the share price closes at or above this line, to harvest the premium while keeping the core holding.
- A standing exit order at $421. The Rules call for closing the full position when the share price closes at or above this line. At that price, the market would be pricing in approximately 11 years of base-case compounding all at once. The implied forward return would fall below what a broadly diversified fund offers, and the instruction is to redeploy — with no re-entry for at least 90 days to avoid anchoring on the prior price.

The three order levels operate independently of the exit conditions in the Case for the Bet. An exit condition triggering at any price overrides the valuation-based orders; similarly, crossing the exit-order price at $421 does not require an exit condition to have fired first.

What's Wobbling

Four of the nine conditions read as mixed rather than confirming, though none has crossed a written exit threshold.

Cloud margin compression. AWS operating margin fell from 39.4% to 37.7% year-on-year — a compression of about 170 basis points — driven by infrastructure depreciation running ahead of the revenue it is enabling. The thesis requires that this pattern resolves as capacity fills. One quarter of compression is not actionable; two quarters below 28% would be. The current reading is 970 basis points above that floor.

Capital expenditure trajectory. Consolidated capital spending is running at an annualised pace well above $150 billion, with AWS alone adding $41.5 billion in net property, plant and equipment in a single quarter — more than double the same quarter a year earlier. Management has explicitly stated it expects spending to rise further in 2026. The written exit condition monitors 2027 and 2028; this quarter's data is simply the current state of an investment cycle that the thesis anticipated.

Capital return discipline. Amazon has not repurchased any shares despite holding $21.3 billion in net cash and a $6.1 billion authorised buyback programme that has not been used since 2022. At the same time, it has committed $50 billion to OpenAI investments and issued $53.8 billion in debt in a single month. The thesis characterises capital allocation as "acceptable but not excellent," and this quarter's evidence is consistent with that assessment — the OpenAI investment comes with a commercial cloud arrangement, making it strategic rather than purely financial.

Tariff and marketplace exposure. The quarterly filing explicitly flags China-based sellers as a significant source of third-party services and advertising revenue, and names tariff policy as an explicit risk. Despite that disclosure, North American operating income grew 41% year-on-year, third-party seller services grew 14%, and advertising grew 24%. The exposure is real and the filing language is more pointed than in prior quarters, but the exit condition monitors advertising growth falling to single digits over two consecutive years — a threshold that current readings are nowhere near.

What Could Still Go Wrong

  • Cloud stalls on AI timing. If enterprise AI workloads take longer to absorb the infrastructure investment than the thesis assumes, AWS revenue growth decelerates while the depreciation wave continues. The bear scenario has AWS slowing to mid-teens growth and operating margins compressing, leaving cash flow thin for several years. At $250, there is no floor in the current earnings power to catch a prolonged deceleration.
  • Antitrust remedy. A court-ordered structural separation of the business — the most severe possible outcome of the Federal Trade Commission lawsuit — is an immediate exit trigger. The filing discloses the case is active; no timeline or settlement is imminent, but a binary risk of this magnitude warrants monitoring even when the probability is low.
  • AI capex becomes stranded. If competing cloud platforms (Microsoft's Azure or Google Cloud) build comparable AI infrastructure faster, Amazon loses workload share without recovering its infrastructure investment through pricing. The exit conditions on AWS margin and revenue growth are designed to detect this, but only after two consecutive quarters — which means exposure during a deteriorating period.
  • Tariff disruption accelerates. If US trade policy restricts Chinese-origin goods more sharply than expected, the third-party marketplace could see meaningful GMV compression. The filing has explicitly acknowledged the dependency on China-based sellers, and a sharp tariff escalation would hit both seller services and advertising simultaneously.
  • Valuation premium contracts without operating improvement. At 42% above fair value, Amazon does not need the business to break — it only needs the market to stop assigning the current growth premium for the share price to fall materially. A broad de-rating of high-growth technology valuations would compress the price even with the operating conditions intact.

Gaps in the Picture

  • Owner earnings are calculated using the total capital expenditure figure reported under accounting rules, not a separate maintenance figure — Amazon does not disclose a maintenance-versus-growth split for its infrastructure spending. The resulting owner-earnings figure may understate true economic earning power, which would make the fair-value estimate of $176 conservatively low. The quarterly filing's management discussion is the best reference for assessing the growth-versus-maintenance split in any given period.
  • A fair-value drift warning is present in the underlying analysis: the peer-method valuation midpoint ($533) and the earnings-yield-based fair value ($176) have diverged significantly. This does not affect the action rules — which run off the earnings-yield threshold — but it signals that the two methods have not been reconciled and a review is warranted before the next re-validation.
  • Risk-free rate unavailable for this holding: neither Amazon's own market-data file nor the reference file carries a current 10-year risk-free rate. The long-run expected-return premium over the risk-free rate could not be computed this cycle.

The Rules

The action plan runs off five conditions, assessed independently at each re-validation:

  • If the thesis is confirmed (all conditions intact or mixed, none broken): hold — no action, continue compounding the AWS and advertising flywheel.
  • If the thesis is weakened (any condition deteriorating but no exit condition triggered): do not add. Re-run the validation against the specific deteriorating driver — most commonly cloud growth or cloud margin. Decide after the next two quarterly filings; one quarter of data, then confirm with a second.
  • If the thesis is broken (any written exit condition fires): exit the full position within 30 days. No averaging down. Particular care: cloud revenue and margin exits require two consecutive quarters of breach before action; an antitrust structural remedy or an auditor change is an immediate exit, no waiting period required.
  • If the share price falls below the buy edge ($140): confirm the thesis is intact first — specifically that cloud revenue growth is at or above 18%. If confirmed, add to the position in tranches over one to two quarters.
  • If the share price rises above the trim edge ($263): trim 25–33% of the position to harvest the premium while keeping the core holding. Re-evaluate the remaining size after trimming.
  • If the share price rises above the exit edge ($421): exit the full position. At that price the market is pricing in roughly 11 years of base-case compounding all at once. Redeploy proceeds at the next cheap-edge crossing or into a cheaper alternative. No re-entry within 90 days.

Scenario Definitions

A scenario is a joint assumption about two independent variables: operating trajectory (how the holding's underlying cash flows grow relative to their long-run trend) and exit multiple (what the market pays for those cash flows at the horizon, relative to a fair-value anchor).

Scenario Operating trajectory Exit multiple
Bear Below trend Below fair value
Base At trend At fair value
Bull Above trend At or above fair value

Non-negotiable rule: In the bear case, both dimensions must be adverse simultaneously — a scenario where only the multiple moves is a re-rating scenario, not a bear.

Bear cases are comparable in structure across all holding types (company, ETF, bond), but not in magnitude. A company bear reflects idiosyncratic execution failure; an ETF bear reflects a broad market drawdown. Different mitigations, different diversification implications.
