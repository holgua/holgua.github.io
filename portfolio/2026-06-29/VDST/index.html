About the Fund

Vanguard's US Treasury 0–1 Year Bond ETF holds a rolling basket of US government bills maturing
within one year. The bills are the shortest-duration debt instruments the US Treasury issues —
they mature quickly and pay no coupon; the return is simply the difference between
the purchase price and face value at maturity. Because the bills mature so fast, the fund's share
price barely moves; almost all of its return accrues as income that is immediately reinvested
into the next round of bills rather than paid out as cash distributions. An investor holding
shares receives no dividend cheque; instead the share's value drifts upward as the accumulated
income is reinvested.

The fund is managed by Vanguard Group (Ireland) Limited and structured as a UCITS exchange-traded
fund domiciled in Ireland (the regulatory wrapper commonly used for European-listed funds). It trades
on the London Stock Exchange under the ticker VDST. The annual charge is 0.07% — roughly 70 cents
per year on every £1,000 held — placing it in the lowest-cost bracket for this category of fund.
No tracking-difference or tracking-error figures are available from the data provider for this
listing; the fee itself is the relevant cost metric for a fund whose basket turns over entirely
within a year. Total fund assets are approximately $2.4 billion (manually verified via Trackinsight,
2026-06-19; the data provider does not surface this figure automatically for UCITS listings).

The Case for the Bet

The bet is that a portfolio holding a small allocation to ultra-short US Treasury bills at low
cost serves a useful function as long as two things remain true: the fund itself stays operationally
sound, and the yield it offers remains competitive enough to justify the opportunity cost of holding
it rather than an EUR cash alternative.

Three conditions must keep holding for that premise to work:

  1. Ongoing annual charges below 0.10% for two consecutive quarterly reviews. At that level,
    comparable ultra-short Treasury funds from other providers become cheaper, and the fee advantage
    disappears. The current charge is 0.07%; the exit fires only if the charge exceeds 0.10% for
    two quarters in a row (a single elevated quarter is treated as noise).

  2. Total fund assets above $1 billion. Below that threshold, the fund risks the kind of
    liquidity or redemption dynamics that would undermine its value as a near-instant cash-equivalent.
    The current assets are $2.4 billion. This figure must be verified manually each quarter, as
    the standard data feed does not return it for UCITS-listed funds.

  3. US sovereign credit rated AA- or above by at least two of the three major ratings agencies
    (Fitch, Moody's, and S&P).     A downgrade below AA- by any two of the three would signal a
    structural shift in the creditworthiness of the underlying borrower — the US government — and
    would require a full reassessment of the cash-equivalent premise. This condition is monitored
    manually; no automated signal exists in the data pipeline.

the share price sits meaningfully above that fair-value anchor — a gap of roughly 28%.

That gap is not alarming in itself. For a fund whose underlying bills mature within a year, the
"price" is essentially NAV; it drifts upward at roughly the current basket yield as income is
reinvested. The $46 fair-value figure reflects a yield of 4.8% — the thesis base case; the current
yield of 3.73% implies a higher price by construction. Put plainly: a lower yield on short-term
bills corresponds to more expensive bills, and hence a higher share price. The price sitting above
the fair-value anchor means yields have fallen since the thesis was calibrated, not that the fund
has mispriced.

The basket yield of 3.73% sits in the upper part of the hold band — above the 3.5% trim-band
edge, below the 5.5% buy-band edge — and no band edge has been crossed. The bands are yield-driven;
the price edges ($40, $63, $73) are computed mechanically from those yield thresholds at the current
NAV and are valid only at this snapshot date. They must be recomputed at each quarterly refresh
as the accumulating share class drifts upward.

The Standing Orders

The rules translate the yield-based band edges into three standing orders and two price alerts.
Each is the operationalisation of a pre-committed rule — not a new judgement.

to the 4.8% base case; no action follows automatically, but it is a useful checkpoint to confirm

- A second alert at $40, the cheap-edge. At that price the basket yield has reached 5.5% — the
thesis bull case — signalling that T-bill rates are unusually attractive relative to alternatives.

Standing orders (actionable).
- A standing buy order at $40. The rules call for adding at the margin when the share price closes
at or below this line and vehicle-integrity conditions remain green. The basket yield at that
price would be 5.5% — the bull-case level where T-bill rates are markedly above EUR alternatives.
Vehicle-integrity guardrails still gate any large addition.
- A standing trim order at $63. The rules call for trimming at the margin when the share price
closes at or above this line. At that price the basket yield has compressed to 3.5%, the
bear-case floor where USD T-bill carry versus EUR cash is materially weaker. Vehicle-integrity
guardrails still gate any exit decision on yield grounds alone.
- A standing exit order at $73. The rules call for exiting the full position when the share price
closes at or above this line. At that price the basket yield has compressed to 3.0%, below even

The three order levels interact with vehicle-integrity conditions: a breach of any of the three
numbered exit conditions (charges, size, credit rating) is an independent path to exit regardless
of where the share price sits. The price-based orders are the valuation channel; the integrity
conditions are the structural channel. Either can fire independently.

What's Wobbling

The basket yield has been compressing steadily. At 3.73%, it now sits only 23 basis points
above the 3.5% trim-band floor — the narrowest margin since the thesis was calibrated. The
Federal Reserve's easing cycle has repriced the short end of the US yield curve meaningfully
lower from the 2023–2024 peak; if that compression continues at the same pace, the trim threshold
could be crossed within one or two quarterly review cycles.

That said, the compression has not crossed any pre-committed line. The 23-basis-point buffer is
thin but real. The exit conditions tied to the fund's cost and size are completely unaffected —
yield compression is a market development, not a vehicle-integrity failure. And by the framing
of the original thesis, holding T-bills at a lower-than-ideal yield is still preferable to
deploying capital into an equity position that does not offer a superior risk-adjusted return.

The practical implication: the 2026-06-24 verdict is hold, but the next quarterly review should
treat the yield level as the primary variable to monitor. If the basket yield drops below 3.5%,
the trim rule activates.

What Could Still Go Wrong

  • The Federal Reserve cuts faster than priced. If short-term US rates fall sharply — as in
    an aggressive easing cycle responding to a US recession — the basket yield could breach the
    3.5% trim floor and eventually the 3.0% exit floor, triggering the valuation-channel exit rules.

  • Charges rise above 0.10%. If Vanguard raises the annual fee for this share class above
    0.10% for two consecutive quarters, exit condition (1) fires. The current charge of 0.07%
    provides 3 basis points of headroom — a thin buffer that could be consumed by a small fee
    adjustment.

  • Total fund assets fall below $1 billion. A sustained outflow — triggered, for example,
    by large institutional redemptions or a competitor launching a lower-cost alternative — could
    shrink the fund below the liquidity floor. This must be verified manually each quarter.

  • US sovereign credit downgrade. Two of three major agencies downgrading the US below AA-
    would fire exit condition (3). A downgrade of this magnitude would be a structural event,
    not a transient market move, and would require reassessment of the entire cash-equivalent premise.

  • Currency and structural considerations. The fund is denominated in USD but held within a
    EUR-denominated portfolio. A sharp strengthening of EUR versus USD reduces the EUR value of
    the position independent of the fund's own performance. This is a portfolio-level risk rather
    than a vehicle-integrity failure — it does not trigger any of the three exit conditions but
    affects the real return experienced in EUR terms.

(For this fund, exit fires when the share price reaches the exit edge, when a vehicle-integrity
condition breaches, or when the named exit-condition trigger fires — any of three paths.)

The Rules

If all three vehicle-integrity conditions are passing and the yield remains within the hold band,
hold the position. Vehicle integrity intact — charges within budget, assets above the floor,
mandate unchanged.

If any driver shows signs of weakening but no exit condition has yet fired, do not add. Re-assess
at the next quarterly review and monitor charge and asset trends before increasing the allocation.

If any of the three numbered exit conditions fires — charges above 0.10% for two consecutive
quarters, assets below $1 billion, or US sovereign credit downgraded below AA- by two agencies —
exit the full position. Reinvest in EUR cash or short nominal German government bonds.

If the basket yield rises to the 5.5% buy-band edge (corresponding share price approximately $40),
add at the margin if cash is available. That yield level marks T-bill rates as unusually attractive
relative to EUR alternatives. Vehicle-integrity guardrails still gate any large addition.

If the basket yield falls to the 3.5% trim-band edge (corresponding share price approximately $63),
trim at the margin. At that yield, USD T-bill carry versus EUR cash is materially weaker.
Vehicle-integrity guardrails still gate any exit decision on yield grounds.

If any of exit conditions (1), (2), or (3) fires as described in the thesis, exit per that
specific condition.
