About Unilever

Unilever is one of the world's largest makers of everyday consumer goods — soap, shampoo, conditioner, laundry detergent, mayonnaise, ice cream, and tea, sold under brands including Dove, Axe, Lynx, Hellmann's, Knorr, Domestos, and Magnum. Roughly a third of humanity uses at least one Unilever product on any given day. The company sells through its own last-mile distribution network spanning more than 190 countries, with approximately 60% of revenue coming from faster-growing emerging markets in Asia, Africa, and Latin America. The economics are straightforward: Unilever charges a premium over the unbranded alternative, generates cash from the repeat-purchase cycle of household staples, and reinvests a portion into brand advertising and distribution to sustain that premium.

The current portfolio is in transition. In December 2025, Unilever completed the demerger of its ice cream division — including the Magnum and Cornetto brands — into a separately listed company called Magnum Ice Cream Co. In March 2026, Unilever signed an agreement to sell its Hellmann's, Knorr, and Marmite brands (plus related products in the Nutrition segment) to McCormick Foods for USD 44.8bn, with the transaction expected to close in mid-2027. The effect of both transactions is to concentrate Unilever in Beauty & Wellbeing, Personal Care, and Home Care — its three highest-margin, fastest-growing segments — while reducing the exposure to the Nutrition businesses most vulnerable to private-label substitution in developed markets and to regulatory pressure in the United States.

The Case for the Bet

The bet on Unilever rests on a single idea: a wide-moat consumer-goods franchise with two structural advantages — brand equity and distribution reach — generating reliable cash across economic cycles, held at a price that reflects fair value for the earnings stream. The bet works as long as the following conditions hold:

  1. Operating margin stays above 14% for no more than one consecutive fiscal year below that threshold. The 14% floor is the level at which the moat would be visibly eroding — a business without pricing power would fall toward commodity margins. At 17.9% in FY2025, Unilever is at the top of its five-year range. Two consecutive years below 14% would signal permanent impairment, not a cyclical setback.

Latest reading: 17.9% operating margin for the year ended 2025-12-31, confirmed by structured filing data (operating income EUR 9,037M on revenue EUR 50,503M). This is up from 16.8% in FY2024 and represents a five-year high. The exit condition is not within reach.

  1. Return on invested capital stays above 10% for no more than one consecutive fiscal year below that threshold. Return on invested capital (the profit the business earns on all the money tied up in running it) needs to stay above the estimated cost of that capital — roughly 8–9% for Unilever. A sustained fall below 10% would signal that the brand equity is no longer generating economic value.

Latest reading: 14.7% for FY2025, recovered from a 11.5% trough in FY2024 caused by elevated restructuring charges and goodwill adjustments. The spread above the 10% threshold is 4.7 percentage points — comfortably intact.

  1. Owner earnings (the cash the business generates after maintaining its physical assets) stay above EUR 5.5bn for no more than one consecutive fiscal year below that floor. EUR 5.5bn is the bear-case floor — the level at which cash generation would be consistent with significant structural deterioration.

Latest reading: EUR 6,850M for FY2025, derived as operating cash flow of EUR 8,350M minus an estimated EUR 1,500M in maintenance investment. EUR 1,350M above the exit floor. The accruals ratio of +0.016 confirms the cash earnings are real, not accounting artefacts.

  1. Goodwill impairment stays below EUR 3bn in any single year, unless a concurrent disposal explains the reduction. Goodwill impairment without a disposal rationale signals management writing down past acquisition mistakes under pressure rather than through deliberate portfolio rationalisation.

Latest reading: Goodwill declined EUR 4,602M in FY2025 (from EUR 22,311M to EUR 17,709M), but the entire reduction is concurrent with and attributable to the Ice Cream demerger. The exit-condition carve-out for disposal-driven impairment applies; exit condition 4 is not triggered.

  1. Accruals ratio stays below +0.10 for no more than one consecutive fiscal year. The accruals ratio measures whether reported earnings are converting to real cash — a value above +0.10 for two consecutive years would signal that profits are being reported ahead of actual cash collection, a classic sign of earnings-quality deterioration.

Latest reading: +0.016 for FY2025. The multi-year pattern (FY2023: −0.039, FY2024: −0.047, FY2025: +0.016) shows that reported earnings have consistently been backed by or exceeded by cash generation. No concern here.

  1. The McCormick Foods transaction closes at or above 80% of the agreed USD 44.8bn value. A collapse or repricing of more than 20% downward from the agreed value would signal execution failure and strand capital in the lower-quality Nutrition portfolio that the strategy intends to exit.

Latest reading: As of 2026-07-30, the transaction signed in March 2026 is under review by competition authorities in three jurisdictions — the UK Competition and Markets Authority, the EU Directorate-General for Competition, and the US Department of Justice. No enforcement action has been reported. The most likely outcome remains clearance with standard remedies.

  1. Power Brand underlying sales growth stays above 1.5% for every pair of consecutive quarterly trading-statement periods. The 1.5% floor is the threshold at which private-label substitution would be demonstrably winning against the brand premium. Two consecutive periods below that level would signal that the brand moat is losing pricing power faster than emerging-market growth can offset.

Latest reading: Power Brand underlying sales growth of +4.3% for the full year 2025 and +5.0% for the three months ending in March 2026 (volume component: +4.0%). No period in the recorded data has fallen below the 1.5% threshold.

The Share Price

The current share price of 4,671p (GBX) sits within the hold band — above the buy edge at 3,754p and below the trim edge at 6,006p. On the primary valuation measure — the yield the business's annual cash generation produces against the current market price — Unilever is generating roughly 6.43%, compared to the 6.5% level that defines fair value. The share price is, in other words, approximately at fair value: not cheap enough to warrant adding, not expensive enough to warrant trimming.

That arithmetic is based on normalised owner earnings of approximately USD 7.6bn (the three-year average of operating cash flow minus estimated annual maintenance investment), divided by the current market capitalisation. At today's price, a holder is effectively purchasing a claim on 6.4 cents of annual cash generation for every dollar of market value — just below the 6.5-cent level that the thesis treats as fair. The market is pricing the base case, with no credit for the optionality of the portfolio-simplification cycle.

The valuation picture has one complication worth naming directly: a secondary valuation method — applying the trading multiples that comparable businesses (Nestlé, Procter & Gamble, and Unilever's own history) attract — points to a fair value of roughly USD 80 per share (or approximately 5,900p at current exchange rates), meaningfully above today's price. The two methods disagree because they ask different questions: the cash-generation yield method anchors to what the business earns today, while the multiple-based method looks through to what the portfolio might earn after the McCormick transaction closes and the restructuring cycle completes. Both approaches are defensible; the gap is documented in Gaps in the Picture. The authoritative signal for the hold-band rules is the cash-generation yield method, which is what the Standing Orders are calibrated against.

What's Wobbling

One driver is reading as mixed rather than confirming: net debt. Unilever carries an estimated USD 23.3bn in net debt — a ratio of roughly three times normalised owner earnings — that is higher than a balance-sheet-conservative wide-moat business ideally would carry. The watch signal is operating cash flow as a share of revenue falling below 12%; at 16.5% in FY2025, that line has not been approached. No exit condition exists for leverage alone, and the thesis explicitly acknowledges the leverage as a pre-committed watch item, not a disqualifying condition.

The reason the wobble does not change the call is the McCormick transaction. If the Hellmann's, Knorr, and Marmite sale closes at the agreed USD 44.8bn, the proceeds would be sufficient to eliminate the net debt position entirely and still leave capital for buybacks. The thesis was written with that deleveraging path in view. If the transaction collapses — which is exit condition 6 — the leverage becomes a materially different risk than it appears today.

There is one further note that sits between a wobble and a data gap. The difference between the cash-generation yield's fair value (4,620p) and the multiple-based model's fair value (approximately 5,900p) is meaningful enough that it warrants attention before the next annual cycle. The two methods can reasonably disagree on what a restructuring business is worth; but a 28% gap in the fair-value estimates is a signal to revisit The Case for the Bet with the post-McCormick portfolio in mind, not defer indefinitely.

What Could Still Go Wrong

  • Emerging-market currency weakness. Approximately 60% of Unilever's revenue comes from emerging markets, priced in local currencies but reported in euros. A sustained 15% decline in the broad EM currency basket against the euro — as in the bear case — would compress reported revenue and profits even if the underlying volumes hold up. Unilever's local-production model limits the direct cost impact of currency moves, but the translation effect on reported earnings is unavoidable.

  • Private-label acceleration in developed markets. If budget-constrained European and North American consumers switch from branded mayonnaise, laundry detergent, and shampoo to unbranded alternatives at a faster rate than the thesis assumes, Power Brand underlying sales growth could fall below the 1.5% exit threshold. This risk is structural in the Nutrition segment — which is precisely why the McCormick disposal is strategically rational. The rump portfolio (Beauty, Personal Care, Home Care) faces lower private-label pressure.

  • McCormick deal collapse or repricing. A failed antitrust review, a material adverse change in the Nutrition business before closing, or a repricing of more than 20% below the agreed USD 44.8bn would simultaneously strand capital in a lower-quality portfolio and remove the primary deleveraging mechanism. This is exit condition 6 and the most consequential near-term risk.

  • Operating margin erosion in Home Care. The weakest sub-segment in the recent data is Home Care, which grew underlying sales at only 1.9% in the nine months ended September 2025. Home Care is the segment closest to commodity positioning — cleaning products face more direct private-label competition than beauty or personal care. Sustained weakness here would be an early signal that the brand moat is narrowing.

  • MAHA food-ingredient regulation becoming permanent law. The US Make America Healthy Again food-ingredient agenda has migrated from executive-branch initiative to state-level legislation in more than a dozen states. If federal or further state-level restrictions are imposed on food ingredients used in Unilever's Nutrition products, reformulation costs of EUR 200–500mn could materialise even before the McCormick transaction closes.

This estimate is consistent with Unilever's fixed-asset base and industry norms, but it has not been independently verified from the filing. The three-year normalised earnings figure of USD 7.6bn used in the valuation is therefore an approximation with a range of roughly ±EUR 300–500M depending on the actual maintenance investment level.

The Rules

The pre-committed rules that govern how different readings of the conditions translate into action are as follows:

  • If the thesis is confirmed — that is, if the cash-generation yield is between 5.0% and 8.0%, operating margin is at or above 14%, return on invested capital is at or above 10%, Power Brand underlying sales growth is at or above 1.5%, and the McCormick transaction remains on track — hold the position. The dividend yield of approximately 3.71% is the primary return mechanism in the base case. Hold for income and optionality on the portfolio-simplification re-rating.

  • If the thesis is weakened — that is, if any driver moves from confirming to challenged without triggering a formal exit condition — do not add to the position. Monitor for one additional quarter before reassessing position size. Key dates to watch: quarterly trading statements for Power Brand underlying sales growth and emerging-market organic growth; the annual ESEF filing for return on invested capital, the accruals ratio, and the goodwill carrying value; and continuously, the McCormick transaction regulatory clearance status.

  • If the thesis is broken — that is, if any of the seven numbered exit conditions fires as confirmed from filed financial data or press releases — exit the full position unconditionally. Operating margin below 14% for two consecutive fiscal years, return on invested capital below 10% for two consecutive fiscal years, and Power Brand underlying sales growth below 1.5% for two consecutive quarterly periods are all unconditional exits with no averaging down permitted after confirmation.

  • If the price falls below the buy edge (owner earnings yield rises to 8.0% or above — approximately 3,754p or below) — confirm all conditions are still intact before adding, specifically that operating margin is above 16% in the most recently reported period. Add in two tranches over one to two quarters.

  • If the price rises above the trim edge (owner earnings yield falls below 5.0% — approximately 6,006p or above) — trim 25–33% of the position. The trim is mechanical and does not require thesis deterioration.

  • If the price rises above the exit edge (owner earnings yield compresses below 4.5% — approximately 8,580p or above) — exit the full position. No re-entry within 90 days.

Scenario Definitions

A scenario is a joint assumption about two independent variables: operating trajectory (how the holding's underlying cash flows grow relative to their long-run trend) and exit multiple (what the market pays for those cash flows at the horizon, relative to a fair-value anchor).

Scenario Operating trajectory Exit multiple
Bear Below trend Below fair value
Base At trend At fair value
Bull Above trend At or above fair value

Non-negotiable rule: In the bear case, both dimensions must be adverse simultaneously — a scenario where only the multiple moves is a re-rating scenario, not a bear.

Bear cases are comparable in structure across all holding types (company, ETF, bond), but not in magnitude. A company bear reflects idiosyncratic execution failure; an ETF bear reflects a broad market drawdown. Different mitigations, different diversification implications.
