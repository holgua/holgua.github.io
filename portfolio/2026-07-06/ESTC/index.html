About Elastic

Elastic makes its money by licensing the Elasticsearch software stack — a distributed search and data-analytics engine that companies embed into their own products and internal systems. Customers use it for three distinct problems: full-text and vector search (the original use case, now extended to artificial-intelligence retrieval), observability (monitoring application performance and infrastructure health in real time), and security information and event management (analysing logs and alerts for potential threats). All three workloads run on the same underlying data layer, which is the architectural feature that creates the switching costs the thesis depends on.

Revenue arrives in two forms. The older, larger stream is a subscription to run Elasticsearch on the customer's own hardware or cloud account — called self-managed. The newer, growing stream is Elastic Cloud, a fully hosted service where Elastic manages the infrastructure. Cloud revenue accounted for 48% of the total in the year ending 2026-04-30, up from 43% in 2024, and management expects it to cross 50% in the coming year. The shift matters because cloud customers typically expand their usage faster and churn less than self-managed customers, and Elastic collects revenue continuously rather than in annual lump sums.

The business is built on a developer-first model: the core Elasticsearch engine is available as an open-source download, and Elastic monetises the enterprise features, the cloud hosting, and the support contracts on top of it. Co-founders Shay Banon (chief technology officer, 6.4% ownership) and Steven Schuurman (board director, 5.7%) together hold about 12% of the shares outstanding, providing unusual alignment between the people who designed the architecture and the people with a financial stake in its success.

The Case for the Bet

The central bet is that three distinct enterprise software use cases — search, observability, and security — can be consolidated onto a single data layer, and that once production pipelines are built on that layer, the cost of switching to a competitor is high enough to keep customers expanding their spending year after year. The supporting bet is that the migration of that workload from self-managed deployments to Elastic's cloud service compounds revenue per customer as adoption deepens, and that the operating leverage inherent in a software business eventually translates into meaningful free cash flow growth.

The bet depends on seven observable conditions holding:

  1. Net expansion rate stays above 105% for any two consecutive quarters. A rate below 105% for two consecutive periods means existing customers are no longer growing their spending faster than they churn — the clearest signal that switching costs are fading.
    Latest reading: 112% as of 2026-04-30. Comfortably above the exit floor; the count of customers spending more than $100,000 per year grew from 1,510 to 1,720, and the count spending more than $1 million per year reached 240.

  2. Elastic Cloud grows at least 10% in any full fiscal year. A deceleration below 10% for a full year would confirm that the open-source fork of Elasticsearch — developed by Amazon Web Services and now hosted under the Linux Foundation with more than 1,400 contributors — is successfully commoditising the cloud segment.
    Latest reading: Elastic Cloud grew 22% in the year ending 2026-04-30. The annual cloud product (multi-year contracts) grew 28%. Both well above the exit floor and above the 15% bear-case threshold.

  3. Free cash flow margin stays above 14% on a full-year view. A compression below 14% without an identifiable one-off cause would signal that the capital-light characterisation of the business is no longer holding.
    Latest reading: 18.5% ($321.8 million of free cash flow on $1.74 billion of revenue). 450 basis points above the exit floor, consistent with management's guidance of approximately 21.5% for the year ahead.

  4. Stock-based compensation stays below 18% of revenue for any second consecutive fiscal year. Compensation in shares is not a non-cash item in any economically meaningful sense — it dilutes owners. A second consecutive year above 18% would signal that compensation discipline has reversed.
    Latest reading: 17.7% in the year ending 2026-04-30, down from 18.2% the prior year. The consecutive-year streak below the exit threshold has been broken. The margin to the 18% line is thin — roughly 27 basis points.

  5. No acquisition exceeds $500 million at more than eight times the target's annual revenue without a disclosed path to free-cash-flow accretion within three years. This condition is designed to prevent capital being destroyed in an overpriced deal that dilutes the existing free-cash-flow yield.
    Latest reading: The only acquisition in the year ending 2026-04-30 was Jina AI, an artificial-intelligence embedding model company, for approximately $36.8 million — well below the $500 million threshold.

  6. The accounting operating margin becomes positive by 2028. The loss narrowed from −3.7% to −1.9% in the year ending 2026-04-30, driven by gross margin improvement to 76%. At the current pace of improvement (roughly 1.8 percentage points per year), the 2028 target is achievable.
    Latest reading: −1.9% operating margin in 2026. Progress confirmed; the 2028 target remains within reach on current trajectory.

  7. Datadog's annual revenue growth does not exceed Elastic's by more than 15 percentage points for two consecutive fiscal years, and Elastic retains its status as a Leader in the Gartner Observability segment assessment. This condition addresses the competitive threat in the observability segment, where Datadog is larger and growing faster.
    Latest reading: The annual filing names Datadog as a primary observability competitor but does not disclose comparative growth data or external analyst rankings — those require cross-referencing Datadog's own filings and Elastic's investor-relations communications. No exit condition is triggered; the condition must be monitored externally each quarter.

The Share Price

At $59, Elastic's free cash flow yield — the ratio of the business's annual cash generation to the market's total valuation of the company — is 5.2%. The yield-based fair-value anchor, calibrated to the discounted cash flow model's base-case intrinsic value of $101 per share (a 15% annual growth assumption over five years, discounted at 10%), implies a fair-value yield of 3.0%. The stock therefore sits in the lower half of the hold band: above the $56 buy edge (which corresponds to a 5.5% yield, the threshold at which the margin of safety is judged sufficient to add to the position), but well below the $101 fair-value anchor and far below the $140 trim edge.

In plainer terms, the market is currently pricing the shares at about 40% below what a buyer who accepts the base-case growth assumptions would consider a full price. That is a wide discount. It is not, however, a clear-cut buy signal at today's price because the yield is 28 basis points short of the 5.5% buy threshold — near enough that a small further price decline would move the reading into buy territory, but not yet there.

A separate model using peer-group revenue and earnings multiples produces a higher central estimate of $244 per share for the base scenario. The two anchors diverge materially because they use different methodologies: the $101 figure is a discounted cash flow calculation using current free cash flow as the starting point; the $244 figure extrapolates from how comparable companies are currently valued by the market. Both are present in the underlying analysis; the $101 figure governs the buy and trim thresholds used here.

What's Wobbling

Stock-based compensation. At 17.7% of revenue, the compensation line declined from 18.2% last year — the right direction. But the exit condition requires that it stay below 18% on an annual basis; one year above and one year below does not confirm a structural improvement, it confirms oscillation. The multi-year trend is encouraging (roughly 20% in 2024, 18.2% in 2025, 17.7% in 2026), but the threshold sits only 27 basis points away, which is well within one quarter's variation. The next annual filing will tell whether the trajectory is genuine.

The buy threshold is very close. At $59, the free cash flow yield is 28 basis points short of the 5.5% buy signal. A move from $59 to $56 would trip the add condition. That proximity is intentional — the buy threshold was set near the current price because the margin of safety was judged adequate but not compelling at the time the thesis was written. The proximity means the add signal could fire quickly, which makes the thesis-confirmation discipline between tranches more, not less, important.

Competitive position cannot be assessed from the annual filing alone. The exit condition that addresses Datadog's growth advantage requires comparing revenue growth rates from two separate companies and cross-referencing an external analyst ranking. The annual filing names Datadog as a competitor but provides no comparative data. This is not a problem with the filing — it is a structural feature of how the condition was written. It must be checked quarterly using Datadog's own filings and Elastic's public communications.

What Could Still Go Wrong

  • Open-source commoditisation. The Amazon Web Services fork of Elasticsearch, now developed under the Linux Foundation by more than 1,400 contributors, could erode the self-managed renewal base faster than Elastic Cloud can replace it. The bear case assigns a 20% probability to a scenario in which cloud growth falls below 10% and the implied annual return on the position is −20%.
  • Datadog pulls further ahead. Datadog grew 28% in its most recent fiscal year while Elastic grew 17%. If that gap widens and persists, Elastic's position in the observability segment deteriorates. Exit condition (7) fires only if the gap exceeds 15 percentage points for two consecutive years and the Gartner ranking falls — both conditions must hold simultaneously — but a widening gap is worth monitoring every quarter.
  • Stock-based compensation ratchets back up. One year below 18% is not a trend. If compensation reverses in the next filing — driven by recruiting pressure in the artificial-intelligence talent market — the two-consecutive-year exit condition could fire within 18 months.
  • A large acquisition at a poor price. Management has a stated preference for tuck-in acquisitions to extend artificial-intelligence capabilities. The Jina AI deal at $36.8 million is consistent with that. A larger deal — say, an enterprise observability or security company at a revenue multiple above eight times — could destroy a meaningful portion of the free cash flow yield without a credible accretion timeline.
  • Profitability convergence stalls. The accounting operating margin improved from −3.7% to −1.9% in one year. The 2028 target of positive accounting operating margin requires roughly another 2 percentage points of improvement. Any revenue growth disappointment combined with fixed-cost pressure could stall that trajectory without triggering the 14% free cash flow margin exit condition.

Gaps in the Picture

Two fair-value anchors coexist. The discounted cash flow model puts base-case intrinsic value at $101 per share. A separate model using peer-group and historical revenue multiples produces $244. The two numbers are both present in the underlying analysis and both intentional — they use different methodologies and different assumptions. This report uses $101 for the buy and trim thresholds because that is what the yield-based band system is calibrated to. A future analysis revision may reconcile or replace one of the two anchors.

Competitive position driver is externally sourced. Exit condition (7) — the Datadog growth gap and Gartner ranking check — cannot be assessed from Elastic's own filings. It requires reading Datadog's quarterly results and Elastic's investor-relations publications each quarter. The latest annual filing was silent on comparative growth data, so no assessment was made for this cycle.

Risk-free rate sourced from proxy. The hurdle rate uses the German inflation-linked government bond yield from a portfolio-wide market data snapshot (2026-07-06) rather than from Elastic's own market data file. This is the standard fallback for company holdings. The figure used was 0.86%.

Free cash flow override in place. The yield calculations use a manually overridden free cash flow figure of $321.8 million (the audited FY2026 number from the financial brief). The default calculation would subtract stock-based compensation from cash flow, producing a significantly lower figure that would not reflect the basis on which the buy and trim thresholds were calibrated. This override must be updated at each annual filing refresh.

The Rules

If the thesis is confirmed — all conditions working, price within the hold band — hold the position and monitor the stock-based compensation line and Elastic Cloud growth rate at each quarterly update. Do not add unless the free cash flow yield clears 5.5% (~$56 per share).

If the thesis weakens — the net expansion rate falls toward 107% or cloud growth decelerates below 15% for two consecutive quarters — do not add. Require two consecutive quarters of deterioration before taking any action; one quarter is not enough to confirm a structural break.

If the thesis breaks — any exit condition fires as written — close the full position within 30 days of the triggering event. No averaging down on a broken thesis. The seven exit conditions are: net expansion rate below 105% for two consecutive quarters; cloud growth below 10% for a full fiscal year; free cash flow margin below 14% on a full-year view; stock-based compensation above 18% for two consecutive fiscal years; an acquisition above $500 million at more than eight times annual revenue without a disclosed accretion timeline within three years; accounting operating margin not positive by 2028; or Datadog's growth rate exceeding Elastic's by more than 15 percentage points for two consecutive fiscal years while Elastic's observability ranking falls below Leader.

If the share price falls below the buy edge — yield clears 5.5%, approximately $56 per share — confirm the thesis is intact, then add to the position in tranches. No more than half the target size in a single tranche. The bear case implies a potential loss of roughly half the current price; staged entry with a thesis confirmation between tranches is the appropriate discipline.

If the share price rises above the trim edge — yield falls below 2.2%, approximately $140 per share — trim roughly a quarter of the position. Harvest the premium over intrinsic value while keeping the core holding for continued compounding.

If a valuation exit edge is later computed and the price rises above it — the full remaining position exits. No re-entry within 90 days to defuse recency bias.
