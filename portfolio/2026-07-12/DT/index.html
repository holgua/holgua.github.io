About Dynatrace

Dynatrace sells software that watches other software. Its customers — almost exclusively large enterprises — run their businesses on complex clouds of interconnected applications, databases, and microservices. When something slows down or breaks, finding the cause in a system with tens of thousands of moving parts is extremely difficult. Dynatrace's platform, called OneAgent, embeds itself automatically across a customer's entire technology infrastructure and watches everything. The telemetry it collects — traces of individual transactions, performance metrics, event logs — is stored in a proprietary data architecture called Grail and analysed in real time by an artificial intelligence engine that identifies the root cause of anomalies without human intervention.

The business model is a subscription: customers pay a recurring annual fee and get access to the platform. Dynatrace's newer licensing approach, called the Dynatrace Platform Subscription, lets customers commit a total annual spend and then draw it down across whichever modules they use, rather than buying each one separately. This has two effects. First, it removes friction from adding new capabilities — customers who want to add security monitoring or application performance tracking do not need a new procurement cycle, just more consumption. Second, it creates a natural floor on renewal: a customer who has embedded Dynatrace across their infrastructure and has years of proprietary telemetry stored in the Grail system faces a multi-year re-instrumentation project to leave. As of the most recent annual results, total annual recurring revenue had surpassed $2 billion, up 18% from the prior year, with revenue of $2.0 billion and net income of $163 million.

The Case for the Bet

The bet is that Dynatrace's switching costs and proprietary technology keep existing customers expanding their spending, new customers accelerate adoption, and free cash flow compounds ahead of revenue growth — while the share price today implies growth assumptions that are too conservative.

The conditions that must hold for the bet to work:

  1. Net revenue retention above 107%. Net revenue retention (the percentage by which existing customers expanded their annual recurring revenue year over year, where anything above 100% means organic growth) must stay above 107%. The thesis calls for a watch after one quarter below 107% and an exit after two consecutive quarters.

Latest reading: 110% for the year ending 2026-03-31, matching 2025 and slightly above 2024 (111%). Three consecutive years at or above 110%. The 107% threshold has not been approached.

  1. Annual recurring revenue growth above 13% in constant currency. Growth in annual recurring revenue must not fall below 13% in constant currency for two consecutive quarters.

Latest reading: 18% reported growth to $2,054 million as of 2026-03-31, up from 15% in the prior year. No separate constant-currency figure was disclosed in the annual filing; at 18% reported, the constant-currency equivalent is expected to remain comfortably above the 13% floor. No exit condition triggered.

  1. Free cash flow margin above 20%. The ratio of free cash flow (cash generated from operations minus spending on fixed assets) to total revenue must not fall below 20% for a full fiscal year.

Latest reading: 26.2% for the year ending 2026-03-31 — $529.5 million of free cash flow on $2,018 million of revenue, up from 25.3% the prior year. Comfortably above the 20% floor and expanding.

Latest reading: The annual filing does not disclose this figure numerically. The prior disclosed figure was 70% as of late 2025. The exit condition cannot be evaluated from this filing alone; watch the quarterly results.

Latest reading: No such sales disclosed as of 2026-03-31. The company repurchased $479 million of stock in fiscal 2026 and announced a new $1 billion authorisation with $849 million remaining.

What's Wobbling

Two drivers returned a mixed rather than a clean reading in the latest filing. Neither crosses a written exit condition.

Gross margin compressed modestly. Subscription gross margin fell one percentage point, from 86% to 85%, because higher infrastructure costs followed growing customer usage of the cloud-hosted platform. Total gross margin actually expanded from 81% to 82% over the same period, so the overall picture is positive. The subscription margin compression is a cost-of-scale pattern common in cloud software at this revenue level and not a pricing-power signal; continued pressure over multiple years would warrant a closer look.

The competitive environment shifted. Palo Alto Networks' acquisition of Chronosphere — a specialist container-monitoring company that was named in the thesis as a potential platform for bundled displacement — is now confirmed in the annual filing. This was the anticipated preparatory move. The actual exit trigger requires Palo Alto or Cisco to announce publicly that they won named Dynatrace accounts. That disclosure does not exist in this filing. Separately, the company's own risk-factor language now explicitly names "vibe coding" and generic large-language-model tools as a competitive category. Net revenue retention of 110% is the primary counter-signal: customers are expanding spend while these alternatives have been accessible.

What Could Still Go Wrong

  • Net revenue retention slips. If net revenue retention declines toward 107% across two consecutive quarters — signalling that expansion revenue is no longer covering gross churn — the switching-cost thesis is weakening in a measurable way, and the exit rule fires.
  • Competitors win displacement accounts. Cisco/Splunk or Palo Alto/Chronosphere publicly announce they displaced Dynatrace at named enterprise customers. This is the most direct falsification of the moat argument.
  • AI substitution accelerates. General-purpose large-language models or "vibe coding" platforms replace the need for purpose-built observability as development teams build their own monitoring from commodity components. This threat is structural and harder to measure quarter by quarter.
  • Free cash flow margin contracts. Infrastructure cost growth, expanding research and development spending, or intensified sales competition could drag the free cash flow margin below 20% for a full fiscal year — the hard exit threshold.
  • Share-based compensation stays elevated. Compensation paid in stock to employees ($300 million in fiscal 2026, 15% of revenue) is a real economic cost. If revenue growth slows while this line stays flat or grows, the gap between reported free cash flow and what accrues to shareholders widens.

The Rules

The rules that govern what happens at each combination of conditions and price:

  • If the business conditions continue working as written — net revenue retention at or above 107%, annual recurring revenue growth above 13%, free cash flow margin above 20%, no open-market insider sales, no confirmed customer displacement — then maintain the full position and continue monitoring the quarterly metrics. Consider adding modestly on dips below $38 while net revenue retention holds.
  • If the conditions are weakening — net revenue retention declining toward 107%, annual recurring revenue growth decelerating toward 14%, or platform subscription penetration stalling below 72% for one quarter — then reduce to a half position within 30 days of the trigger. Monitor for two additional quarters before deciding whether to exit fully or rebuild.
  • If any written exit condition is confirmed — net revenue retention below 107% for two consecutive quarters, annual recurring revenue growth below 13% for two consecutive quarters, free cash flow margin below 20% for a full fiscal year, confirmed open-market sales by the chief executive or chief financial officer above $5 million, or named customer displacement by Cisco/Splunk or Palo Alto/Chronosphere — then exit the full position within one quarter. Do not average down on a broken thesis.
  • If the share price falls to or below $31 — the owner earnings yield above 5.9%, the long-run return estimate approximately 22% a year — then accumulate toward full position size, provided the conditions above are still working.
  • If the share price rises to or above $55 — the owner earnings yield below 3.3% — then trim toward a half-to-two-thirds position to harvest the premium while keeping the core holding. Do not exit fully unless the thesis is simultaneously breaking.
