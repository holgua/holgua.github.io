Elastic: The Bet Still Works

The Bottom Line

The bet is working — every one of the eight conditions tracked in the latest full-year filing came back confirming or silent, with no exit signal triggered. The share price sits in the lower half of the hold band, deeply below fair value; the long-run expected return is about 20% a year, which represents an exceptional equity premium over the risk-free rate. The one condition worth watching closely is the stock-based compensation ratio, which just edged below its exit ceiling for the first time in two years.

Recent Development

The full-year filing for the twelve months ending 2026-04-30 confirmed that the business is performing in line with the central scenario. The most significant data point was the net expansion rate — the measure of how much existing customers grow their spending year over year — coming in at 112%, comfortably above the 105% level at which the exit rule triggers. A rate above 100% means that customers are spending more each year even before new customers are counted; at 112%, the embedded base is compounding meaningfully.

The revenue mix continued its planned shift toward cloud delivery. Cloud subscriptions now account for 48% of total revenue, up from 46% the prior year and 43% the year before that. The company generated $321.8 million in free cash flow (operating cash receipts minus capital spending) on $1.74 billion of revenue — an 18.5% cash conversion rate, well above the 14% floor in the exit conditions. Management guided the following year toward roughly $2 billion in revenue and a 21.5% free-cash-flow rate.

The one area requiring continued monitoring is the stock-based compensation ratio. Compensation paid in the form of new shares rather than cash represented 17.73% of revenue in the year just filed, down from 18.19% the prior year. The exit condition triggers if this ratio exceeds 18% for two consecutive fiscal years; the prior year crossed that line, but the current year pulled back below it, breaking the consecutive streak. The margin is thin enough that a single bad year would restart the clock. For a fuller account of all eight conditions, see The Conditions Today.

About Elastic

Elastic operates a software platform built around Elasticsearch, a search and analytics engine first published as open-source code in 2010. The platform serves three categories of corporate customer: those who need to search across large collections of documents and data (the Search and AI segment), those who need to monitor the health of their software systems in real time (Observability), and those who need to detect and investigate security threats from machine-generated data (Security). All three use cases run on the same underlying technology stack — data is indexed once and queried from any of the three angles.

The business earns its revenue primarily from subscriptions. Customers can run Elastic software on their own servers under a self-managed licence, or pay to run it through Elastic's own cloud infrastructure. The cloud route, branded as Elastic Cloud, is now approaching half of total revenue. Gross margins — the share of each sales dollar remaining after the direct cost of delivering the software — sit at 76%, typical for enterprise software businesses where most of the cost is in the initial engineering rather than in incremental delivery.

The Case for the Bet

The bet is that Elastic has built a platform that is expensive to remove once it is embedded in a company's production operations, and that this stickiness will compound over time as customers adopt more cloud capacity and as artificial intelligence workloads create new demand for the kind of hybrid search — combining classical text relevance scoring with modern neural-network vector similarity — that Elastic's technology supports.

For the bet to keep working, seven conditions must continue to hold:

  1. Customer expansion rate above 105%. The net expansion rate must not fall below 105% for two consecutive quarters. A sustained reading below that level would signal that the embedded pipelines are not deepening but fraying.

  2. Cloud revenue growing above 10% per year. If full-year cloud revenue growth falls below 10%, it would suggest the open-source fork of Elasticsearch's core engine has successfully commoditised the platform and that the cloud transition story is over.

  3. Free cash flow margin above 14%. Free cash flow (operating cash receipts less capital spending) must not fall below 14% of revenue on a full-year basis. Below that floor, the capital-light character of the business model is gone.

  4. Stock-based compensation below 18% for two consecutive years. The compensation ratio must not exceed 18% of revenue for two consecutive fiscal years. If it does, it signals that per-share economics are being permanently diluted by new share issuance regardless of the headline cash figures.

  5. Acquisitions within disciplined limits. Any acquisition exceeding $500 million in purchase price must carry a disclosed plan showing the deal will generate positive incremental cash flow within three years, priced at no more than eight times the target's last-twelve-months revenue. An acquisition outside these limits without the required disclosure signals that capital is being deployed speculatively.

  6. Positive operating profit by 2028. The company must reach an operating profit on an accounting basis no later than the fiscal year ending in 2028. The accounting figure has been improving: −3.7% of revenue in the prior year, −1.9% in the year just filed. Failure to cross into positive territory on the current trajectory would invalidate the profitability story.

  7. No structural collapse in competitive position. Datadog — the primary observability competitor — must not outgrow Elastic by more than 15 percentage points per year for two consecutive fiscal years while simultaneously Elastic loses its top-tier rating in the Gartner observability analyst report. Both conditions must be true simultaneously; either alone does not trigger the exit.

The Conditions Today

Customer expansion rate (condition 1). The rate came in at 112% for the year ending 2026-04-30 — 7 percentage points above the 105% trigger. The number of customers spending more than $100,000 per year grew from over 1,510 to over 1,720; customers spending more than $1 million per year grew to over 240. The switching-cost story is holding.

Cloud revenue growth (condition 2). Cloud revenue grew 22% in the full year, with the annual cloud segment — customers on long-term cloud commitments — growing 28%. The 10% exit floor was not approached. The forward pipeline is supportive: remaining performance obligations (the total value of signed contracts not yet recognised as revenue) grew 28% to just under $2 billion, with about 61% of that scheduled to convert to revenue in the next twelve months.

Free cash flow margin (condition 3). Operating cash receipts were $326.9 million; capital spending was $5.1 million; free cash flow was $321.8 million on $1.74 billion of revenue, equalling 18.5%. The 14% floor was not approached.

Stock-based compensation (condition 4). Total stock-based compensation and related employer taxes were $308.2 million, or 17.73% of revenue. The prior year stood at 18.19%, which exceeded the 18% threshold. The current year at 17.73% breaks the consecutive sequence — the exit condition requires two years in a row above 18%, and the streak now resets to zero. The multi-year trend (20% two years ago, 18.2% last year, 17.7% this year) is moving in the right direction, but the margin is thin.

Acquisition discipline (condition 5). The only acquisition in the year was an AI embedding model company acquired for $36.8 million — well below the $500 million threshold.

Path to positive operating profit (condition 6). The accounting operating loss narrowed from $54.9 million (−3.7% of revenue) to $33.5 million (−1.9% of revenue), an improvement of 39% in absolute terms. At roughly 1.8 percentage points of improvement per year, the 2028 target is achievable on the current trajectory.

Competitive position (condition 7). The annual filing names Datadog as a primary observability competitor but does not disclose comparative revenue figures or analyst rankings. Exit condition 7 requires data from Datadog's own filings and third-party analyst reports, which cannot be assessed from the Elastic filing alone. The condition is unresolved from this data cycle — no exit signal was triggered, but no confirmation is available either.

The Share Price

At $58.78, the share sits in the lower half of its hold band — below the fair-value anchor of $101 but above the $56 price at which the buy rule arms. The current cash-flow yield (cash generated divided by market value) is 5.2%, against a fair-value yield of 3.0%. In plainer terms: the share is priced as though the business generates meaningfully more cash per dollar of value than it would at fair value — which is another way of saying it is trading at a discount to what the central scenario implies the company is worth.

The discount is substantial: roughly 42% below the fair-value anchor derived from a discounted future cash flow model that assumes 15% annual cash-flow growth over five years. The bear-case scenario in that model — slower growth driven by competitive pressure from the open-source fork — would put the per-share value around $29, which is below the current price. That is the risk that keeps the verdict at hold-and-watch rather than add: the downside scenario destroys capital, not just returns.

What's Wobbling

The stock-based compensation edge. The compensation ratio at 17.73% is declining but close to the 18% ceiling. One fiscal year — the year ending 2025-04-30 — logged 18.19%, crossing the threshold. The year just filed reversed the trend, but a single year of reversal does not give much runway. If the ratio creeps back above 18% in the year ending 2027-04-30, the consecutive-year clock restarts. This is not an exit trigger yet — it is the condition to watch most closely at each annual filing.

The valuation model split. Two separate valuation frameworks are in use for this holding and they produce very different central estimates. The discounted cash-flow model anchors fair value at $101 per share. A separate peer-based model — comparing Elastic's market multiples to those of its main competitors — produces a central estimate roughly two to three times higher. The discounted cash-flow estimate is the one used to set the buy, trim, and alert prices. The divergence is not a sign of a data error; the two models measure different things and are calibrated on different inputs. But a reader who encounters both figures and tries to reconcile them will find no simple answer. This is flagged in Gaps in the Picture.

The competitive position blind spot. The seventh exit condition — Datadog outgrowing Elastic by more than 15 percentage points for two consecutive years while Elastic loses its top-tier analyst rating — cannot be assessed from Elastic's own filings. It requires data from a competitor's filings and from third-party research that is not part of the standard data cycle. The condition is therefore always flagged as unresolved at annual review. It is not a wobble in the sense that something has deteriorated — it is a structural gap in the monitoring coverage.

What Could Still Go Wrong

  • The open-source fork accelerates. The fork of Elasticsearch's core engine, maintained under a non-commercial open-source licence and now developed by over 1,400 contributors under a foundation structure, has grown significantly. If the fork reaches feature parity with Elastic's commercial version on enough dimensions that price-sensitive customers stop renewing self-managed licences, revenue growth would decelerate sharply and the cloud transition would need to accelerate just to compensate for the loss.

  • Bear-case cash-flow growth scenario. If free cash flow grows at 9% annually rather than the 15% central case — which could happen if cloud growth decelerates and compensation expense stays elevated — the discounted value of the business would fall to around $29 per share at current prices and a 10% cost of capital. That represents a loss of roughly half the current price and would constitute permanent capital destruction, not a recoverable drawdown.

  • Stock-based compensation stays structurally high. If compensation paid in new shares does not converge toward 12–14% of revenue by 2029, per-share earnings growth will lag aggregate cash-flow growth. The company could generate substantial operating cash while individual shares barely increase in value, because the share count keeps growing.

  • Cloud hosting commitment inflexibility. Elastic has committed to $613.6 million of cloud hosting costs over future periods. If revenue growth slows sharply, those fixed costs become a drag on cash-flow margins rather than a manageable variable expense. This would compress free cash flow margins at the worst possible time.

  • Structural competitive displacement in observability. The observability monitoring market is dominated by Datadog, which is growing faster and has greater scale. If Datadog's growth advantage over Elastic widens to more than 15 percentage points per year for two consecutive years, and if Elastic's analyst positioning simultaneously weakens, the seventh exit condition fires. That scenario would imply structural displacement in what is currently Elastic's largest revenue segment.

Gaps in the Picture

Two fair-value anchors coexist. The buy, trim, and alert prices are derived from a discounted cash-flow model that implies a fair value of $101 per share. A separate scenario analysis based on peer and self multiples produces a central estimate of $244 per share. These are different methodologies measuring different things, and the gap is wide. The discounted cash-flow figure is more conservative and is used as the operational anchor for standing orders. The peer-multiple figure is noted for context. A future schema migration will resolve the divergence.

Risk-free rate sourced from proxy. The hurdle rate for this holding's scoring uses the ten-year risk-free rate read from a proxy file rather than a dedicated per-holding data point. The rate used reflects a snapshot from 2026-07-03.

Competitive position (condition 7) requires external data. The Datadog revenue growth gap and the Gartner observability analyst ranking cannot be sourced from Elastic's own filings. Each annual review cycle leaves condition 7 unresolved pending external data that is not automatically pulled into the standard refresh process.

Fair-value model drift expected and by design. The validation system flags a significant divergence between the calibrated fair-value yield anchor (3.04%) and the current cash-flow yield (5.22%). This is not a miscalibration — it confirms that the share is trading below the price implied by fair value, which is the expected condition for a hold recommendation. The flag is documented and expected.

The Rules

The conditions and share-price position together determine which rule applies:

  • If all conditions are working and no exit signal has fired: hold-and-watch at the current price. The cash-flow yield at $58.78 is approaching but has not yet reached the 5.50% level at which the add rule triggers. Do not add until the yield clears that line; monitor the net expansion rate and cloud revenue growth every quarter and the compensation ratio at each annual filing.

  • If one or more conditions show early deterioration but no exit threshold is crossed: do not add. If the net expansion rate drops toward 107% or cloud revenue growth decelerates below 15% for two consecutive quarters, reassess at the next validation. Require two consecutive quarters of deterioration before acting — a single quarter is insufficient to confirm a structural break.

  • If any written exit condition is triggered: exit the full position within thirty days. The exit conditions are: net expansion rate below 105% for two consecutive quarters; cloud revenue growth below 10% for a full fiscal year; free cash flow margin below 14% on a full-year basis; stock-based compensation above 18% for two consecutive fiscal years; any acquisition exceeding $500 million at more than eight times last-twelve-months revenue without a disclosed accretion plan within three years; positive operating profit not reached by the 2028 fiscal year; or Datadog's revenue growth exceeding Elastic's by more than 15 percentage points for two consecutive fiscal years while Elastic simultaneously falls below the top tier in the Gartner observability ranking. No averaging down after a thesis exit.

  • If the share price falls to the buy edge (yield above 5.50%, around $55 per share on the current earnings base): confirm no exit condition has been triggered. If intact, add in tranches. Do not deploy more than half a target position in a single add given the severity of the bear-case scenario.

  • If the share price rises to the trim edge (yield below 2.20%, around $140 per share on the current earnings base): trim 25% of the position to harvest the premium while keeping the core holding for continued compounding.

  • If the share price rises to the exit level (yield below 1.50%, around $200 per share on the current earnings base): exit the entire remaining position. At that price, the share is pricing in the bull-case scenario in full; the implied forward return falls below the long-term equity premium even before accounting for growth, and reallocation is the superior option. No re-entry within ninety days to remove the anchoring effect of the recent sale price.
