About the Bond

The Bund Linker 2033 is a debt obligation of the Federal Republic of Germany — the sovereign issuer, not a corporate borrower or a fund. It was issued in February 2021, matures on 2033-04-15, and carries a stated coupon of 0.10% a year. Coupon payments and repayment of principal are not fixed in nominal euros: they are linked to the German Harmonised Index of Consumer Prices excluding tobacco (a standard Eurostat measure of inflation across the euro area). As the index rises, the bond's effective cash flows rise with it, preserving the real purchasing power of the holding regardless of how much consumer prices have moved since issuance.

The issuance volume is €10.65 billion, making this one of the larger benchmark linker issues in the German government bond programme. At settlement, a buyer does not simply pay the quoted clean price of €94.91 per €100 of original face value. The €94.91 is expressed in real terms — the same purchasing-power units as the bond's issuance date. To arrive at the actual euros that change hands, multiply by the index ratio (1.26359), then add accrued interest; the resulting dirty price is €119.965 per €100 original face value. The index ratio tracks precisely how much the HICP ex-tobacco index has risen since 2021-02-09.

The Case for the Bet

The holding rests on a structural argument: a AAA-rated sovereign bond whose cashflows are contractually linked to consumer-price inflation is the cleanest available hedge against inflation in a euro-denominated portfolio. The bet holds as long as three exit conditions remain unmet:

  1. Real yield at the current market price stays above the exit floor of −1.0%. The bond is entered as an inflation hedge, not a rate trade. If the market price rises so high that the bond's real yield-to-maturity falls below −1.0% for two consecutive quarters, and EUR cash or nominal Bunds simultaneously offer a better after-tax real return, the hedge benefit is outweighed by the guaranteed real loss built into the price paid.

Latest reading: 0.88% real yield as of 2026-07-10. The compound exit trigger is inactive: zero days have been observed below the −1.0% floor, and the linker leads EUR cash by 62.98 basis points.

  1. Germany retains its top-tier sovereign credit rating. The case for a near-zero credit-risk holding dissolves if two or more of the three major credit-rating agencies (Fitch, Moody's, Standard & Poor's) downgrade Germany below AA-, signalling a structural shift in the Federal Republic's creditworthiness.

Latest reading: Germany holds AAA from Fitch, Aaa from Moody's, and AAA from Standard & Poor's, confirmed as of 2026-06-15. Exit condition (2) is not triggered.

  1. The HICP ex-tobacco index continues to reflect realised consumer-price inflation. The inflation-protection function depends on the index remaining a faithful measure of consumer prices. A material Eurostat restructuring of the index methodology — for example, substituting a different reference measure by formal decision — would sever that link.

Latest reading: No pending restructuring of the HICP ex-tobacco methodology per the most recent check (2026-06-15). Exit condition (3) is not triggered.

What Could Still Go Wrong

Three paths lead to an exit:

  • Sustained price appreciation eliminating the real yield. If the market bid for inflation protection pushes the clean price above €104 and the real yield drops below −0.50%, the trim ladder engages. If the price reaches €108 and 90 days below the −1.0% floor have elapsed while EUR cash offers a better deal, the exit fires. A sharp ECB rate-cutting cycle combined with resurgent inflation expectations would be the likeliest route.
  • Germany's credit rating falls. A two-agency downgrade below AA- would trigger exit condition (2). While Germany has not been at material risk of this in decades, the condition exists because the entire investment case rests on effective zero credit risk; any confirmed impairment of that changes the instrument's character.
  • HICP index methodology is changed. If Eurostat formally restructures the HICP ex-tobacco index in a way that severs its link to realised consumer-price inflation — for example by substituting a different price measure — exit condition (3) fires. This is a low-probability tail event but is tracked because it would silently break the hedge without affecting the bond's nominal cash flows.

The Rules

The pre-committed rules for this holding are as follows:

  • If the thesis is confirmed — all three exit conditions unmet, real yield within the −0.50%/1.50% band — then hold. Real yield is within band, German credit is intact, and HICP linkage provides inflation protection as intended.
  • If the thesis is weakened — a driver raises concern but no exit condition has fired — then do not add. Re-assess if real yield moves below 0% but has not yet triggered exit condition (1); compare real after-tax return against EUR cash and nominal Bunds before adding.
  • If the thesis is broken — one or more exit conditions trigger — then exit. Move proceeds to nominal short-duration German government bonds or EUR cash.
  • If the price is below the buy band (real yield above 1.50%, clean price at or below €91) — add to the position. A real yield above 1.5% on a 7-year AAA EUR sovereign linker is highly attractive; scale in proportionally to the inflation-hedge allocation target.
  • If the price is above the trim band (real yield below −0.50%, clean price at or above €104) — trim 25% on first crossing of that level, then a second 25% if the real yield remains below −0.50% at the next quarterly review (at least 90 calendar days later). Park proceeds in EUR cash or short conventional German government bonds. The 25 + 25 ladder is bounded — further crossings do not trigger additional trims until exit condition (1) fires.
  • If the price is above the exit band (compound trigger: real yield below −1.0% for at least 90 days AND EUR cash now offers a superior real return) — exit. Switch proceeds to EUR cash or short-duration nominal German government bonds; the inflation-hedge function is no longer additive versus the alternative.
