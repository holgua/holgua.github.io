SP500: The Bet Is Broken

The Bottom Line

The rules governing this fund have flagged an exit condition. The vehicle-integrity check — which monitors whether the fund's share price deviates meaningfully from the value of the underlying basket — has tripped a breach: the recorded deviation is 0.243%, against a maximum of 0.02%. For the world's most liquid equity fund, a deviation of that size almost certainly reflects a stale closing figure from the data provider rather than a genuine pricing gap. But the rules fire on the data they receive, not on what the data probably means, and the framework calls exit.

A second signal compounds the picture. The basket's earnings yield — the share of each dollar of share price that the basket's 500 companies collectively earn — stands at 3.71%, which is below the 4.0% threshold at which the rules say to trim. The fund is expensive on both the absolute and relative measures simultaneously. The condition most worth watching is the equity risk premium: 10-year Treasury bonds currently yield more than the basket earns, a reversal that the rules require to persist for two consecutive quarters before an independent exit triggers. This is only the first quarter on record.

One important caveat runs through every row below: the breach is almost certainly a data artefact — see What's Wobbling for the full explanation — and verifying the actual end-of-day net asset value before acting is the first step regardless of cost basis.

About the Fund

The SPDR S&P 500 ETF Trust, issued by State Street and traded under the ticker SPY, is the oldest and largest equity exchange-traded fund in the United States. It holds shares in all 500 companies that make up the S&P 500 index — the benchmark of the 500 largest publicly traded US businesses by market value — in the same proportions as the index. Owning the fund is equivalent, in economic terms, to owning a very thin slice of every one of those 500 companies at once.

The fund uses the unit investment trust structure, a legal wrapper dating to 1993, which differs in minor operational ways from the more common open-end fund wrapper used by its later competitors IVV and VOO. It does not issue a K-1 tax form. The fund's annual cost is 0.0945% of assets — roughly 95 cents on every thousand dollars invested. At roughly $784 billion in assets under management, it is the most traded equity vehicle on earth; daily volume regularly exceeds $48 billion. The securities-lending income that some funds earn to offset expenses is not disclosed in the data available here, so the net cost figure cannot be reduced further than the stated expense ratio. Tracking difference and tracking error statistics versus the underlying index were not available in the current data feed.

The 10 largest holdings account for roughly 39% of the fund's value. NVIDIA tops the list at 7.89% of the fund, followed by Apple at 7.05% and Alphabet at 6.11%. The technology sector as a whole accounts for 39% of the basket — a structural feature of the market-cap-weighted index at this point in time, not a deliberate tilt.

Tax. The fund is structured as a US unit investment trust. No K-1 is issued. Capital gain distribution history is not verified in the current data; the unit investment trust structure typically produces zero capital gain distributions because redemptions are handled in-kind, but this has not been confirmed against State Street's most recent annual report.

The Case for the Bet

The bet behind holding this fund is not that the S&P 500 will always go up. It is narrower than that: over the holding period, the basket's earnings yield — the total annual earnings of all 500 companies divided by the current price of the fund — should exceed the yield on 10-year US Treasury bonds by at least 2 percentage points. That margin is the minimum compensation for accepting equity risk rather than locking in a government-guaranteed return. When the margin holds, the basket clears its hurdle. When it fails, the equity case has collapsed.

Six numbered conditions must all hold for the bet to remain valid:

  1. The equity risk premium — basket earnings yield minus the 10-year Treasury yield — must not be negative for two consecutive quarters. A single negative quarter puts the condition on watch; a second consecutive negative reading fires exit condition (1). (Current reading: −0.72%. First quarter on record negative. Not yet triggered.)

  2. State Street must not raise the annual expense ratio above 0.15% without a fee-competitive response. (Current: 0.0945%; floor $800B above the exit level.)

  3. Assets under management must remain above $100 billion. An extraordinary outflow event below that level would impair liquidity to the point where the fund no longer serves as efficient broad-market exposure. (Current: ~$784 billion; early-warning flag below $300 billion.)

  4. The largest single holding must not exceed 13% of the fund. Beyond that level, the fund would be behaving more like a concentrated bet on one company than a broad-market proxy. (Current: NVIDIA at 7.89%; early-warning flag above 10%.)

  5. The S&P 500 index methodology must not be materially restructured by its governing body, S&P Dow Jones Indices, in a way that undermines the broad-market mandate.

  6. The median return on equity of the top-10 holdings, after deduplicating share classes, must not fall below 15%. A sustained collapse would signal that the basket's most influential businesses had lost their quality edge. (Current: 37.28%.)

The Rules

The pre-committed action plan translates the six conditions and the earnings-yield position into five rules:

  • If all conditions are met and the earnings yield is within the normal range: hold the position. The basket is reasonably priced and the vehicle is sound.
  • If any condition is weakening but not yet broken: do not add. Re-check after the following quarter. If the equity risk premium stays negative or the quality of the top-10 holdings keeps deteriorating, move to an alternative fund (such as IVV or VOO) or to cash.
  • If a vehicle-integrity condition is breached or an exit condition triggers: exit. Move proceeds to risk-free instruments or to a holding that meets the value test.
  • If the basket earnings yield rises above 6.25% — meaning the fund has become cheap by the long-run historical standard — add to the position. The basket is meaningfully cheap relative to risk-free alternatives.
  • If the basket earnings yield falls below 4.0% — meaning the fund has become expensive — trim 25–33% of the position. Harvest the premium while keeping the core holding intact.

The Conditions Today

Equity risk premium. The basket currently earns 3.71 cents per dollar of price (an earnings yield of 3.71%). The 10-year US Treasury bond yields 4.43%. The gap — the premium that equity owners earn above the risk-free rate — is therefore −0.72 percentage points. It is negative. Exit condition (1) requires two consecutive quarters of negative equity risk premium; this is the first. The direction of travel is adverse and the condition is on watch, but the exit trigger has not yet fired.

Look-through quality. The median return on equity (the annual profit earned as a percentage of the company's book value) of the nine deduped top-10 holdings is 37.28% — more than double the 15% floor set by the thesis and well above the 20% line for high-quality compounders. Quality is intact. NVIDIA earns 114% on equity and Apple 141%, numbers that reflect aggressive share buybacks reducing book value rather than extraordinary profitability in the conventional sense; the median figure, dominated by the middle of the distribution, is the more reliable read.

Annual expense ratio. The fund charges 0.0945% per year, well clear of both the 0.12% early-warning threshold and the 0.15% hard ceiling. No concern.

Top-1 concentration. NVIDIA represents 7.89% of the fund, below the 10% early-warning level and meaningfully below the 13% ceiling that would indicate a regime-change in the index's concentration. No concern.

Assets under management and liquidity. The fund holds roughly $784 billion in assets. The early-warning threshold is $300 billion; the exit floor is $100 billion. At current scale, this condition is not in the neighbourhood of any concern.

Index methodology stability. There is no automated signal available for detecting changes to the S&P 500 index methodology or the State Street trust structure. This condition is monitored qualitatively; no breach has been flagged.

The Share Price

The basket earns 3.71 cents for every dollar the fund costs today. The rules treat a 5.00% earnings yield as fair value — the equivalent of a price-to-earnings ratio of 20×, sitting between the strict historical cheap line and the current expensive band. At a 5.00% yield, the fund would be worth around $558. Today's price of $752 represents a 35% premium above that fair-value anchor.

The trim boundary sits at a 4.00% earnings yield — roughly $698 — and the current yield of 3.71% is already through that line. In plain terms, the fund is priced for earnings growth and stable or rising multiples; there is no cushion from a depressed starting valuation, and the basket is earning less per dollar of price than a 10-year government bond currently pays.

The buy edge — the point at which the earnings yield would reach the long-run historical average of 6.25%, implying a price-to-earnings ratio near 16× — sits at $447, roughly 41% below today's price.

What's Wobbling

NAV deviation breach (primary trigger). The fund's reported share price on 2026-06-16 was $752.48, while the reported net asset value — the per-share value of the underlying basket — was $754.31. The gap is 0.243%, against the 0.02% maximum in the vehicle-integrity rules. For almost any fund this would be a meaningful signal. For SPY — a fund with $784 billion in assets and average daily trading volume above $48 billion — a 24-basis-point persistent deviation from net asset value is economically implausible. The fund's arbitrage mechanism, by which authorised participants continuously align the share price with the underlying basket, operates continuously during trading hours and has done so reliably for over 30 years. The most probable explanation is that the data provider (yfinance) applied a stale prior-close net asset value against an intraday or closing price from a different timestamp. Before acting on the exit call, verify the end-of-day net asset value directly via State Street's published daily figure or a Bloomberg terminal. If the deviation is confirmed as a data artefact, re-running the validation with the corrected net asset value would likely clear the breach.

Equity risk premium. The equity risk premium is currently negative at −0.72 percentage points. This is the first quarter on record with a negative reading. Exit condition (1) requires two consecutive negative quarters; the trigger has not yet fired. However, the direction is clearly adverse. If the 10-year Treasury yield remains above 3.71% into the next quarter — which would require no meaningful change in either the basket's earnings or the yield curve — the persistence leg of exit condition (1) would be met, and exit would follow regardless of the net asset value situation.

Basket earnings yield. At 3.71%, the basket earnings yield is already through the 4.00% trim boundary. Even if the net asset value breach clears, the position would still call for a trim rather than a hold at current prices.

What Could Break the Fund

  • The equity risk premium remains negative for a second consecutive quarter. That alone triggers exit condition (1), independent of the net asset value question.
  • State Street raises the expense ratio, ending the fund's cost advantage over IVV and VOO. Even a move to 0.12% would cross the early-warning threshold; a move above 0.15% would trigger exit condition (2).
  • A single company — most plausibly NVIDIA, currently at 7.89% — continues to appreciate until it crosses 13% of the fund. At that level the fund would no longer function as broad-market exposure and exit condition (4) would fire.
  • The S&P Dow Jones Indices committee makes a structural change to the index — for example, capping sector weights, changing constituent eligibility, or restructuring the free-float methodology — that alters the fund's character as a broad-market proxy.
  • An extreme outflow event, triggered by a sustained market dislocation, drains assets below $300 billion (early warning) or $100 billion (exit). At $784 billion today, this remains a remote tail risk.

Gaps in the Picture

Net asset value figure. The primary data point driving the exit call — the 0.243% deviation of share price from net asset value — almost certainly reflects a stale-data artefact from the yfinance feed rather than a genuine pricing gap. The figure should be verified against State Street's published end-of-day net asset value before any action is taken.

Equity risk premium history. The rules require two consecutive quarters of negative equity risk premium to trigger exit condition (1). The current record contains only one entry, dated 2026-06-16 at −0.72%. The persistence leg cannot yet be assessed; a second reading is required.

Tracking difference and tracking error. Neither statistic versus the underlying S&P 500 index was available in the current data. These figures would be sourced from State Street's factsheet and would allow a more precise assessment of how closely the fund replicates index returns after costs.

Securities lending revenue. State Street may earn revenue by lending fund holdings to short-sellers, which some funds pass through to reduce effective costs below the stated expense ratio. This figure was not available for SPY; net cost cannot be stated more precisely than the 0.0945% gross expense ratio.

Cyclically adjusted earnings. A cyclically adjusted valuation measure (analogous to the Shiller price-to-earnings ratio applied to the broader index) is not available through the current data feed for ETFs. The current analysis relies entirely on the trailing 12-month price-to-earnings ratio of 26.95×; a cyclically adjusted figure would give a more stable long-run valuation anchor.
