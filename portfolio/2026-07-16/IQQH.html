About Clean Energy

The fund tracks the S&P Global Clean Energy Transition Index, a basket of roughly 100 companies spanning solar, wind, grid infrastructure, electrification, and the hardware and software that enables the shift away from fossil fuels. The index is deliberately thematic — it is not a broad equity market vehicle, and it does not try to be. About a third of the basket is in technology companies (solar inverters, energy-storage hardware, smart-grid software), a third in regulated utilities with renewable-energy programmes, and a third in industrial manufacturers (wind turbines, grid equipment). The geographic reach is global, with significant weights in the United States, Europe, and parts of Asia.

Because the index concentrates by design across three sectors that are sensitive to interest rates, government policy, and industrial cycles, the fund behaves more like a sector fund than an all-market one. Its earnings profile is structurally lower than that of a broad index — utilities and infrastructure businesses earn modest returns on equity, and the basket includes early-stage companies that are loss-making. That structural characteristic is built into the thesis, not a reason for concern in isolation.

Annual cost: 0.65% of assets each year. Tracking difference and tracking error against the index are not currently available from published sources; replication fidelity cannot be assessed until those figures are pulled.

The Case for the Bet

The bet is that global decarbonisation spending — driven by regulatory mandates, falling renewable energy costs, and persistent industrial capital redirection — will generate sustained earnings growth for the companies in this basket over a ten-year horizon. Six conditions must hold for the position to remain valid:

  1. Earnings-yield trap absent. The primary valuation-derived exit fires only when the basket's earnings yield rises above 8.0% for four consecutive quarters while the index is also recording capital losses. That double condition signals constituent-fundamentals collapse, not an attractive buying point.

Latest reading: basket earnings yield 4.59% as of 2026-07-12, more than three percentage points below the 8.0% trigger. The equity premium over the 10-year government bond yield stands at +0.02 percentage points — positive but close to zero. Eight consecutive snapshots have been positive; the persistence leg for exit condition (1) is not met.

  1. Vehicle-integrity intact. The fund's annual charge must stay below 0.80%, assets under management must remain above €200 million, and no single constituent may exceed 20.0% of the basket. Any breach, or a product wind-down notice from iShares, triggers exit condition (2).

Latest reading: annual charge 0.65% (15 basis points of headroom to the ceiling, as of 2026-07-12); assets under management approximately €4.17 billion (roughly 21 times the minimum floor); largest constituent Bloom Energy at 14.76% (5.24 percentage points below the cap). No breach; all three integrity metrics are clear.

  1. Index methodology unchanged. If S&P Dow Jones Indices materially alters the Clean Energy Transition Index — changing the sector scope, constituent criteria, or weighting scheme — the economic exposure this position was designed to capture changes with it. That is exit condition (3).

Latest reading: no automated signal for a methodology change as of 2026-07-12. The fund family (BlackRock Asset Management Ireland) and legal structure (UCITS exchange-traded fund) are unchanged. Monitoring requires external subscription to S&P index governance notifications; the automated pipeline carries no signal on this driver.

  1. Policy tailwind intact. The thesis rests on regulatory mandates — specifically the EU Green Deal and US Inflation Reduction Act clean-energy tax credits — remaining in force. A durable, legislated repeal of either (not a temporary pause) removes the regulatory underpinning and fires exit condition (4).

Latest reading: no legislated repeal as of 2026-07-12. Both programmes remain formally in force, though political headwinds in the US are an acknowledged risk. The pipeline carries no automated signal on this driver.

  1. Structural cost advantage of renewables persists. If solar and wind power cease to undercut fossil-fuel incumbents on the cost of generating electricity in the majority of geographies, and if capacity additions flatline for two consecutive years, the primary economic driver of the thesis is gone — exit condition (5).

Latest reading: no automated signal for a reversal in renewable cost trends as of 2026-07-12. This condition is monitored externally.

  1. No superior replacement vehicle available. If a lower-fee, broader, or higher-quality clean-energy fund with equivalent regulatory-jurisdiction exposure becomes available and the switching cost is justified, exit condition (6) applies.

Latest reading: no superior replacement identified as of 2026-07-12.

What's Wobbling

The only non-confirming driver in the 2026-07-12 snapshot is the equity premium — the gap between the basket's earnings yield and the 10-year government bond yield. At +0.02 percentage points, it is positive, but only barely. The 10-year Treasury yield has risen to 4.57%, closing most of the gap to the basket's 4.59% earnings yield. That makes the compensation for holding the basket instead of a risk-free bond close to nil on this metric.

This wobble does not cross any of the lines set out in the Case for the Bet. The earnings-yield trap (exit condition 1) requires the basket earnings yield to exceed 8.0% — the current reading is 4.59%, far from that level. Exit condition (1) also requires persistent capital losses alongside that elevated yield; neither condition is met. A near-zero equity premium is worth monitoring at each refresh, but it is not an exit signal.

The trend in the last five snapshots is noteworthy: readings of +0.06%, +0.06%, +0.05%, +0.02%, +0.02% show a steady compression driven by rising government bond yields rather than falling basket earnings. If government bond yields continue climbing while the basket earnings yield stays flat, the premium could turn negative. That would still not trigger an exit under the current rules, but it would represent a deterioration of the thematic value case.

What Could Still Go Wrong

The fund-break scenarios fall into four categories:

  • Cost creep. The annual charge currently sits 15 basis points below the 0.80% ceiling. If iShares were to raise the charge — through a stated fee increase or through rising operational costs passed on at the next restructuring — the vehicle-integrity ceiling could be approached. A charge above 0.80% fires exit condition (2).

  • Asset outflows. The fund holds approximately €4.17 billion in assets, well above the €200 million floor. However, a sustained period of policy headwinds or sector underperformance could trigger large-scale redemptions. A prolonged outflow cycle that brought assets below €200 million would fire the same exit condition.

  • Index methodology change. The S&P Global Clean Energy Transition Index is maintained by a third party. A material change in what the index includes — shifting sector scope, altering constituent criteria, or changing the weighting scheme — could change what this fund actually owns without any action from iShares. That fires exit condition (3), and it is not detectable by automated monitoring.

  • Single-name concentration drift. Bloom Energy currently represents 14.76% of the basket. The S&P index rebalances quarterly, and between rebalances, price moves can push the top holding higher. A constituent reaching 20.0% or above fires exit condition (2).

(For this fund, exit fires when the share price reaches the exit edge, when a vehicle-integrity condition breaches, or when the named exit-condition trigger fires — any of three paths.)

The Rules

The pre-committed rules for this position:

  • If the thematic thesis remains intact and the vehicle is sound: hold. Do not add on the basis of the earnings-yield signal alone; add only if the decarbonisation capital-expenditure case is actively strengthening.
  • If the thesis shows signs of weakening — a driver is deteriorating but no exit condition has fired: do not add. Re-validate after the next quarter and after any material policy development. If exit conditions (3) through (5) are approaching, accelerate the review timeline.
  • If an exit condition has fired or a vehicle-integrity breach is confirmed: exit the full position. Move proceeds to a risk-free position or into a broad-market fund as replacement.
  • If the share price falls to the buy edge (€7.1) with the thematic thesis intact: add to the position, provided the basket earnings yield is below 8.0%. A basket earnings yield above 8.0% combined with capital losses at that price signals exit condition (1) — verify before adding.
  • If the share price rises to the trim edge (€11): trim 25–33% of the position. Move proceeds to a risk-free position or to a broad-market fund rather than holding uninvested within the thematic position.
  • If the share price rises to the exit edge (€15): exit the full position. The basket is fully priced relative to risk-free assets at that level. Redeploy proceeds to a risk-free position or to a holding that still meets the value test.

Scenario Definitions

A scenario is a joint assumption about two independent variables: operating trajectory (how the holding's underlying cash flows grow relative to their long-run trend) and exit multiple (what the market pays for those cash flows at the horizon, relative to a fair-value anchor).

Scenario Operating trajectory Exit multiple
Bear Below trend Below fair value
Base At trend At fair value
Bull Above trend At or above fair value

Non-negotiable rule: In the bear case, both dimensions must be adverse simultaneously — a scenario where only the multiple moves is a re-rating scenario, not a bear.

Bear cases are comparable in structure across all holding types (company, ETF, bond), but not in magnitude. A company bear reflects idiosyncratic execution failure; an ETF bear reflects a broad market drawdown. Different mitigations, different diversification implications.
