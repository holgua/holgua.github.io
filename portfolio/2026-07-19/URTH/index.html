About All World

All World is the iShares MSCI World exchange-traded fund, a passively managed basket that tracks the MSCI World Index — roughly 1,300 large- and mid-cap companies across 23 developed countries, weighted by their free-float market capitalisation. About 70% of the basket is in the United States; the remaining 30% spans Japan, the United Kingdom, France, Switzerland, Germany, Canada, and other developed markets. The fund holds no emerging-market exposure. In practical terms, it is a single-ticket way to own a slice of the large-company universe across the developed world, with the mix tilting automatically as market values shift.

The rationale for holding it alongside a US-focused index fund (the S&P 500 basket in the same portfolio) is diversification of regime risk: concentrating entirely in US equities exposes the portfolio to regulatory, political, and dollar-cycle events that affect all US companies simultaneously. Adding 30% ex-US developed exposure narrows that risk without sacrificing the quality character of the basket — the median profitability of the largest holdings (37.28% return on equity) confirms this is a basket of capital-efficient businesses, not a diluted average.

The annual running cost is 0.24% of the invested amount — 4 basis points above the functionally similar iShares MSCI World UCITS fund available in Europe at 0.20%. Tracking difference and tracking error against the index are not available in this snapshot; those figures would require the published iShares URTH factsheet.

The Case for the Bet

The bet is not that this basket is always worth buying — the fund itself was purchased knowing that the entry price was already on the expensive side. The bet is that, over a long holding period, the basket's look-through earnings yield will exceed the risk-free rate by at least two percentage points. The conditions that must keep holding:

  1. Equity risk premium positive and recovering toward 2%. The basket's earnings yield must exceed the 10-year US Treasury yield. The written exit condition fires if earnings yield stays below Treasury yield for two consecutive calendar quarters, regardless of the absolute level.

Latest reading: Basket earnings yield 4.14% vs. Treasury 4.54% as of 2026-07-19. Equity risk premium −0.40%. Fourteen consecutive negative snapshots since 2026-06-16; the second full calendar quarter of negative readings has not yet elapsed (Q3 2026 is ongoing).

  1. Expense ratio below 0.35%. BlackRock/iShares must not raise the running cost above the threshold. At 0.35% the cost advantage over the cheaper UCITS structure disappears without justification, signalling fee drift.

Latest reading: Expense ratio 0.24% as of 2026-07-19. Eleven basis points below the hard ceiling. No change flagged.

  1. Fund size above $2.0 billion. Below this level, bid-ask spreads on a global blend fund of this type typically widen materially, raising the effective cost of trading the position.

Latest reading: Assets under management $8.06 billion as of 2026-07-19 — just over four times the floor. Average daily trading volume approximately $222 million.

  1. Top-one holding weight below 10%. If a single company grows to represent 10% or more of the fund, that signals a structural regime shift in the index — not normal drift within a cap-weighted basket.

Latest reading: NVIDIA (the graphics-chip and AI-computing business) holds 5.17% of the fund as of 2026-07-19. 4.83 percentage points below the exit threshold.

  1. MSCI World index methodology unchanged. The developed-markets mandate, float-adjusted cap weighting, and 23-country scope must remain intact. A material restructuring — country reclassification, removal of major developed-market constituents, or a shift away from float-adjusted weighting — would undermine what is being held.

Latest reading: No automated signal exists for methodology changes in this pipeline. External monitoring is required for this condition; it remains on watch.

  1. Median profitability of the largest holdings above 15%. The median return on equity of the largest holdings (a measure of how efficiently the underlying businesses use the capital invested in them) must not fall below 15%. Below that threshold, the basket has lost its quality character as a compounding vehicle.

Latest reading: Median return on equity of the nine largest deduped holdings is 37.28% as of 2026-07-19, nearly 2.5 times the exit threshold. The average (weighted by fund weight) is a significantly higher 70%, but this figure is distorted by NVIDIA and Apple, whose buyback programmes have reduced their stated equity bases to near zero. The median is the load-bearing metric and shows no deterioration.

The Share Price

At $202, the basket trades at a price-to-earnings multiple of 24.1 — implying an earnings yield of 4.14%. The fair-value anchor for this fund is set at a 5% earnings yield (a multiple of 20), a level consistent with the long-run historical average for the S&P 500. On that basis, the current price sits meaningfully above fair value: $202 against a fair-value reading of $168.

The price chart shows this position clearly. The buy edge is at $134 (a 6.25% earnings yield — the historical average level at which stocks have historically been considered attractively priced relative to risk-free alternatives). The trim edge is at $210 (4% earnings yield — the level at which the basket starts to look fully priced). At $202, the fund is sitting approximately $8 below the trim line. That proximity, combined with the negative equity risk premium, is why the current reading is cautious rather than comfortable.

What's Wobbling

The single meaningful wobble is the equity risk premium. It has been negative without interruption since 2026-06-16, running from a least-negative reading of −0.11% to a most-negative reading of −0.48%. As of 2026-07-19 the reading is −0.40%. That is the widest sustained negative equity-risk-premium episode since the fund was first tracked.

The exit condition as written requires this to persist for two consecutive calendar quarters. The second quarter of 2026 closed with a negative reading; the third quarter of 2026 has opened with the same. If the reading remains negative through 2026-09-30, the exit condition will have completed. Nothing in the Case for the Bet has been crossed yet — but the mechanism is live.

A secondary, quieter wobble is price proximity to the trim edge. At $202, the basket is approximately $8 below the trim line of $210. The two wobbles point in the same direction: the basket is simultaneously priced more richly than its risk-adjusted return warrants and approaching the level at which the rules call for reducing the position. Neither wobble overrides the other; both are monitoring items until the quarterly re-validation determines whether exit condition one has completed.

What Could Still Go Wrong

  • The equity-risk-premium exit condition completes. If the earnings yield remains below the Treasury yield through the close of the third quarter of 2026 (2026-09-30), exit condition one fires and the rules call for closing the full position. This is the highest-probability near-term risk given fourteen consecutive negative readings.
  • The price crosses the trim edge before the equity-risk-premium resolves. A further rise in the basket's price-to-earnings multiple, even a small one, would push the earnings yield below 4% and trigger the trim order independently of the equity-risk-premium situation. Both conditions are live simultaneously.
  • A fee increase by BlackRock/iShares. URTH already runs 4 basis points above its functionally equivalent UCITS counterpart. A further fee hike above the 0.35% threshold would trigger condition two; the cheaper UCITS structure provides no rationale for the extra cost.
  • NVIDIA concentration accelerating. NVIDIA's weight in the fund has grown alongside its market value. At 5.17% today it is well within limits, but sustained outperformance by a single company could drive concentration toward the 10% exit threshold over several years.
  • A broad market de-rating. The bear scenario in the five-year return analysis projects a −20% annualised return — driven by both earnings contraction and multiple compression simultaneously. Given that the basket is already priced above its long-run fair-value earnings yield, the room for multiple expansion in a downturn is limited. (For this fund, exit fires when the share price reaches the exit edge, when a vehicle-integrity condition breaches, or when the named exit-condition trigger fires — any of three paths.)

The Rules

The rules that govern this holding are derived from the written thesis and are applied at each re-validation:

  • If the basket remains reasonably priced and the vehicle is sound (thesis confirmed), hold the position.
  • If the thesis is weakened — specifically, if the equity risk premium is negative or median look-through profitability is falling — do not add to the position. Re-validate after the next quarter. If the equity risk premium stays negative or quality keeps deteriorating, the rules call for swapping into the cheaper UCITS equivalent or moving to cash according to the position-sizing rule.
  • If the thesis is broken — vehicle-integrity condition breached or an exit condition triggered — exit the full position and move the proceeds to risk-free instruments or to a holding that currently meets the value test.
  • If the share price falls to or below the buy edge ($134) and the conditions are working, add to the position. The basket is meaningfully cheap relative to the risk-free rate at that level.
  • If the share price rises to or above the trim edge ($210), trim 25–33% of the position. The basket is meaningfully expensive relative to the risk-free rate at that level; the rules call for harvesting the premium.
  • If the share price rises to or above the exit edge ($280), exit the full position. At that level the basket earnings yield falls to 3%, which is fully priced versus risk-free and leaves no equity risk premium. Redeploy the proceeds to risk-free instruments or to a holding that meets the value test.

Scenario Definitions

A scenario is a joint assumption about two independent variables: operating trajectory (how the holding's underlying cash flows grow relative to their long-run trend) and exit multiple (what the market pays for those cash flows at the horizon, relative to a fair-value anchor).

Scenario Operating trajectory Exit multiple
Bear Below trend Below fair value
Base At trend At fair value
Bull Above trend At or above fair value

Non-negotiable rule: In the bear case, both dimensions must be adverse simultaneously — a scenario where only the multiple moves is a re-rating scenario, not a bear.

Bear cases are comparable in structure across all holding types (company, ETF, bond), but not in magnitude. A company bear reflects idiosyncratic execution failure; an ETF bear reflects a broad market drawdown. Different mitigations, different diversification implications.
