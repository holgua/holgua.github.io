About SP500

The SPDR S&P 500 ETF Trust (ticker: SPY) tracks the S&P 500 index — a market-capitalisation-weighted basket of roughly 500 of the largest publicly traded companies in the United States. SPY was the first exchange-traded fund listed in the US, launched in January 1993, and remains the most liquid equity fund in the world by average daily trading volume; roughly $44 billion in SPY shares changes hands on a typical day.

The fund uses a unit investment trust structure, one of the oldest legal forms available to US exchange-traded funds. The practical difference from more modern fund formats is modest: dividends collected from the underlying shares are held in a non-interest-bearing reserve between distribution dates rather than being reinvested intraday, creating a small cash drag. The annual cost is 0.0945%. The fund does not lend its securities.

The basket is heavily concentrated in technology companies, which account for approximately 39% of total assets. NVIDIA alone represents about 7.9% of the fund, and the top nine holdings together account for roughly 39% of total assets. The median return on equity of the top nine holdings — a measure of how efficiently the underlying businesses generate profit from shareholders' capital — stands at approximately 37%.

Annual cost: 0.0945% (source: yfinance). Tracking difference and tracking error against the S&P 500 index are not available from the data feed and have not been verified from the State Street SPDR factsheet.

The Case for the Bet

SPY is held as US large-cap passive core exposure. The idea is not that the S&P 500 is always a buy. The bet is narrower: over the holding period, the basket's earnings yield — the aggregate profit earned per dollar of share price across the 500 companies — must exceed the return on a risk-free 10-year government bond plus a reasonable premium of at least two percentage points. When that condition fails for two consecutive quarterly reviews, the economic rationale has broken down.

Six conditions govern the bet:

  1. The gap between the basket's earnings yield and the 10-year Treasury yield must not remain negative for two consecutive quarterly reviews. If it does, risk-free bonds are offering a better yield than the basket; the rationale for holding equities over bonds has failed.
  2. State Street's annual expense ratio must stay below 0.15% (current: 0.0945%). Above this level, cheaper alternatives — such as the iShares Core S&P 500 ETF and the Vanguard S&P 500 ETF — are available.
  3. Total fund assets must stay above $100 billion (current: approximately $784 billion). A sustained fall to this floor would signal an extraordinary liquidity event and require reassessment.
  4. No single holding's weight may exceed 13% (current top holding: NVIDIA at 7.9%). Concentration above this level signals a methodology change or extreme market distortion.
  5. The S&P 500 index methodology must remain substantively unchanged by its custodian, S&P Dow Jones Indices. Material restructuring — such as a shift to an equal-weighting scheme or a change in the qualifying universe — would change what the fund actually represents.
  6. The median return on equity of the deduped top ten holdings must remain above 15% (current: approximately 37%). A collapse below this floor signals that the basket's visible quality anchor has eroded.

- A second alert at $447, the cheap edge. A crossing arms the buy order below; the latest re-validation should confirm the conditions are still working before adding.

Standing orders (actionable).

- A standing trim order at $698. The rules call for trimming a quarter to a third of the position when the share price closes at or above this line, to harvest the premium while keeping the core holding.

Vehicle-integrity breaches (an expense ratio above the ceiling, total assets below the floor, or concentration above the hard cap) and the earnings-yield-gap trigger (two consecutive quarterly reviews negative) are independent additional paths to exit that do not require the share price to reach the exit order level.

What's Wobbling

Two signals are active simultaneously, and both point in the same direction.

On valuation, the basket's earnings yield of 3.808% has crossed the 4.0% trim threshold. The rules call for trimming when the yield falls below 4.0%; it has now done so.

On risk premium, the gap between the basket's earnings yield and the 10-year Treasury yield has been negative for five consecutive readings within the second quarter of 2026: −0.717% on 2026-06-16, −0.717% on 2026-06-17, −0.712% on 2026-06-21, −0.758% on 2026-06-23, and −0.594% on 2026-06-24. The most recent reading was the best of the five, driven entirely by a fall in Treasury yields rather than any improvement in basket earnings. The exit condition requires two consecutive quarterly reviews with a negative gap — this streak spans only one quarter and has not yet met that bar.

Neither signal has reached its exit threshold independently. Together they confirm that the basket is both expensive by its own standards and offers a worse yield than the risk-free alternative. The bet is wobbling rather than broken.

What Could Still Go Wrong

  • Earnings-yield gap persists into the next quarter. If the gap between the basket's earnings yield and the Treasury yield remains negative through the third-quarter re-validation, exit condition (1) fires. Five negative readings in one quarter is a meaningful start toward that outcome. An improvement in earnings or a sustained fall in Treasury yields could close the gap; neither is certain.
  • Multiple contraction without an earnings recovery. The basket currently trades at a trailing multiple of approximately 26.3 times earnings — above the 25-times ceiling at which the trim order is anchored and above all three terminal multiples modelled in the five-year scenarios (15, 20, and 25 times). Any re-rating toward the long-run historical average of roughly 16 times would produce significant losses even with steady underlying earnings growth.
  • Total-assets shock. An extreme market event or forced redemption pressure could in theory drive total assets below the $100 billion floor. At $784 billion currently, this scenario would require the fund to lose approximately 87% of its assets — highly unlikely but the tail risk of ETF-wrapper failure.
  • Expense-ratio creep. State Street has not announced fee changes and the current 0.0945% is far below the 0.12% early-warning level. Any increase would need to double before triggering a review.

(For this fund, exit fires when the share price reaches the exit edge, when a vehicle-integrity condition breaches, or when the named exit-condition trigger fires — any of three paths.)

The Rules

If the conditions remain intact and the price stays within the band: hold — the basket is reasonably priced and the vehicle is sound.

If any condition weakens without reaching the exit threshold: do not add. Re-validate after the next quarter. If the earnings-yield gap stays negative or the median look-through return on equity keeps falling, consider moving to a lower-cost S&P 500 alternative or to cash.

If a vehicle-integrity breach fires or exit condition (1) fully triggers: exit. Move proceeds to the risk-free alternative or to a holding that meets the value test.

If the share price falls to or below $447: add to the position. The basket is meaningfully cheap versus the risk-free alternative.

If the share price rises to or above $698: trim 25–33%. The basket is meaningfully expensive; harvest the premium.

If the share price rises to or above $931: exit the full position. The basket is fully priced versus the risk-free alternative; redeploy proceeds to the risk-free alternative or to a holding that meets the value test.
