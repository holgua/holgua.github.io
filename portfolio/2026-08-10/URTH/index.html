About All World

The iShares MSCI World exchange-traded fund tracks the MSCI World Index — a float-adjusted, market-capitalisation-weighted index of around 1,300 large- and mid-sized companies across 23 developed-market countries. The geographic split is roughly 70% United States and 30% from the rest of the developed world: Japan, the United Kingdom, France, Switzerland, Germany, Canada, and others. That international slice is the fund's distinguishing characteristic relative to a pure US index fund — it dilutes the regime-risk of an all-US allocation, exposing the basket to economies and currencies that do not move in lock-step with the American market cycle.

The annual cost is 0.24% of assets (the total expense ratio, deducted from fund assets daily and reflected in the net-asset value). The fund is structured as a US Delaware statutory trust, issues no partnership tax forms, and has a distribution yield of approximately 1.40%. Tracking difference and tracking error figures are not available from the current data snapshot; they should be verified from the iShares URTH factsheet directly.

The Case for the Bet

The holding exists to provide developed-markets global equity exposure at a reasonable price, with explicit guardrails that force an exit when the price of that exposure becomes unacceptably rich or the vehicle's operational integrity slips. Six conditions govern whether the bet continues to hold:

  1. The equity risk premium stays positive. The basket earnings yield — what the 1,300 constituent companies earn as a share of the price paid for them — must exceed the 10-year US Treasury yield by at least two percentage points over the long run, signalling that the equity risk is being compensated. The exit fires when this premium is negative for two consecutive full calendar quarters.

This is the fifteenth consecutive negative snapshot since 2026-06-16. The two-quarter calendar gate requires confirmation at the 2026-09-30 quarter-end; the condition is not yet triggered.

What Could Still Go Wrong

The bear case for this fund has a distinct structure from a single-company bear: the adversity would need to be broad, not idiosyncratic.

  • Equity market contraction with de-rating. The bear scenario modelled over five years assumes earnings across the basket contract at −10.8% a year and the market simultaneously re-prices the basket from a trailing multiple of 24 times earnings to 14 times. Both must happen at once for a bear to occur — a multiple compression alone without earnings contraction is a re-rating scenario, not a bear. Under this joint assumption, the five-year annualised return is approximately −19.7%.
  • Expense ratio rises above 0.35%. An iShares fee increase of 11 basis points or more, with no matching reduction, fires exit condition (2) immediately.
  • Assets under management fall below $2.0 billion. A drawdown of approximately 75% from current levels would trigger exit condition (3). At that point bid-ask spreads are likely to have widened materially.
  • NVIDIA concentration exceeds 10.0%. If a sustained price run pushes NVIDIA above 10% of the basket, exit condition (4) fires.
  • MSCI World index restructuring. A material change to the developed-markets definition — country scope, weighting methodology, or constituent universe — would undermine the rationale and trigger exit condition (5).

(For this fund, exit fires when the share price reaches the exit edge, when a vehicle-integrity condition breaches, or when the named exit-condition trigger fires — any of three paths.)

The Rules

The following conditions and actions are pre-committed:

  • If the basket is reasonably priced and the vehicle is sound, hold the position.
  • If the equity risk premium driver is weakening — as it is now — do not add. Re-validate after the next quarter. If the equity risk premium stays negative or the median look-through return on equity keeps falling, swap to the UCITS equivalent fund at 0.20% annual cost or move to cash per the position-sizing rule.
  • If the thesis breaks — vehicle integrity breached or the exit condition triggered — exit the position. Move proceeds to a risk-free instrument or to a holding that meets the value test.
  • If the price falls below the buy band (basket earnings yield at or above 6.25%), add to the position. A globally diversified basket at 6.25% or above earnings yield versus the risk-free rate is an unusual and decisive signal.
  • If the price rises above the trim band (basket earnings yield at or below 4.0%), trim 25–33% of the position. The basket is meaningfully expensive versus the risk-free rate; harvest the premium.
  • If the price rises above the exit band (basket earnings yield at or below 3.0%), exit the full position. The basket is fully priced versus the risk-free rate. Redeploy proceeds to a risk-free instrument or to a holding that meets the value test.

Scenario Definitions

A scenario is a joint assumption about two independent variables: operating trajectory (how the holding's underlying cash flows grow relative to their long-run trend) and exit multiple (what the market pays for those cash flows at the horizon, relative to a fair-value anchor).

Scenario Operating trajectory Exit multiple
Bear Below trend Below fair value
Base At trend At fair value
Bull Above trend At or above fair value

Non-negotiable rule: In the bear case, both dimensions must be adverse simultaneously — a scenario where only the multiple moves is a re-rating scenario, not a bear.

Bear cases are comparable in structure across all holding types (company, ETF, bond), but not in magnitude. A company bear reflects idiosyncratic execution failure; an ETF bear reflects a broad market drawdown. Different mitigations, different diversification implications.
