About the Fund

Clean Energy is the iShares Global Clean Energy Transition UCITS ETF, managed by BlackRock's Irish UCITS platform. The fund tracks the S&P Global Clean Energy Transition Index, a basket of roughly 100 companies selected for their involvement in the transition away from fossil fuels — solar and wind power producers, electricity-grid infrastructure companies, battery-storage and electrification businesses, and the equipment makers that supply all of them. Geographically the basket spans both developed and emerging markets, with holdings across the United States, Europe, and Asia.

The three biggest sectors by weight are technology (37%), utilities (32%), and industrials (29%). That concentration means the basket does not behave like a broad stock-market index: it is exposed to the particular fortunes of renewable-energy capital-expenditure cycles, regulatory mandates in the United States and the European Union, and the competitive economics of solar and wind generation against gas and coal. The fund is structured as a UCITS (an Irish-domiciled collective investment scheme regulated under EU law), which means it is available on European exchanges and carries no United States tax-reporting complications. The annual fee is 0.65%.

The Case for the Bet

The bet is that a decade-long shift in global capital expenditure toward clean energy will be reflected in the share prices of the companies that supply and operate that infrastructure. Specifically, the five conditions that must keep holding for the position to remain worth holding are:

  1. Earnings yield trap not triggered. The basket's earnings yield must not stay above 8.0% for four consecutive quarters while also suffering persistent index-level capital losses. That pattern would indicate constituent businesses collapsing rather than a genuine buying opportunity, and would invalidate the capital-expenditure-cycle thesis.

  2. Vehicle integrity intact. The annual expense charge must stay below 0.80% (early-warning flag at 0.70%). Total fund assets must not fall below EUR 200 million (early-warning flag at EUR 400 million). The largest single holding must not exceed 20.0% of the basket (early-warning flag at 17.0%). No product wind-down notice issued by iShares.

  3. Index methodology stable. The S&P Global Clean Energy Transition Index must not materially alter its inclusion rules, sector scope, or weighting method in a way that changes the economic exposure the position was intended to own.

  4. Policy tailwind intact. No durable, legislated repeal of United States Inflation Reduction Act clean-energy tax credits or EU Green Deal binding targets. Temporary policy pauses do not trigger this condition; a legislated rollback does.

  5. Structural cost advantage persists. Solar and wind generation must continue to undercut fossil-fuel incumbents on cost in the majority of geographies. If that cost advantage disappears and capacity additions flatline for two consecutive years, the core economic driver is gone.

A sixth condition tracks the opportunity cost: a materially better clean-energy vehicle emerging with lower fees, broader scope, or higher quality would warrant switching, but that is a monitoring condition rather than a firm numeric trigger.

The Conditions Today

Condition 1 (earnings yield trap). The basket earnings yield is 4.59% — well below the 8.0% trap threshold. No persistent capital losses have been recorded alongside that level. The earnings-yield trap condition is not active.

Condition 2 (vehicle integrity). All four sub-tests pass. The annual expense charge is 0.65%, below both the 0.70% early-warning level and the 0.80% ceiling. Fund assets are €5.1 billion, more than 25 times the €200 million minimum. The largest single holding, Bloom Energy, represents 12.1% of the basket — below the 17.0% early-warning level and the 20.0% ceiling. No wind-down notice has been recorded.

Condition 3 (index methodology). No automated signal for S&P Global Clean Energy Transition Index methodology drift has been detected. This condition is monitored externally; the pipeline carries no data feed for index-committee decisions.

Condition 4 (policy tailwind). No automated signal. Neither the United States Inflation Reduction Act clean-energy provisions nor EU Green Deal binding targets have been durably repealed as of this snapshot.

Condition 5 (structural cost advantage). No automated signal. Solar and wind generation cost data is not monitored by this pipeline.

On balance, the conditions that can be measured confirm or are silent. The yield advantage of the basket over the 10-year government bond — the basket's earnings yield minus the risk-free rate — sits at a thin positive 0.11 percentage points. That is not a failure of condition 2 but it does sit in the mixed zone rather than at the 2.0 percentage-point confirmation floor. See the wobbles section below.

What's Wobbling

The yield advantage of the basket over 10-year government bonds is the single condition in the mixed zone. The basket's earnings yield of 4.59% sits only 0.11 percentage points above the 10-year government bond yield of 4.40%. By one reading, that means owning this basket of volatile clean-energy equities earns almost no extra return over a risk-free government bond — thin compensation for the risk taken.

The framework requires two consecutive negative readings (basket yield falling below the risk-free rate) to satisfy the persistence leg of exit condition 1. The current reading is positive, and the most recent prior reading was also positive at 0.19 percentage points. The persistence trigger has not been met.

The primary holding rationale is structural: the bet is on a decade-long decarbonisation capital-expenditure cycle driven by regulatory mandates and the economics of renewables. The yield-vs-government-bond comparison is tracked for portfolio consistency but carries less decision weight for this position than it would for a broad-market fund. A near-zero yield advantage over government bonds is a reason to avoid expanding the position on valuation grounds — it is not in itself a reason to close it.

If the yield advantage turns negative in the next quarterly snapshot, that would be the first of the two consecutive readings needed to advance exit condition 1. Watch the 2026 third-quarter check.

What Could Still Go Wrong

  • Multiple compression with modest earnings growth. The bear case in the five-year forward scenario requires only 5% annual earnings growth from the basket — modest by any measure — but combines it with the basket's price-to-earnings multiple falling from today's 21.8 times to 15 times, a level consistent with utility-sector norms if the thematic premium fades. That scenario produces a small negative annualised return over five years. The basket is priced for the thematic premium to hold.

  • Constituent bifurcation worsens. Two of the top-ten holdings — Plug Power and SolarEdge Technologies — are currently loss-making, with deeply negative equity returns. Together they represent about 6.6% of the basket. If further loss-making names enter the top holdings as the index rebalances, the quality picture deteriorates. The capital-weighted return on equity for the top ten is already 1.5%, far below the median of 14.9%.

  • Policy reversal. A legislated repeal of United States Inflation Reduction Act clean-energy tax credits or EU Green Deal binding targets would remove the regulatory tailwind underwriting much of the capital-expenditure thesis. Temporary executive-branch policy changes do not trigger exit condition 4; a legislative rollback does.

  • Index methodology restructure. S&P's quarterly rebalances introduce normal basket turnover. A more substantial change to the index's inclusion criteria or sector scope — for instance, adding fossil-fuel adjacent companies under a broadened "transition" definition — could alter the economic exposure the position was intended to own.

  • Vehicle failure. For a €5.1 billion fund this is a remote risk, but if assets were to decline significantly toward the €400 million early-warning level, the probability of a product closure would rise. (For this fund, exit fires when the share price reaches the exit edge, when a vehicle-integrity condition breaches, or when the named exit-condition trigger fires — any of three paths.)

The Rules

The following rules govern this position. They are stated as conditions, not as new judgements from this snapshot.

  • If the thesis is confirmed (all conditions intact, vehicle sound): hold the position. Do not add on the basis of the earnings-yield signal alone; adding is appropriate only if the decarbonisation capital-expenditure case is actively strengthening.

  • If the thesis is weakened (one or more conditions in the mixed or challenged zone, but no exit condition fired): do not add. Re-validate after the next quarter and after any material policy development involving Inflation Reduction Act clean-energy credits or EU Green Deal targets. If exit conditions 3 through 5 are approaching, accelerate the review timeline.

  • If the thesis is broken (vehicle-integrity breach or a named exit condition triggered): exit the position. Move proceeds to risk-free cash or into a broad-market position as a replacement for the thematic exposure.

  • If the price falls to the buy edge (basket earnings yield at or above 6.25% and the thesis intact): add to the position. A basket earnings yield above 8% alongside persistent capital losses is the earnings-yield-trap condition — verify that pattern is absent before adding.

  • If the price rises to the trim edge (basket earnings yield at or below 4.00%): trim 25–33% of the position. Move proceeds to risk-free cash or to broad-market positions rather than holding cash within this position.

  • If the price rises to the exit edge (basket earnings yield at or below 3.00%): exit the full position. Redeploy proceeds to risk-free cash or to a holding that meets the value test.
