About Booking Holdings

Booking Holdings operates the world's largest accommodation reservation marketplace. The main brand, Booking.com, lists approximately 4.5 million properties across 220 countries — 4.0 million of them alternative accommodations (homes, apartments, villas, and similar) alongside 500,000 hotels, motels, and resorts. Beyond Booking.com, the group also runs Priceline (the original bid-based booking service), Agoda (strong in Asia-Pacific), KAYAK (a travel-search engine that compares prices across platforms), and OpenTable (restaurant reservations). The business earns a commission — referred to as a take-rate — each time a traveller books through one of its platforms, without owning a single hotel room or aircraft seat. That asset-light model produced $26.9 billion in revenue in the full year ending in December 2025, with roughly 34 cents of every revenue dollar flowing into free cash (cash the business generates after all operating costs and capital spending).

The core operating dynamic is a two-sided marketplace: the more properties Booking.com lists, the more travellers it attracts; the more travellers it attracts, the more motivated properties are to list. This self-reinforcing loop is the source of the business's competitive strength and the reason it can generate exceptional returns on a very small physical capital base — the company spent roughly 1.2 per cent of its revenue on physical capital in 2025, producing a return on invested capital of 45 per cent.

The Case for the Bet

The central bet is that Booking.com's two-sided network is durable enough to survive the three structural threats currently pressing against it: AI travel agents threatening to route travellers around the booking layer, Google's own direct-search push extracting margin from the paid-search funnel, and hotel-chain direct-booking campaigns trying to pull customers off the intermediary entirely. The supporting bet is that management runs a disciplined capital-return engine — returning seven to nine billion dollars a year through share repurchases and dividends — that compounds the value of each remaining share even if growth slows.

Seven specific conditions must keep holding for the bet to work:

  1. Room-night growth does not decline to low-single-digit or negative territory across multiple regions for two or more consecutive quarters absent an isolated external shock. That pattern would signal the network effect is leaking.

  2. Marketing as a percentage of gross bookings does not rise meaningfully for two or more consecutive years. A sustained rise would signal the company is paying to acquire growth that the moat used to produce for free.

  3. The revenue take-rate (the share of the total value of bookings that becomes Booking's revenue) does not compress materially or persistently. Compression would signal that competitors, regulators, or AI agents are squeezing the economics of intermediation.

  4. Capital-return discipline does not break — specifically, share repurchases do not slow or stop while the free-cash yield is still attractive, especially if the cash is redirected into a large value-destructive acquisition or a culture-shifting strategic pivot.

  5. The owner-earnings yield (the free cash generated each year as a percentage of the total market value of the business) does not fall below 4%. Below that threshold, the market is pricing in optimistic compounding and the per-share economics no longer justify the AI-disintermediation risk.

  6. A coherent AI-first strategy is articulated and visibly executed by the third quarter of 2027. The strategy must cover how Booking.com competes when AI agents become trip-planning front doors, with concrete shipped product showing measurable engagement uplift or direct-traffic improvement, and explicit acknowledgement of disintermediation risk in earnings calls and the annual letter. The existing approach — Smart Filters on Booking.com and the Penny assistant on Priceline — is not yet a strategy. If no coherent picture is visible by the end of the third-quarter 2027 earnings cycle, the moat-survivor thesis is broken.

  7. EU regulatory enforcement does not result in a formal decision imposing structural product remedies (for example, mandatory removal of price-parity clauses that affect pricing power) or fines exceeding 3% of annual global revenue in a single year.

The Rules

The rules governing what action follows from any combination of these conditions and the share price were set in advance:

  • If the conditions are all intact: hold — no action; continue compounding.
  • If conditions are showing signs of deterioration but no exit condition has fired: do not add to the position; re-validate on the specific deteriorating driver after the next two quarterly filings before deciding.
  • If any exit condition fires: exit the full position within 30 days; no averaging down.
  • If the share price falls to the buy edge ($156) and the conditions are intact: add to the position in tranches over one to two quarters.
  • If the share price rises to the trim edge ($273): trim 25 to 33 per cent of the position to harvest the premium while keeping the core holding.
  • If the share price rises to the exit edge ($437): exit the full position; at that reading the market is pricing in roughly seven years of base-case compounding instantly, and the implied forward return no longer justifies the ongoing AI and regulatory risk.

The Conditions Today

The latest evidence comes from Booking Holdings' quarterly report for the three months ending in March 2026, filed on 2026-04-28. The overall picture is net confirmatory — most drivers are on-thesis or showing positive trends — with four drivers in mixed territory, none of which has crossed a written exit threshold.

Room nights booked. Room nights grew 5.9% in the three months ending in March 2026 (338 million, up from 319 million a year earlier). A conflict in the Middle East is estimated to have depressed that number by roughly 2 percentage points; management's estimate of underlying growth excluding the conflict is approximately 8%. Condition (1) requires low-single-digit or negative growth across multiple regions for two or more consecutive quarters absent an isolated shock. One quarter of conflict-impacted growth does not meet that threshold.

Gross travel bookings. Total gross bookings reached $53.8 billion in the quarter, up 15.2% from the same period a year earlier (+8% in constant currency terms, stripping out foreign-exchange tailwinds). The merchant booking channel (where Booking.com collects payment directly) grew 24%, while the older agency channel contracted 3%, reflecting a deliberate mix shift toward higher-margin direct payment processing. Condition (3) on take-rate compression is not triggered.

Take-rate. Revenues as a percentage of gross bookings ticked up slightly, from 10.2% in the same quarter a year ago to 10.3% in the March 2026 quarter, driven by growth in payment-facilitation revenue. Condition (3) reads as confirming.

Marketing efficiency. Marketing spend held flat at 3.8% of gross bookings in both periods. The conflict in the Middle East depressed performance-marketing return on investment (some bookings sourced through paid channels were subsequently cancelled, making those channel costs unproductive). Management states that underlying marketing leverage was positive when the conflict effect is stripped out. Search-engine-optimisation traffic has been declining structurally — a long-term watch item — but it remains a small component of the overall distribution mix. Condition (2) requires a rising ratio for two or more consecutive years; flat is not rising.

Alternative accommodations. The mix of room nights booked through alternative-accommodation listings (homes, apartments, and unique properties) reached 38% in the March 2026 quarter, up from 37% in the same quarter a year earlier. The long-term growth trend is intact.

AI strategy. The Connected Trip vision is articulated, and mobile-app bookings now account for a share in the high-50s per cent of room nights (up from the mid-50s per cent). However, no concrete shipped-product metrics — engagement uplift, measurable direct-traffic changes attributable to AI features — were disclosed in the March 2026 filing. The deadline for condition (6) is the third-quarter 2027 earnings cycle, roughly 18 months from the filing date. The clock is running.

Capital-return discipline. In the three months ending in March 2026, Booking Holdings repurchased $3.8 billion of its own shares and paid $343 million in dividends, returning $4.4 billion in total. The remaining share-repurchase authorisation stood at $18.2 billion. No acquisitions were announced. Condition (4) reads as clearly confirming.

Revenue growth. Revenue in the three months ending in March 2026 was $5.5 billion, up 16.2% from the same period a year earlier (+10% in constant-currency terms). The full-year 2025 figure rose 13.4% from the prior year.

Forward demand outlook. Management offered no formal guidance for the three months ending in June 2026. Cautionary language about the Middle East conflict carrying into future quarters was included in the filing. No systemic multi-region demand deterioration was signalled. The absence of formal guidance is normal practice for Booking Holdings — the company stopped providing quarterly revenue guidance after the COVID period.

What's Wobbling

Four of the nine thesis drivers carry a mixed reading. None has crossed a written exit line, but each deserves a sentence.

Room nights. The growth rate slowed to 5.9% in the quarter. Management's own estimate of the conflict-adjusted underlying rate is approximately 8%, which is well above the "low-single-digit or negative" threshold that would trigger exit condition (1). One quarter of conflict-impacted data is not a trend; the condition requires two or more consecutive quarters, and the Middle East shock is explicitly classified as an isolated event.

Marketing efficiency. Performance marketing returned on investment poorly in the quarter because some bookings sourced through paid channels were cancelled following the conflict — making the spend wasteful rather than ineffective in a structural sense. The underlying ex-conflict marketing leverage was described by management as positive. The structural watch item is the decline in search-engine-optimisation traffic, which has been running for several quarters; the question is whether the direct-booking flywheel (mid-50s per cent direct mix) can compensate as that channel slowly shrinks.

AI strategy. The Connected Trip is well articulated in public communications. Mobile bookings are growing. No measurable product output has yet been disclosed. The thesis does not require a visible AI-native product until the third-quarter 2027 earnings cycle — roughly 18 months from the March 2026 filing — but that deadline is now embedded in the thesis as a binary. Either something concrete and measurable ships and is reported by then, or exit condition (6) fires.

Forward demand. The absence of formal guidance is normal for Booking Holdings and not in itself a signal. The cautionary language about the Middle East carrying into future quarters is appropriate context. No sign of multi-region systemic demand deterioration was present in the filing.

What Could Still Go Wrong

  • AI agents route around the booking layer. Google's agentic travel-booking capability launched in late 2025 is the single most plausible path to permanent impairment of the business model. If AI agents become the dominant trip-planning and booking interface, Booking.com's value as an intermediary between travellers and properties could erode before the Connected Trip strategy produces a counter. The KAYAK metasearch write-down of $457 million in 2025 is already confirmed financial evidence that one layer of the discovery funnel has been impaired.
  • The EU regulatory environment hardens. The Digital Markets Act gatekeeper designation for Booking.com became active in November 2024. A formal non-compliance decision could carry a fine of up to 10% of global annual revenue — approximately $2.7 billion at 2025 revenue levels. Separately, a collective action by more than 15,000 hotels in the Netherlands (coordinated under the HOTREC hotel association) has entered evidence-retrieval proceedings at Booking.com's Amsterdam headquarters. Both are bounded risks at the current free-cash-flow level, but bounded does not mean negligible.
  • Airbnb completes its full-OTA pivot. In May 2026, Airbnb announced plans to expand beyond short-term rentals into the broader accommodation market. If Airbnb succeeds in offering the same breadth of hotel inventory that Booking.com does, it adds competitive pressure from a platform with a large existing user base and a strong direct-booking habit.
  • A recession compresses travel demand. The 2020 COVID period showed that travel is highly discretionary: Booking's revenue fell roughly 50% from 2019 levels. A macro recession compressing average daily rates and room-night volumes simultaneously would squeeze both the top line and the free-cash-flow margin.
  • Management makes a large value-destructive acquisition. The KAYAK write-down demonstrates that inorganic bets can go wrong. The current $21.8 billion share-repurchase authorisation is a much higher-return use of capital than most acquisitions available at current multiples; a large deal at an expensive price would redirect capital destructively and signal a change in management's capital-allocation discipline.

Gaps in the Picture

The owner-earnings computation uses free cash flow as the proxy for true owner earnings, since Booking Holdings does not separately disclose maintenance versus growth capital expenditure in its quarterly filings. The assumption is that the business requires minimal ongoing physical reinvestment — consistent with the 1.2% capital-expenditure-to-revenue ratio and the asset-light platform model — but this estimate is unverified against a detailed breakdown of the capital-expenditure line.

Stock-based compensation is deducted from the free-cash-flow figure to compute owner earnings: $613 million in 2025. Whether this is the economically correct treatment depends on whether one views equity grants as a real cash cost (because they dilute existing holders) or a non-cash accounting charge. The computation above deducts it; some analysts do not.

Two fair-value anchors coexist: the yield-method $199 (derived from a 5.5% target owner-earnings yield applied to free cash) and the bottom-up $306 (peer and self-multiple analysis from a separate scenario model). A future schema migration will resolve the divergence. The $199 anchor drives all valuation-band prices in this evaluation.

The risk-free rate used in the score calculation is sourced from a separate US equity portfolio holding's market-data snapshot (the 10-year government bond yield is not carried directly in the BKNG market-data file); that figure was current as of 2026-06-24.
