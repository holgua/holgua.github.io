About the Bond

The Federal Republic of Germany issues a series of inflation-linked bonds — called Bundesobligationen or Bundesanleihen with an index component — whose principal and annual coupon adjust in line with the German version of the Harmonised Index of Consumer Prices, excluding tobacco (HICP ex-tobacco), as published by the European statistical office Eurostat. The bond covered here matures on 2033-04-15 and was first issued in February 2021. Its stated coupon rate is 0.10% a year, but that coupon is applied to an inflation-adjusted face value, so the actual cash received at each coupon date rises or falls with cumulative HICP inflation since issuance.

A buyer pays the dirty price of €119.67 per €100 original face value at settlement, not €94.69. The €94.69 is the quoted clean price in real terms (same purchasing-power units as the issuance date). To get the actual euros that change hands, multiply by the index ratio (1.26353), then add accrued interest. The index ratio of 1.26353 means that cumulative HICP inflation since the bond's issuance date has been approximately 26.4% — the bond's face value has grown by that amount in nominal terms.

The Case for the Bet

The holding is sized as a structural inflation-hedge allocation, not a rates trade. The bet rests on three exit conditions remaining unbroken:

  1. Real yield at current market price remains above −1.0%, or EUR cash does not simultaneously offer a superior real return. A real yield is what the bond pays above and beyond the rate of inflation — a positive real yield means the bond preserves purchasing power with a margin; a negative one means the bond locks in a real loss. The rule tolerates a negative real yield of up to −1.0%, but only if EUR cash is not simultaneously offering a better real return. If both legs fire — real yield persistently below −1.0% for at least 90 days, and EUR cash ahead on a real after-tax basis — the inflation-hedge function is no longer additive and the rationale for holding collapses.

Latest reading: Real yield 0.91% as of 2026-07-08. The compound exit trigger requires two conditions to fire simultaneously: zero days have been observed below −1.0%, and the linker currently leads EUR cash by 64.98 basis points on a real yield basis (EUR cash real yield approximately 0.26%). Neither leg is satisfied.

  1. Germany's sovereign credit rating remains at or above AA− at two of the three major agencies. The bond's value proposition rests entirely on Germany honouring its obligations. A downgrade below AA− by two of Fitch, Moody's, or S&P would signal a structural shift in issuer creditworthiness serious enough to trigger exit.

Latest reading: As of the 2026-06-15 sidecar, Germany is rated AAA by Fitch and S&P, and Aaa by Moody's. Exit condition (2) is not triggered.

  1. The HICP ex-tobacco index methodology is not materially restructured by Eurostat. The bond's inflation linkage depends on the integrity of the HICP index. A change that severs the connection between the index and realised consumer-price inflation would undermine the inflation-hedge rationale.

Latest reading: No pending HICP index methodology restructuring is recorded in the sidecar as of 2026-06-15. Exit condition (3) is not triggered.

What Could Still Go Wrong

  • Sustained real-yield compression. If the European Central Bank cuts rates aggressively, the ECB deposit rate falls, which narrows the cash comparison advantage. Simultaneously, if nominal Bund yields decline faster than break-even inflation, the linker's real yield would fall toward the trim threshold.
  • Persistent negative real yield plus superior EUR cash. If real yields turn sharply negative (below −1.0%) and remain there for 90 or more days while EUR cash simultaneously clears a better real return, both legs of exit condition (1) fire. This is the most likely path to exit.
  • German sovereign downgrade. Highly unlikely given Germany's fiscal position and AAA standing, but a two-agency downgrade below AA− would trigger exit condition (2) regardless of the real-yield picture.
  • HICP index methodology change. Eurostat periodically revises how HICP is computed. A material change that severs the link between the index and actual consumer-price experience would trigger exit condition (3). No such change is currently signalled.
  • Illiquidity at the point of exit. The bond has an issuance volume of €10.65 billion, making it one of the larger German linker issues. Liquidity in this instrument is structurally thin compared to conventional Bunds, however, and in a risk-off environment bid-offer spreads can widen. A forced exit at the wrong moment could result in execution at a meaningful discount to the quoted clean price.

The Rules

The rules below apply directly from the pre-committed action plan. They describe states of the evaluation, not new judgements.

  • If all conditions hold and the real yield is within the −0.50% to 1.50% band: hold the position. Real yield within band, German credit intact, and HICP linkage provides inflation protection as intended.
  • If the thesis shows signs of weakening (real yield moves below 0% but exit condition (1) has not yet fired): do not add. Re-assess by comparing the real after-tax return against EUR cash and nominal conventional Bunds before making any change.
  • If one or more exit conditions fire (real yield below −1% persistently, German sovereign downgrade below AA−, or HICP methodology change): exit the full position. Move the proceeds to nominal short-duration EUR government bonds or EUR cash.
  • If the real yield rises above 1.50% (buy edge): add to the position. A real yield above 1.50% on a 7-year AAA EUR sovereign inflation-linked bond is considered highly attractive; scale in proportionally to the inflation-hedge allocation target.
  • If the real yield falls below −0.50% (trim threshold): trim 25% on the first crossing, then a further 25% at the next quarterly review 90 or more calendar days later if the real yield remains below −0.50%. The two-step trim ladder is bounded — further crossings do not trigger additional trims until exit condition (1) fires. Park the proceeds in EUR cash or short conventional Bunds.
  • If the compound exit trigger fires (real yield below −1.0% for at least 90 days and EUR cash offering a superior real return): exit the full position. The inflation-hedge function is no longer additive versus the alternative. Switch proceeds to EUR cash or short-duration nominal Bunds.
