About the Fund

World Equal Weight is Invesco's UCITS implementation of the MSCI World Equal Weighted Index: roughly 1,500 companies from 23 developed markets, each assigned an identical weight of approximately 0.07% when the fund rebalances every quarter. The practical effect is deliberate dilution of the mega-cap concentration found in standard global indices. Where a market-capitalisation-weighted world index places roughly 70% of assets in the United States and a double-digit slice in its ten largest companies, this fund distributes capital evenly — so Japanese manufacturers, European banks, and mid-sized industrials from across the developed world count for exactly as much as any American technology giant.

The basket is 99.76% invested in equities. Industrials (19.52%), Financial Services (17.45%), and Technology (12.67%) are the three largest sectors — together about half the fund — a markedly more even spread than cap-weighted equivalents where Technology alone commonly exceeds 20%.

The fund is structured as an Irish-domiciled UCITS accumulating fund (capital gains are retained, not paid out), uses optimised rather than full replication, and trades on Xetra under the ticker MWEQ.DE. The annual cost to hold, as stated by Invesco, is 0.20%. The yfinance data pipeline does not return this figure automatically, so the vehicle-integrity guardrail on cost relies on the Invesco factsheet rather than an automated feed. Tracking difference and tracking error relative to the index are not available in this pipeline's data sources; those figures require the Invesco factsheet directly.

The fund is young — launched roughly two years ago — which matters for the total-assets floor: a UCITS fund below roughly EUR 300–500 million faces elevated closure risk, and Invesco has wound down sub-scale products before.

The Case for the Bet

The structural case is that equal weighting produces a higher basket earnings yield than cap-weighted alternatives, pays a broader equity premium across geographies and sectors, and avoids the single-point-of-failure that an extreme concentration in a handful of mega-cap names creates. For that case to keep holding, six conditions must remain unbroken:

  1. The equity risk premium stays positive. The basket's earnings yield must remain above the ten-year risk-free rate for the case to hold. Two consecutive quarters of a negative premium — meaning the basket earns less in proportional terms than a sovereign bond — would trigger exit regardless of the absolute yield level.

Latest reading: Basket earnings yield 4.87% minus the ten-year US Treasury yield of 4.57% equals a premium of 0.30 percentage points as of 2026-07-12. Positive, but the thinnest reading in the fund's recorded history. The persistence test (two consecutive negative quarters) has not been triggered.

  1. The annual cost stays at or below 0.30%. Equal-weight funds carry structurally higher costs than cap-weighted equivalents because of the quarterly rebalance turnover. The ceiling is set at 0.30% — 50% above the current level — to allow for ordinary drift before the product case breaks.

Latest reading: Invesco reports a cost of 0.20% as of the latest factsheet. The automated data feed (yfinance) does not return this figure for this UCITS listing; the guardrail relies on the manual factsheet check. Ten basis points of headroom remain before the ceiling.

  1. Total assets remain above USD 500 million. Below this level, UCITS fund-closure risk rises materially. The fund is roughly two years old; its track record is short enough that total assets remain a meaningful survival watchpoint.

Latest reading: Total assets approximately USD 1.64 billion as of 2026-07-12 — 3.28 times the floor. Exit condition three is not triggered.

  1. The largest single holding stays at or below 1.0% of the basket. By design, every holding should sit near 0.07% at each quarterly rebalance. Any concentration above 1.0% — roughly 14 times the target weight — signals a methodology change, a rebalance failure, or extreme intra-quarter price drift the sampling strategy has not corrected.

Latest reading: Astera Labs (ALAB) is the top holding at 0.1509% as of 2026-07-12 — 84 basis points below the ceiling. Equal-weight integrity intact.

  1. The MSCI Equal Weighted index methodology remains intact. A shift to factor-tilted weighting, removal of the quarterly rebalance, or a change in the index universe to a narrower parent would alter the product that was purchased. This condition is monitored externally; no automated signal exists within this pipeline.

Latest reading: No automated signal for methodology drift or Invesco sponsor restructuring as of 2026-07-12. User monitors externally.

  1. The median return on equity of the visible basket tip stays above 12%. Equal weighting structurally pulls in smaller and lower-quality names than cap-weighted alternatives, so the quality bar is set lower (12% versus 15% for the cap-weighted world fund). The top ten holdings by post-rebalance drift are the visible proxy; below 12% means even the basket's drift winners have lost quality.

Latest reading: Median return on equity of the deduped top-10 holdings is 29.27% as of 2026-07-12 (Dell Technologies excluded — figure unavailable from the data feed; median computed from nine of ten names). Well above the 12% floor and above the 15% confirm threshold.

What's Wobbling

The equity risk premium is thin. At 0.30 percentage points, the gap between the basket's earnings yield and the ten-year US Treasury yield is the lowest recorded reading in the fund's short history. The compression has come from the Treasury side: the yield on ten-year US government bonds has moved from roughly 4.30% to 4.57% over the recent period, while the basket's earnings yield, sourced from the MSCI factsheet for the quarter ending June 2026, has held steady near 4.87%. A further rise in Treasury yields of 30 basis points, with no change in the basket, would push the premium to zero. The exit condition requires two consecutive negative-quarter readings, so a single dip below zero would not trigger exit — but the margin is thin enough to warrant close attention at the next quarterly rebalance.

The expense-ratio guardrail is running on manual rather than automated data. The yfinance data feed does not return the annual cost for this UCITS listing; the 0.20% figure comes from the Invesco factsheet. If Invesco updates the cost without the factsheet being re-checked at the next refresh, the automated system would not catch it.

Neither of these wobbles changes the current verdict. The hold stands — the Bottom Line states the overall call.

What Could Still Go Wrong

  • Multiple compression with stagnant earnings. The basket currently trades at a price-to-earnings multiple of roughly 20.5 times. A return to the historical equal-weight average of around 12 times — the bear-case terminal multiple — combined with only modest earnings growth of 3% a year would produce an annualised return of approximately −4.9% over five years. The bear case is not extreme; it requires only mean reversion in valuation.
  • Risk-free rates rise further. If ten-year Treasury yields push above 5%, the equity risk premium turns negative. Two consecutive negative-quarter readings trigger exit condition one regardless of the absolute basket yield.
  • Asset erosion toward the fund-closure floor. The fund is roughly two years old, with USD 1.64 billion in assets. A sustained market drawdown of 70% from current levels would approach the USD 500 million floor. That scenario is remote, but the fund's short track record means it has not been stress-tested through a full bear market.
  • Equal-weight methodology drift. MSCI or Invesco could alter the rebalance frequency, the index universe, or the weighting methodology. This condition is monitored externally and carries no automated signal within this pipeline.
  • Expense-ratio increase. Equal-weight funds bear structurally higher rebalance costs than cap-weighted equivalents. Invesco could raise the annual cost above the 0.30% ceiling if it determines the product is underpriced; exit condition two would then fire.

The Rules

When all conditions hold and the share price is within the hold band, hold — equal-weight basket is reasonably priced, vehicle is sound, equal-weight earnings-yield premium over cap-weighted global funds intact.

If any condition weakens but no exit condition has fired, do not add. Re-validate after the next quarter. If the equal-weight earnings-yield premium over the cap-weighted world fund compresses below 0.5 percentage points for two consecutive quarters, the diversification benefit no longer justifies the higher annual cost — consider consolidating into the cap-weighted global fund.

If any exit condition fires — vehicle integrity breached on assets under management, annual cost, top-one concentration, or methodology change; or the value test fails — exit the position. Move proceeds to the cap-weighted global core fund or to risk-free.

If the share price falls to or below the cheap edge of €4.6, add to the position. The equal-weight basket is meaningfully cheap relative to the risk-free rate; the structural earnings-yield premium over cap-weighted alternatives should make this rare, and when it arrives it is decisive.

If the share price rises to or above the trim edge of €7.2, trim 25 to 33% of the position. The basket is meaningfully expensive relative to the risk-free rate; harvest the premium and redeploy at the next rebalance.

If the share price rises to or above the exit edge of €9.6, exit the full position. The basket earnings yield at that price falls to 3.00% — fully priced versus the risk-free rate. Redeploy proceeds to risk-free or to a holding that meets the value test.
