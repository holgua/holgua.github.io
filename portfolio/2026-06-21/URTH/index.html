About the Fund

The iShares MSCI World exchange-traded fund tracks the MSCI World Index — a float-adjusted, cap-weighted basket of approximately 1,300 large- and mid-cap company shares spread across 23 developed markets. The US accounts for roughly 70% of the weight; the remaining 30% spans Japan, the United Kingdom, France, Switzerland, Germany, Canada, and a further 17 developed markets. The fund is issued by iShares, the exchange-traded fund arm of BlackRock — the world's largest asset manager. Its annual cost is 0.24%, charged as a deduction from the fund's assets (not billed separately). Tracking difference and tracking error against the index are not reported in the current data; they remain an open item for the next data refresh.

The fund is structured as a Delaware statutory trust and is registered in the United States. It does not issue K-1 tax forms — holders receive standard 1099-DIV reporting. Historical capital gain distributions are not yet verified (in-kind redemption structures often result in zero distributions); the tax profile section requires confirmation against the iShares annual report and tax supplement. No securities lending revenue offset has been verified, so the net cost stands at the gross 0.24%. Annual cost: 0.24%.

The Case for the Bet

The bet is that, over a multi-year holding period, the basket's earnings yield — what the underlying companies collectively earn relative to what investors pay for them — will exceed the ten-year government bond rate by a meaningful margin. A basket of diversified developed-market companies, compounding at high returns on equity and growing earnings over time, should reward patience with a premium over simply holding government paper. The fund also serves a portfolio-construction purpose: by including roughly 30% non-US developed-market exposure, it reduces the dependence on a single country's economic cycle and regulatory regime that an all-US holding would carry.

For the bet to work, six conditions must hold:

  1. Earnings yield above the government bond rate. The basket's earnings yield (what company profits represent as a percentage of the share price) must exceed the ten-year US Treasury yield, and the gap should be at least 2 percentage points — the minimum premium that compensates for the extra risk of equities over bonds. Early-warning signal: the gap turns negative for any reading. Exit condition: the gap remains negative across two consecutive calendar quarters.

  2. Annual expense ratio below 0.35%. The fund charges 0.24% per year at present; an early-warning flag triggers at 0.30% (fee creep begins to threaten parity with the cheaper UCITS equivalent running at 0.20%), and the exit ceiling is 0.35%.

  3. Assets under management above $2.0 billion. The fund currently holds approximately $8.1 billion in assets; an early-warning flag triggers below $4.0 billion, and the exit floor is $2.0 billion (below which trading costs for a global basket typically widen materially).

  4. Single largest holding below 10% of the fund. The top holding — NVIDIA — currently represents 5.42% of the fund; an early-warning flag triggers above 8.0%, and the exit ceiling is 10.0%. A single company reaching 10% of a global cap-weighted index signals a regime-change concentration that the mandate was not designed to carry.

  5. MSCI World index methodology remains stable. Material restructuring of the MSCI World Index — country re-classification, removal of major developed markets, or a shift away from float-adjusted cap weighting — would undermine the mandate and trigger an exit.

  6. Basket quality remains high. The median return on equity (a measure of how efficiently the underlying companies generate profit from their shareholders' capital) across the ten largest holdings must stay above 15%. It currently stands at 37%.

The Rules

Five situations, five responses:

  • If the basket is meaningfully cheap (earnings yield at or above 6.25%, equivalent to a multiple of roughly 16 times earnings): add to the position. The basket is priced well below its long-run fair value and the risk-to-return trade-off is favourable.
  • If the basket is meaningfully expensive (earnings yield at or below 4.00%, equivalent to a multiple of roughly 25 times earnings): trim the position by 25–33%. The market is pricing in a premium over the long-run fair value; harvest it.
  • If any of the six conditions above is in breach (vehicle integrity violated, or exit condition met): exit the position in full. Move proceeds to a risk-free holding or one that meets the value test.
  • If the thesis is weakening (one or more conditions deteriorating but no exit condition yet met): do not add. Hold and re-validate after the next quarter. If the deterioration continues, consider swapping to the UCITS equivalent at lower cost or moving to cash.
  • If all conditions are intact and the basket is between cheap and expensive: hold. The basket is reasonably priced and the vehicle is sound.

This is the sole condition that is currently failing. Three consecutive weekly readings have all been negative, satisfying the pipeline's persistence requirement. Quality is passing convincingly.

What's Wobbling

The only condition not currently passing is the earnings-yield-to-bond-rate relationship. As of 2026-06-21, the basket earns 4.15% against every dollar of market value; the ten-year government bond rate is 4.45%. The gap is −0.30 percentage points.

Three consecutive readings — on 2026-06-16, 2026-06-17, and 2026-06-21 — have all been negative. The fund's own thesis acknowledged from the outset that this reading was already negative when the position was initiated (the note from 2026-06-05 records −0.37%), so the weakness is not a surprise. The quarterly gate was written precisely to distinguish between a transient dip and a sustained failure of the risk premium case.

What matters now is the next reading in the three months beginning July 2026. If that reading is also negative, the exit condition will have been formally met, and the rules call for closing the position entirely. If the reading turns positive — because the basket earnings yield rises (the market falls or earnings grow) or the bond yield falls — the condition is reset and the thesis remains intact.

The position is also close to the trim threshold from the valuation side: 15 basis points of earnings yield separate the current reading from the 4.0% level that would trigger a mechanical trim. A sustained market rally that pushes earnings yields below 4.0% would add a second layer of pressure.

What Could Break the Fund

  • Persistent negative equity premium. If the earnings yield on the basket stays below the government bond rate into a second consecutive calendar quarter, the rules call for exiting in full. This is the most proximate risk today, and it could fire on the very next quarterly reading.
  • Concentrated drawdown in US technology. Technology shares represent 31% of the fund — a structural feature of a cap-weighted global index, not a manager choice, but a concentration that means a correction in large-cap technology would drive the dominant share of any drawdown.
  • Sustained rise in government bond yields. The fund's earnings-yield case depends partly on the spread between basket earnings and the bond rate. If ten-year yields rise further while basket earnings do not grow commensurately, the negative-premium condition widens and the quarterly gate closes faster.
  • Fee increases. The 0.24% annual cost is already at the high end of the iShares global lineup relative to the UCITS equivalent at 0.20%. The gap to the early-warning threshold (0.30%) is 6 basis points — a single fee revision would cross it. Two revisions would reach the exit ceiling.
  • Significant fund outflows. The fund currently holds $8.1 billion in assets; a 75% drawdown would be required to breach the exit floor of $2.0 billion. This is a remote scenario, but large sustained outflows from global equity funds in a risk-off environment are not unknown.
