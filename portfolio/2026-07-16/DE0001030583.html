About Bund Linker 2033

The Bund Linker 2033 (International Securities Identification Number DE0001030583) is a bond issued by the Federal Republic of Germany that matures on 2033-04-15. Germany carries the highest possible credit rating — AAA from Fitch and Standard & Poor's, Aaa from Moody's — which means default risk is, for practical purposes, zero. The bond pays a coupon of 0.10% per year, but that headline number understates what the bondholder actually receives: both the coupon and the principal repayment are scaled upward by the German Harmonised Index of Consumer Prices excluding tobacco (a pan-European consumer-price index maintained by Eurostat), so the real purchasing power of every cash flow is preserved regardless of how much prices rise between now and maturity.

The mechanics of settlement are worth explaining once. When shares in an ordinary bond trade, settlement involves paying the quoted clean price plus accrued interest. This bond works the same way, but the clean price is quoted in real terms — that is, in the same purchasing-power units as when the bond was first issued in 2021. To find the actual euros that change hands at settlement, the clean price (currently €94.92) is multiplied by the index ratio (currently 1.26369, reflecting cumulative German inflation since issuance), and then accrued interest is added. At today's readings, purchasing €100 of original face value requires a dirty settlement payment of approximately €119.99 — roughly €25 of that is accumulated index uplift.

The Case for the Bet

The bond is held as a structural hedge against sustained inflation — not as a trade on interest rates. The bet rests on three written conditions, all of which must keep holding for the position to remain intact:

  1. Real yield above the compound-exit floor. The bond's real yield (the return net of inflation that a buyer earns to maturity) must stay above −1.0% for the inflation-hedge function to remain positive. The more nuanced trigger: exit condition one fires only when real yield has been below −1.0% for at least 90 consecutive days AND European Central Bank cash simultaneously offers a superior after-tax real return. Both legs must apply at the same time.

Latest reading: Real yield 0.88% as of 2026-07-15. Zero days have been observed below the −1.0% exit threshold. The linker currently leads EUR cash by 69 basis points in real terms. Exit condition one is inactive.

  1. Germany retains its top-tier sovereign credit rating. The German government is rated AAA or equivalent by all three major agencies. Exit condition two fires if any two of those three agencies downgrade Germany below AA−, signalling a structural shift in creditworthiness.

Latest reading: Fitch AAA, Moody's Aaa, Standard & Poor's AAA as of 2026-06-15. Exit condition two is not triggered.

  1. The inflation index remains intact. The Harmonised Index of Consumer Prices excluding tobacco, published by Eurostat, is the reference index for this bond. Exit condition three fires if Eurostat materially restructures the index in a way that severs its link to actual consumer-price inflation — for instance by substituting a different reference basket.

Latest reading: No pending methodology restructuring recorded as of 2026-06-15. Exit condition three is not triggered.

The Share Price

The clean price of €95 places the bond at the buy edge of the hold band. The band is defined in real-yield terms, not in price terms: a real yield above 1.50% corresponds to a price below €91 (the buy order level), a real yield below −0.50% corresponds to a price above €104 (the trim level), and a real yield below −1.00% corresponds to a price above €108 (the exit level). At 0.88%, the bond sits comfortably inside the band — closer to the buy edge than to fair value, but not at a level that triggers any action. The fair-value yield of 0.50% implies a clean price of €97.

The relevant comparison is EUR cash. The European Central Bank's deposit facility rate stands at 2.25%. With break-even inflation at 2.06%, EUR cash earns a real yield of approximately +0.19%. The linker's 0.88% real yield is therefore +69 basis points ahead. This comparison is pre-tax — German income tax applies to both legs; the after-tax direction is not modelled here.

The Standing Orders

The band edges translate into three standing orders and two informational price alerts.

Price alerts (informational).

A first alert at €97, the fair-value anchor. When the clean price crosses €97 on the way up, the real yield is approaching 0.50% — the point at which the bond is neither cheap nor expensive relative to the ECB deposit rate plus the configured spread. No action follows automatically, but the crossing warrants a fresh check of whether the inflation-hedge advantage over cash is narrowing.

A second alert at €91, the cheap edge. When the clean price falls to €91 or below, the real yield is at or above 1.50%, making a 7-year AAA-rated inflation-linked bond highly attractive in the context of EUR alternatives. The buy order below arms at this point.

Standing orders (actionable).

A standing buy order at €91. The rules call for adding to the position in proportion to the inflation-hedge allocation target once the clean price closes at or below €91 and all three conditions above remain intact.

A standing trim order at €104. The rules call for trimming 25% of the position on first crossing of −0.50% real yield, which corresponds to a clean price of approximately €104. If the real yield remains below −0.50% at the next quarterly review — at least 90 calendar days later — a further 25% trim applies. The 25 + 25 ladder is bounded: no further trims fire until exit condition one is reached.

A standing exit order at €108. The compound trigger has two legs: real yield below −1.00% for at least 90 days, and EUR cash simultaneously offering a superior real return. When both legs are satisfied at the same time, the full position is closed and proceeds are switched to EUR cash or short-duration nominal German government bonds.

What's Wobbling

Nothing in the 2026-07-15 snapshot crosses any of the three written exit conditions or approaches a band edge. The one modest observation is the tick-up in nominal Bund yields. Yields on conventional German government bonds maturing in early and mid-2033 rose about 6 basis points between the previous snapshot and this one — from 2.87%–2.90% to 2.93%–2.96%. That rise lifted the break-even inflation rate (the market's implied forecast of German consumer prices through 2033) from 2.00% to 2.06%.

The practical consequence is minimal. A higher break-even inflation rate means the market is pricing in slightly more inflation protection being embedded in the linker, which slightly reduces the linker's real-yield advantage over cash (because the cash-real-yield calculation is: ECB rate minus break-even). In practice, the ECB deposit rate at 2.25% minus break-even of 2.06% gives a cash real yield of 0.19% — meaning the linker's 0.88% remains well clear. The compound exit trigger requires the linker to fall behind cash on a real-yield basis; at 69 basis points ahead, that is not close to firing. No condition from the Case for the Bet is under pressure.

What Could Still Go Wrong

Three scenarios would cause exit conditions to fire:

The real-yield trap. If the European Central Bank cuts its deposit rate sharply — say, in response to a European recession — while inflation expectations remain elevated, the bond price would rise and the real yield could fall through the −0.50% trim band and eventually through −1.00%. At the same time, if the ECB cuts are deep enough, EUR cash would earn an even lower real return, meaning the compound trigger (requires cash to be better) might still not fire. The trim band would fire well before the exit: the first trim at −0.50% gives notice; the exit requires both sustained low yield and a cash-comparison advantage.

A German sovereign credit event. Germany has never defaulted on a euro-denominated obligation, but the written exit condition requires only two downgrades below AA−, not default. A prolonged European fiscal crisis in which German debt sustainability came into question — an extreme but not impossible tail — would trigger exit condition two. At present, all three major rating agencies have Germany at the top of their scales.

An index methodology change. Eurostat periodically reviews the Harmonised Index of Consumer Prices methodology. A change significant enough to sever the bond's link to actual inflation — for example, a fundamental rebasing that excluded large categories of spending — would trigger exit condition three. No such change is pending as of the latest ratings sidecar.

The Rules

If the thesis is confirmed — real yield within band, German credit intact, and index methodology stable — hold the position.

If the thesis is weakened — real yield moving toward zero but not yet below −0.50%, or a single credit concern emerging short of a two-agency downgrade — do not add. Reassess against EUR cash and nominal conventional German government bonds before any additional purchase.

If one or more written exit conditions fire — real yield persistently below −1.0% and cash superior in real terms, a two-agency downgrade below AA−, or a material Eurostat index restructuring — close the full position and move proceeds to short-duration conventional German government bonds or EUR cash.

If the real yield rises above 1.50% (clean price falls below €91) and no written exit condition is active, add to the position in proportion to the inflation-hedge allocation target. A 7-year AAA-rated inflation-linked bond at a real yield above 1.50% is a highly attractive EUR alternative.

If the real yield falls below −0.50% (clean price rises above €104), trim 25% of the position on the first crossing. If the yield remains below −0.50% at the next quarterly review — at least 90 calendar days later — trim a further 25%. The two-step ladder is bounded; further crossings do not trigger additional trims until exit condition one fires.

If the compound trigger fires — real yield below −1.0% for at least 90 days and EUR cash simultaneously offering a superior real return — close the full position and switch to EUR cash or short-duration nominal German government bonds.

Scenario Definitions

A scenario is a joint assumption about two independent variables: operating trajectory (how the holding's underlying cash flows grow relative to their long-run trend) and exit multiple (what the market pays for those cash flows at the horizon, relative to a fair-value anchor).

Scenario Operating trajectory Exit multiple
Bear Below trend Below fair value
Base At trend At fair value
Bull Above trend At or above fair value

Non-negotiable rule: In the bear case, both dimensions must be adverse simultaneously — a scenario where only the multiple moves is a re-rating scenario, not a bear.

Bear cases are comparable in structure across all holding types (company, ETF, bond), but not in magnitude. A company bear reflects idiosyncratic execution failure; an ETF bear reflects a broad market drawdown. Different mitigations, different diversification implications.
