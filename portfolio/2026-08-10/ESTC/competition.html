ESTC Competition Analysis

Source: 10-K filed 2026-06-08 (period ending 2026-04-30); ESTC-Financial-Brief.md | Analysis date: 2026-07-20

1. Competitive Landscape Overview

Elastic competes across three product segments, each with distinct competitive dynamics:

Search & AI Elastic faces competition from traditional search offerings (Algolia, Apache Solr, Coveo), native vector databases (Pinecone, Qdrant, Weaviate), database platforms with integrated vector search (MongoDB Atlas), and search tools from hyperscalers (Google Gemini Enterprise Agent Platform / formerly Vertex AI, and Microsoft Azure AI Search). Elasticsearch's position as the most-downloaded open-source vector database is a significant moat in this segment.

Elastic Observability Primary competitors: Datadog, Dynatrace, Splunk (Cisco), AppDynamics (Cisco), New Relic. This is the most crowded and fastest-evolving segment. Elastic's unified data platform (single data store across logs, metrics, traces) and AI-native agentic observability differentiate it from point-solution vendors.

Elastic Security Primary competitors: Azure Sentinel (Microsoft), CrowdStrike, Google SecOps, Palo Alto Networks, Splunk (Cisco). Elastic Security combines SIEM, XDR, endpoint security, cloud security, and native SOAR in a single platform — a platform breadth that most point-solution competitors cannot match at comparable cost.

The 10-K acknowledges the structural competitive challenge: "many of our competitors have substantially greater financial, technical and other resources, greater brand recognition, larger sales forces and marketing budgets, broader distribution networks and presence" (Item 1A, Competition Risk).

2. Datadog vs. Elastic (Observability)

Revenue Growth Comparison — FY2026

  • Elastic FY2026 total revenue growth: +17% YoY ($1,739M vs. $1,483M)
  • Elastic Cloud revenue growth: +28% YoY ($837M vs. $654M)
  • Datadog FY2025 (calendar year 2024): Revenue of approximately $2.68B, growth of ~26% YoY. Datadog's calendar Q4 2024 ARR growth was approximately 25%.

Datadog's overall revenue growth rate runs approximately 8-9pp ahead of Elastic's total revenue growth rate (26% vs. 17% in comparable periods). However, Elastic's cloud-native revenue (Elastic Cloud, +28%) is competitive with Datadog's growth. The relevant comparison for exit condition 7 is total revenue growth rate differential, where Datadog's lead is approximately 8-9pp — below the 15pp threshold.

Datadog Scale Advantages

Datadog's FY2025 revenue of ~$2.68B is approximately 1.5× Elastic's FY2026 revenue. Datadog is exclusively SaaS (no self-managed option), which drives higher gross margins and simpler go-to-market. Datadog's NRR (Net Revenue Retention) has historically exceeded 125%, above Elastic's ~112%. Datadog also benefits from tighter integrations with cloud-native DevOps workflows, while Elastic must serve both self-managed and cloud customers.

Exit Condition 7 Assessment — Datadog Gap

The exit condition triggers if Datadog's YoY revenue growth exceeds Elastic's by more than 15pp for two consecutive fiscal years AND Elastic is below Gartner Leader status in Observability.

Current status from Financial Brief (§10, Exit Condition 7): "Not triggered; Elastic is Leader." The growth gap in comparable recent periods is approximately 8-9pp — meaningful but below the 15pp threshold. Exit Condition 7 is NOT breached for FY2026.

3. AWS OpenSearch Fork Risk

Fork Status

AWS forked Elasticsearch in 2021 (OpenSearch, now donated to the Linux Foundation). The 10-K explicitly addresses this risk: "Amazon offers some of the features that we had previously made available under an open source license as part of its AWS offering. Through these offerings, Amazon competes with us for potential customers, and while Amazon cannot provide our proprietary software, Amazon's offerings reduce the demand for our products and the pricing of Amazon's offerings may limit our ability to adjust the prices of our products" (Item 1A, Open Source Risk).

The filing categorizes certain cloud providers and MSPs as competitors: "Certain cloud hosting providers and MSPs, including AWS, which offer products or services based on a forked version of our platform. These offerings are not supported by Elastic and come without any of Elastic's proprietary features, whether free or paid" (Item 1, Competition section).

Impact on Self-Managed Renewal Base

OpenSearch has captured some of the self-managed/open-source user base. This is reflected in Elastic's "Other Subscription Revenue" (self-managed) declining -0.9% in FY2026 ($797M vs. $804M). This is the most direct observable impact of fork competition. Cloud migration offsets this: Elastic Cloud grew +28% to $837M, now representing 48% of total revenue.

Elastic's Response

Elastic has actively responded to the fork risk through multiple strategies disclosed in the 10-K: 1. License change (November 2024): Added AGPL as a licensing option for Elasticsearch and Kibana source code, expanding open-source community engagement while maintaining proprietary feature protection. 2. Cloud migration: Elastic Cloud (consumption-based, FedRAMP-eligible) offers features unavailable in OpenSearch. 3. AI differentiation: ELSER (Elastic Learned Sparse Encoder), Better Binary Quantization, and Jina AI embedding models are proprietary features not available in any fork. 4. Elastic Cloud Serverless: Auto-scaling serverless deployment mode removes operational burden that OpenSearch self-managed cannot match.

4. Microsoft Azure AI Search / Copilot Search

Azure AI Search Enterprise Positioning

Microsoft Azure AI Search (formerly Azure Cognitive Search) competes directly in Elastic's Search & AI segment. The 10-K names it as a direct competitor: "search tools, such as Google Gemini Enterprise Agent Platform (formerly Vertex AI) and Microsoft Azure AI Search" (Item 1, Competition).

Azure AI Search benefits from Microsoft's distribution advantages — pre-bundled with Azure, integrated with Azure OpenAI Service, and sold as part of enterprise M365/Azure agreements. For enterprises already standardized on Azure, Azure AI Search reduces procurement friction. However, it lacks Elastic's vector database depth (particularly hybrid BM25 + dense vector retrieval), real-time ingest performance, and multi-cloud deployment flexibility.

Microsoft M365 / Copilot Integration

Azure Sentinel (renamed Microsoft Sentinel) competes in the security segment. Microsoft's "Security Copilot" product integrates AI into the security workflow — a direct competitive response to Elastic's AI-driven SIEM/XDR. Microsoft's bundling advantage (Sentinel often priced as part of broader M365 E5 agreements) creates pricing pressure in the SIEM market. The 10-K lists Azure Sentinel among Elastic Security's named competitors.

5. Cisco/Splunk (SIEM/Security Segment)

Post-Acquisition Integration Status

Cisco completed its acquisition of Splunk in March 2024. The 10-K lists both "AppDynamics and Splunk (each owned by Cisco Systems)" as Observability competitors and "Splunk (owned by Cisco Systems)" as a Security competitor. Splunk's integration into Cisco's security portfolio is now in its third year, with Cisco cross-selling Splunk SIEM alongside its networking and firewall products.

Market Share Implications for Elastic Security

Cisco/Splunk's integration has created a period of customer uncertainty — some Splunk customers evaluating alternatives during integration. Elastic's open-source roots and unified platform (SIEM + XDR + SOAR in one) position it as a cost-effective Splunk alternative. Elastic's pricing model (resource-based consumption) is structurally more transparent than Splunk's legacy per-GB ingest pricing.

However, Cisco's distribution scale (global SI partnerships, existing network security relationships) means the combined Cisco/Splunk entity remains formidable. Elastic's federal security wins (CISA SIEMaaS-style contracts) are partly opportunistic captures from Splunk's federal base during the transition period.

6. New Entrants and AI Threat

LLM-Native Observability Tools

AI-native observability startups (e.g., Arize AI for LLM observability, Coralogix for AI-driven log management) represent the "attack from below" risk. These vendors offer AI-first interfaces that bypass traditional log/metric/trace pipelines. Elastic's LLM observability module (tracking latency, errors, prompts, responses across LLM services) is a defensive response to this category.

The 10-K addresses the broader structural risk: "The rapid adoption of generative and agentic AI tools may change how software developers design, build and deploy applications... changes in developer workflows, tooling or decision-making processes resulting from the adoption of AI-enabled development tools could affect how developers select databases or other data platforms" (Item 1A, AI Competition Risk).

Elastic's AI-Powered Defensive Moat

Elastic's AI capabilities (as disclosed in the 10-K) that serve as competitive differentiation:

  1. ELSER (Elastic Learned Sparse Encoder): Proprietary NLP model for semantic search — not available in OpenSearch fork.
  2. Better Binary Quantization (BBQ): Proprietary vector compression algorithm — "the only platform with Better Binary Quantization to reduce the memory required without sacrificing accuracy" (Item 1, Technology Features).
  3. Agent Builder: Native AI agent framework that connects LLMs to Elasticsearch data via natural language — positions Elastic as an AI application infrastructure layer.
  4. Jina AI integration (acquired FY2026): Multi-modal, multi-lingual embedding models and semantic reranking — expands AI retrieval capabilities.
  5. Elastic AI Ecosystem: Integrations with Anthropic Claude, OpenAI, Cohere, NVIDIA, LangChain, LlamaIndex — positions Elasticsearch as the preferred vector database for the LLM/RAG ecosystem.
  6. Agentic observability: AI agents that handle alert investigation, root cause analysis, and remediation — ahead of Datadog's current agentic capabilities.

7. Competitive Position Assessment (Exit Condition 7)

Explicit Datadog Growth Gap Evaluation

Exit Condition 7 Definition: Datadog's YoY revenue growth must exceed Elastic's by more than 15pp for two consecutive fiscal years AND Elastic must be below Gartner Observability Leader status.

FY2026 Data: - Elastic total revenue growth: +17% YoY - Elastic Cloud growth: +28% YoY - Datadog comparable period growth: approximately +26% YoY (calendar 2024 / Datadog FY2024) - Implied gap (total revenue): approximately 9pp (Datadog 26% vs. Elastic 17%) - The 15pp threshold is NOT breached - Elastic maintains Gartner Magic Quadrant Leader status in Observability (confirmed via Financial Brief §10, Exit Condition 7 assessment)

Assessment: Exit Condition 7 is NOT triggered for FY2026. The Datadog growth gap is real (~9pp on total revenue basis) but well below the 15pp threshold. Elastic's cloud revenue (+28%) is directly competitive with Datadog's overall growth. The gap is partially explained by Elastic's larger self-managed base (declining -0.9%) diluting total revenue growth; the cloud-only comparison is near-parity.

Gartner Magic Quadrant Observability Position

Per the Financial Brief thesis driver checklist (§10), Elastic is confirmed as a Gartner Leader in Observability as of FY2026. This satisfies the second condition of Exit Condition 7 (which requires BOTH Datadog gap >15pp AND non-Leader status for the condition to trigger). Since Elastic is a Leader, even if the growth gap reached 15pp, the exit condition would not yet trigger.

Overall Competitive Moat Assessment

Elastic's competitive moat rests on three reinforcing pillars:

  1. Platform breadth: Unified data platform serving Search, Observability, and Security from a single Elasticsearch data store — a structural advantage that pure-play observability (Datadog) or pure-play SIEM (Splunk) vendors cannot match without significant architectural investment.
  2. Open-source community and developer mindshare: Elasticsearch is the most-downloaded open-source vector database globally. This creates a bottom-up adoption pipeline that is expensive to replicate via pure commercial sales motion.
  3. AI-native differentiation: ELSER, BBQ, Agent Builder, and Jina AI integrations create proprietary capabilities unavailable in any fork (OpenSearch) or most point-solution competitors.

Moat Assessment: Moderate-Strong — durable but not impregnable. The primary erosion vectors are Datadog's superior cloud growth economics, Microsoft's bundling advantages in both search (Azure AI Search) and security (Sentinel), and AWS's continued investment in OpenSearch as a self-managed alternative. Elastic's response — accelerating Elastic Cloud (48% of revenue, growing 28%) while adding AI-native features — is the correct strategic direction.
