Recent Development

The latest snapshot (2026-07-07) confirms that the S&P 500 basket's earnings yield — the share of each dollar of current share price that the basket earns in aggregate — has fallen to 3.72%. The ten-year US Treasury yield stands at 4.53%, which means investors who buy the basket today accept a lower current earnings yield than they would get from a riskless government bond. That gap, −0.81 percentage points, is what the thesis calls the equity risk premium, and it has been negative for at least eight consecutive weekly readings, ranging from −0.59% to −0.81%.

The written exit condition requires two consecutive quarterly readings of negative equity risk premium, not weekly ones. The weekly streak, while striking, does not yet constitute two full quarterly periods. The next quarterly check falls at the end of September 2026; if that reading is also negative, the exit trigger will fire. Until then the assessment is that the bet is wobbling, with the trim already pre-committed.

All vehicle-integrity measures remain comfortable: the annual expense ratio is 0.09%, assets under management stand at $781 billion (nearly eight times the $100 billion floor), and the largest single holding — NVIDIA — accounts for 7.5% of the fund, well inside the 13% ceiling. The basket's quality signal (median return on equity of the nine largest holdings: 37%) also remains far above the 15% collapse threshold.

About the Fund

SP500 is a passive fund that tracks the S&P 500 index — a basket of approximately 500 large US companies, weighted by their market capitalisation (meaning larger companies have a proportionally larger presence in the fund). The fund is managed by State Street Investment Management under the ticker SPY. It launched in January 1993 as the first US-listed exchange-traded fund and has operated continuously under the same trust structure since then.

The fund's mandate is to replicate the S&P 500 index as closely as possible. No active judgement is applied; the basket changes only when S&P Dow Jones Indices (a subsidiary of S&P Global) changes the index's composition or rules. The annual cost is 0.09%, among the lowest for any large-cap US equity fund. Assets under management stand at $781 billion, making SPY the largest equity exchange-traded fund in the world. Tracking difference and tracking error figures are not available from the current data pull and should be verified from the State Street SPDR factsheet.

The fund's tax structure is a Unit Investment Trust — the oldest US exchange-traded-fund wrapper. Unlike newer fund structures, a Unit Investment Trust cannot reinvest dividends between distribution dates (which contributes to the small cash sleeve of 0.24%), and it cannot lend securities to generate additional income. No K-1 tax form is issued to shareholders.

The Case for the Bet

The bet is not that the S&P 500 always delivers a satisfactory return. It is that the basket's earnings power, relative to the risk-free rate and the price paid, clears a minimum bar for sensible long-term holding. Six conditions must keep holding for the position to remain warranted:

  1. The basket earns more than the risk-free rate. The equity risk premium — the basket's earnings yield minus the ten-year Treasury yield — must remain positive over consecutive quarterly periods. If it stays negative for two full quarters, the return case has deteriorated below the minimum acceptable threshold.

Latest reading: Basket earnings yield 3.72% vs. ten-year Treasury 4.53% → equity risk premium −0.81% (as at 2026-07-07). Eight consecutive weekly readings have been negative, ranging from −0.59% to −0.81%. Two consecutive quarterly readings of negative equity risk premium have not yet been recorded; exit condition (1) is not yet triggered, but the weekly trend is the direction of risk.

  1. The annual expense ratio stays at or below 0.15%. If State Street raises the fee materially without a competitive response from rival funds, the cost-efficiency argument for SPY weakens. The practical alternative would be to switch to a lower-cost equivalent (such as IVV or VOO).

Latest reading: Annual expense ratio 0.09% (2026-07-07). The hard ceiling of 0.15% is not breached. No early-warning threshold is separately configured.

  1. Assets under management stay above $100 billion. A collapse in fund size to this level would signal an extraordinary loss of investor confidence or a broader market dislocation. It would not necessarily mean the fund is about to close, but it would indicate something unusual enough to warrant exiting.

What's Wobbling

The equity risk premium is the one condition under active stress. The basket's earnings yield (3.72%) has been below the ten-year Treasury yield (4.53%) for at least eight consecutive weekly snapshots, a streak that started in mid-2026-06. The gap has been widening slightly rather than recovering: the earliest reading was −0.72%, the most recent −0.81%.

The exit condition requires two consecutive quarterly readings, not weekly ones — a deliberate choice to avoid being shaken out by temporary rate spikes. That standard has not yet been met; one more quarterly snapshot with negative equity risk premium would trigger it.

It is worth noting that the direction of Treasury yields matters here as much as the direction of share prices. If Treasury yields fall back toward 3.2% without any change in the basket's earnings, the equity risk premium would recover to roughly +0.5% and the wobble would resolve on its own. The worry is not a single snapshot; it is a sustained structural shift in which equities yield less than government bonds and the excess return case for holding equities has disappeared.

The basket's quality and vehicle metrics — all five of the remaining conditions — are intact and comfortably inside their thresholds.

What Could Still Go Wrong

  • The quarterly exit trigger fires. If the September 2026 quarterly reading also shows negative equity risk premium, condition (1) triggers and the full position is closed. At current price and Treasury-yield levels, this is the most likely path to an exit.
  • Treasury yields rise further. A continued rise in the ten-year Treasury yield — whether from persistent inflation, fiscal concerns, or a change in Federal Reserve policy — would widen the negative equity risk premium without any change in the basket's earnings. The exit trigger would arrive sooner.
  • Earnings disappoint at the index level. The basket's earnings yield is derived from trailing earnings. If corporate profits contract materially — as happened in the bear case of the forward-return analysis — the earnings yield would fall further, not recover.
  • Multiple contraction. The bear-case analysis assumes the trailing price-to-earnings multiple compresses from the current 26.9 to 15 over five years, producing an annualised return of approximately −7% a year. That level of compression does not require an economic collapse; it requires only a return to historical average valuations. At current prices, the downside scenario carries more magnitude than the upside.
  • Vehicle-integrity breach. Any of the five remaining conditions could breach without warning — a methodology change by S&P Dow Jones Indices, a surprise fee increase, or an unexpected concentration event. These are lower-probability tail risks but not zero.

The Rules

If the basket is reasonably priced and all six conditions are met: hold.

If any condition is weakening but not yet broken — in particular, if the equity risk premium is negative for fewer than two consecutive quarters: do not add. Re-validate after the next quarter. If the equity risk premium stays negative or the median quality metric keeps falling, swap to an equivalent low-cost fund (such as IVV or VOO) or move to cash according to the position-sizing framework.

If any vehicle-integrity condition breaches (exit conditions 2–6 fire) or if condition (1) — two consecutive quarterly readings of negative equity risk premium — is confirmed: exit the full position. Move the proceeds to the risk-free rate or to a holding that meets the value test.

If the share price falls to or below $445: add to the position. The basket is meaningfully cheap versus the risk-free rate at that level.

If the share price rises to or above $695: trim 25–33%. The basket is meaningfully expensive versus the risk-free rate; harvesting the premium is pre-committed at this threshold. The current price of $748 already satisfies this condition.

If the share price rises to or above $927: exit the full position. The basket earnings yield falls to or below 3.00% at that level — fully priced versus the risk-free rate. Redeploy the proceeds to the risk-free rate or to a holding that clears the value test.
