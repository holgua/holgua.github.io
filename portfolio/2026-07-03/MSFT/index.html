Microsoft: The Bet Still Works

The Bottom Line

The bet is working. Seven clear watchpoints were set in advance and the latest
quarterly filing confirms all seven are still on the right side of their lines.
The share price sits at the trim edge — one moderate move
upward separates the position from the rule that calls for a partial sale — and
the share price is well above fair value by an earnings-yield
measure, which means there is no bargain to be had at today's price. The
one condition worth watching is the free-cash-flow conversion ratio: it has been
running below the threshold on a nine-month basis, though both required legs of
the exit condition remain unmet, and that story has more to it.

About Microsoft

Microsoft earns its money in three broad areas: productivity software (Microsoft 365,
which includes Outlook, Word, Excel, and Teams, plus LinkedIn); cloud computing
infrastructure through Azure; and personal computing (Windows, Xbox, and Bing). The
largest and most structurally important is cloud computing, but the thing that ties
all three together is the enterprise identity system underneath them.

Every company using Microsoft's products routes its employee logins, access
permissions, and security policies through a single Microsoft-managed service called
Azure Active Directory (now rebranded Entra ID). Migrating away from this service
would require a company to simultaneously replace its email, directory, security
layer, productivity tools, and every application plugged into it — in practice, a
multi-year project. That lock-in is the core of the investment case.

Microsoft's operating profit margin was roughly 46 cents on every dollar of revenue
in financial year 2025 — one of the highest sustained margins in enterprise
technology. Free cash flow (operating cash generation after capital spending) was
$71.6 billion in that year, though the figure is compressed by a surge in data-centre
building that has more than tripled capital spending since 2021.

The Case for the Bet

The bet rests on one idea: the enterprise identity lock-in is deep enough that
customers cannot leave without years of effort, creating a platform from which
Microsoft sells cloud capacity, AI tools, productivity software, and security at
high margins. The open question is whether the surge in data-centre capital spending
is building a wider moat — as Azure becomes the dominant platform for AI workloads —
or permanently eroding returns by committing billions to infrastructure that earns
insufficient revenue.

For the bet to remain on track, seven conditions must keep holding:

  1. Azure growth stays above 25% per quarter — combined with capital spending above
    20% of revenue, two consecutive quarters below this level would signal the AI
    infrastructure bet failing to generate sufficient revenue.

  2. Free cash flow stays above 65 cents per earnings dollar, annually — two
    consecutive fiscal years below this level, with no Azure AI improvement, would
    signal permanent impairment rather than a temporary peak in capital spending.

  3. Operating profit margin stays above 42% — two consecutive quarters below this
    line would signal structural margin compression that the capital investment cycle
    alone does not explain.

  4. The IRS transfer-pricing dispute stays out of Tax Court. The US Internal Revenue
    Service is seeking $28.9 billion plus penalties for transactions from 2004–2013. A
    court referral, or any single-quarter tax charge exceeding $5 billion, triggers
    mandatory reassessment.

  5. No formal antitrust action seeking structural remedies. A complaint from the US
    Federal Trade Commission or a European regulator seeking the divestiture of a
    business unit — rather than a fine — would signal a qualitatively different threat
    to the platform business model.

  6. Gross margin stays above 65% — two consecutive quarters below this level would
    signal that AI infrastructure costs are compressing the core software margin.

  7. Commercial revenue backlog keeps growing above 30% per year. An absolute
    quarter-on-quarter decline in the backlog would signal that enterprise customers
    are pulling back their forward commitments.

Operating margin (condition 3). The profit margin was 46.3% in the most recent

IRS dispute (condition 4). The $28.9 billion transfer-pricing claim remains at the

Antitrust risk (condition 5). Competition law scrutiny appears in the filing's risk
factors as standard language; no formal complaint seeking structural remedies has been

Gross margin (condition 6). The gross profit margin — revenue after direct delivery
costs — was 67.6% in the most recent quarter and 68.2% across the nine months, both

Commercial revenue backlog (condition 7). The contracted revenue backlog reached
$627 billion as of 2026-03-31, up 99% from the prior year — more than three times the
30% growth threshold. No absolute decline. Clear.

The Share Price

The share price stood at $390.49 as of the most recent refresh. The valuation measure
used here is an owner earnings yield — the proportion of the company's market value
represented by normalised cash earnings, after stripping out the capital spending
required purely for growth. At $390.49, with owner earnings of roughly $59.6 billion
and a market capitalisation near $2.9 trillion, that yield is approximately 2.06%.

The fair-value anchor is 3.5% — the level representing a neutral reading. The current
reading of 2.06% means the market is paying considerably more per dollar of earnings
than the thesis considers fair value; expressed as a price, the 3.5% yield anchor
corresponds to roughly $229. That is not where the price is.

The trim threshold sits at 2.0% yield, corresponding to roughly $402. At $390.49, the
price is about $11 below that line — close but not across it. The trim order has
not triggered. A note on the fair-value figure: a separate scenario analysis arrives
at a very different central estimate of $2,050; see Gaps in the Picture.

What's Wobbling

Two conditions are under pressure rather than clearly confirming.

Free cash flow conversion. Across the nine months to 2026-03-31, free cash flow
was $47.4 billion against net profit of $98.0 billion — roughly 48 cents on the
dollar, below the 65-cent annual threshold. The exit condition requires two consecutive
full fiscal years below that level and no improvement in Azure's AI revenue; neither
leg is met. The ratio to watch as capital spending peaks — if and when it does.

Capital spending as a share of revenue. Capital spending ran at 37% of revenue
in the most recent quarter and 33% across nine months — well above the 20% threshold
in the first exit condition. But that condition also requires Azure growth below 25%.
With Azure at 40%, both legs of condition one would need to close on each other
simultaneously, and that has not happened. The thing to watch is whether Azure growth
decelerates in coming quarters while capital spending remains elevated.

What Could Still Go Wrong

  • AI infrastructure fails to pay off. Azure decelerating to mid-teens growth while
    capital spending stays above 30% of revenue would leave free cash flow compressed
    without the compensating revenue. The bear scenario (20% likelihood) has the
    annualised return at −1.4% a year over three years.

  • Open-weight AI models commoditise Azure's compute advantage. If the AI workloads
    enterprises run on Azure can be served equally well by cheaper open-source
    alternatives, the premium Microsoft charges for its AI infrastructure compresses.

  • IRS dispute materialises at the upper end. The $28.9 billion claim plus penalties
    remains at the administrative appeals stage with no change of status in the latest
    filing. A Tax Court referral or settlement near the claimed amount would be a
    material cash event.

  • Antitrust action seeking structural remedies. A Federal Trade Commission or
    European enforcement action seeking divestiture of Teams or unbundling of cloud and
    AI services would cross the fifth watchpoint and require an immediate review.

  • Capital spending concentration on NVIDIA chips. The data-centre expansion depends
    heavily on graphics chips from a single manufacturer; supply disruptions or export
    restrictions would affect the pace of the buildout.

Gaps in the Picture

Two fair-value anchors coexist. An owner earnings yield method implies a
fair-value price of roughly $229 on current earnings (used in The Share Price
and The Standing Orders). A separate bottom-up scenario analysis
using peer and self multiples applied to projected financials arrives at a base-case
central estimate of $2,050. The multiple-based method applies current high market
multiples to projected future earnings; the yield method anchors to current earnings
at a yield level that reflects steady-state value. A future methodology update will
adopt a single anchor. Until then: the band thresholds in The TL;DR use
the yield method exclusively.

AI revenue run-rate figure not re-disclosed quarterly. The thesis cites an AI
annual revenue run-rate of $37 billion. This non-standard metric is not required in
quarterly filings. The proxy evidence — Microsoft 365 commercial cloud revenue growing
19%, Copilot infrastructure spending cited as a cost driver — is consistent with
continued growth but does not allow the specific figure to be confirmed each quarter.

Owner earnings deducts full capital spending. The $59.6 billion owner earnings
figure deducts all capital spending including the expansion programme. If capital
spending peaks and falls, the earnings figure would rise substantially, requiring the
band thresholds to be recalibrated.

Risk-free rate sourced from the S&P 500 holding's market data. The 10-year
government bond yield used in the hurdle-rate calculation (4.485%, as of 2026-07-03)
is drawn from the portfolio's S&P 500 index holding rather than a Microsoft-specific
source — the standard fallback when the individual holding does not carry the field.

The Rules

The pre-committed rules are as follows:

  • If the thesis is confirmed — hold without adding. The current price does not
    provide a sufficient margin of safety for a new buy; continue monitoring at each
    quarterly earnings release.

  • If the thesis shows signs of weakening — do not add. Run a fresh re-validation
    against the specific deteriorating driver and monitor for one additional quarter.
    Distinguish cyclical (capital spending timing) from structural (competitive
    displacement). Escalate to exit review if a second consecutive quarter confirms
    the same signal.

  • If the thesis is broken — any one of the seven exit conditions fires — wind down
    the full position within 30 days. No averaging down. Log the triggered condition.

  • If the share price falls to the buy edge (~$161) — confirm the thesis is intact,
    then begin adding in tranches. Intermediate guideposts at $352 and $322 apply before
    the yield threshold is reached.

  • If the share price rises to the trim edge (~$402) — trim 20–25% of the position.
    Do not trim below 50% of the holding on valuation grounds alone.

  • If the share price rises to the exit edge (~$1,606) — close the full position.
    Observe 90 days before re-entering.
