About US Treasury 0-1y

The fund — formally the Vanguard U.S. Treasury 0–1 Year Bond UCITS ETF (accumulating) — holds exclusively US Treasury bills that mature within one year. Treasury bills are short-term obligations of the US federal government: they are issued at a discount and repaid at face value on maturity, generating a return equal to the difference. Because the fund holds bills maturing in less than a year, the entire basket rolls over continuously; the portfolio's weighted-average maturity was approximately four and a half months at the last snapshot. Income from maturing bills is reinvested inside the fund rather than paid out as a cash distribution, so the fund's share price rises gradually with accumulated returns rather than paying a regular dividend.

The fund is domiciled in Ireland and listed in USD on the London Stock Exchange. It is managed by Vanguard Group (Ireland) Limited, whose cooperative ownership structure removes the typical commercial incentive to raise fees. The fund's annual running cost is 0.07% of assets — 7 pence per £100 invested per year — making it one of the lowest-cost ways to hold USD Treasury-bill exposure on a European exchange. No tracking-difference or tracking-error data was available in this snapshot; those metrics are best verified against the fund's annual KIID.

The Case for the Bet

US Treasury 0-1y is held as a near-cash placeholder — a home for capital that earns the short-term US government rate while the portfolio awaits equity opportunities at fair prices. Three conditions must keep holding for that premise to remain sound:

  1. Ongoing charges figure stays below 0.10% for no more than one consecutive quarter above that ceiling. An annual running cost above 0.10% for two consecutive quarters would mean comparable ultra-short-duration Treasury funds — available at similar or lower cost — offer a materially better deal, and the fee advantage that justifies this vehicle over a simple cash alternative would be gone.

Latest reading: 0.07% as of the most recent Vanguard factsheet (carry-forward; yfinance does not surface this figure for this fund's listing). Three basis points of headroom beneath the ceiling; exit condition one is inactive.

  1. Assets under management stay above USD 1 billion. A fund managing less than $1 billion of assets risks widening bid-ask spreads, reduced creation/redemption efficiency, and an elevated probability of eventual closure — all of which would undermine the daily-liquidity premise that is the fund's core utility.

Latest reading: Data provider returns no figure for this fund. An independent aggregator's carry-forward estimate of approximately $2.4 billion sits well above the floor, but the figure requires manual confirmation against the Vanguard product page each quarter. Exit condition two is assessed as inactive.

  1. US sovereign credit is not downgraded below AA− by two or more major rating agencies. The fund's entire return rests on the creditworthiness of the US federal government as obligor. A two-agency downgrade below AA− would signal a structural shift in that creditworthiness and require a full reassessment of whether the cash-equivalent premise still holds.

Latest reading: Fitch AA+, Moody's Aaa, S&P AA+ as of 2026-08-12. All three are above the AA− trigger; exit condition three is not triggered.

What Could Still Go Wrong

  • Fee creep. If Vanguard raises the annual running cost above 0.10% — still well above the current 0.07% — and that elevated rate persists for two consecutive quarterly reviews, exit condition one fires. Comparable alternatives exist; the transition cost is low.
  • Fund shrinkage. A sustained wave of redemptions could take assets below $1 billion, triggering exit condition two. The manual nature of the fund-size check means a breach might not be detected until the next quarterly review. Daily monitoring of the fund's size is impractical without an automated data source.
  • US sovereign downgrade. A two-agency downgrade of US federal debt below AA− would trigger exit condition three. Such a downgrade is a low-probability event but cannot be excluded; it would affect every USD Treasury holding simultaneously, not just this fund. The appropriate response per the Rules is to exit and reinvest in EUR cash or short nominal Bunds.
  • Yield compression. An aggressive Federal Reserve cutting cycle could push the basket yield below 3.5%, crossing the trim threshold, and eventually below 3.0%, crossing the exit threshold. At that point the opportunity cost of holding USD T-bills versus EUR alternatives would become material and the Rules call for trimming and then exiting.
  • Data blindspot. The pipeline's inability to source live basket yield data or live fund-size figures for this fund's listing means that a condition breach could go undetected between quarterly manual checks. This is a process risk, not an investment risk, but it is the most operationally relevant caveat for day-to-day oversight.
  • (For this fund, exit fires when a vehicle-integrity condition breaches, when the basket yield falls to the exit level, or when the named exit conditions fire — any of those paths leads to the same action.)

The Rules

  • If the thesis is confirmed (vehicle-integrity conditions clear, basket yield within band), hold. Vehicle integrity intact — ongoing charges figure within budget, assets under management above floor, mandate unchanged.
  • If the thesis weakens (one condition approaching but not crossing a threshold), do not add. Re-assess at the next quarterly review; monitor the charges figure and fund size before increasing the allocation.
  • If the thesis breaks (one or more numbered exit conditions trigger — charges figure above 0.10% for two consecutive quarters, assets under management below $1 billion, or US sovereign downgrade below AA− by two agencies), exit. Reinvest proceeds in EUR cash or short nominal Bunds.
  • If the basket yield reaches 5.5% or above, add at the margin if cash is available — T-bill rates would be unusually attractive relative to EUR alternatives. Vehicle-integrity conditions still gate any large addition.
  • If the basket yield falls to 3.5% or below, trim at the margin — yield compression to this level means USD T-bill carry versus EUR cash is materially weaker. Vehicle-integrity conditions still gate a full exit.
  • If the basket yield falls to 3.0% or below, exit per the applicable exit condition (fee ceiling, fund-size floor, or sovereign downgrade — whichever applies).

Scenario Definitions

A scenario is a joint assumption about two independent variables: operating trajectory (how the holding's underlying cash flows grow relative to their long-run trend) and exit multiple (what the market pays for those cash flows at the horizon, relative to a fair-value anchor).

Scenario Operating trajectory Exit multiple
Bear Below trend Below fair value
Base At trend At fair value
Bull Above trend At or above fair value

Non-negotiable rule: In the bear case, both dimensions must be adverse simultaneously — a scenario where only the multiple moves is a re-rating scenario, not a bear.

Bear cases are comparable in structure across all holding types (company, ETF, bond), but not in magnitude. A company bear reflects idiosyncratic execution failure; an ETF bear reflects a broad market drawdown. Different mitigations, different diversification implications.
