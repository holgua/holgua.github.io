About Bund Linker 2033

Bund Linker 2033 is an inflation-linked bond issued by the Federal Republic of Germany (ISIN DE0001030583). It matures on 2033-04-15 and carries a stated coupon of 0.10% a year — but that low number is not the whole story. Both the coupon payments and the principal repaid at maturity are scaled upward by an indexation coefficient that tracks the German Harmonised Index of Consumer Prices excluding tobacco (HICP ex-tobacco), compiled by Eurostat. In practice, this means that as consumer prices rise, the bondholder receives correspondingly more in euros. Credit risk is negligible: Germany has never defaulted on a euro-denominated obligation and carries a AAA rating from all three major rating agencies.

Settlement mechanics are slightly more involved than for a conventional bond. A buyer pays the dirty price — €119.55 per €100 original face value at settlement, not the €94.58 quoted as the clean price. The €94.58 is the clean price expressed in real terms, using the same purchasing-power units as the issuance date in 2021. To arrive at the actual euros that change hands, multiply the clean price by the indexation coefficient (currently 1.26370) and then add accrued interest. The indexation coefficient is the cumulative rise in the HICP ex-tobacco index since issuance; it grows over time as prices rise, ensuring the bondholder keeps pace with inflation.

The Case for the Bet

The bond is held as a structural EUR inflation hedge: provided Germany remains creditworthy, HICP methodology stays intact, and real yields do not collapse to deeply negative territory, the bond preserves purchasing power in a way that nominal bonds and cash cannot guarantee. Three conditions govern whether that bet remains worth holding:

  1. Real yield stays above −1.00% persistently, and EUR cash does not offer a superior real return. The compound trigger requires both legs simultaneously: the bond's real yield must fall below −1.00% for at least 90 calendar days, and EUR cash must at the same time offer a better real return. Neither condition need fire alone to trigger exit.

Latest reading: Real yield 0.94% as of 2026-08-12, 194 basis points above the exit threshold. EUR cash real yield approximately 0.22% (ECB deposit 2.25% minus break-even inflation 2.03%). Linker advantage: +72 basis points. Neither leg of the compound trigger is satisfied; persistence counter at 0 of 90 days.

  1. German sovereign credit rating holds above AA- at two of the three major agencies. A downgrade below AA- by Fitch, Moody's, and S&P simultaneously — or any two of the three — would signal a structural shift in issuer creditworthiness and would fire exit condition (2).

Latest reading: All three agencies above the AA- exit trigger as of 2026-06-15: Fitch AAA, Moody's Aaa, S&P AAA. No rating action pending. Exit condition (2) not triggered.

  1. HICP ex-tobacco index methodology remains unchanged. A material restructuring by Eurostat that severs the link between the index and realised consumer-price inflation — for example, substituting a different reference index — would fire exit condition (3) and remove the inflation-hedge rationale entirely.

Latest reading: No pending HICP methodology restructuring recorded in the ratings sidecar as of 2026-06-15. Exit condition (3) not triggered.

What Could Still Go Wrong

  • Sustained deeply negative real yield and a cash alternative. If European Central Bank rate cuts push nominal yields below inflation for a prolonged period, the bond's real yield could fall below −1.00%. If EUR cash simultaneously offers a better real return, the compound exit trigger fires and the inflation-hedge rationale collapses.
  • German sovereign downgrade. A deterioration in Germany's fiscal position severe enough to prompt two of the three major agencies to cut the rating below AA- would fire exit condition (2). While remote given Germany's current position, a structural shift in European sovereign risk — driven by large fiscal programmes or a euro-area crisis — could not be ruled out over a seven-year horizon.
  • HICP index methodology change. If Eurostat materially restructures the HICP ex-tobacco index — substituting a different reference basket or methodology — the bond's inflation-linkage would no longer track realised consumer-price inflation in the intended way. Exit condition (3) would fire. This is a tail risk; Eurostat methodology changes are rare and heavily signalled in advance.

Scenario Definitions

A scenario is a joint assumption about two independent variables: operating trajectory (how the holding's underlying cash flows grow relative to their long-run trend) and exit multiple (what the market pays for those cash flows at the horizon, relative to a fair-value anchor).

Scenario Operating trajectory Exit multiple
Bear Below trend Below fair value
Base At trend At fair value
Bull Above trend At or above fair value

Non-negotiable rule: In the bear case, both dimensions must be adverse simultaneously — a scenario where only the multiple moves is a re-rating scenario, not a bear.

Bear cases are comparable in structure across all holding types (company, ETF, bond), but not in magnitude. A company bear reflects idiosyncratic execution failure; an ETF bear reflects a broad market drawdown. Different mitigations, different diversification implications.
