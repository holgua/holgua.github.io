Overall Competitive Position derivation: Unilever qualifies as Strong on the basis of: (1) top-3 share in the three segments it will retain post-McCormick (Beauty, Personal Care, Home Care); (2) operating margin consistency (16.6–17.9% over 5 years) demonstrating a moat that holds under input cost pressure; (3) share trajectory turning positive with volume-led growth in Q1 2026; and (4) M&A activity (bolt-on premium acquisitions + McCormick disposal) actively sharpening the portfolio against the strongest competitive positions.

Narrative Assessment

Unilever's competitive position is improving at the margin: the volume acceleration visible in Q1 2026 (Power Brand +4.0% volume) after several years of pricing-led growth confirms that the brand moat is holding and that consumers are not switching away at scale despite elevated prices. The most important competitive question is whether the McCormick disposal completes cleanly, because it determines whether Unilever's competitive identity crystallises as a beauty-and-personal-care compounder (where its EM distribution and brand investment are genuinely differentiating) or remains diluted by a Nutrition segment competing with private-label on shelf. P&G remains a formidable head-to-head rival with greater scale in North America; Unilever's structural advantage is its depth in EM markets (approximately 60% of revenue) where P&G's reach is thinner. L'Oréal's 2025 slowdown (1.3% sales growth vs. Unilever B&W's 4.1%) indicates that premium beauty growth is rotating toward mid-market brands — territory where Unilever's Dove Prestige, TRESemmé, and newly acquired K18/Wild compete. The single most important competitive threat over the next 3 years is the structural DTC channel shift in beauty and personal care, which erodes the traditional retailer-shelf distribution moat and requires Unilever to build direct-to-consumer capabilities at speed — the bolt-on acquisition strategy (Wild, Minimalist, Dr. Squatch) is the correct response, but execution at Unilever's scale ($50bn revenue) is inherently slower than a DTC-native competitor's. Competition is manageable, not existential: this is a business defending and extending a genuine moat, not one in structural retreat.
