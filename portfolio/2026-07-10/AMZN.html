Amazon: The Bet Still Works

The Bottom Line

Data as of 2026-07-10 · Latest filing 2026-04-30.

The bet is working. Amazon's cloud computing division grew 28% in the three months ending in March 2026, advertising revenue has compounded to the scale of a standalone business, and none of the eight written conditions that would force a sale has fired. The share price sits in the upper half of the hold band, well above the fair-value anchor — meaning the market is paying ahead of where the underlying numbers justify, but not yet at the point where the rules say to trim. The condition that most warrants attention is whether the surge in infrastructure spending translates into cloud revenue at the pace the investment assumed; that question will only be answered by two or three more quarterly filings.

Recent Development

The latest quarterly report, covering the three months ending in March 2026, confirmed the central thesis at full force. Amazon Web Services — the cloud computing division that supplies roughly 57% of the company's total operating profit on only 18% of its revenue — grew 28% compared with the same period a year earlier, reaching $37.6 billion in sales for the quarter. Advertising revenue, the third compounding engine alongside cloud and retail, reached $17.2 billion, up 24% year on year. Neither of the two most closely watched exit conditions — those tied to cloud growth falling below 15% and advertising growth slowing to single digits — is within sight of the written thresholds.

The numbers that are less comfortable also arrived in the same filing. Capital expenditure for the quarter came in at $43.2 billion, more than twice what it was a year earlier, as Amazon continued building out the data-centre and computing capacity that the cloud business requires. Over the trailing twelve months, operating cash flow of $148.5 billion was almost entirely consumed by infrastructure spending, leaving roughly $1.2 billion in net free cash flow by the management definition — a sharp compression from $25.9 billion the year before. The rule that governs this monitors whether spending stays above $120 billion in both 2027 and 2028 without a corresponding acceleration in cloud revenue; that clock has started running, but it cannot trigger until 2028 at the earliest.

For the current position, nothing has changed. The share price sits below the trim edge, the thesis is intact, and the rules say hold.

About Amazon

Amazon operates three businesses under one corporate structure, and the interaction between them is what makes the investment case unusual. The first is the retail and logistics platform — the familiar online marketplace — which generates most of the company's revenue but a comparatively modest share of its profit. In 2025, the North America retail segment produced $426 billion in sales at a 6.9% operating margin. The second is Amazon Web Services, the cloud computing business that rents computing power, storage, and increasingly artificial-intelligence infrastructure to businesses of all sizes. With $128.7 billion in 2025 revenue and a 35.4% operating margin, it supplies the majority of the company's operating income. The third is advertising, a $68.6 billion business in 2025 that monetises the purchase-intent data generated by the retail platform every day. The advertising business grew 22% in 2025 and carries high margins that Amazon does not separately disclose.

What makes the structure durable is that each business subsidises and reinforces the others. Retail scale drives advertising revenue; advertising revenue funds the Prime subscription bundle; the Prime bundle drives retail volume; retail volume and cloud contracts together expand the customer base that advertising can reach. Jeff Bezos, who founded Amazon and remains its executive chairman with an 8.8% beneficial ownership stake, built the company explicitly around this kind of self-reinforcing loop.

The Case for the Bet

The bet is that Amazon Web Services retains its growth momentum as artificial-intelligence workloads absorb the large infrastructure build-out of 2024 to 2026, and that advertising and the international retail segment continue compounding operating profit faster than revenue for several more years:

  1. Cloud revenue growth stays above 15% for any two consecutive quarters. The thesis requires Amazon Web Services to keep growing at 18–20% or better. If growth slips below 15% for two consecutive quarters, that signals the artificial-intelligence infrastructure investment has not converted to revenue demand as expected, and the thesis is broken.

Latest reading: Amazon Web Services grew 28% in the three months ending in March 2026, reaching $37.6 billion in sales. The contracted backlog of cloud commitments stood at $364 billion with a weighted-average remaining life of 5.5 years. An expanded eight-year arrangement with OpenAI added $100 billion to that backlog in the same period. Exit condition (1) is not in view.

  1. Cloud operating margin stays above 28% for any two consecutive quarters. Amazon Web Services currently runs a 37.7% operating margin. If that falls below 28% for two consecutive quarters without an explainable one-off charge, it signals that the reinvestment economics have broken — the capital is not earning what it was deployed to earn.

Latest reading: The three-months-ending-March-2026 margin was 37.7%, approximately 170 basis points below the same period a year earlier. The compression reflects deliberate infrastructure investment, not pricing deterioration; the margin sits 970 basis points above the 28% exit floor. One quarter of compression at this distance from the threshold is not actionable under the rules.

  1. No court-ordered structural remedy in the antitrust case. The United States Federal Trade Commission is pursuing a monopoly case against Amazon's retail operations. A court order requiring Amazon to separate any of its businesses would be an immediate exit condition, regardless of the share price.

Latest reading: The latest official filing references the antitrust proceedings in generic risk-factor language. No structural remedy, no consent decree, and no new trial timeline has been disclosed. Exit condition (3) is not triggered.

  1. Capital expenditure does not stay above $120 billion in both 2027 and 2028 without accelerating cloud revenue. The Share Price

The share price of $250 sits in the upper half of the hold band, well above the fair-value anchor. In concrete terms: the rules set the fair-value anchor at an owner-earnings yield of 6.0%, which at the current free-cash-flow level corresponds to a price of approximately $176. The current price of $250 implies a yield of 4.2%, which is still within the hold band — the trim edge is at $263, the buy edge at $140 — but is already pricing in several years of compounding beyond what the fair-value anchor assumes.

The 42% premium to the fair-value anchor is addressed in Why the Market Sees It Differently, which sets out the three hypotheses behind the market's more optimistic reading.

Why the Market Sees It Differently

The price says the business will earn more over time than the fair-value anchor assumes. The gap is 42% — the fair-value estimate sits at $176, the market price is $250. To justify that price on steady-state assumptions, the market is implicitly forecasting long-run owner-earnings growth of 8.9% rather than the 8.0% embedded in the fair-value anchor. The difference is nine-tenths of a percentage point — narrow in absolute terms but, compounded over many years, producing a large divergence in terminal value.

Three hypotheses explain how the market arrives at 8.9%:

First, structural demand acceleration. Amazon Web Services grew 28% in the three months ending in March 2026, and a $364 billion contracted backlog gives visibility for years ahead. If enterprise adoption of artificial-intelligence workloads continues to expand the total addressable market for cloud computing beyond what the base model assumed, the higher growth rate is warranted. The latest filing supports this hypothesis: cloud growth is running ahead of the base-case trajectory, not behind it.

Second, margin re-expansion after the infrastructure trough. The current operating margin compression in Amazon Web Services — from 39.4% a year ago to 37.7% — is happening because the company is depreciating a rapidly expanding asset base faster than the new capacity generates revenue. If that depreciation wave passes and margins re-expand toward and beyond 40%, the free-cash-flow trajectory improves without any acceleration in revenue growth. The filing's management discussion frames the compression as a deliberate investment in future capacity, partially supporting this hypothesis, though current data can only partially validate a forward claim.

Third, optionality from the artificial-intelligence infrastructure layer. Amazon holds a stake in Anthropic (an artificial-intelligence laboratory) valued at approximately $74 billion in the March 2026 balance sheet, and has committed $50 billion to OpenAI alongside a commercial cloud arrangement. These positions are not captured in a free-cash-flow model built on current earnings — the free cash flow is being suppressed by the very capital expenditure that creates the arrangements. If either stake produces returns beyond the cloud revenue already contracted, the growth rate is higher than the model shows. A $12.3 billion upward revaluation of the Anthropic stake in the first quarter of 2026 alone illustrates the scale of the optionality.

Two of three hypotheses have direct support in the latest filing. The market's 8.9% implied growth rate may not be particularly optimistic — it is only slightly above a base-case assumption that was set conservatively. For the share price to converge to the fair-value anchor without any change in fundamentals, the business would need to grow more slowly than the market currently expects, and the latest filing gives little evidence of that.

What's Wobbling

Four of the nine thesis drivers produced a mixed reading in the latest filing.

The most closely watched is the cloud operating margin. At 37.7%, it remains far above the 28% exit floor, but the direction is downward — approximately 170 basis points below the same period a year earlier. The compression is driven by depreciation on a rapidly expanding asset base: Amazon Web Services added $41.5 billion of net property, plant, and equipment in a single quarter, a figure that grew 103% year on year. The margin will likely remain under pressure until the new capacity generates enough revenue to outpace depreciation — a timing question the current data cannot answer with confidence.

Capital expenditure is the second wobble. Spending is running at an annualised rate above $150 billion, with management guidance pointing to further increases in 2026. The exit condition that governs this monitors whether spending stays above $120 billion in both 2027 and 2028 without a corresponding cloud acceleration. The clock is running, but the condition cannot fire for at least two years.

Capital return discipline is the third. Amazon held a $6.1 billion share-repurchase authorisation unused since 2022, even as the share price at that time was materially lower than today. In the first quarter of 2026, the company invested $15 billion in OpenAI's latest funding round and committed a further $35 billion. These are strategic deployments accompanied by commercial cloud arrangements, which makes them more than purely financial bets — but they consume capital that might otherwise have been returned. The thesis characterises capital allocation as acceptable but not excellent, and that characterisation remains accurate.

Tariff and marketplace risk is the fourth. The latest official filing explicitly identifies Chinese sellers as significant contributors to third-party seller revenue and advertising revenue, and names tariff policy changes as a material risk. North American operating income nevertheless rose 41% in the three months ending in March 2026, suggesting the business has resilience to the current tariff environment — but the risk language in the filing is more specific than in prior periods.

None of these four readings crosses a written exit threshold. The rules say hold.

What Could Still Go Wrong

  • The infrastructure investment does not convert to revenue. Amazon has been spending at an annualised rate above $150 billion on data-centre and computing capacity. If enterprise demand for artificial-intelligence computing grows more slowly than the contracted backlog implies, or if Microsoft Azure and Alphabet's Google Cloud take a disproportionate share of incremental demand, Amazon could find itself sitting on excess capacity with depreciation charges that suppress earnings for years.
  • An antitrust structural remedy is imposed. The Federal Trade Commission's monopoly case against Amazon's retail operations remains unresolved. A court order requiring separation of any part of the business — the marketplace, the fulfilment network, or a combination — would be an immediate exit condition, regardless of valuation.
  • The European regulatory burden expands. A €746 million ruling under European data-protection rules has already been enforced. Ongoing competition proceedings under European Union legislation governing large online platforms represent a category of risk that does not show up in any single earnings line but could impose ongoing compliance costs and constrain how the marketplace operates across the continent.
  • The Anthropic and OpenAI stakes lose value. The $74 billion Anthropic stake and the $50 billion OpenAI commitment represent a large concentration of capital in assets where mark-to-market moves can be large in either direction. A downward revaluation would suppress reported earnings without affecting the operating business — but it would be visible in the accounts and would influence how the market prices the shares.
  • The bear-scenario midpoint is below today's price. The value analysis places the bear-scenario midpoint at approximately $154. At $250, a sustained bear scenario would represent a loss of roughly 38% from the current price. This is not a tail risk — the bear case carries a 25% probability — and there is no earnings-power floor anywhere near the current share price.

Gaps in the Picture

  • Owner-earnings figures use the narrow accounting definition of free cash flow (operating cash flow minus purchases of property, plant, and equipment). At Amazon, this captures only a fraction of total infrastructure spending. Management defines capital expenditure at approximately $128 billion for 2025, far above the narrow accounting figure. The yield of 4.2% used in the valuation bands is based on the more generous accounting definition; on the management definition, it would be substantially lower. This is disclosed and documented, but the valuation figures carry an inherent optimism about what constitutes maintenance spending.
  • The fair-value anchor in the rules (6.0% yield, corresponding to approximately $176) has not been reconciled with the value analysis's base-scenario midpoint price, which implies a substantially different level. The two sources diverge by a material amount. The rule-based recommendation is unaffected — it uses the configured anchor — but the discrepancy is a signal that the anchor settings should be revisited before the next full refresh.
  • The risk-free rate used to compute the hurdle rate and the equity premium comparison was read from a separately held position rather than directly from an Amazon-specific data file. The rate used reflects a snapshot current as of 2026-07-10.
  • The reconciliation between the scenario-based return (+28.6% base case, looking three years ahead from today's price) and the yield-plus-growth return (approximately 12% a year at today's price on a long steady-state horizon) was suppressed because the two figures point in opposite directions — the scenario model shows a positive base return relative to today's price, while the valuation model implies the share price is above the fair-value estimate. This contradiction typically arises when the valuation inputs need refreshing. Both figures are presented; neither has been overridden or reconciled here.

The Rules

  • If the thesis is confirmed — all drivers reading in line with the written conditions, no exit condition triggered, share price within the hold band: hold. No action. Continue compounding the cloud and advertising flywheel.
  • If the thesis is weakening — any driver is deteriorating toward but has not crossed an exit threshold: do not add to the position. Re-validate against the specific deteriorating driver at the next two quarterly filings before deciding.
  • If the thesis is broken — any written exit condition has fired: exit the full position within 30 days. No averaging down. Cloud revenue and margin exits require confirmation across two consecutive quarters before action; a court-ordered antitrust remedy or an auditor change triggers an immediate exit.
  • If the share price falls to or below $140 (the buy edge): confirm the thesis is intact first — cloud growth at or above 18%, no exit condition triggered. If confirmed, add to the position in tranches over one to two quarters.
  • If the share price rises to or above $263 (the trim edge): trim 25–33% of the position to harvest the premium. Re-evaluate the remaining position size after the trim.
  • If the share price rises to or above $421 (the exit edge): exit the full position. The market would at that point be pricing in roughly 11 years of base-case compounding instantly; the implied forward return falls below the long-run equity premium even before further growth, and redeployment beats continuing to hold. No re-entry within 90 days.
