5. Owner Perspective (Buffett Frame)

"Would Berkshire deploy cash into this basket today?"

At 3.851% nominal YTM with sub-6-month average maturity, VDST IS the cash equivalent in this portfolio — not a bet on rate direction, not a credit spread play. Buffett's framework treats T-bills as the cost of optionality: their return is foregone while waiting for a compelling equity idea at a fair price. Within that frame, VDST is doing its job. The 0.07% OCF (last known Vanguard published TER) costs 7 bp per year for daily-liquid, USD-denominated, LSE-listed access to the T-bill curve — negligible. Against the equity ERP context, with the 10-year Treasury at 4.60% and S&P 500-level earnings yields in the 4–5% range, the T-bill yield of 3.851% is marginally below both — the case for holding is intact, but not for adding aggressively. Berkshire would park cash here while awaiting better equity opportunities, not grow the allocation at these yields.
