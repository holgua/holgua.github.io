About All World

The iShares MSCI World ETF (ticker: URTH) tracks the MSCI World Index — a float-adjusted market-capitalisation-weighted basket covering 23 developed countries and approximately 1,300 large and mid-cap companies. The US accounts for roughly 70% of the index by weight, with the remaining 30% spread across Japan, the United Kingdom, France, Switzerland, Germany, Canada, and other developed markets. URTH holds this basket directly, offering diversified developed-market equity exposure in a single listed instrument.

The Technology sector accounts for 31.25% of the basket by weight, reflecting current US mega-cap market dominance within the float-adjusted index rather than an active manager tilt. NVIDIA is the largest single holding at 5.42%; the top nine holdings together account for approximately 27.7% of total assets. The median return on equity of the top nine holdings stands at approximately 37%, well above the 15% exit floor for look-through quality.

The fund is managed by BlackRock under the iShares brand and is structured as a Delaware statutory trust (Exchange Traded Fund) tracking the MSCI World Index. The annual expense ratio is 0.24% — 4 basis points above the functionally equivalent IWDA UCITS at 0.20%.

Total assets: approximately $8.1 billion as of 2026-06-29. Average daily volume: approximately $228 million. Annual cost: 0.24% (source: yfinance). Trailing earnings multiple: approximately 23.5 times.

The Case for the Bet

All World is held as developed-markets global equity core exposure. The bet is not that the basket is always a buy. The bet is narrower: over the holding period, the basket's earnings yield — the aggregate profit earned per dollar of share price across roughly 1,300 companies in 23 developed markets — must exceed the return on a risk-free 10-year government bond plus a reasonable equity risk premium. When that condition fails for two consecutive quarterly reviews, the economic rationale has broken down. URTH plays a distinct role from the US-only core position: it dilutes concentration in a single country's equity market with approximately 30% exposure to other developed economies, narrowing the regime-risk of an all-US passive allocation.

Six conditions govern the bet:

  1. The gap between the basket's earnings yield and the 10-year Treasury yield must not remain negative for two consecutive quarterly reviews. If it does, risk-free bonds are offering a better yield than the basket; the rationale for holding equities over bonds has failed.
  2. The annual expense ratio must stay below 0.35% (current: 0.24%). Above this level, the IWDA UCITS at 0.20% provides an equivalent mandate at lower cost; the additional drag is no longer justified.
  3. Total fund assets must stay above $2.0 billion (current: approximately $8.1 billion). A sustained fall to this floor would cause bid-ask spreads to widen materially for a fund of this type, raising transaction costs for the strategy.
  4. No single holding's weight may exceed 10.0% (current top holding: NVIDIA at 5.4%). Concentration above this level in a global cap-weighted index signals a structural shift in market composition rather than normal drift.
  5. The MSCI World index methodology must remain substantively unchanged by MSCI Inc. Material restructuring — such as country re-classification, removal of major developed-market constituents, or a fundamental weighting change — would alter what the fund actually represents.
  6. The median return on equity of the deduped top-ten holdings must remain above 15% (current: approximately 37%). A collapse below this floor signals that the basket's visible quality anchor has eroded.

Six negative readings in one quarter is a meaningful start toward that outcome. An improvement in basket earnings or a sustained fall in Treasury yields could close the gap; neither is certain.
  • Multiple contraction without an earnings recovery. The basket currently trades at a trailing earnings multiple of approximately 23.5 times. Any re-rating toward the long-run historical average for global equities would produce significant losses even with steady underlying earnings growth.
  • US dollar strength compressing non-US returns. Approximately 30% of the basket is listed outside the US. A sustained appreciation of the US dollar would reduce the dollar-denominated returns from Japanese, European, and other non-US holdings without affecting the underlying business performance of those companies.
  • Concentration creep in the top holding. NVIDIA's weight has declined from 5.73% at thesis inception to 5.42% at the latest snapshot. If the company's market capitalisation re-accelerates relative to the global index, its weight could drift toward the 8.0% early-warning level.

A significant market decline or sustained redemption pressure could close that gap; below $4.0 billion, bid-ask spreads typically begin to widen for global blend funds of this type.
