About World Equal Weight

World Equal Weight is an index fund listed on Germany's Xetra exchange under the ticker MWEQ. It tracks the MSCI World Equal Weighted Index, which takes the roughly 1,500 companies in the MSCI World universe — large and mid-size companies from twenty-three developed countries — and assigns each one roughly equal weight, reset at a quarterly rebalance. That is the central design choice: rather than allowing the biggest companies by stock-market value to dominate the index, the fund spreads weight evenly across the entire universe. At each rebalance date, every holding starts at approximately 0.07% of the fund.

The practical effect is a portfolio that leans more toward Japan, the United Kingdom, and continental Europe than a conventional market-cap index, and less toward the ten or fifteen giant US technology companies that dominate market-cap-weighted global indices. The fund is structured as an Irish-domiciled UCITS accumulating fund: it retains dividends within the fund rather than distributing them, and it does not issue a Schedule K-1 tax form. The annual cost — from the Invesco factsheet, since this figure is not returned by standard data feeds — is 0.20%, 33% below the 0.30% ceiling defined in the thesis. Tracking-difference and tracking-error figures for the full year are not yet available from data providers.

The Case for the Bet

The bet rests on equal-weight global developed-market equities remaining a structurally attractive alternative to cash and to cap-weighted global indices. The six conditions that must continue to hold are:

  1. The basket must earn more than risk-free cash, measured as a positive equity risk premium. The thesis requires the basket's earnings yield (the inverse of its price-to-earnings ratio) to exceed the ten-year government bond yield; a negative premium for two consecutive quarters would trigger an exit regardless of the absolute yield level.

Latest reading: 0.33% equity risk premium as of 2026-07-19. The premium has been positive in all nine recorded snapshots, but at 0.33% it sits 167 basis points below the 2.0% level that fully confirms the case.

  1. Invesco must not raise the expense ratio above 0.30%. Equal-weight funds have higher running costs than their market-cap counterparts because of quarterly rebalancing turnover; the 0.30% ceiling represents the point at which the added cost exceeds the diversification benefit.

Latest reading: The annual cost is 0.20% per the Invesco factsheet, 10 basis points below the ceiling. The figure is not available from standard data feeds; the vehicle-integrity check is currently disabled in the automated system and must be re-enabled once a known-value override is set.

  1. Assets under management must remain above $500 million. The fund launched approximately 22 months ago. UCITS funds below roughly €300–500 million face elevated closure risk; Invesco has wound up sub-scale UCITS products before. A sustained drawdown to $500 million would be a survival signal, not a valuation signal.

Latest reading: $1.64 billion as of 2026-07-19, 3.3 times the floor — no closure risk is in view.

  1. The largest single holding must not exceed 1.0% of the fund. By construction, the equal-weight mandate pins every holding at roughly 0.07% on each quarterly rebalance date; any drift above 1.0% (roughly fourteen times the target weight) signals a methodology breakdown, a rebalance failure, or extreme intra-quarter price dispersion. The threshold is deliberately tight because equal-weighting is the core product.

Latest reading: Astera Labs (a US semiconductor interconnect company) at 0.15% as of 2026-07-19, 85 basis points below the 1.0% ceiling. Basket structure is intact.

  1. MSCI Inc. must not materially restructure the Equal Weighted methodology. A shift to factor-tilted weighting, removal of the quarterly rebalance, or a change in the parent universe would alter the product the thesis was written for.

Latest reading: No automated signal available in the current pipeline. Monitored externally via the MSCI factsheet. No changes detected.

  1. The median return on equity of the visible top-ten holdings must remain above 12%. Equal-weighting structurally pulls in smaller companies with lower returns on equity than the mega-caps that dominate a market-cap index; the 12% floor (lower than the 15% used for cap-weighted global funds) represents the point at which the basket's quality has deteriorated meaningfully.

What Could Still Go Wrong

The bear case for this fund is a broad market drawdown rather than any single-company failure. Four specific paths lead to an exit:

  • The equity risk premium turns negative for two quarters running. If the ten-year government bond yield rises above the basket's earnings yield and stays there for six months, exit condition one fires. This is not a distant risk: at today's premium of 0.33%, a 34-basis-point rise in the risk-free rate — a single move well within normal quarterly range — would push it to zero.
  • The fund's annual cost rises above 0.30%. Equal-weight funds carry higher turnover costs than market-cap equivalents. Invesco has held the cost at 0.20% since launch, but there is no permanent guarantee; a cost increase to or above 0.30% would trigger an exit under condition two.
  • Assets under management fall below $500 million. A severe market drawdown combined with redemptions could bring the fund's scale to the level at which UCITS fund-closure risk is elevated. The fund currently has $1.64 billion; a 70% drawdown from today's level would reach the floor. That scenario requires both a large market loss and significant redemptions simultaneously.
  • The MSCI Equal Weighted methodology changes materially. If MSCI restructures the index — shifting to factor weighting, removing the quarterly rebalance, or narrowing the universe — the product being held is no longer the product the thesis was written for. (For this fund, exit fires when the share price reaches the exit edge at $9.6, when a vehicle-integrity condition breaches, or when the named exit-condition trigger fires — any of three paths.)

The Rules

The pre-committed rules for every combination of conditions and price are:

  • If the thesis is confirmed and the basket is fairly priced, hold — the equal-weight basket is reasonably priced, the vehicle is sound, and the equal-weight earnings-yield premium over cap-weighted global indices is intact.
  • If the thesis is weakened — any driver reads as adversely as the written thresholds allow without crossing an exit — do not add. Re-validate after the next quarter. If the equal-weight earnings-yield premium over the cap-weighted alternative compresses below 0.5 percentage points for two quarters running, the diversification benefit no longer justifies the higher running cost versus the cap-weighted alternative, and consolidation into the cap-weighted fund should be considered.
  • If the thesis is broken — any written exit condition fires, or vehicle integrity is breached — exit the full position. Move the proceeds to the cap-weighted global fund or to risk-free.
  • If the share price falls to or below the buy edge at $4.6, add to the position. The basket would be meaningfully cheap relative to the risk-free rate at that level; the structural earnings-yield premium over the cap-weighted alternative should make such an opportunity rare and worth acting on decisively.
  • If the share price rises to or above the trim edge at $7.2, trim 25 to 33% of the position. The basket would be meaningfully expensive relative to the risk-free rate; harvest the premium and redeploy on the next rebalance.
  • If the share price reaches or exceeds the exit edge at $9.6, exit the full position. The basket's earnings yield would be at or below 3.0% — fully priced relative to the risk-free rate. Redeploy proceeds to risk-free or to a holding that still meets the value test.

Scenario Definitions

A scenario is a joint assumption about two independent variables: operating trajectory (how the holding's underlying cash flows grow relative to their long-run trend) and exit multiple (what the market pays for those cash flows at the horizon, relative to a fair-value anchor).

Scenario Operating trajectory Exit multiple
Bear Below trend Below fair value
Base At trend At fair value
Bull Above trend At or above fair value

Non-negotiable rule: In the bear case, both dimensions must be adverse simultaneously — a scenario where only the multiple moves is a re-rating scenario, not a bear.

Bear cases are comparable in structure across all holding types (company, ETF, bond), but not in magnitude. A company bear reflects idiosyncratic execution failure; an ETF bear reflects a broad market drawdown. Different mitigations, different diversification implications.
