As of 2026-07-07, the basket's trailing earnings yield (the share of every dollar of market value represented by underlying corporate profits) stands at 4.12%, while the 10-year US Treasury yields 4.53%. The sole stress point remains the spread. Whether it resolves in the basket's favour — through equity prices falling or corporate earnings rising — will determine whether the Q3 2026 quarterly gate is triggered. For the full picture on the pre-committed orders, see the Standing Orders.

About the Fund

All World is iShares' exchange-traded fund tracking the MSCI World Index — a float-adjusted, market-capitalisation-weighted index covering large- and mid-cap equities across 23 developed markets. The index is roughly 70% US and 30% rest-of-developed-world (Japan, United Kingdom, France, Germany, Switzerland, Canada, and others), spanning approximately 1,300 holdings. The mandate is passive: the fund owns what the index owns, weighted as the index weights it, with no active stock selection.

The role All World plays in a portfolio is distinct from an S&P 500 fund. By including the non-US developed markets as roughly 30% of the basket, it dilutes the regime risk of a purely American equity allocation — if US valuations compress while European or Japanese equities hold their ground, All World absorbs the blow less severely than a US-only fund would. The trade-off is that its dominant 70% US weight means it is not a hedge against expensive US equity pricing so much as a partial diversifier of it.

Cost. Annual expense ratio: 0.24%. Securities-lending offset unconfirmed; tracking metrics absent — see Gaps in the Picture. No K-1 is issued (Delaware statutory trust structure).

The Case for the Bet

The thesis is not that All World is always a buy. The bet, stated plainly, is that over the holding period the basket's look-through earnings yield — the share of every dollar of market value represented by underlying corporate profits — will exceed the risk-free rate by at least 2 percentage points. When that condition holds, equities in this basket are doing what equities are supposed to do: compensating for their risk. When it does not, the pre-committed action plan has specific responses — set out at the end in the Rules.

Six numbered conditions must keep holding for the bet to work:

  1. The basket's earnings yield exceeds the 10-year US Treasury yield by at least 2 percentage points for any given quarter, and does not remain negative for two consecutive quarterly readings. This is the core value condition. An earnings yield below the risk-free rate means the basket's look-through profits are rewarding shareholders less generously than a no-risk government bond. Two consecutive quarterly confirmations of negative spread — not just a day or a week — are required before a sell is called.

Latest reading: As of 2026-07-07, basket earnings yield is 4.12% and the 10-year Treasury yields 4.53% — a spread of −0.41 percentage points. This is the ninth consecutive negative reading (2026-06-16 through 2026-07-07), and the widest yet recorded. Daily and weekly readings confirm persistence, but the quarterly gate has not yet been reached. The next quarterly snapshot is due at end of the third quarter of 2026.

  1. BlackRock's iShares does not raise the annual expense ratio above 0.35% without a compensating fee response. At 0.24% today, the fund already runs above the equivalent UCITS structure (0.20%). A further increase without a fee cut signals cost drift in a direction the cheaper alternative does not require.

Latest reading: Expense ratio confirmed at 0.24% as of 2026-07-07. The 0.35% ceiling leaves 0.11 percentage points of headroom. Exit condition inactive.

  1. Assets under management remain above $2.0 billion. Below this level, bid-ask spreads on a global blend fund typically widen materially. The threshold is a practical liquidity floor, not a closure prediction.

Latest reading: Assets under management approximately $8.1 billion as of 2026-07-07 — roughly four times the floor. Average daily dollar volume approximately $232 million. Exit condition inactive.

  1. The single largest holding in the basket does not exceed 10% of total assets. At roughly 10% in a global index, a single company crossing that threshold signals a regime change — the index is no longer measuring a broadly diversified basket.

Latest reading: NVIDIA holds 5.17% of the basket as of 2026-07-07, well below the 10% ceiling. Exit condition inactive.

  1. MSCI Inc. does not materially restructure the MSCI World Index in a way that undermines the developed-markets mandate. The threshold examples: country reclassification, dropping major developed-market constituents, or shifting away from float-adjusted market-capitalisation weighting.

Latest reading: No automated signal for MSCI World index methodology changes. This condition is monitored externally; no alert has been received.

  1. The median return on equity of the basket's top holdings remains above 15%. A collapse below 15% would mean the most influential companies have lost their capacity to compound capital efficiently — the basket would no longer function as a compounding vehicle, only for market exposure.

Latest reading: Median return on equity of the top holdings is 37.28% as of 2026-07-07, more than double the 15% exit floor. The weighted-mean figure of 70.42% is materially above the median because NVIDIA and Apple carry buyback-inflated return on equity figures; the median is the load-bearing read. Exit condition inactive.

What Could Still Go Wrong

  • The quarterly gate triggers in Q3 2026. If the 10-year Treasury stays at or above the basket's earnings yield through end of September 2026, exit condition (1) fires and a sell is called. There is no discretion — the order is pre-committed.
  • US equity valuations contract sharply. The basket is roughly 70% US stocks. A sustained rerating toward historical averages — from 24× trailing earnings toward 16–18× — would produce years of weak or negative returns even with continued earnings growth. The bear case (3% EPS growth, terminal multiple of 14×) implies −6.1% a year over five years.
  • A single mega-cap company dominates the basket. NVIDIA's weight has been rising. At the 10% ceiling, the basket ceases to function as a diversified vehicle — exit condition (4) covers this.
  • The expense ratio drifts upward. URTH already charges a 4-basis-point premium over the comparable UCITS structure. A further increase widens the cost gap.
  • MSCI changes the index methodology. Monitored externally; no current signal. No automated detection exists in the pipeline, so a material change could go unnoticed until the next manual review.

(For this fund, an exit fires when the share price reaches the exit edge, when a vehicle-integrity condition breaches, or when the named earnings-yield persistence trigger fires — any of three paths.)

The Rules

If the basket is reasonably priced and the vehicle is sound, hold — basket is reasonably priced and the vehicle is sound.

If the thesis is wobbling — any condition challenges the bet — do not add. Re-validate after the next quarter. If the earnings-yield spread stays negative or the median look-through return on equity keeps falling, swap to the UCITS-structure equivalent (0.20% expense ratio) or move to cash per the position-sizing rule.

If the thesis breaks — vehicle integrity breached or an exit condition triggered — exit. Move proceeds to a risk-free instrument or to a holding that meets the value test.

If the share price falls to the cheap edge and no conditions are challenging — add to the position. The basket is meaningfully cheap relative to risk-free.

If the share price rises to the trim edge — trim 25–33%. The basket is meaningfully expensive relative to risk-free; harvest the premium.

If the share price rises to the exit edge — exit. The basket's earnings yield at that level falls to 3.0% or below — fully priced relative to risk-free. Redeploy proceeds to a risk-free instrument or to a holding that meets the value test.
