Recent Development

The three months ending in March 2026 told a story of a healthy travel engine running into one localised disruption. Gross bookings grew 15% in dollar terms and 8% in constant-currency terms, with room nights up 6% overall — but the company estimated the Middle East conflict subtracted roughly 2 percentage points from that growth, and the month of March alone saw room-night growth slow to 1% as cancellations spiked at the conflict's onset. Strip out that isolated shock and the underlying growth rate is closer to 8%, which is consistent with the thesis. The take-rate — revenue as a percentage of gross bookings — nudged up from 10.2% to 10.3%, driven by payment-facilitation revenue, a small but directionally correct signal that pricing power is intact.

Capital return ran at full throttle: $4.0 billion in share buybacks and $343 million in dividends in a single quarter, against $18.2 billion of remaining buyback authorisation. The wobbles are real but contained, as described in What's Wobbling. The AI strategy remains the structural overhang: the quarterly filing described the Connected Trip vision in narrative terms but disclosed no concrete AI product metrics. The deadline for a coherent, measurable AI-first strategy is now five quarters away. That is not a crisis today; it is a clock that is running. The net picture — a business delivering 16% revenue growth, expanding take-rates, and $4.4 billion returned to shareholders in a single quarter — supports the assessment in The Bottom Line that the bet is working.

About Booking Holdings

Booking Holdings is the world's largest online travel intermediary. Its flagship platform, Booking.com, lists approximately 4.5 million properties — from hotels to apartments, villas, and treehouses — across more than 220 countries. Travellers use it to search, compare, and book accommodation; the platform earns a commission on each completed reservation. Alongside Booking.com, the group operates Priceline (a named-price deal platform, primarily in the US), Agoda (accommodation and flights focused on Asia), Kayak (a travel-search aggregator), and OpenTable (restaurant reservations and dining experiences).

The business model is an agency-and-merchant marketplace: for agency bookings, the platform collects a commission from the property after the guest checks out; for merchant bookings, it collects the full payment from the guest at the point of booking and remits the property's share later. The merchant model has been growing faster and now accounts for the majority of gross bookings. Both paths earn a take-rate — revenue divided by total bookings processed — which ran at 10.3% in the three months ending in March 2026, up slightly from the prior year.

In full-year 2025, the company generated $26.9 billion in revenue, $9.1 billion in free cash flow (cash from operations minus capital expenditure), and a 34% free cash flow margin. Capital requirements are minimal — technology and office spending amount to just over 1% of revenue — and the company has returned more than $54 billion to shareholders through share buybacks over its history. At the current pace of roughly $6 billion in annual buybacks, the outstanding share count shrinks by approximately 4% per year, which compounds per-share earnings at a rate above headline revenue growth.

The Case for the Bet

The central bet is that Booking.com's two-sided marketplace network — a web of 4.5 million listed properties and the traveller demand needed to fill them — is durable enough to survive the pressures arrayed against it: AI-powered trip-planning agents that could route travellers directly to hotel websites, Google's expanding presence in travel search, and hotel chains pushing direct-booking programmes. The supporting bet is that management runs a disciplined capital-return machine — returning $7 to $9 billion a year in cash to shareholders via buybacks and dividends — that compounds per-share value even in a slower-growth scenario.

Seven specific conditions must hold for the bet to remain intact:

  1. Room-night growth must stay above low-single-digit or negative territory across multiple regions for no more than one consecutive quarter absent an isolated shock. The network effect is measured by traveller demand. If room nights booked turn negative or near-zero across Europe, the US, and Asia simultaneously for two or more quarters without an identifiable one-off cause, that is the clearest signal the network is leaking.

Latest reading: Q1 2026 room nights grew 5.9% overall (338 million vs. 319 million a year earlier). Management estimates the Middle East conflict subtracted approximately 2 percentage points; the adjusted rate is roughly 8%. March alone slowed to 1% as cancellations spiked at the conflict's onset. This is one quarter, one region, one identifiable cause — the exit threshold (two quarters, multiple regions, absent isolated shock) is not met.

  1. Marketing spend as a percentage of gross bookings must not rise meaningfully for two or more consecutive years. A sustained increase signals that the company is having to buy growth that the network moat previously produced for free — the definition of moat erosion.

Latest reading: Marketing expenses were 3.8% of gross bookings in Q1 2026, flat against the same quarter a year earlier. The Middle East conflict degraded performance marketing returns on investment (some bookings sourced through paid channels were subsequently cancelled), but management reports that excluding the conflict, the quarter showed modest marketing leverage. Search-engine-optimisation traffic is declining structurally, which is a watch item, but one quarter of flat-to-slightly-elevated spend does not trigger the multi-year threshold.

  1. Revenue as a percentage of gross bookings (the take-rate) must not compress materially or persistently. Compression signals that competitors, regulators, or AI agents are squeezing the economics of intermediation.

Latest reading: The take-rate was 10.3% in Q1 2026, up from 10.2% a year earlier. Payment-facilitation revenue drove the improvement; the higher-margin-dilutive mix from flight bookings partly offset it. Directionally positive.

  1. Capital-return discipline must hold — buybacks must not slow or stop while the earnings yield is attractive, and the authorisation must not be redirected into a large acquisition or culture-shifting pivot. This condition protects the per-share compounding engine.

Latest reading: Q1 2026 saw $4.0 billion in buybacks and $343 million in dividends. The remaining authorisation stands at $18.2 billion (of a $20 billion programme). No acquisition was announced. This exit condition is not triggered.

  1. The earnings yield must not fall below 4%. At a yield below 4%, the market is pricing in optimistic compounding and the per-share maths no longer justify the AI-disintermediation risk. The earnings yield is the business's annual free cash flow divided by its market value — the inverse of a price-to-earnings multiple.

Latest reading: The earnings yield is 6.0% at the current price of $182, well above the 4% trim threshold. The trim edge corresponds to a share price of $273 — the price would have to rise 50% from today before this condition approached its threshold.

  1. By the third quarter of 2027, management must have articulated and visibly executed a coherent AI-first strategy with concrete shipped product showing measurable engagement, direct-traffic, and margin uplift. The current Connected Trip approach is a vision statement, not yet a strategy with measurable outcomes.

Latest reading: The latest quarterly filing describes the Connected Trip as an AI-powered traveller experience but reports no AI-specific product engagement metrics, no direct-traffic lift attributable to AI features, and no explicit acknowledgement of disintermediation risk. Mobile app usage advanced (from mid-fifties to high-fifties as a share of room nights booked over the trailing twelve months), and technology spending grew 20%. The deadline is five quarters away — the clock is running.

  1. EU Digital Markets Act or Digital Services Act enforcement must not result in a formal structural remedy or fine exceeding 3% of annual global revenue in a single year. The European regulatory overhang becomes a structural economic impairment at this threshold (approximately $810 million at 2025 revenues).

The Italian competition authority opened a separate investigation in April 2026. The Spanish fine ($476 million on the balance sheet) is under appeal. None of these individually crosses the threshold. A separate April 2026 data-breach notification to authorities adds a compliance cost but is not a structural product remedy.

The Share Price

The share price sits in the middle of the hold band, modestly below fair value. In plain terms: at $182, Booking is neither cheap enough to trigger the buy rule nor expensive enough to trigger the trim rule. The earnings yield — the business's annual free cash flow divided by the company's market value — is 6.0%. The fair-value yield is 5.5%, implying a fair-value share price of approximately $199. The buy edge sits at $156 (a 7.0% yield), and the trim edge at $273 (a 4.0% yield).

There is a separate bottom-up estimate of fair value — derived from applying a market multiple to forecast free cash flow — that puts the base-case figure closer to $251 to $342. The gap between the yield-based $199 and the multiple-based higher range is not a contradiction: the yield method uses today's earnings power as the anchor; the multiple method applies a view about what the market will pay for future earnings growth. Both approaches agree that $182 is below fair value; they disagree on how far below. See Gaps in the Picture for the detail on why the two estimates diverge.

What's Wobbling

Three conditions are showing mixed evidence rather than clean confirmation — none rises to the level of a challenge, but each deserves a quarterly check.

Room-night growth and the Middle East shock. The Q1 2026 growth rate of 6% overall (8% ex-conflict) would normally be unremarkable for a business of this size. The Middle East disruption is real: cancellations spiked in March, and management explicitly flagged that the conflict may soften consumer demand in future quarters. The written exit condition requires low-single-digit or negative growth across multiple regions for two consecutive quarters without an identifiable shock. That threshold is not met. But if the conflict persists or spreads, the one-quarter carve-out cannot protect indefinitely.

Marketing efficiency and the search-engine structural headwind. Booking explicitly disclosed that search-engine-optimisation traffic is declining and is expected to continue declining in the short to medium term, which may require more spending on paid marketing channels. On a headline basis, marketing was flat as a percentage of gross bookings — but the Middle East conflict degraded the return on that spend, and the underlying search-engine erosion is a slow-moving but structural drag. Two consecutive years of rising marketing intensity would trigger the second exit condition; the business is currently one quarter into an ambiguous reading.

AI strategy and the deadline clock. The Connected Trip is Booking's answer to the question of what happens when AI agents become the front door to travel planning. The Q1 2026 filing narrated the vision with some precision — personalised trip planning, in-trip services, loyalty integration — but reported no metrics for AI-driven engagement, conversion, or direct-traffic uplift. The mobile app's share of room nights is advancing, which is a useful proxy, but it predates AI. The deadline is Q3 2027, five quarters away. That is time enough for management to ship evidence; it is also time enough for the gap between the vision and the measurable to become awkward.

What Could Still Go Wrong

  • AI-mediated disintermediation. Google's agentic booking capabilities, and similar tools from other large language-model providers, route trip-planning traffic directly to hotel websites, collapsing Booking's paid-search return on investment. Marketing costs inflate from 3.8% of gross bookings toward 5%, compressing margins permanently. This is the bear case, carrying a 25% probability, and implies roughly a 34% decline from the current price.

  • EU regulatory enforcement beyond compliance cost. The Digital Markets Act self-preferencing inquiry escalates to a formal investigation, with fines approaching 10% of global revenue (approximately $2.7 billion). A forced structural remedy — mandatory removal of rate-parity clauses, for example — could constrain the take-rate structurally rather than just imposing a one-time fine. At current free cash flow of $9 billion annually, a $2.7 billion fine is survivable. A permanent take-rate impairment would not be.

  • Google's travel-search dominance tightens further. Google's hotel and flight modules continue to capture a larger fraction of travel search at zero marginal cost to the traveller, forcing Booking into higher-cost paid channels to maintain visibility. This operates on a slower timeline than AI disruption but through a more familiar mechanism.

  • A large, value-destroying acquisition. The $18.2 billion remaining buyback authorisation could be redirected. Booking's history of buybacks over acquisitions is a feature of the investment case; a major acquisition at a high price would alter the capital-allocation character of the business.

  • Macro travel demand shock. The COVID episode proved Booking can survive severe demand contractions and recover quickly — revenue in 2021 was roughly half the pre-2020 level; full recovery took eighteen months. A repeat shock would compress earnings sharply in the near term without threatening the moat's long-run durability.

Gaps in the Picture

  • Two fair-value anchors coexist in the underlying analysis: the yield-method estimate of $199 (based on today's earnings power) and the bottom-up scenario analysis that puts the base-case central estimate between $251 and $342 (using forecast free cash flow multiplied by a market multiple). Both appear in the underlying documents. The $199 figure is the one used in The Standing Orders and the conviction-score calculation; the higher range reflects what the analysis believes the business is worth if base-case growth materialises. A future methodology consolidation will resolve the divergence — until then, both figures coexist.

  • The share-count divergence between the official filing record (32.6 million shares in the structured filing data) and the market-implied count (774.9 million) reflects a 25-for-1 stock split that completed in April 2026. The underlying analysis uses the market-implied count, and the yield calculation is invariant to which count is used (it divides earnings by market capitalisation, not by share count). No distortion to the economics.

  • Earnings are calculated as operating cash flow minus total capital expenditure, net of stock-based compensation. Maintenance capital expenditure is not separately disclosed in the filings; depreciation and amortisation are used as a proxy. For an asset-light digital platform, true maintenance capital is likely below depreciation, which means the earnings figure likely understates the true cash the business generates. The stated yield of 6.0% is therefore conservative.

  • The risk-free rate used in the conviction-score hurdle is sourced from a US equity market data snapshot (the German linker yield is the standard anchor for the portfolio). The hurdle rate of 4.88% reflects this approach.

The Rules

If the thesis is confirmed — all conditions are working, no exit condition triggered, price in the hold band — hold. No action. Continue compounding.

If the thesis is weakened — one or more conditions are deteriorating but no exit condition has fired — do not add to the position. Run a check on the specific deteriorating condition after the next two quarterly filings; one quarter of data, then confirm or clear with a second.

If the thesis is broken — a written exit condition has fired — exit the full position within 30 days. No averaging down. Note which exit condition fired for future reference.

If the price falls below the buy edge of $156 — and the thesis is confirmed intact first — add to the position in tranches over one to two quarters.

If the price rises above the trim edge of $273 — trim 25 to 33% of the position to harvest the premium while keeping the core holding. Re-evaluate the remaining position size after the trim.

If the price rises above the exit edge of $437 — exit the full position. The market is pricing in roughly 6 years of base-case compounding instantly; redeploy proceeds at the next cheap-edge crossing or into a cheaper alternative. No re-entry within 90 days.
