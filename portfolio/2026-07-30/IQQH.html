About Clean Energy

The iShares Global Clean Energy Transition UCITS ETF (IQQH) tracks the S&P Global Clean Energy Transition Index, a basket of roughly 100 companies involved in the production, storage, and enabling technology of clean energy — solar panels, wind turbines, grid-connected electricity storage, electrification infrastructure, and the industrial equipment that underpins all of it. The fund gives exposure to the companies expected to benefit most from two durable structural forces: regulatory mandates (the European Union's Green Deal and the United States Inflation Reduction Act, both of which direct capital toward low-carbon energy), and the falling cost of generating electricity from sun and wind, which now undercuts fossil-fuel alternatives in most geographies. The fund is domiciled in Ireland and operates under the UCITS regulatory framework, managed by BlackRock's iShares business — the world's largest exchange-traded fund provider. The annual cost of holding the fund is 0.65% of assets.

The basket is not a broad market fund. Three sectors — Technology (34%), Utilities (33%), and Industrials (31%) — account for virtually all of the exposure, reflecting the fund's tight mandate across electrification and renewables. This concentration is by design: the fund is a directional thematic bet, not a diversified market proxy. The largest single holding, Bloom Energy, represents roughly 15% of the basket; two of the top-ten names (Plug Power and SolarEdge Technologies) are currently loss-making, which is a persistent structural feature of the clean-energy universe rather than a new development. The fund carries no tracking-difference or tracking-error data in the current snapshot — those figures would give a cleaner picture of how faithfully the vehicle replicates its index at minimal cost.

Tax profile. Wrapper type: UCITS (Irish-domiciled); no K-1. Details on reporting-fund status and capital-gain distribution history are not yet confirmed — see Gaps in the Picture.

The Case for the Bet

The holding rests on a single structural conviction: that the global shift of capital toward clean energy is a durable, decade-long trend supported by legislative mandates and the economics of renewable power generation. The tradeable question is whether the specific conditions sustaining that trend remain in place:

  1. Earnings yield trap not engaged. The primary exit trigger requires the basket's earnings yield to stay above 8.0% for four consecutive quarters while the index suffers persistent capital losses — a pattern that would signal constituent-fundamentals collapse rather than a cheap-buying opportunity.

Latest reading: Basket earnings yield 4.59% as of 2026-07-30. The 8.0% trap threshold is 341 basis points away. Exit condition (1) is not engaged.

  1. Vehicle integrity intact. The fund must stay viable as a holding instrument: the annual expense ratio must remain below 0.80%, assets under management must stay above €200 million, no single constituent may exceed 20% of the basket, and BlackRock must not initiate a product wind-down.

Latest reading: Annual expense ratio 0.65% (0.80% ceiling, 15 basis points of headroom). Assets under management €4.17 billion — more than 20 times the €200 million floor. Largest single constituent (Bloom Energy) at 14.76%, against the 20% ceiling. All vehicle-integrity checks: clean as of 2026-07-30.

  1. Index methodology unchanged. If S&P materially restructures the S&P Global Clean Energy Transition Index — redefining its inclusion criteria, narrowing its sector scope, or changing its weighting scheme — the economic exposure this position was intended to own would change. That constitutes exit condition (3).

Latest reading: No automated signal for methodology drift as of 2026-07-30. The fund family (BlackRock / iShares) and legal structure (UCITS Exchange Traded Fund) are unchanged. This driver requires external monitoring of S&P's index-governance alerts.

  1. Regulatory tailwind intact. A durable, legislated repeal of the Inflation Reduction Act's clean-energy tax credits or of the European Union's Green Deal binding targets would remove the policy underwriting the capital-expenditure cycle. A temporary policy pause does not qualify; a legislated reversal does.

Latest reading: No automated signal. External monitoring required. Policy continuity is the load-bearing assumption for the five- to ten-year horizon.

  1. Structural cost advantage of renewables persists. If solar and wind power cease to undercut fossil-fuel alternatives in the majority of geographies and capacity additions flatline for two consecutive years, the structural cost-advantage driver is gone — exit condition (5).

Latest reading: No automated signal. This driver requires external monitoring of levelised-cost-of-energy surveys and capacity-addition statistics.

  1. No superior thematic vehicle available. If a lower-fee, broader, or higher-quality clean-energy fund with equivalent regulatory-jurisdiction exposure becomes available and the cost of switching is justified, the current vehicle should be replaced.

What's Wobbling

The equity risk premium is the sole driver reading as mixed, and the wobble is largely a measurement artefact. Using the US ten-year Treasury as the risk-free benchmark, the basket's 4.59% earnings yield sits 0.03 percentage points below the 4.62% Treasury yield — a fractionally negative equity premium. Two consecutive snapshots have now returned a negative reading under this benchmark, which technically satisfies the equity-premium persistence leg of exit condition (1). However, the exit condition additionally requires the basket's earnings yield to exceed 8.0% — the basket is 341 basis points away from that level, so the condition remains firmly unengaged.

The benchmark itself is the more important nuance. IQQH is a euro-denominated fund; comparing its earnings yield against a US dollar interest rate introduces a currency mismatch. Substituting the German ten-year government bond — the more appropriate risk-free anchor for a euro-denominated investor — at roughly 2.5% yields a positive equity premium of approximately 2.1 percentage points, comfortably above the 2.0% threshold for a confirm reading. The USD benchmark is tracked for portfolio consistency, and the thesis records this as a known, unresolved gap.

The weighted-mean return on equity across the top-ten holdings is 3.34%, well below the median of 14.87%. This divergence is a persistent structural feature: two loss-making constituents — Plug Power (return on equity −129%) and SolarEdge Technologies (return on equity −73%) — each contribute negative values that drag the average far below the median. The load-bearing read is the median, which clears the lowered 10% confirm floor set for this holding. Nothing here crosses any of the lines in the Case for the Bet.

What Could Still Go Wrong

  • Cost creep. The 0.65% annual expense ratio sits 15 basis points below the 0.80% hard ceiling. If iShares raises the fee — through a fund restructure or a removal of fee waivers — the vehicle-integrity check fires. At 0.80% or above, the exit rule for thesis broken applies.
  • Asset drain below €200 million. IQQH currently manages €4.17 billion, more than 20 times the survival floor. A sustained investor outflow from clean-energy thematic funds — plausible after a prolonged drawdown or a major policy reversal — could erode this buffer. At €200 million assets under management, the vehicle-integrity check fires and the fund becomes a closure risk.
  • Single-name concentration. Bloom Energy at 14.76% is comfortably below the 20% ceiling, but intra-quarter price moves before the next quarterly rebalance could push the weight toward the threshold. A spike in Bloom Energy's share price without a corresponding move in the rest of the basket would trigger the concentration check.
  • Index methodology restructure. S&P's decision to alter the S&P Global Clean Energy Transition Index — narrowing its sector scope, changing its weighting scheme, or redefining which companies qualify — would change the economic exposure this position was intended to hold. This is exit condition (3) and has no automated detection.
  • Policy reversal. A legislated repeal of the Inflation Reduction Act's clean-energy tax credits or of the European Union's Green Deal binding targets would remove the regulatory underpinning of the decarbonisation capital-expenditure cycle. This is exit condition (4). A temporary policy pause does not qualify; a durable statutory change does.
  • Bear case: simultaneous operating and multiple compression. The five-year bear scenario requires earnings per share for the basket to fall 17% and the price-to-earnings multiple to compress from 21.77 to 15 — both a fundamental deterioration and a re-rating event. At −24% annualised, the bear outcome is severe. The basket is best understood as a directional thematic bet, not a conventional margin-of-safety holding. (For this fund, exit fires when the share price reaches the exit edge at €14, when a vehicle-integrity condition breaches, or when the named exit-condition trigger fires — any of three paths.)

The Rules

The rules that govern what action follows from each state of the evaluation, set in advance:

  • If the thematic thesis is confirmed — all drivers confirm or give no signal, vehicle is sound — then hold. Do not size up on the earnings-yield signal alone; add only if the decarbonisation capital-expenditure case is actively strengthening.
  • If the thesis is weakened — any driver signals a challenge but no exit condition is triggered — then do not add. Re-validate after the next quarter and after any material policy development. If exit conditions three through five are approaching, accelerate the review timeline.
  • If the thesis is broken — a vehicle-integrity condition is breached or any written exit condition fires — then exit. Move proceeds to risk-free or into a broad-market replacement.
  • If the share price falls to the buy band (basket earnings yield at or above 6.25%) — then add, provided the basket earnings yield is below 8.0% and the thematic thesis is intact. An earnings yield above 8.0% coinciding with persistent capital losses is exit condition (1); verify before adding.
  • If the share price rises to the trim band (basket earnings yield at or below 4.0%) — then trim 25 to 33 per cent. The basket is meaningfully expensive relative to risk-free. Move proceeds to risk-free or to a broad-market replacement.
  • If the share price rises to the exit band (basket earnings yield at or below 3.0%) — then exit. The basket is fully priced versus risk-free. Redeploy proceeds to risk-free or to a holding that meets the value test.

Scenario Definitions

A scenario is a joint assumption about two independent variables: operating trajectory (how the holding's underlying cash flows grow relative to their long-run trend) and exit multiple (what the market pays for those cash flows at the horizon, relative to a fair-value anchor).

Scenario Operating trajectory Exit multiple
Bear Below trend Below fair value
Base At trend At fair value
Bull Above trend At or above fair value

Non-negotiable rule: In the bear case, both dimensions must be adverse simultaneously — a scenario where only the multiple moves is a re-rating scenario, not a bear.

Bear cases are comparable in structure across all holding types (company, ETF, bond), but not in magnitude. A company bear reflects idiosyncratic execution failure; an ETF bear reflects a broad market drawdown. Different mitigations, different diversification implications.
