About the Fund

SPY is the SPDR S&P 500 ETF Trust, launched in January 1993 as the first US-listed exchange-traded fund and managed by State Street Investment Management. It holds all 500 companies in the S&P 500 index in proportion to their market capitalisation — larger companies get a bigger slice of the basket — and passes their combined earnings, dividends, and price moves through to shareholders in a single tradeable unit.

The S&P 500 index itself is maintained by S&P Dow Jones Indices, a subsidiary of S&P Global. Membership requires a company to be US-domiciled, publicly listed, and profitable on a trailing basis; the committee reviews the list continuously and rebalances quarterly. Technology stocks currently make up roughly 39% of the basket by weight, driven by the dominant market values of companies such as Nvidia, Apple, Alphabet, and Microsoft — this is a structural feature of a cap-weighted index in a technology-heavy era, not a deliberate tilt by the fund.

The fund uses a Unit Investment Trust (UIT) structure — the oldest US ETF wrapper, predating the more common open-end fund format used by its main competitors. A UIT cannot reinvest dividends between distribution dates; it holds a small cash sleeve (currently 0.11%) to manage this lag. The annual running cost is 0.0945% — about nine-and-a-half cents per hundred dollars invested per year, among the lowest of any large-cap US equity fund. No K-1 tax form is issued. Annual cost: 0.09% expense ratio. The Case for the Bet

The bet is that over the holding period, the basket's look-through earnings yield — what the underlying companies collectively earn per dollar of share price — will exceed the 10-year Treasury yield by at least 2 percentage points (the equity risk premium, or the extra return demanded for bearing equity risk instead of holding risk-free bonds). When that premium exists and the vehicle is sound, the basket is worth holding. When it disappears for long enough, it is not.

Conditions that must keep holding:

  1. The equity risk premium (basket earnings yield minus 10-year Treasury yield) must remain at or above 0% on average across two consecutive calendar quarters. Condition (1) fires if this premium is negative for two consecutive quarters.
  2. State Street must not raise the expense ratio above 0.15%. Condition (2) fires if the fee rises past this ceiling without a competitive response.
  3. Assets under management (the total size of the fund) must remain above $100 billion. Condition (3) fires if an extraordinary outflow event pushes the fund below this floor.
  4. The single largest holding must not exceed 13% of the basket by weight. Condition (4) fires if concentration reaches this level — a reading that would signal a regime-change in the index's structure, not ordinary market drift.
  5. S&P Dow Jones Indices must not materially restructure the S&P 500 index methodology in a way that undermines its broad-market mandate. Condition (5) fires if such a restructuring is announced.
  6. The median return on equity (a measure of how profitably the top ten companies deploy shareholder capital) among the basket's ten largest holdings must not fall below 15%. Condition (6) fires if the quality of the basket's largest names collapses.

Any of conditions (1) through (6) firing is treated as a vehicle-integrity breach, triggering the full-exit rule.

Early-warning bands (conditions 2–4): - Condition 2: early-warning flag below 0.12% expense ratio (current 0.0945%). - Condition 3: early-warning flag below $300B in assets under management (current $784B). - Condition 4: early-warning flag above 10% for the single largest holding (current NVIDIA at 7.89%).

The Rules

These are the five pre-committed branches:

  1. If all conditions hold and the basket is at a normal valuation: hold — the basket is reasonably priced and the vehicle is sound.
  2. If any condition is wobbling (but no exit has fired): do not add. Re-validate after the next quarter. If the equity risk premium stays negative or the median look-through return on equity keeps falling, swap to a comparable fund (such as Vanguard's VOO or BlackRock's IVV) or move the proceeds to cash per the position-sizing rule.
  3. If an exit condition fires or vehicle integrity is breached: exit the full position. Move proceeds to risk-free instruments or to a holding that passes the value test.
  4. If the basket is priced below the buy-edge yield of 6.25% (equivalent to about $447 per share today): add to the position — the basket is meaningfully cheap relative to risk-free.
  5. If the basket is priced above the trim-edge yield of 4% (equivalent to about $698 per share today): trim 25–33% — the basket is meaningfully expensive relative to risk-free; harvest the premium.

What's Wobbling

The equity risk premium is the live concern. The gap between the basket's earnings yield (3.75%) and the 10-year Treasury yield (4.51%) has been negative since 2026-06-16 — four snapshots in a row, all in the same quarter. The gap is not shrinking: the risk-free rate rose six basis points between the 2026-06-21 and 2026-06-23 snapshots, while the basket's earnings yield ticked up only one basis point. The trend, such as it can be called a trend across four days, is that Treasuries are pulling further ahead.

The exit condition's clock does not run on days. It runs on calendar quarters. The current quarter's readings satisfy one of the two required consecutive quarters of negative risk premium. If the first reading of the quarter beginning in September 2026 also shows a negative risk premium, exit condition (1) fires — and the action that follows is to close the full position, not to trim.

Nothing else is wobbling. The vehicle's structural conditions are all solidly inside their limits. The sole live signal is the equity risk premium, and that signal is at the wobbling stage, not the broken stage.

What Could Break the Fund

  • Equity risk premium persists negative. If the 10-year Treasury yield stays above the basket's earnings yield through a second consecutive calendar quarter, exit condition (1) fires and the position closes regardless of the share price at that point.
  • Multiple compression at high starting valuation. All three forward-return scenarios (bear, base, and bull) assume the basket's current price-to-earnings multiple of 26.7 contracts from today's level — because all three terminal multiples are set below today's starting level. In the bear case (terminal multiple of 15, reverting toward the long-run historical average), the five-year annualised return is −6.9%; earnings growth of 3% per year cannot offset a contraction of that magnitude.
  • Concentration in technology. Technology stocks make up 39% of the basket by market weight — a structural feature of the cap-weighted S&P 500 in the current era, not a discretionary choice. A sharp, sustained de-rating of the technology sector (regulatory action, AI-investment reversal, interest-rate sensitivity) would disproportionately affect this basket versus an equal-weighted or sector-diversified alternative.
  • Macro shock to the risk-free rate. A further rise in 10-year Treasury yields would simultaneously widen the negative equity-risk-premium gap (accelerating the exit clock) and compress the basket's price (since higher discount rates reduce present values). Both effects are negative for SPY holders at today's prices.
  • Index methodology change or sponsor operational failure. Conditions (5) and (2) guard against these. Both are monitored manually; no automated signal is available for S&P DJI methodology bulletins.
