About the Fund

The Vanguard U.S. Treasury 0–1 Year Bond UCITS ETF holds a basket of US government Treasury bills — short-term sovereign debt instruments issued by the United States Treasury — with remaining maturities of zero to one year. The fund is domiciled in Ireland, listed on the London Stock Exchange, and managed by Vanguard Group (Ireland) Limited. It uses an accumulating share class, meaning income from the underlying bills is reinvested rather than paid out as cash.

With a weighted-average maturity of roughly 4.5 months across the basket, the fund behaves almost like a money-market account: its price drifts gently upward as the reinvested yield accumulates, and it has no meaningful sensitivity to longer-term interest-rate movements. The annual cost is 0.07% of the value held — seven pence per year on every £100 invested — well within the threshold that would make a cheaper alternative worth switching to. No top-holding or sector data is available from standard market sources for this fund, which reflects a structural quirk of how UK-listed UCITS funds are reported rather than any opacity on the part of the issuer.

The Case for the Bet

The bet is that US Treasury bills represent the best available USD cash-equivalent for the portfolio at low cost, while no equity holding is available at a price that clearly beats the return on near-cash. The cost of holding this optionality is 0.07% a year. Three conditions must keep holding.

Condition 1 — The annual charges figure stays below 0.10%.

If the ongoing charges figure rises above 0.10% and stays there for two consecutive quarterly reviews, cheaper alternatives — such as other short-duration Treasury funds with lower fees — become available, and the fee advantage is lost. Exit condition (1) fires.

Latest reading: The charges figure is confirmed at 0.07%, sourced from the Vanguard published rate. This is 3 basis points below the threshold and is not trending higher. Exit condition (1) is inactive.

Condition 2 — The fund's total assets stay above $1 billion.

If total assets fall below $1 billion, the risk of unusual liquidity stress or early fund closure rises meaningfully. The fund's primary value proposition is instant liquidity equivalent to holding Treasury bills directly; that proposition deteriorates at very small fund sizes.

Latest reading: Total assets stand at approximately $2.4 billion as verified by Trackinsight in 2026-06. This is 2.4 times the floor, and exit condition (2) is inactive. Manual re-verification against the Vanguard factsheet is required quarterly, as standard market-data services do not reliably report this figure for UK-listed UCITS funds.

Condition 3 — The US sovereign credit rating stays above AA- at two of the three major agencies.

If US sovereign debt is downgraded below AA- by any two of Fitch, Moody's, and S&P, the fundamental premise of holding this fund as a credit-risk-free cash equivalent must be reassessed. Exit condition (3) fires.

Latest reading: No downgrade signal has been detected. The pipeline does not automate credit-rating monitoring; this condition is watched externally via Vanguard regulatory filings and the rating agencies' public announcements. Exit condition (3) is inactive.

What Could Still Go Wrong

The structure of this holding means the risk scenarios are different from an equity position. The main failure modes are:

  • Yield compression through the trim boundary. If Federal Reserve rate cuts push T-bill yields below 3.5%, the yield-carry advantage over EUR cash narrows to the point where the trim rule engages. The current reading is 23 basis points away from this line.
  • Yield compression through the exit boundary. If rates fall to 3.0% or below, the exit rule fires and the position is closed. This would require more aggressive Fed easing than currently priced.
  • Fee creep. If Vanguard raises the annual charges figure to above 0.10% and keeps it there for two consecutive quarterly reviews, the cost advantage over alternatives is lost and exit condition (1) fires. The current figure of 0.07% gives 3 basis points of headroom — narrow in absolute terms, though no upward trend has been observed.
  • Fund-size decline. If total assets fall below $1 billion — whether through redemptions, a market-wide event, or a structural change in how the fund is marketed — liquidity may deteriorate and exit condition (2) fires. Current assets of $2.4 billion are more than twice the floor.
  • US sovereign credit deterioration. A downgrade below AA- by two of the three major agencies would fundamentally change the credit-risk character of the underlying holdings and trigger exit condition (3). This is the lowest-probability scenario at present.

For this fund, exit fires when the share price reaches the exit edge, when a vehicle-integrity condition breaches, or when the named exit-condition trigger fires — any of three paths.

The Rules
