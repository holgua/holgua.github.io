About the Fund

World Equal Weight tracks the MSCI World Equal Weighted Index: roughly 1,500 companies drawn from developed markets worldwide, each receiving the same weight — about 0.07% — on every quarterly rebalance date. The index covers the same universe as the MSCI World Index, but instead of weighting companies by their total market value (which concentrates position in the largest names), it pins every holding near equal footing. The practical effect is a basket that is less US-heavy than cap-weighted MSCI World (where the United States represents roughly 70% of the weight), less tilted toward mega-cap technology, and more evenly spread across sectors — Industrials at 18%, Financial Services at 17%, and Technology at 16% are the three largest groupings, each modest by comparison with the 25–30% technology concentration typical of cap-weighted developed-market indices.

The fund is managed by Invesco and is domiciled in Ireland as a UCITS-regulated, accumulating share class — meaning dividends and income are reinvested automatically rather than paid out. Capital gains are not distributed. No K-1 form is issued (not applicable under UCITS rules). Annual cost is 0.20%, sourced from the Invesco factsheet; securities-lending income is not disclosed, so a net-cost figure cannot be computed.

The Case for the Bet

The core bet is that spreading weight equally across developed-market companies — rather than concentrating it in the largest — produces a structural earnings-yield advantage over cap-weighted indices, compensating for the higher annual turnover and slightly lower average business quality that come with the broader exposure.

For the bet to work, six conditions must hold:

  1. Equity risk premium positive on a two-quarter rolling basis — basket earnings yield above the 10-year risk-free rate for at least one of the last two snapshots. Two consecutive negative readings mean the risk-premium case for equities over bonds has failed. (Early-warning: any single negative reading.)

  2. Annual expense ratio below 0.30% (current 0.20%; early-warning above 0.27%). Equal-weight funds carry higher turnover costs than cap-weighted peers; above 0.30% erodes the structural yield advantage.

  3. Assets under management above €500 million (current ~€1.48 billion; early-warning below €800 million). UCITS funds below the €300–500 million range face elevated closure risk. A drawdown to this floor is a vehicle-survival signal, not a market signal.

  4. Top-1 holding concentration below 1.0% (current: Dell Technologies 0.27%; early-warning above 0.50%). Any drift above 1.0% — roughly 14× the rebalance target weight — signals a methodology failure or extreme price drift not yet corrected.

  5. MSCI Equal Weighted methodology unchanged — no shift to factor-tilted weighting, no removal of the quarterly rebalance, no change of the parent universe.

  6. Median return-on-equity of the visible top-10 above 12% (current 14.1%; early-warning below 15%; exit below 12%). Set lower than the 15% used for cap-weighted funds because equal weighting structurally includes smaller, lower-profitability businesses.

The Rules

Five pre-committed rules determine what follows from each combination of conditions and price:

  1. If all conditions hold and the vehicle is sound — hold. The basket is reasonably priced, the vehicle is sound, the earnings-yield premium over cap-weighted peers is intact.

  2. If one or more conditions are weakening but no hard exit has fired — do not add. Re-validate after the next quarter. If the earnings-yield premium over a cap-weighted alternative compresses below 0.5 percentage points for two consecutive quarters, consider consolidating into a cap-weighted global fund.

  3. If any exit condition fires or the vehicle is structurally breached — exit. Move proceeds to a cap-weighted global equity fund or to risk-free.

  4. If the basket's earnings yield rises above 6.25% (roughly €4.44 per share at current earnings) — add in tranches. This is the buy zone; it should be rare.

  5. If the basket's earnings yield falls below 4.0% (roughly €6.94 per share at current earnings) — trim 25–33%. Harvest the premium and redeploy on the next rebalance.

Recent history is slightly downward (0.35% on 2026-06-17, 0.33% on 2026-06-21, 0.31% on 2026-06-23), though not yet close to the negative territory that would arm the exit condition. The look-through quality metric — median return-on-equity of 14.1% across the visible top-10 — sits in mixed territory by design. The current top-10 is semiconductor-heavy due to intra-quarter price drift, not deliberate overweighting; the full basket's sector mix is far more balanced. The 12% exit threshold is not under pressure.

Neither wobble changes the call. Equal weighting accepts mixed quality and thin spread in exchange for genuine diversification away from mega-cap concentration.

What Could Break the Fund

  • Earnings yield falls to the trim line. Below 4.0% earnings yield (above roughly €6.94 per share), the trim order activates — the basket would be priced at roughly 25 times earnings, expensive by historical standards.

  • Equity risk premium turns negative for two quarters. At current levels (0.31 percentage points of spread), a 35-basis-point rise in the Treasury yield without a matching rise in earnings would flip the sign and start the two-quarter exit clock.

  • Fund closure from asset-base decline. A sustained drawdown toward the €500 million floor triggers the vehicle-survival exit. The current €1.48 billion provides meaningful but not unlimited cushion.

  • Invesco raises the annual cost above 0.30%. The current 0.20% is competitive; above 0.30% erodes the structural yield advantage over cap-weighted alternatives.

  • MSCI restructures the methodology. A shift away from equal weighting, removal of the quarterly rebalance, or a change of the parent universe would invalidate the structural thesis. This risk is qualitative and externally monitored.
