Ferrari N.V. (RACE) — Growth Analysis

Analyst: Growth-Focused Equity Lens | Date: 2026-07-21 | Source files: RACE-Financial-Brief.md, RACE-Market-Data.md, RACE-Political-Exposure.md

SECTION 1 — GROWTH INVESTOR'S VERDICT

1.1 One-Paragraph Thesis

Ferrari N.V. is a luxury-goods business wearing an automotive costume — a deliberate production-cap franchise (approximately 14,000 units annually) that has expanded EBITDA margins from 34.8% to 38.8% over the three years to FY2025 purely on pricing power and mix, without touching volume. The most compelling reason to own it today is that the structural moat is demonstrably widening: EPS grew at a 20.7% three-year CAGR under CEO Vigna while OCF/NI consistently exceeded 1.25× — proof that reported growth is cash-backed and not accounting-inflated. The single biggest risk is the Elettrica BEV transition: if early adopters are disappointed by the driving experience or the waiting list fails to materialise at current ASPs, Ferrari would have broken the loyalty-staircase demand architecture that underpins the entire pricing-power thesis, and no surcharge mechanism can repair that damage.

SECTION 2 — QUALITY OF GROWTH

2.1 Revenue Growth Decomposition

Over the five years FY2021–FY2025 Ferrari grew revenue from EUR 4,270.9M to EUR 7,145.8M — a compound annual growth rate of approximately 13.7%.

Volume vs. price: Production has been deliberately capped at approximately 13,500–14,000 units per year throughout this period. All revenue growth is therefore attributable to ASP increases, model mix shift toward higher-specification variants (Purosangue SUV, SF90 Stradale, 12Cilindri), and personalisation packages that are margin-accretive. Revenue YoY growth decelerated from 19.3% in FY2022 to 7.0% in FY2025, consistent with price/mix normalization after COVID-suppressed deliveries rebounded in FY2021–FY2022. The ongoing deceleration is arithmetic — as the ASP base rises, additional percentage growth requires ever-larger absolute price increases.

Organic vs. acquired: Growth is 100% organic. There are no acquisitions in the ESEF filing history; the InvCF outflow (EUR 866M–EUR 987M FY2023–FY2024, EUR 944M FY2025) reflects entirely internal capex and development spend for the Maranello expansion and Elettrica e-building (Financial-Analysis §5.1).

Geographic mix: Ferrari explicitly manages geographic allocation as part of scarcity architecture. The Financial-Brief does not decompose revenue by region; however the global production-cap model implies demand exceeds supply in every major market (Financial-Analysis §3.4). The US represents Ferrari's largest single market by deliveries — making the 15% tariff management outcome relevant to future ASP.

Recurring vs. transactional: Revenue is episodic at the transaction level — each vehicle delivery is a discrete event. However, Ferrari's loyalty-staircase model (access to limited-edition models gated by ownership history) generates a high-retention customer base with extremely low churn. Personalisation programmes, licensing revenues, and engine supply to Haas F1 provide modest but recurring supplemental streams. The core business has low contractual recurrence but extremely high repeat-purchase probability among the eligible UHNWI pool.

Conclusion: This is exceptionally high-quality growth — entirely organic, entirely pricing-and-mix driven, with no volume dilution risk, no M&A integration risk, and no revenue concentration in a single geography. The primary quality risk is the decelerating headline rate (7.0% FY2025) — a growth investor must underwrite whether mix and ASP expansion can sustain the base-case 9% three-year CAGR as the Purosangue cycle matures.

FCF conversion (OCF / Net Income): This metric is computable from ESEF XBRL:

The FY2025 spike to 1.47× reverses the FY2022–FY2024 trend of modest compression, likely reflecting working-capital discipline as the Maranello capex cycle peaks. Every year has exceeded 1.25× — a high and stable conversion rate (Financial-Analysis §5.1).

CapEx / true FCF: Direct CapEx is not tagged in Ferrari's ESEF XBRL. The FCF proxy (OCF + InvCF) gives EUR 550.0M / 597.9M / 850.2M / 939.6M / 1,405.2M (FY2021–FY2025), implying a sharp FY2025 uplift as InvCF outflow fell from EUR 987.1M to EUR 944.1M while OCF surged. True FCF margin is therefore approximately 20% of revenue in FY2025 — consistent with the base-case scenario's 21% FCF margin assumption (Financial-Analysis §1.2).

Accruals ratio: Negative in every observable year — FY2025: −0.078; FY2021: −0.066. A consistently negative accruals ratio means cash generation systematically exceeds book earnings — the most important single quality indicator in manufacturing companies, where aggressive capitalisation of development costs often inflates reported earnings above cash reality. Ferrari shows the opposite: cash exceeds earnings every year.

One-time items: No non-recurring items are flagged in the ESEF XBRL data or parent Financial-Analysis. The FY2025 FinCF outflow of EUR 1,667M (vs. EUR 325M FY2025 prior-year) likely reflects a special dividend or enlarged buyback — a capital return event, not an earnings-inflating item.

Working capital dynamics: InvCF and FinCF structure suggests Ferrari collects revenue in advance of delivery on many models (order-book structure), generating a float-like working capital advantage — customers pay deposits for models years before delivery. This is structurally similar to aircraft OEM dynamics.

Conclusion: Ferrari translates reported earnings into real cash at a high, stable, and demonstrably improving rate. The negative accruals ratio in every year is a clear signature of accounting conservatism and genuine cash generation — earnings quality is exceptional.

FY2022 was a transient dip (development cost ramp); every subsequent year expanded. The five-year trajectory is from 35.9% to 38.8% — 290 bps of EBITDA margin expansion at near-constant volume. This is extraordinary: most manufacturers see margin pressure as volumes plateau.

Operating margin trend: Operating margin expanded from 25.2% (FY2021) to 29.5% (FY2025) — 430 bps over five years.

Incremental margin estimate: Between FY2023 and FY2025 revenue grew EUR 1,175.7M (from EUR 5,970.1M to EUR 7,145.8M) and EBITDA grew EUR 492.0M (from EUR 2,279.7M to EUR 2,771.7M). Incremental EBITDA margin: EUR 492.0M / EUR 1,175.7M = 41.8%. This is well above the current average margin of 38.8%, confirming strong operating leverage — each additional EUR of revenue at current mix and volume yields EUR 0.42 of EBITDA.

Gross margin (TTM): 51.61% per Market Data §4 (Yahoo Finance TTM, USD denominated). No five-year gross margin trend is available from ESEF XBRL (GrossProfit tag absent in Ferrari's IFRS extension).

Conclusion: This is precisely the business profile growth investors prize: volume is deliberately flat, yet margins are expanding because each price or mix increment falls almost entirely to the bottom line. Revenue growth improves the economics. The Elettrica risk is that BEV development cost amortisation reverses this dynamic — the bear case assumes EBITDA compresses to 34–35%, which would still be above most manufacturers' peak margins.

SECTION 3 — REINVESTMENT ENGINE

3.1 ROIC vs. Cost of Capital

ROIC: Formally uncomputable from ESEF XBRL — Ferrari's IFRS extension does not tag CurrentLiabilities, preventing invested-capital calculation. The XBRL series shows [N/A] for ROIC across all five years (Financial-Analysis §5.1).

Proxy via ROE: ROE is computable for three years: FY2023 40.9%, FY2024 43.1%, FY2025 40.9% (Financial-Analysis §5.4). ROE of ~41% implies an exceptionally high return on the equity base — though ROE can be inflated by leverage, Ferrari's Debt/Equity (72.25) per Market Data is modest in absolute terms and the company is Net Industrial Debt negative per public disclosure (Financial-Analysis §5.1, XBRL note).

Estimated WACC: Using 10-year US Treasury yield as a proxy for risk-free (approximately 4.3–4.5% in mid-2026), an equity risk premium of 5%, and Ferrari's 5-year monthly beta of 0.59 (Market Data §8): Cost of Equity ≈ 4.4% + 0.59 × 5.0% = ~7.4%. Ferrari carries moderate financial leverage; estimated WACC ~8–9%. This assumption is labelled [NOT IN SOURCE FILES — estimated from Market Data beta and standard ERP].

ROIC spread: ROE 40.9% vs. estimated WACC ~8–9% implies a ROIC spread of approximately +32pp — one of the widest spreads in any capital-intensive industry globally. Every EUR reinvested at this spread creates substantial economic value.

Trajectory: The spread is stable-to-widening: FY2025 ROE matches FY2023 at 40.9% and remains above FY2023. EBITDA margin continued to expand. No evidence of erosion.

3.2 Reinvestment Rate & Efficiency

CapEx (proxy from InvCF): EUR 944.1M FY2025 (InvCF proxy). D&A: EUR 661.9M FY2025. Net CapEx (above maintenance): EUR 944.1M − EUR 661.9M = EUR 282.2M. No acquisition outflow is identifiable in the ESEF XBRL; change in working capital not directly tagged (CapEx tag absent).

NOPAT (Operating Income × (1 − estimated tax)): Operating Income EUR 2,109.7M FY2025 × (1 − 0.24 effective rate estimate [NOT IN SOURCE FILES]) ≈ EUR 1,603M. Reinvestment Rate: EUR 282.2M / EUR 1,603M ≈ 17.6%. This is a modest reinvestment rate — consistent with an asset-light-within-manufacturing model where the primary growth driver is pricing, not capacity expansion.

Implied organic growth rate (ROIC × Reinvestment Rate): Using ROE as proxy: 40.9% × 17.6% ≈ 7.2% — which closely matches FY2025's actual revenue growth of 7.0%. This internal consistency check supports the model: Ferrari's growth rate is almost entirely explained by its exceptional returns on a modest reinvestment base.

Commentary: Ferrari is not harvesting the asset — it is in the right phase for a luxury franchise: minimal capital required to maintain and extend the moat, with pricing power doing the compounding. The Elettrica capex cycle (EUR ~500M investment in the e-building, partially government co-funded) is an outlier period that the FY2025 InvCF normalisation has already partially absorbed.

3.3 Capital Allocation Track Record

Reinvestment vs. shareholder returns (FY2021–FY2025): FinCF outflows total EUR 4,434.7M over five years (EUR 579.7M + EUR 553.6M + EUR 1,109.4M + EUR 324.8M + EUR 1,667.2M). OCF totals EUR 8,678.6M over the same period. Net cash returned to shareholders plus debt service = roughly 51% of OCF, with the remainder reinvested or accumulated as cash.

Acquisitions: No M&A activity identifiable in the ESEF XBRL corpus. Ferrari has grown entirely organically — capital allocation discipline is exemplary on this dimension.

Share count: Approximately 178.2M diluted shares (derived from NI EUR 1,596.9M / EPS EUR 8.96); shares outstanding per Market Data: 175,888,530 (July 2026). This is modestly below the XBRL-derived estimate, consistent with a modest buyback programme — no dilution.

FY2025 special return: The EUR 1,667M FinCF outflow in FY2025 is approximately 5× the FY2024 level — suggesting a special distribution or enlarged buyback in FY2025, consistent with Ferrari's stated capital return policy.

Overall rating: Excellent. No acquisitions, consistent buybacks, dividends growing in line with EPS ($4.23/share annual dividend per Market Data §3, implying 33.2% payout ratio), and an opportunistic special distribution in FY2025. Zero dilution. Management is returning capital at a pace consistent with a business that cannot productively deploy large amounts of incremental capital — the correct answer for a scarcity-architecture franchise.

SECTION 4 — GROWTH RUNWAY ASSESSMENT

4.1 Total Addressable Market (TAM) & Penetration

Ferrari's stated production cap of approximately 13,500–14,000 units per year is a management choice, not a market-imposed constraint. Global UHNWI (ultra-high-net-worth individuals, net worth >$30M) numbered approximately 395,000 globally as of recent years [MANAGEMENT ESTIMATE — VERIFY WITH INDUSTRY SOURCES]; Ferrari's eligible addressable buyer pool extends well into six figures globally. Ferrari's annual deliveries represent a deliberate penetration of well under 5% of its addressable buyer universe in any given year — scarcity is engineered, not natural.

Penetration trajectory: Ferrari is not growing penetration — it is growing ASP within a fixed-volume constraint. This is unusual and deliberate: the brand rests on the inaccessibility of ownership. The scarcity moat would erode if Ferrari crossed 16,000 units (Exit Condition 1 per Financial-Analysis §8.5).

White space: The most important untapped opportunity is the Elettrica BEV — a new model in the ultra-luxury EV segment where Ferrari has no existing product as of FY2025. If the Elettrica achieves sufficient demand at an ASP premium to the existing ICE lineup, it represents pure additive revenue within the production cap, accelerating ASP growth. The hypercar pipeline (one-of-a-kind and limited-run specials) extends at the very top of the price ladder and is structurally demand-constrained by Ferrari's own pace of production.

4.2 Growth Drivers & Duration

Driver 1: Model mix shift to higher-ASP variants (Purosangue, Elettrica)

  • Structural: Purosangue is Ferrari's first SUV — a new segment for the brand that attracted first-time buyers into the loyalty staircase. Duration: 3–5 years (Purosangue launch cycle; successor adds further).
  • Kill switch: Purosangue demand plateaus before the unit allocation is renewed with a next-generation model; loyalty-staircase converts break at the SUV threshold (i.e., SUV ownership not accepted as a gateway to sports car access).

Driver 2: Personalisation programme revenue (Atelier, Tailor-Made)

  • Structural: Zero capacity consumed; near-100% gross margin. As ASP rises, personalisation expenditure per unit rises proportionately. Duration: 5+ years — no ceiling while ASP expands.
  • Kill switch: Macro shock to UHNWI discretionary spending compresses personalisation attachment rates.

Driver 3: Elettrica BEV launch (expected deliveries late 2026+)

  • Structural if successful: Additive model within the production cap at a premium ASP; accesses a different buyer profile (EV-preferring UHNWI). The Ferrari Elettrica is the single highest-stakes product launch in Ferrari's recent history. Duration: 5+ years for the product lifecycle if demand confirms.
  • Kill switch: Slow order book uptake, price concessions, or postponement of deliveries (Exit Condition 6 per Financial-Analysis §8.5).

Driver 4: Licensing, engine supply (Haas F1), Ferrari World theme parks

  • Structural but modest: These revenue streams carry high margins and grow with brand equity. Duration: 5+ years at current pace.
  • Kill switch: Ferrari withdraws from F1 (virtually zero probability given control structure) or theme park attendance structurally declines.

Driver 5: Geographic pricing power across markets (US, Middle East, Asia)

  • Cyclical / partially structural: Ferrari raised prices in the US by up to 10% to offset the 2025 tariff; the 15% tariff environment now provides a floor for US ASP. Middle Eastern and Asian UHNWI demand continues to deepen. Duration: 3–5 years at current macro trajectory.
  • Kill switch: Coordinated global UHNWI wealth shock (financial crisis, war); or US tariff return to 25%+ eroding pass-through feasibility.

4.3 Unit Economics & Scalability

Ferrari is the archetypical luxury franchise — unit economics are computed at the vehicle level.

Key unit economics: At approximately 14,000 units annually and EUR 7,145.8M revenue (FY2025), the average selling price per vehicle is approximately EUR 510,000 (EUR 7,145.8M / 14,000). EBITDA per vehicle: EUR 2,771.7M / 14,000 ≈ EUR 198,000. This implies an EBITDA margin per unit of approximately 38.8% — consistent with the aggregate margin and confirming no cross-subsidy between entry and premium models that would distort the overall picture.

Scalability constraints: Ferrari is explicitly not pursuing scale. The binding constraint on growth is the production cap itself — the Maranello factory capacity is intentionally managed below its theoretical maximum. The Elettrica e-building adds capacity within the overall cap. Capital, talent, and regulatory constraints are secondary; the primary constraint is the management commitment to scarcity.

Are unit economics improving? Yes — every year from FY2022 to FY2025, EBITDA per vehicle rose as ASP expanded faster than cost per vehicle. This is the strongest possible signal for a fixed-volume luxury franchise: the business is becoming more profitable per unit without adding complexity.

5.2 Growth-Adjusted Valuation (PEG Analysis)

PEG ratio: Trailing P/E 36.25 / consensus growth rate 4.08 PEG per Market Data §2 (Yahoo Finance 5-year expected PEG). The PEG of 4.08 is decisively above the 2.0 threshold for a standard quality compounder — it requires specific justification.

Justification for elevated PEG: Ferrari's historical EPS CAGR of 20.7% (FY2021–FY2025 three-year) would yield a PEG of approximately 36.25 / 20.7 ≈ 1.75 — firmly in "fair value for a quality compounder" territory. The Yahoo Finance 5-year PEG of 4.08 implies a near-term consensus EPS growth rate of approximately 36.25 / 4.08 ≈ 8.9% — consistent with the base-case revenue CAGR of 9% and the base-case EPS progression from $10.25 (TTM) to $14.22 over three years (CAGR ~11.6%). The elevated PEG relative to historical growth reflects the deceleration from the Vigna-era 20.7% EPS CAGR to the more normalised ~10–12% base case.

P/FCF-to-FCF-growth (PFCFG): TTM FCF $985.85M is depressed relative to the base-case FCF of $2.168B projected three years out. Using FCF/share TTM of $5.61 and forward of approximately $12.32 (base, FCF $2.168B / 175.9M shares): FCF CAGR implied ≈ 30% per year. PFCFG = 66.2 / 30 ≈ 2.2. This is better than the PEG suggests but still in stretched territory using near-term FCF.

Required EPS growth for fair PEG of 1.5: At current P/E 36.25 and PEG target 1.5: required EPS growth = 36.25 / 1.5 = 24.2% per year — comfortably above the base case of 11.6% and above even the historical 20.7% CAGR. The current price embeds a significant luxury-goods re-rating premium that goes beyond pure earnings growth.

Conclusion: At $371.52, investors are paying a significant premium for the quality and scarcity of the franchise — a premium that is only justified if the bull case (Elettrica margin accretion + institutional re-classification toward Hermès peer set) materialises. At the base case, the stock is fairly priced to modestly stretched. At the bear case, it is expensive.

5.4 Margin of Safety Assessment

Base case intrinsic value: $533 (central estimate, median of six methods). Current price $371.52. Upside to base: +$161.48, or +43.5%. Ferrari is trading at a 30.2% discount to its base-case intrinsic value — a meaningful margin of safety exists against the base case, which is unusual for a stock at a 36× trailing P/E.

Bear case: Central estimate $398 vs. entry $371.52. Maximum bear loss: −$26.52 per share or −7.1% from entry (before dividends). Including three years of dividends ($12.69), the net bear-case outcome is approximately +3.2% total or flat — the bear case for Ferrari is not a catastrophic downside scenario in price terms, because the franchise's scarcity-protected earnings power limits re-rating risk.

Minimum price for 20% margin of safety vs. base: $533 × (1 − 0.20) = $426.40. The current price of $371.52 is already 12.9% below this threshold — Ferrari currently offers more than a 20% margin of safety against its base-case fair value.

Assessment: Entry point is attractive for a growth investor willing to accept the 3–5 year horizon required for Elettrica and the base-case scenario to play out. The asymmetry is positive: the bear case is nearly neutral in total-return terms and the probability-weighted expected return of approximately 14% annualised exceeds most reasonable cost-of-equity hurdles. The risk is not permanent capital loss — it is that the Elettrica cycle disappoints and the stock treads water in the bear case at roughly flat total returns over three years.

5B.1 Political Tailwinds and Headwinds for the Growth Thesis

Active tailwinds:

  • Italian government Maranello electrification co-investment (>€106M MOU, confirmed closed): The Italian MISE / Invitalia / Emilia-Romagna framework agreement contributed more than €106M in public funds to the ~€500M Maranello e-building investment that manufactures the Elettrica. This reduces Ferrari's net capex burden for its most important growth driver by approximately 20% and aligns Italian industrial policy interests with the Elettrica's success. Rating: Structural (3–5yr+) — Italian government interest in preserving Emilia-Romagna automotive employment is bipartisan and durable regardless of coalition composition. (Political-Exposure §2)

  • EU low-volume manufacturer CO2 derogation (confirmed, <10,000 units pa exempt; 0g target from 2036 vs. 2035 for mass-market): ACEA-secured derogation gives Ferrari an extra year of regulatory runway on ICE/hybrid products. Combined with the 2023 e-fuels carve-out (synthetic fuels preserve a post-2035 ICE path), Ferrari's existing model lineup faces no forced discontinuation before 2036. Rating: Structural (3–5yr+) — derogation is enacted in EU regulation; would require legislative reversal to remove. (Political-Exposure §5)

  • EU-US trade deal (July 1, 2026 — 15% all-inclusive tariff ceiling): Ferrari vehicles now enter the US at a 15% tariff, down from the 25% Section 232 threat scenario. Ferrari demonstrated in FY2025 that it can absorb and pass through tariffs at this level (EBITDA margin 38.8% achieved vs. ≥38.3% guidance despite tariff). The 15% rate is manageable for Ferrari's UHNWI clientele — EUR 50,000–75,000 on a EUR 350,000–500,000 vehicle. Rating: Near-term (1–2yr) — deal could be renegotiated by a future administration; not permanent legislation. (Political-Exposure §5 and §6)

Active headwinds:

  • US tariff re-escalation tail risk: The July 2026 EU-US deal is an executive agreement, not legislation. A future US administration could revert to 25%+ Section 232 tariffs on EU autos. Ferrari's Exit Condition (5) — "US tariff 25% AND guidance EBITDA margin below 38%" — is currently untriggered but remains an active tail risk. Rating: Cyclical (reversible) — depends on US administration trade posture. (Political-Exposure §6)

  • EU 2035 ICE phase-out (residual risk for non-exempt ICE models): Although Ferrari's low-volume derogation protects most products until 2036, the regulatory direction favours BEV transition. Ferrari has already revised its 2030 electrification target downward (from 40% BEV to 20% BEV / 40% hybrid / 40% ICE per Capital Markets Day October 2025) — acknowledging that full electrification is not near-term viable for all segments. Rating: Structural (3–5yr+) but mitigated — derogation provides meaningful buffer. (Political-Exposure §5)

Administration dependency risk: The Italian government co-investment and EU derogation are structural and broadly bipartisan. The US tariff resolution is the most administration-dependent element — but even if it reverses, Ferrari demonstrated in FY2025 that pricing power is sufficient at the 15% tariff level. An active growth thesis for Ferrari is not contingent on any specific administration remaining in power.

5B.2 Government Contract Pipeline as a Growth Driver

Ferrari derives zero procurement revenue from government contracts. Government contract revenue as a percentage of total revenue is non-material (essentially zero from procurement). The only government relationship is the Italian public grant (capital support, not recurring revenue).

Signal for growth thesis: Non-material. This section has no impact on the growth case. The growth thesis is entirely commercial — UHNWI demand, ASP expansion, and the Elettrica launch — and is independent of any government contract pipeline. (Political-Exposure §4)

5B.3 Lobbying as a Growth Investment

Ferrari does not independently lobby US or EU authorities and has no US corporate PAC. The primary political channel is ACEA (European Automobile Manufacturers' Association) — the industry body that successfully secured the low-volume CO2 derogation until 2036 and the e-fuels carve-out in 2023.

ACEA lobbying impact on Ferrari's growth: The e-fuels carve-out is the most growth-relevant lobbying outcome — it preserves the viability of ICE and hybrid Ferrari models in the EU post-2035 if synthetic fuels are certified. This extends the product lifecycle of Ferrari's highest-margin ICE vehicles (naturally-aspirated 12-cylinder engines, V8 turbos) beyond the regulatory cut-off that would otherwise apply to mass-market OEMs.

Results produced: The ACEA lobbying on low-volume derogation and e-fuels carve-out has produced two concrete regulatory outcomes: (1) derogation enacted in EU Regulation 2019/631 for manufacturers <10,000 units pa; (2) e-fuels path secured in 2023 EU legislative revision. Both are confirmed and enacted. (Political-Exposure §2)

Rating: Defensive (moat-protecting) — The lobbying protects Ferrari's existing high-margin ICE lineup from regulatory forced discontinuation. It does not open new markets or expand the TAM. It is high-value defensive spend: ensuring that Ferrari's pricing-power model is not disrupted by regulatory mandate. For a growth investor, this is a positive — the moat is being actively defended on the regulatory front at a cost so low it is not even separately disclosed in Ferrari's financials.

SECTION 7 — KEY TALKING POINTS

  • Ferrari is the only publicly listed luxury-goods company that deliberately limits its production to protect its pricing power — and it works: EBITDA margins have expanded every year from FY2022 to FY2025, reaching 38.8%, while volume has stayed flat at ~14,000 units.

  • Why it matters: This is the core of the growth thesis — a business that compounds earnings through ASP and mix without volume risk, M&A integration, or operational leverage on cost. Strengthens the thesis.

  • The EPS CAGR under CEO Vigna was 20.7% over three years, fully backed by cash: OCF/NI exceeded 1.25× in every year through FY2025, and the accruals ratio was negative in all five observable years.

  • Why it matters: In a world of narrative-driven valuation, Ferrari's growth is genuine and cash-verified. The negative accruals ratio is a textbook quality signal. Strengthens the thesis.

  • At $371.52, Ferrari trades at a 30% discount to its base-case fair value of $533 (median of six valuation methods, Scenario-Drivers.json base scenario) — despite an apparently expensive 36× trailing P/E.

  • Why it matters: The paradox is real: the stock looks expensive on multiples but cheap on fair value. This is because the scenario analysis reflects Ferrari's earnings trajectory, not its current TTM earnings base. A patient investor entering today gets a 43.5% upside to base case and a bear-case loss of only ~7%. Strengthens the thesis.

  • The Elettrica BEV is the single most important binary event in Ferrari's near-term history: if the order book fills and pricing holds at a premium to the base ICE lineup, the bull case becomes the base case; if it disappoints, the loyalty-staircase demand model is structurally impaired.

  • Why it matters: This is the primary swing factor. Elettrica success = bull case at $687 (+85%); Elettrica failure = bear case at $398 (+7%). The asymmetry is large and the outcome is knowable within 12–18 months of launch. Challenges the thesis unless monitored.

  • The US tariff risk is actively managed and no longer the primary downside scenario: the July 2026 EU-US deal caps Ferrari's US exposure at 15%, Ferrari demonstrated in FY2025 that 10% surcharges are sticky for UHNWI buyers, and Exit Condition (5) is not triggered.

  • Why it matters: The political tail risk that dominated 2025 headlines is partially resolved. Ferrari's pricing power is real and tested. Strengthens the thesis.

  • Revenue growth is decelerating from 19.3% (FY2022) to 7.0% (FY2025) — a pattern that demands scrutiny. The base case assumes 9% CAGR, but that requires the Elettrica to add incremental ASP within the production cap. Without Elettrica success, 5–7% is the more likely trajectory.

  • Why it matters: The base-case growth assumption is not aggressive, but it is Elettrica-dependent. A growth investor must verify order book momentum as the primary leading indicator. Challenges the thesis.

  • The PEG ratio of 4.08 is difficult to justify on pure earnings growth — but it reflects a real option: the probability that Ferrari re-rates to the Hermès / LVMH peer set (~50–60× P/E for luxury-goods companies at comparable quality) rather than remaining in the automotive sector (~15–20× P/E).

  • Why it matters: If the re-classification trade plays out, the bull-case target of $687 uses peer P/E multiples at the luxury end (38.9× per RMS) not the auto end. A growth investor is buying both the earnings CAGR and the re-rating option. Strengthens the thesis for those who believe the franchise merits luxury-house multiples.

  • Capital allocation is exceptional by any standard: zero acquisitions in five years, no share dilution, growing dividends, and a EUR 1,667M special return to shareholders in FY2025 — a business that knows it cannot productively deploy unlimited capital and returns the surplus.

  • Why it matters: Management discipline is the most underappreciated element of Ferrari's investment case. It eliminates the dilution and M&A overpayment risks that destroy value in most manufacturer-to-luxury transitions. Strengthens the thesis.

8.1 Bear Case — "Elettrica Disappoints, BEV Costs Bite"

Probability: 20% (Financial-Analysis §1.2)

The bear case is triggered by a combination of a UHNWI luxury spending slowdown and a structurally disappointing Elettrica launch: order book fills more slowly than planned, pricing concessions are required to maintain delivery cadence, and BEV development cost amortisation (elevated capex cycle through FY2026–FY2027) compresses EBITDA margins toward 34–35% over the scenario horizon. Operationally, Ferrari continues to deliver approximately 14,000 units annually but revenue CAGR is only 5% because the Elettrica is priced at parity rather than at a premium, and the Purosangue cycle matures without a compelling successor. EPS reaches $10.87 by the three-year horizon (below current TTM of $10.25), implying near-zero EPS growth, and the Fair-Value-Bands central estimate of $398 reflects a market that prices Ferrari on automotive rather than luxury-house multiples. The early confirmation signal — Elettrica order book disclosed as below 18 months' production capacity at launch pricing — would be visible in Ferrari's first half-year report after the Elettrica opens for orders.

8.2 Base Case — "Scarcity Compounder"

Probability: 55% (Financial-Analysis §1.2)

In the base case, Ferrari maintains production discipline (below 14,500 units), the Purosangue continues at full capacity allocation, and the Elettrica launches to a fully subscribed order book at a premium ASP within the production cap. EBITDA margin holds at 38–39% as the Italian government co-investment absorbs a portion of the e-building amortisation burden and personalisation attach rates remain elevated. Revenue CAGR reaches 9% over three years, driving EPS from $10.25 TTM to $14.22 by the scenario horizon — an annualised earnings CAGR of approximately 11.6%. At the Fair-Value-Bands base central estimate of $533, Ferrari is valued at approximately 37.5× projected EPS — the upper end of its historical self-multiple range and consistent with a luxury-goods premium persisting at current institutional consensus. The confirming signal for the base case is a first-year annual report showing Elettrica deliveries commence in H2 2026 with no pricing concessions, combined with EBITDA margin at ≥38% for FY2026.

3-Year Base Return: +13.6% annualised (total return including dividends from $371.52 to $533 + $12.69)
Confirming Signal (12-month): FY2026 EBITDA margin ≥38% AND Elettrica deliveries begin H2 2026 with no disclosed price concessions

8.3 Bull Case — "Luxury Re-Classification"

Probability: 25% (Financial-Analysis §1.2)

The bull case requires two simultaneous outcomes: the Elettrica is margin-accretive (its ASP substantially exceeds the ICE portfolio average, driving blended ASP above EUR 550,000) and institutional investors complete a reclassification of Ferrari from "automobile manufacturer" to "luxury goods house," driving the peer-set multiple toward RMS (Hermès) rather than PAH3 (Porsche). Under this scenario, EBITDA margins expand toward 41%, FCF reaches $2.841B over the three-year horizon, and EPS reaches $17.54 — an annualised earnings CAGR of approximately 19.7% from TTM. At the Fair-Value-Bands bull central estimate of $687, Ferrari trades at approximately 39× forward EPS on a scenario-year basis — a premium consistent with a Hermès-quality franchise but below Hermès's own multiple of ~50×. The hypercar pipeline extension (one-of-a-kind specials above the Laferrari price point) provides the additional upside push on revenue per unit. The 12-month confirming signal is a disclosed Elettrica ASP at first delivery that is materially above the Purosangue base price (approximately EUR 390,000), confirming the premium positioning thesis.

3-Year Bull Return: +24.0% annualised (total return including dividends from $371.52 to $687 + $12.69)
Confirming Signal (12-month): Elettrica ASP at first delivery confirmed above EUR 450,000 (>15% premium to Purosangue base), AND FY2026 EBITDA margin ≥39%
