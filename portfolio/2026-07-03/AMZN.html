Amazon: The Bet Still Works

The Bottom Line

The bet is working. AWS — Amazon's cloud-computing franchise — grew its quarterly revenue by 28 per cent in the three months ending March 2026, well above the 18 per cent threshold that must hold for the thesis to remain intact. The share price sits in the upper half of the hold band, well above fair value — the market is pricing in the base case as the floor, leaving no room for underperformance. The one condition to watch is the capital-expenditure trajectory: Amazon nearly doubled its infrastructure spending over the past year, and whether that spending converts into AWS revenue on schedule is the question the next two or three annual filings must answer.

Recent Development

The first quarter of 2026 — the three months ending in March — was the most important data point the thesis has produced since the AI infrastructure build began. AWS net sales reached $37,587 million, up 28 per cent from the same period a year earlier. That rate is the highest in at least six quarters, and it arrives alongside $364 billion of contracted backlog with an average remaining term of 5.5 years. The combination — accelerating growth, multi-year commitments from enterprise customers — is the clearest evidence so far that Amazon's AI infrastructure investment is converting to revenue rather than sitting idle.

Two items in the same filing warrant attention without yet changing the call. Infrastructure spending reached roughly $147 billion on a trailing twelve-month basis as of March 2026, nearly double the year-prior figure, driven by a near-doubling of property and equipment additions within AWS. The written exit condition that applies here requires that capital expenditure remain above $120 billion in both 2027 and 2028 without commensurate AWS revenue acceleration — the "without" clause is the load-bearing part, and with AWS growing at 28 per cent in March 2026, the clause is not met today. The other watch item is marketplace exposure to tariff policy: the filing discloses that China-based sellers represent significant portions of third-party services and advertising revenue, and mentions tariff policy changes multiple times as a risk factor. Despite those headwinds, North America operating income grew 41 per cent in the quarter — suggesting that managed exposure has so far absorbed rather than transmitted the pressure.

The overall reading is that the bet is working and the share price is above fair value. Both facts matter equally.

About Amazon

Amazon makes its money in three overlapping ways. The first and largest by revenue is retail and logistics: a marketplace where third-party sellers list and fulfil products through Amazon's warehousing and delivery network, alongside Amazon's own direct sales and its Prime subscription bundle — combining delivery, streaming video, music, and grocery access. The retail segment generated over $588 billion of revenue in 2025, with North America operating income of around $27 billion for the year. It is the engine that built Amazon's scale, and scale is the flywheel — the bigger the logistics network, the faster and cheaper delivery becomes, which attracts more shoppers and sellers, which justifies expanding the network further.

The second business, Amazon Web Services, is the disproportionate profit pool. On $128.7 billion of 2025 revenue — about 18 per cent of the total — AWS produced $45.6 billion of operating income, roughly 57 per cent of Amazon's consolidated profit. AWS rents computing power, storage, and software services to businesses that would otherwise have to build and run their own data centres. The cloud-computing model means customers pay as they use, with long-term reserved contracts offering discounted rates in exchange for commitment. The third business, digital advertising, generated $68.6 billion in 2025, growing 22 per cent, by selling sponsored placements to sellers who want their products found among the hundreds of millions of items on Amazon's marketplace.

The Case for the Bet

The central idea is that Amazon has two compounding profit pools — cloud computing and advertising — that are still well below their economic maturity, funded by a retail and logistics platform that has already proved it can scale globally. The thesis asks whether those two pools can keep compounding for three to five more years without one of eight specific failure modes interrupting the process.

The conditions that must keep holding:

  1. AWS quarterly revenue growth must stay at or above 18 per cent on a year-over-year basis. Growth below 15 per cent for two consecutive quarters is the primary exit trigger — it would signal that the AI infrastructure the company has spent heavily to build has not produced corresponding customer demand.

  2. AWS segment operating margin must stay above 30 per cent (the target), with a hard exit trigger if it falls below 28 per cent for two consecutive quarters without an explainable one-off — this would indicate that reinvestment economics have broken down.

  3. No structural remedy in the FTC monopoly case requiring business separation.

  4. Capital expenditure must not remain above $120 billion in both 2027 and 2028 without commensurate AWS revenue acceleration — that combination would signal the AI infrastructure build has overshot demand.

  5. Advertising revenue growth must not decelerate to single digits for two consecutive years, which would signal that the third compounding engine has saturated.

  6. Financial integrity must remain intact — no material weakness in internal control, no unexpected auditor change.

  7. Insider selling by founder Jeff Bezos must not exceed 40 million shares in a single Form 144 filing, which would indicate abnormal acceleration beyond his established pre-programmed sale schedule.

  8. Earnings yield (a measure of the cash the business generates relative to its market price) must not fall below 3 per cent, which would signal the share price has priced in an optimistic assumption about how quickly infrastructure spending converts to cash flow.

The Conditions Today

AWS growth (condition 1) is the strongest reading in the filing: 28 per cent year-on-year in the three months ending March 2026, the highest in at least six quarters, well above the 18 per cent thesis target and far above the 15 per cent exit threshold. The $364 billion backlog provides multi-year revenue visibility.

AWS operating margin (condition 2) came in at 37.7 per cent in the same quarter, down roughly 170 basis points — that is 1.7 percentage points — from the prior year period. The filing attributes the compression to additional infrastructure investment. At 37.7 per cent, the margin sits nearly 10 percentage points above the 28 per cent exit threshold. The compression is consistent with the thesis's expectation that the AI investment cycle would temporarily dilute margins before converting.

FTC antitrust (condition 3): The filing references ongoing FTC litigation in its risk factors — including allegations of monopolisation — but no structural remedy has been imposed and no new trial schedule update appeared in this filing. The condition is not triggered.

Capital expenditure trajectory (condition 4): Trailing twelve-month capital spending reached approximately $147 billion as of March 2026, nearly double the prior year. The exit condition, however, requires that level to persist in both 2027 and 2028 without commensurate AWS revenue acceleration — and with AWS growing at 28 per cent alongside a $364 billion backlog, the "without" clause does not apply today. This is the highest-priority item to reassess in full-year 2026 guidance.

Advertising growth (condition 5): Advertising services reached $17.2 billion in the quarter, up approximately 24 per cent from the prior year. Single-digit growth is not in sight.

Financial integrity (condition 6): No material weakness disclosed; Ernst & Young has been the auditor since 1996 with no restatement on record. The Beneish M-Score — a statistical model for detecting earnings manipulation — sits at −2.66, well below the threshold that would warrant an accounting review.

Insider selling (condition 7): The filing discloses "Trading Arrangements: None" for Bezos in the quarter, and no Form 144 filing exceeding 40 million shares in the current period. Not triggered.

Earnings yield (condition 8): An owner-earnings yield — the ratio of owner-available cash flow to market value — of 4.2 per cent sits above the 3.0 per cent exit trigger. Not triggered, though the yield sits only 1.2 percentage points above the threshold.

The Share Price

At $250, the share price sits in the upper half of the hold band — above the $140 buy edge by a wide margin, and $13 below the $263 trim edge. In terms of distance to edges, the price is closer to trim than to buy. The valuation method translates Amazon's cash earnings into a yield: at $250, an earnings yield of 4.2 per cent compares against a fair-value reference of 6.0 per cent. The implied fair-value price is $176. That makes the current $250 approximately 42 per cent above the fair-value anchor — in plain terms, the share price is well above fair value.

The spread between those two numbers is not a contradiction of the thesis; it is the cost of owning a fast-compounding business at current market prices. The hold band exists precisely because a thesis-intact business can trade above its fair-value anchor for extended periods when growth continues to compound. The trim edge at $263 is where the market's pricing begins to embed so much optimism that harvesting a portion of the position becomes rational — regardless of whether the thesis remains intact.

What's Wobbling

The capital expenditure trajectory is the dominant watch item. Amazon spent approximately $147 billion on infrastructure on a trailing twelve-month basis through March 2026 — almost double the prior year's figure. The business funded part of this by issuing $37 billion of new dollar-denominated debt and €14.5 billion of euro-denominated debt in March 2026. Exit condition (4) is explicitly structured to activate only if that spending persists in both 2027 and 2028 without AWS revenue growth accelerating to match it. With AWS at 28 per cent in March 2026 and a $364 billion backlog, the "without" clause does not apply today. But the test resets with each annual filing, and the required evidence — that AI workloads are absorbing the infrastructure — must accumulate over the next eighteen months.

Tariff exposure in the marketplace is the second wobble. The filing explicitly flags that Chinese sellers represent a significant portion of third-party seller services and advertising revenue, and that trade-policy changes are a risk to those lines. North America operating income grew 41 per cent in the quarter despite this — suggesting managed exposure — but the risk is real and not modelled in any written exit condition. If advertising growth slows to single digits for two consecutive years (condition 5), that is the point at which the tariff drag would become disqualifying.

A third item worth naming is the divergence between the share-price fair-value anchor used in the hold-band framework ($176, derived from a 6 per cent earnings-yield threshold) and a separate bottom-up scenario analysis that derives a central estimate of $532. The two approaches answer different questions — the yield-based number is the price at which the business earns a required return on today's cash flow; the scenario-based number reflects the present value of compounding over a multi-year horizon at base-case assumptions. The framework operates normally at today's prices under either method, but the two numbers point in different directions on how cheap or expensive the share is, and that divergence is worth noting before the next add or trim decision.

What Could Still Go Wrong

  • AWS loses AI workload share. Microsoft's Azure platform and Google's cloud division are both growing faster than AWS in the current AI-workload cycle. If that relative shift accelerates, AWS growth decelerates into the 15 per cent exit zone and the primary thesis pillar breaks.
  • Infrastructure spending stalls revenue. At roughly $147 billion of trailing capital spending, Amazon is betting that AI customers will show up. If enterprise AI adoption proves slower than the infrastructure build assumed — or if the hyperscaler industry collectively overbuilt — Amazon would face years of elevated depreciation and potentially impaired assets.
  • Antitrust structural remedy. The FTC's monopoly case against Amazon has produced a $2.5 billion settlement on the FTC stores antitrust case (recognised in Q3 2025); the underlying monopolisation litigation is ongoing. A court-ordered structural remedy requiring business separation would constitute an immediate exit by the terms of the thesis.
  • Earnings yield breach. At today's $250, an earnings yield of 4.2 per cent sits above the 3.0 per cent exit trigger. At current earnings power, a price around $378 would breach that trigger. The trim edge is $263 — so a brief breach of the trim edge is plausible before the 3 per cent threshold is reached; both are worth tracking.
  • Bezos sales acceleration. Founder Jeff Bezos has disposed of approximately $5.65 billion of stock in the past twelve months through pre-programmed sale plans, with a May 2026 filing covering an additional 25 million shares. The exit trigger — a single Form 144 filing exceeding 40 million shares — has not been reached, but the disposition rate is an item to monitor each filing cycle.

Gaps in the Picture

Owner earnings are computed as operating cash flow minus the narrowly reported capital expenditure figure — a definition that uses purchases of property and equipment as filed in the cash flow statement ($6.7 billion in 2025). Amazon's management definition of infrastructure spending is far broader ($128.3 billion, including finance leases), and the narrow reported figure may materially overstate the true earnings available to shareholders once growth capital is accounted for. The valuation framework uses the narrow number explicitly; that choice is documented but it means an earnings yield of 4.2 per cent assumes a more generous capital treatment than most analysts would apply.

The Fair-Value-Bands base-scenario central estimate ($532) implies an earnings yield of approximately 1.8 per cent, diverging from the hold-band fair-value setting of 6.0 per cent by a wide margin. Two fair-value anchors coexist: the yield-method anchor ($176) and the bottom-up scenario anchor ($532, peer/self multiples). A future schema migration will resolve the divergence. The framework's hold-band operates at current prices under either anchor, but the divergence should inform any add or trim decision.

Risk-free rate for the score section was read from a portfolio-wide market-data file rather than Amazon's own, as Amazon does not carry a per-holding risk-free rate field. The fallback is documented but means the rate reflects the broadest available approximation rather than a security-specific one.

The Rules

The rules below represent the pre-committed responses to each possible state. They are stated as conditions, not as current advice.

  • If the thesis is confirmed — all monitored conditions are in range and no exit condition has triggered — hold the position and continue compounding. No action required.
  • If the thesis is weakened — one or more drivers are deteriorating without yet crossing a written exit threshold — do not add to the position. Revalidate against the next two quarterly filings before deciding. The most likely deteriorating driver is AWS growth or margin.
  • If the thesis is broken — a written exit condition fires — close the full position within 30 days. No averaging down. Note the specific exit condition triggered. The AWS revenue and margin exits require two consecutive quarters of evidence before action is taken; an antitrust structural remedy or auditor change requires immediate exit.
  • If the share price falls to the buy edge ($140) — confirm the thesis is intact first (no exit condition triggered; AWS growth at or above 18 per cent). If confirmed, add to the position in tranches over one to two quarters.
  • If the share price rises to the trim edge ($263) — trim 25 to 33 per cent of the position to harvest the premium while keeping the core holding. Re-evaluate the remaining position size after trimming.
  • If the share price rises to the exit edge ($421) — close the full position. At that price, the market is pricing in roughly 11 years of base-case compounding instantly; the implied forward return falls below the long-term equity premium, and reallocation beats continuing to hold. No re-entry within 90 days to avoid anchoring to the prior price.
