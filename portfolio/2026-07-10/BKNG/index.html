Recent Development

The first quarter of 2026 filing showed a travel-demand engine running hot in almost every dimension except one. Gross bookings reached $53.8 billion in the three months ending in March 2026, up 15.2% from a year earlier — or roughly 8% when adjusted for currency movements — while revenue as a share of those bookings edged up from 10.2% to 10.3%, a small but meaningful sign that the platform can improve its economics even as it adds lower-margin products like flights. Capital return continued at a pace the thesis demands: $4.0 billion in share repurchases and $343 million in dividends in a single quarter, with $18.2 billion of authorisation remaining and no acquisition announced.

The complication is the Middle East conflict. Room nights grew 5.9% overall but management estimates the conflict knocked roughly two percentage points off that figure; the March figure alone slowed to 1% as cancellations spiked at the onset. The written thesis requires low or negative growth across multiple regions for two or more consecutive quarters — absent an isolated shock — before the exit condition fires. One quarter of conflict-induced weakness does not meet that test, but if the conflict persists the headwind will carry forward.

The bigger structural watch item is the AI-strategy clock. The 10-Q describes the "Connected Trip" as the long-term vision, and mobile app bookings are rising — now in the high-fifties percentage of total room nights, up from mid-fifties a year ago — but the filing reports no concrete metrics on AI engagement, no shipped product that demonstrably routes travellers directly through an AI layer, and no explicit acknowledgement of disintermediation risk. The deadline for a coherent AI-first strategy with measurable evidence is the third-quarter 2027 earnings cycle — five quarters from now. The clock is running. About Booking Holdings

Booking Holdings earns its money as the world's largest digital travel marketplace. The core business is straightforward: a traveller searches for a hotel, apartment, or vacation rental on Booking.com; Booking connects them to one of its 4.5 million listed properties; and when the booking completes, Booking earns a commission — roughly ten cents of every dollar of total bookings passes through as revenue. The platform also sells flights, car rentals, restaurant reservations through OpenTable, and attraction tickets, all intended to build a fuller travel itinerary in one place. The company's other consumer-facing brands — Priceline, Agoda, and the flight-search aggregator KAYAK — operate in overlapping markets and share backend infrastructure.

The business model has two structural features that matter to the investment case. First, it is almost entirely asset-light: the company does not own hotels or aircraft; it owns software, licences, and relationships. In the twelve months ending in 2025, the company spent roughly $322 million on physical infrastructure against $9.1 billion of cash generated from operations — a ratio that is the envy of any capital-intensive industry. Second, it operates a two-sided marketplace, where the value of the platform to travellers rises with the number of properties, and the value to properties rises with the number of travellers. Once both sides are large, the platform becomes very difficult to displace. Booking.com's supply base of 4.5 million properties — including 4.0 million alternative accommodations such as apartments and private homes — represents roughly a decade of accumulation that a new entrant would need to replicate country by country.

The Case for the Bet

The bet on Booking Holdings is that its two-sided travel marketplace is durable enough to survive the three forces trying to erode it — AI-mediated trip planning, Google's direct-search push, and hotel-chain direct-booking campaigns — and that management will continue to compound per-share value by returning the bulk of cash flow to shareholders through buybacks and dividends. The specific conditions are:

  1. Room-night growth does not collapse across regions. The exit condition fires when room-night growth reaches low-single-digit or negative territory across multiple regions for two or more consecutive quarters — absent an isolated, identifiable shock. A structurally declining booking volume would signal that the network effect is losing its pull.

Latest reading: Room nights grew 5.9% in the three months ending in March 2026 (338 million versus 319 million). Management estimates the Middle East conflict removed roughly two percentage points, implying underlying growth of approximately 8%. Growth slowed to 1% in March specifically as cancellations spiked at conflict onset. One quarter of conflict-affected data, attributable to an identifiable shock, does not satisfy the exit threshold — but the conflict carries forward as a watch item.

  1. Marketing cost as a share of gross bookings does not rise persistently. The exit condition fires when marketing expenses as a percentage of total gross bookings rise meaningfully for two or more consecutive years. A rising marketing-to-bookings ratio would signal the company is buying growth that the moat used to produce organically.

Latest reading: Marketing as a share of gross bookings was 3.8% in the three months ending in March 2026, flat versus the same period a year earlier. Management attributes the absence of leverage to the Middle East conflict eroding return on paid-search spend; ex-conflict, they claim modest improvement. The structural concern is that search-engine optimisation traffic is declining and the company expects it to continue declining in the near term, which could push more volume toward paid channels. One quarter of flat spend against the exit threshold of a rising trend over multiple years is not yet a problem, but both items — the conflict overlay and the search-engine headwind — warrant watching each quarter.

  1. Revenue as a share of gross bookings does not compress persistently. The take-rate (revenue divided by total gross bookings) measures whether competitors, regulators, or AI tools are squeezing the economics of each booking. A persistent decline would signal structural margin erosion.

Latest reading: The take-rate was 10.3% of gross bookings in the three months ending in March 2026, up from 10.2% in the same period a year earlier. The improvement was driven by payment-facilitation revenue growing as the company shifts more transactions through its own payment infrastructure. The trend was partly offset by a higher mix of flight bookings, which carry a lower take-rate than accommodation. No compression is visible.

  1. Capital-return discipline holds. The exit condition fires when buybacks slow or stop while the owner-earnings yield (a measure of how much cash the business generates relative to its share price, explained below) is attractive — particularly if cash is redirected into a large acquisition at an inflated price.

Latest reading: The company repurchased $4.0 billion of its own shares and paid $343 million in dividends in the three months ending in March 2026. The remaining authorisation under the current programme stands at $18.2 billion. No acquisition was announced. Capital return is at full throttle.

  1. The owner-earnings yield stays above 4%. The owner-earnings yield is the annual cash the business generates divided by the market value of all its shares — roughly analogous to the earnings yield on a bond. At a yield above 4%, the valuation requires a moderately optimistic future to be justified. Below 4%, the market would be pricing in very rapid growth for a long time, and per-share arithmetic no longer compensates for the AI disruption risk.

Latest reading: The owner-earnings yield stood at approximately 6.0% as of the latest calculation, based on $8.5 billion of owner earnings (cash generated from operations minus capital spending, then adjusted for share-based compensation) against a market value of roughly $141 billion. The trim edge of 4.0% implies a share price of $273. The current price of $182 is well inside the safe zone.

  1. A coherent AI strategy is in place by the third quarter of 2027. The exit condition fires if, by the earnings cycle covering the third quarter of 2027, management has not articulated and visibly executed a strategy for how Booking.com competes when AI agents become the primary way travellers plan and book trips — with concrete evidence of engagement, conversion, or margin uplift from shipped AI products, and explicit acknowledgement of disintermediation risk in official communications.

Latest reading: The first-quarter 2026 filing describes the "Connected Trip" as the AI-powered travel experience strategy, and the mobile app booking share has risen from mid-fifties to high-fifties over the past year — a positive but thin signal. No AI engagement metrics, no product launch with measurable uptake, and no explicit disintermediation acknowledgement appear in the filing. The deadline is five quarters away. The clock is running but has not yet rung.

  1. EU regulatory enforcement does not cross structural impairment. The exit condition fires if a European Union enforcement action results in a formal decision imposing structural product remedies — such as mandatory removal of pricing clauses that affect the company's economic position — or fines exceeding 3% of annual global revenue in a single year (roughly $810 million at 2025 revenue levels).

Latest reading: The European Union has designated Booking.com a gatekeeper under its Digital Markets Act, with compliance obligations live since November 2024. A self-preferencing inquiry is open. Separately, a Spanish competition authority fine of $476 million sits on the balance sheet under appeal; an Italian authority investigation opened in April 2026; Greek and Hungarian investigations are ongoing. None of these individually crosses the 3% fine threshold, and the Spanish fine is suspended pending appeal. The regulatory environment is active and costly, but has not yet crossed into structural impairment.

The Share Price

The owner-earnings yield currently sits at approximately 6.0%. The fair-value benchmark — the yield level at which the business is priced for a steady long-run outcome — is 5.5%. A yield above the fair-value level means the share price is offering a slight discount to that neutral reading; a yield below it means the market is pricing in above-average growth or multiple expansion. At 6.0% against 5.5%, the share is modestly below fair value.

Put in price terms: the current share price is $182. The implied fair-value price — the level at which the yield equals 5.5% — is approximately $199. The buy edge, where the yield rises to 7.0% and the thesis delivers a clear margin of safety, implies a price of $156. The trim edge, where the yield falls to 4.0%, implies $273. The exit edge, where the yield falls to approximately 2.3%, implies $437.

Today's price is in the middle of the zone between fair value and the buy edge — not distressed, not expensive, but not offering the same margin of safety as a purchase made at $156 or below would. The market's current implied fair value of $199 is notably more cautious than the bottom-up valuation in the value analysis, which puts the base-case price at roughly $276 using a scenario-weighted approach from the three-year outlook. The source of that gap is discussed in Gaps in the Picture.

Why the Market Sees It Differently

The current share price of $182 sits about 8.5% below our fair-value estimate of $199. The market, in other words, is pricing the business more cautiously than the base case warrants — the direction of the gap is that the price says the business will earn less over time. Our view says the opposite.

The arithmetic of the gap is approximately 8.6 percentage points of implied growth. Our base case calls for roughly 13% annual earnings growth. The market, at current prices, is implying approximately 4.4% — a figure closer to nominal economic growth than to the platform's recent trajectory.

Three hypotheses explain why the market might be right and the base case wrong. First, AI-mediated disintermediation may already be eroding the paid-search funnel in ways that are not yet visible in quarterly data; the inflection could arrive faster than a two-quarter trailing indicator would catch. Second, the regulatory front — the European Digital Markets Act, the Spanish fine, the Italian investigation — may collectively represent a sustained compression of take-rate that the market is pricing in but the base case does not fully capture. Third, the management team is approaching the outer limit of what buybacks can do to sustain per-share growth; if the $18.2 billion authorisation is deployed at prices above intrinsic value, the capital-return engine that drives much of the per-share compounding reverses into value destruction.

Of these, the first is the one the Q1 2026 filing does not yet resolve — the direct-booking mix and the marketing-to-bookings ratio are both stable, which is inconsistent with an accelerating disintermediation. The second and third are live concerns. For price to catch up to our view, the next two quarters of filings would need to show marketing costs flat to declining as a percentage of bookings, room-night growth returning to 8% or above ex-conflict, and a concrete AI product disclosure with measurable uptake — at which point the market's pessimistic growth assumption would likely compress back toward the base case.

What's Wobbling

Three items are on the watch list without crossing the exit threshold.

Marketing efficiency and search-engine headwind. The structural decline in search-engine optimisation traffic is a multi-year headwind the company has now explicitly flagged in its filings. If direct-channel growth does not offset the SEO decline, paid marketing spend will need to grow faster than gross bookings to sustain volume — the beginning of the cost spiral that exit condition (2) is designed to catch. The first-quarter 2026 data does not show this happening yet, but the setup is in place.

AI strategy: narrated but not evidenced. The Connected Trip is described in management communications as the AI-powered answer to disintermediation. Mobile app share is rising. But the filing contains no concrete AI metrics — no conversion lift from AI-assisted search, no engagement data for the AI trip planner, no disclosure of what share of bookings flows through AI-assisted funnels. Five quarters remain before the Q3 2027 deadline. The concern is that each quarter without concrete evidence narrows the window for demonstrable traction before the clock runs out.

Forward demand signal muted. The company gave no formal guidance for the second quarter of 2026 and flagged the Middle East conflict as a potential softener for future demand. The $8.2 billion in deferred merchant bookings on the balance sheet at March 2026 provides a near-term revenue floor, but the absence of forward booking commentary is unusual for a company that typically projects traveller demand with some confidence.

What Could Still Go Wrong

  • AI agents route travellers directly to hotels, bypassing Booking.com. If Google Gemini, a large-language-model booking assistant, or a direct-booking app becomes the primary interface for trip planning and reservation, the marketing-to-bookings ratio rises structurally, the take-rate compresses, and the platform re-rates at a fraction of its current value. The bear case assigns this a 25% probability.
  • EU enforcement crosses a structural threshold. A Digital Markets Act decision imposing mandatory product changes — not just fines — could constrain how Booking.com presents search results, manages pricing parity, or integrates its own services. A fine at 10% of global revenue would cost roughly $2.7 billion, painful but survivable. A forced business-model change would be harder to absorb.
  • Marketing cost inflates as search-engine optimisation declines. If the SEO-to-paid shift accelerates and direct-booking mix does not rise to compensate, the margin that makes the buyback programme so compelling starts to shrink. Two consecutive years of rising marketing-to-bookings would satisfy exit condition (2).
  • Capital allocation deteriorates. The $18.2 billion buyback authorisation, deployed at prices above the base-case intrinsic value, would be value-destructive. A large acquisition — the company has the financial capacity — into a fast-moving but unpredictable AI travel market could similarly destroy capital. Neither has happened; both are live risks given the scale of the authorisation.
  • Revenue recognition timing masks demand weakness. The deferred merchant bookings balance ($8.2 billion at March 2026, representing booked but not yet checked-in travel) means reported revenue lags real demand by weeks to months. A demand deterioration would show up in room-night growth before it shows up in revenue — the indicator to watch is the quarterly room-night figure, not the revenue line.

Gaps in the Picture

  • Two fair-value anchors coexist in the underlying analysis: the yield-method figure of $199 (derived from applying the 5.5% fair-value yield to current owner earnings) and a bottom-up scenario-weighted figure of approximately $276 (derived from scenario free-cash-flow projections applied to through-cycle multiples). These diverge because the yield method anchors to the current level of owner earnings as a perpetuity, while the scenario method projects earnings growth over three years and applies an exit multiple. A future update will reconcile the two into a single framework.
  • The electronically tagged diluted share count in the latest filing (32.6 million) diverges from the market-implied figure (774.9 million) by roughly 24 times, consistent with a share split that occurred between the latest filing date and today. The calculation uses the market-implied count; the owner-earnings yield metric is mathematically invariant to this choice, but the discrepancy should be resolved in the next annual filing.
  • Owner earnings use total capital expenditure rather than a split between maintenance and growth spending. For an asset-light digital platform, true maintenance spending is likely materially below total capital expenditure, meaning the owner-earnings figure presented here understates the true cash the business generates. This is a conservative assumption; the direction of any correction is upward.
  • The risk-free rate used in the hurdle-rate calculation is read from the portfolio's broad-market index holding rather than a BKNG-specific market-data file, because the per-holding file does not carry this field. The rate as of 2026-07-10 is used.

The Rules

The action plan has six branches, each mapping a specific condition to a specific instruction.

If the thesis is confirmed — all conditions passing, no exit condition triggered, share price within the hold band — hold the position without action. Continue compounding.

If the thesis is weakened — one or more conditions showing deterioration but no exit condition formally triggered — do not add to the position. Run a detailed check on the specific deteriorating driver. Wait for the next two quarterly filings before deciding; one quarter of data is insufficient confirmation.

If the thesis is broken — a written exit condition fires — exit the full position within 30 days. No averaging down on a position where the thesis has broken. Record which exit condition triggered, for future learning.

If the share price falls below the buy edge ($156) — and the thesis is confirmed, with no exit condition triggered — add to the position in tranches over one to two quarters.

If the share price rises above the trim edge ($273) — trim 25 to 33 per cent of the position to harvest the premium while keeping the core holding. Re-evaluate the remaining position size after the trim.

If the share price rises above the exit edge ($437) — exit the full position. The market is pricing in roughly six years of base-case compounding in today's share price; the implied forward return falls below the long-run equity premium and reallocation into a cheaper alternative becomes the right move. No re-entry within 90 days.
