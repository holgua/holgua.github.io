5. Administration Connectivity & Current Political Climate (Austrian / EU)

Austrian Federal Government

The Austrian federal government (coalition led by ÖVP and SPÖ as of 2025) holds 51% of VERBUND via ÖBAG. VERBUND's CEO Michael Strugl is a former ÖVP politician with direct government experience at the provincial level as Energy Spokesman. ÖBAG Managing Director Edith Hlawati sits on the supervisory board.

This is not an arm's-length commercial relationship — it is a structurally embedded government-utility relationship. The Austrian government's financial interests (ÖBAG dividend of ~€496M from VERBUND in 2025, contributing to ÖBAG's €1.26bn total dividend to the state) create strong alignment between maintaining VERBUND's financial health and government policy. No announcement of any stake disposal or privatisation of VERBUND has been made. [ÖBAG privatisation discussions in Austria are ongoing in principle but no concrete VERBUND stake disposal is publicly announced — Confirmed (Parlament.gv.at, ÖBAG.gv.at)]

The windfall tax (EKB-S) extension to 2030 demonstrates that the government will prioritise short-term consumer price affordability and fiscal revenue over VERBUND's investment scope when politically expedient. The government simultaneously receives VERBUND dividends AND levies the windfall tax — a dual cash-extraction model that constrains VERBUND's capital allocation.

EU Institutions

VERBUND has 20 documented high-level meetings with EU Commission officials on energy market design issues. VERBUND's hydropower flexibility assets are actively supported by EU energy transition policy: the EU Electricity Market Design Regulation (EMD 2023), EU Taxonomy classification of hydro, and the EU Grids Action Plan all favour flexible renewable generation capacity, of which VERBUND is Austria's dominant provider. The Austrian government's 2025 Electricity Industry Act (ElWG) and the EU-level framework are directionally aligned.

Narrative Assessment

For an investor, VERBUND's political picture is unusual: the government is simultaneously the most important ally and the most important risk. The 51% Austrian government ownership (via ÖBAG) provides structural protection against hostile regulatory action, competitive threats, and debt market pressure — no investor should underestimate the value of having the Austrian federal government as a majority partner with a direct financial interest in VERBUND's solvency. The CEO's ÖVP political background and the ÖBAG supervisory board seat create an unusually direct communication channel between company management and the government. EU energy policy is directionally aligned with VERBUND's hydro-and-grid model: EU Taxonomy hydro classification, the EU Grids Action Plan, and EU capacity mechanism rules all benefit a flexible, low-carbon TSO like VERBUND. The principal risk to this picture is the willingness of the Austrian government to extend and tighten the EKB-S windfall tax (now running to 2030 at 95% rates above €90/MWh), which management says constrains investment capacity. This creates a structural conflict between the government's role as guardian of consumer electricity affordability and its role as a financial beneficiary of VERBUND's strong earnings. The resolution of the ongoing E-Control tariff review (expected 2026) will be the key near-term political event: an adverse tariff determination would directly threaten config.yaml exit condition 1 (regulated ROE below 4%). The overall political risk is Medium, reflecting a genuinely powerful structural alignment that is partially offset by the government's demonstrated willingness to use VERBUND as a fiscal and price-management instrument.
