PayPal: The Bet Still Works

The Bottom Line

The bet is working. PayPal's latest quarterly filing confirms the two pillars the investment case rests on — the buyback machine and the credit book — while the pricing, account growth, and regulatory lines remain mixed but below any exit-condition threshold. The Share Price sits in the lower half of the hold band, modestly below fair value. The one condition to watch is the branded checkout volume trend: it is not separately reported in the latest filing, and two class-action lawsuits allege that management has withheld information about it — that is the open question, not a trigger.

Recent Development

The three months ending in March 2026 confirmed the two pillars the thesis rests on, while leaving the branded-checkout question unanswered. PayPal repurchased 34 million shares for $1.5 billion at an average price of $44.60 in the quarter — the share count fell from 920 million to 892 million, a three-percent reduction in a single quarter. The authorisation remaining stood at $12.4 billion. On the credit side, the consumer net charge-off rate improved to 3.0% annualised from 4.5% a year earlier, well below the 10% line that would trigger exit condition (3). Total payment volume grew 11% compared to the same period a year earlier, with payment transactions rising 7% to 6,475 million.

The operating margin declined to 18% from 20% in the same quarter a year earlier, driven by the ongoing mix shift toward Braintree, which carries a higher transaction-expense rate than PayPal's branded product. Full-year 2025 operating income of $6,065 million remains 10% above the $5.5 billion exit-condition floor.

One new regulatory development: the UK Financial Conduct Authority opened an investigation in March 2026 into PayPal's contractual agreements with Visa and Mastercard. This covers commercial competition matters, not a product restriction — exit condition (2) requires a consent order materially restricting core product features, and no such order exists. The PPH Securities Action was dismissed with prejudice in March 2026, removing a legacy litigation overhang. The branded checkout question — whether share is being lost faster than the data admits — feeds directly into the Share Price and remains the single open wobble.

About PayPal

PayPal is a digital payments network that sits between consumers and merchants when money moves online. A consumer with a PayPal wallet or Venmo account can pay at checkout on a merchant's website; the merchant processes the transaction through PayPal's infrastructure and pays a small fee on each payment. The same infrastructure underlies Braintree, PayPal's service for large merchants that want to embed payment processing directly into their own systems. PayPal also extends short-term credit — buy-now-pay-later products and consumer loans — that earns interest revenue alongside the transaction fees.

The network spans roughly 439 million active consumer accounts and operates across approximately 200 countries under a portfolio of money-transmitter licences that took more than twenty years to assemble. That licence portfolio is the structural foundation of the moat: Apple Pay and Google Pay can compete for the checkout moment, but neither operates a two-sided credit, dispute-resolution, and cross-border settlement infrastructure at PayPal's geographic breadth. Revenue in the twelve months ending in December 2025 was $33.2 billion, with operating income of $6.1 billion.

The Case for the Bet

The central bet is that $5–7 billion a year of free cash flow, returned almost entirely through share buybacks at a depressed share price, is sufficient to compound per-share earnings at a double-digit rate even if top-line growth stays in the mid-single digits. That bet depends on five conditions remaining intact:

  1. Branded checkout volume is not losing share at a structural rate. The exit condition fires if branded checkout share falls by more than 2 percentage points in a single fiscal year, signalling that Apple Pay, Google Pay, and other OS-native wallets are permanently displacing PayPal at the consumer checkout moment rather than just chipping at the edges.

Latest reading: Branded checkout volume is not separately disclosed in the quarterly filing for the three months ending in March 2026. Revenue attributed to PayPal's branded products grew approximately $140 million compared to the same quarter a year earlier, suggesting growth — but the pace is slower than Braintree. Two class-action lawsuits (the Darcy and Norfolk securities actions) allege that the company concealed impediments to its branded checkout strategy; both remain at the pleadings stage with no judicial finding and no admission. No exit condition is triggered.

  1. No regulatory consent order restricts core product features. The exit condition fires if the Consumer Financial Protection Bureau or the US Department of Justice issues a consent order that materially limits what PayPal can offer consumers.

Latest reading: The CFPB and the Federal Trade Commission each have open civil investigative demands — formal requests for information — but neither has converted to an enforcement order. The UK Financial Conduct Authority investigation opened in March 2026 covers contractual terms with card networks, not PayPal's core product. Accruals for legal proceedings were not material as of March 2026. Condition intact.

  1. Consumer credit losses stay below 10%. If the consumer net charge-off rate rises above 10% without a disclosed recovery path, the bet on credit quality breaks.

Latest reading: The consumer net charge-off rate improved to 3.0% annualised in the three months ending in March 2026, down from 4.5% in the same quarter a year earlier. The share of current loans and receivables held at 96.3%. Condition intact with a wide margin.

  1. Buyback discipline is maintained. The exit condition fires if management abandons the repurchase programme or takes on material incremental debt above 2.0 times net debt to earnings before interest, taxes, depreciation, and amortisation (the ratio that measures how many years of operating earnings it would take to repay borrowings) without a compelling strategic reason.

Latest reading: PayPal repurchased 34 million shares for $1.5 billion in the three months ending in March 2026, with $12.4 billion of authorisation remaining. A strategic reorganisation announced in April 2026 targets $1.5 billion in annualised run-rate savings over two to three years. No debt build-up; net cash position stands at $14 billion. Condition intact.

  1. Operating income stays above the floor. The exit condition fires if annual operating income (as reported) falls below $5.5 billion, or if management explicitly guides its adjusted operating income figure below $5.0 billion for the following year.

Latest reading: Full-year 2025 operating income was $6,065 million, 10% above the $5.5 billion floor. The three months ending in March 2026 delivered $1,488 million, down from $1,530 million in the same period a year earlier — the decline reflects Braintree mix drag, a known structural dynamic, not a new deterioration. No guidance below the floor has been issued. Condition intact.

The Share Price

The share price of $45.65 sits in the lower half of the hold band, modestly below fair value. The owner earnings yield — the ratio of owner earnings (cash profit after maintenance spending) to the market's total price for the business — currently stands at 11.1%. The system considers that yield fairly valued at 10.5%, a generous level to buy above 12.5%, and a signal to trim below 8.5%. At 11.1%, the price is between the fair-value yield and the buy signal, closer to the buy end.

The implied fair-value price, computed from those thresholds, is $48. At $45.65, shares trade about 6% below that anchor — not a steep discount but a modest one. The shares are not cheap in the sense of sitting below the buy line; they are also not expensive. For a holder already in the position, the correct posture is to hold and continue compounding. For a prospective buyer, waiting for the $41 buy line offers a better entry.

The market's stance is notable: at 8.6 times trailing earnings and 6.4 times operating cash flow, the price embeds an assumption of sustained earnings decline that the last three years of filings do not support.

The Standing Orders

Pre-committed rules translate the share-price bands into three standing orders and two price alerts. Each is the operationalisation of a fixed condition — not a new judgement made by this note.

Price alerts (informational).
- A first alert at $48, the fair-value anchor. The crossing means the share price has returned to a neutral reading; no action follows, but a fresh read of the conditions is warranted.
- A second alert at $41, the cheap edge. The crossing arms the buy order below; before adding, the latest re-validation should confirm the conditions are still intact.

Standing orders (actionable).
- A standing buy order at $41. Per the Rules: add in tranches once the share price closes at or below this line and the conditions are still intact. The order may be staged across one or two quarters rather than filled in a single transaction.
- A standing trim order at $60. Per the Rules: trim a quarter to a third of the position when the share price closes at or above this line, to harvest the premium while keeping the core holding.
- A standing exit order at $78. Per the Rules: close the full position when the share price closes at or above this line. The market would be pricing in roughly 11 years of base-case compounding instantly at that reading; the implied forward return falls below the long-term equity premium, and redeployment into a cheaper alternative beats continuing to hold. No re-entry within 90 days defuses anchor bias.

The three order levels do not contradict the cost-basis verdicts in the Bottom Line: those verdicts describe the current reading; the standing orders describe what follows if the share price moves to each line.

What's Wobbling

Three things are mixed but none cross a line.

Branded checkout volume is dark. The company does not separately disclose branded checkout volume in its quarterly filings, so the most important leading indicator of moat health is unobservable each quarter. The two class-action lawsuits allege the company concealed problems with its branded checkout growth strategy; that allegation remains at the pleadings stage. The risk is real: if branded checkout share is eroding faster than the aggregate numbers imply, the exit condition could fire before it becomes visible in the headline figures.

The Braintree margin drag is ongoing. Braintree — the processing service for large merchants — generates lower margins than PayPal's branded checkout product. As Braintree grows faster, it pulls the blended margin down. Operating margin in the three months ending in March 2026 fell to 18% from 20% a year earlier. Management has guided that this dynamic stabilises as Braintree scale matures, but it has not yet done so. The full-year 2025 operating income remains above the floor.

Three regulatory investigations are open. The CFPB and Federal Trade Commission civil investigative demands are ongoing. The UK Financial Conduct Authority opened a new investigation in March 2026 into PayPal's Visa and Mastercard contractual provisions. None of these has produced a consent order or product restriction. Amounts accrued for probable legal losses were not material as of March 2026.

What Could Still Go Wrong

  • Branded checkout displacement accelerates. The bear case has Apple Pay and Google Pay — which are built into the operating systems of most smartphones — continuing to claim the consumer checkout moment, reducing PayPal's branded volume share by more than 2 percentage points in a single year. If that happens, the exit condition fires and the free-cash-flow base that supports the buyback thesis shrinks.

  • Lores makes a large acquisition. The thesis depends on continuing to deploy $5–7 billion of annual free cash flow into buybacks at depressed prices. A single acquisition above $3 billion at a high price-to-earnings multiple would destroy per-share value and signal a shift away from capital-return discipline. New chief executives without deep sector experience have a history of large deals.

  • A regulatory order restricts the product. If the open CFPB or Department of Justice civil investigative demands convert to enforcement orders that restrict PayPal's credit product, BNPL, or core wallet features, operating income would decline and the exit condition fires.

  • Credit losses rise materially. The buy-now-pay-later and consumer loan books are cyclically sensitive. A macroeconomic downturn could push charge-off rates well above the current 3.0%, tightening the credit business and reducing the free-cash-flow base.

  • CEO instability continues. Enrique Lores is the third chief executive in five years. If the board replaces him again before a sustained strategy takes hold, the market's already-depressed confidence in management continuity would deteriorate further, and the probability of a value-destroying acquisition would rise.

The Rules

Six rules cover every combination of thesis state and price:

  • If the filing evidence shows the bet is still working and the share price sits in the hold band: hold, no action, continue compounding.
  • If any condition is deteriorating but no exit condition has fired: do not add. Revisit after two consecutive quarters of confirming data — one quarter of weakness is not sufficient to act.
  • If any exit condition fires: close the full position within 30 days. No averaging down. Record which exit condition fired for future reference.
  • If the share price falls to or below $41 and no exit condition has fired: confirm the conditions are intact, then add in tranches over one to two quarters.
  • If the share price rises to or above $60: trim 25 to 33 percent of the position to harvest the premium. Keep the core holding.
  • If the share price reaches $78: close the full position. At that price the market is pricing in roughly 11 years of base-case compounding immediately; the implied forward return falls below the long-term equity premium and reallocation beats holding. No re-entry within 90 days.

Scenario Definitions

A scenario is a joint assumption about two independent variables: operating trajectory (how the holding's underlying cash flows grow relative to their long-run trend) and exit multiple (what the market pays for those cash flows at the horizon, relative to a fair-value anchor).

Scenario Operating trajectory Exit multiple
Bear Below trend Below fair value
Base At trend At fair value
Bull Above trend At or above fair value

Non-negotiable rule: In the bear case, both dimensions must be adverse simultaneously — a scenario where only the multiple moves is a re-rating scenario, not a bear.

Bear cases are comparable in structure across all holding types (company, ETF, bond), but not in magnitude. A company bear reflects idiosyncratic execution failure; an ETF bear reflects a broad market drawdown. Different mitigations, different diversification implications.
