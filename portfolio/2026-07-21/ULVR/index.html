About Unilever

Unilever sells everyday consumer products — soap, shampoo, condiments, laundry detergent, and deodorant — to roughly a third of the world's population on any given day. Its brands include Dove, Axe, Domestos, Magnum (until December 2025), and until mid-2027, Hellmann's and Knorr. Revenue is generated through a combination of pricing power in developed markets and volume growth in emerging ones; the business earns by charging a premium over cheaper private-label alternatives, sustaining that premium through decades of brand investment, and distributing through a proprietary last-mile logistics network spanning 190-plus countries that competitors cannot replicate quickly or cheaply.

The business has been through a period of deliberate simplification. The Ice Cream division — home to Magnum and Wall's — was separated as an independent company in December 2025. The Nutrition portfolio, including Hellmann's mayonnaise, Knorr sauces, and Marmite, is being sold to McCormick Foods for USD 44.8bn under a deal signed in March 2026 and expected to close in mid-2027. What remains will be a leaner business concentrated in Beauty and Wellbeing, Personal Care, and Home Care — categories where brand loyalty is stronger, private-label substitution risk is lower, and margins are structurally higher. Operating income across the five years through FY2025 held within a band of EUR 8.7bn to EUR 10.8bn on revenues that swung between EUR 50bn and EUR 60bn — a degree of earnings stability that is unusual for a company of this size operating across as many geographies.

The Case for the Bet

Unilever is held as a wide-moat franchise whose brand equity and emerging-market distribution network can sustain above-cost-of-capital returns for a decade or more, returning the majority of earnings to shareholders through a progressive dividend while executing a portfolio simplification that concentrates capital in its highest-quality segments:

  1. Operating margin stays above 14% across two consecutive fiscal years. The moat earns its name through pricing power: the ability to pass commodity cost increases to consumers without losing volume. A margin falling below 14% for two consecutive years would indicate that this pricing power has structurally eroded — not a cyclical dip, but a permanent reduction in what the brands can charge.

Latest reading: 17.9% operating margin in FY2025 (EUR 9,037M operating income on EUR 50,503M revenue), the highest in five years. The range across FY2021–FY2025 has been 16.6%–17.9%; the exit threshold has never been approached.

  1. Return on invested capital stays above 10% across two consecutive fiscal years. A business with a genuine cost-of-capital advantage earns above its cost of capital. When that spread collapses — when the capital return ratio falls below the estimated cost of capital of 8–9% plus a buffer — it signals that brand equity is no longer generating economic value, only sustaining scale.

Latest reading: 14.7% return on invested capital in FY2025, recovered from a FY2024 trough of 11.5% that was driven by goodwill impairments and restructuring costs, not operating deterioration. The spread above cost of capital is approximately 5–7 percentage points.

  1. Owner earnings stay above EUR 5.5bn across two consecutive fiscal years. The annual cash the business generates above what it needs to maintain its asset base — operating cash flow minus estimated maintenance capital expenditure of EUR 1.5bn — must not fall below EUR 5.5bn for two consecutive years. That floor represents the bear-case minimum identified in the Value-Analysis.

Latest reading: EUR 6.85bn owner earnings in FY2025 (EUR 8.35bn operating cash flow minus EUR 1.5bn estimated maintenance capital expenditure), EUR 1.35bn above the floor. The three-year average is approximately EUR 7.6bn.

  1. No goodwill write-down above EUR 3bn in a single year without a disposal explanation. Large, unexplained goodwill impairments are a signal that management is acknowledging past acquisition failures under pressure — not rationalising the portfolio in an orderly way.

Latest reading: The FY2025 goodwill decline of EUR 4.6bn (from EUR 22.3bn to EUR 17.7bn) is fully explained by the Ice Cream demerger completed in December 2025. The exit condition carve-out applies: this was a deliberate, concurrently announced disposal, not a distress impairment.

  1. The accruals ratio stays below +0.10 for two consecutive fiscal years. The accruals ratio measures how closely reported earnings track actual cash generation. A ratio persistently above +0.10 signals that earnings are running ahead of cash — a potential indicator of accrual inflation or earnings management.

Latest reading: +0.016 in FY2025, within a long series of near-zero to slightly negative readings (FY2024: −0.047; FY2023: −0.039). Well inside the threshold.

  1. The McCormick Foods transaction closes at no more than 20% below the agreed USD 44.8bn. A deal collapse or a forced repricing would leave capital stranded in lower-quality portfolio assets and damage management's strategic credibility.

Latest reading: As of July 2026, the deal remains on track. Regulatory review is proceeding across three jurisdictions (CMA, EU, US Department of Justice); no enforcement action has been reported.

  1. Power Brand underlying sales growth stays above 1.5% across two consecutive trading periods. If Unilever's most important brands consistently lose pricing premium to private-label alternatives — measured by the company's own reported underlying sales growth metric — the brand moat is eroding faster than the emerging-market growth tailwind can compensate.

Latest reading: Power Brand underlying sales growth of +4.3% in FY2025 and +5.0% in Q1 2026, with volume growth of +4.0% — the first signal that the recovery has shifted from price-led to volume-led, the more durable form.

The Share Price

At GBX 4,671 (equivalent to approximately $4,671 in the valuation model's yield terms), the share price sits in the middle of the hold band. The owner earnings yield — the ratio of annual cash generated above maintenance requirements to the current market value — is 6.43%, against a fair-value yield of 6.50%. In other words, the share is priced at almost exactly what the analysis calls fair value. The difference is less than one tenth of one percentage point.

The band runs from a buy edge at a yield of 8.0% (approximately GBX 3,754, where a buyer gets a 25% margin of safety relative to fair value) to a trim edge at a yield of 5.0% (approximately GBX 6,006, where the yield premium over the risk-free rate no longer justifies the equity risk). Neither edge is close: reaching the buy edge would require a roughly 20% price decline; reaching the trim edge would require a roughly 29% price rise. At the current reading, no valuation-band action is triggered.

What's Wobbling

The one wobble from the latest re-validation is net leverage. Unilever carries approximately USD 23.3bn in net debt — roughly three times its normalised annual owner earnings. This is not a new development; it is a pre-acknowledged element of the thesis. Debt at this level limits financial flexibility: if operating cash flow fell sharply toward the bear-case level of USD 4.3bn, debt coverage would become strained. For now, however, the operating cash flow to revenue ratio — a proxy for the business's ability to service debt from operations — runs at 16.5%, well above the 12% level that would trigger a formal watch signal. The leverage picture is also expected to improve materially when the McCormick transaction closes and USD 44.8bn in disposal proceeds arrive.

The leverage wobble does not cross any of the conditions in the Case for the Bet. There is no exit condition tied to net debt alone; the only leverage-related threshold is the OCF/Revenue watch, which has not been breached.

A separate technical signal — the Fair-Value-Bands model (a cross-referencing valuation tool) implying a materially different fair-value yield of 4.2% against the 6.5% used in the primary owner-earnings methodology — does not affect the verdict but flags a calibration question for the next annual review cycle. The owner-earnings yield is the authoritative method; the bands model is a cross-check, not the primary signal.

What Could Still Go Wrong

  • EM currency basket declines 15% or more against the euro. Approximately 60% of Unilever's revenue comes from emerging markets, where local currencies can weaken sharply against the reporting currency. A sustained basket decline would compress EUR-reported revenue and owner earnings without reflecting any deterioration in the underlying business volumes — triggering a bear scenario at approximately USD 4.3bn in annual owner earnings and a potential −60% loss from the current price.

  • Private-label penetration in developed-market food categories accelerates before the McCormick disposal closes. Hellmann's and Knorr face structural private-label competition in European and North American grocery. If Power Brand underlying sales growth falls below +1.5% for two consecutive trading periods, exit condition 7 fires — the moat erosion signal is in the sales data before it appears in the income statement.

  • The McCormick Foods transaction collapses or is forcibly repriced more than 20% below the agreed USD 44.8bn. This is the highest single-outcome risk in the near term. A deal collapse would leave capital stranded in lower-margin Nutrition assets, delay deleveraging by several years, and damage the Growth Action Plan's strategic credibility.

  • Operating margin falls below 14% for two consecutive years under commodity re-inflation. A return of the 2022-era commodity shock — without the pricing power to pass it through — would compress margins toward bear-case levels. The five-year record suggests Unilever can defend margins under most cost cycles, but a sustained commodity re-inflation combined with private-label pressure would challenge this history.

  • Capital allocation deteriorates: a large acquisition at an excessive price. The most value-destructive risk is a management decision to deploy the McCormick proceeds in a large acquisition at a premium multiple — repeating the pattern of the aborted GlaxoSmithKline consumer bid in 2022. No current indication of this risk, but it is structurally present for any company receiving USD 44.8bn in a single cash inflow.

Gaps in the Picture

The most significant data gap is the narrative failure of Unilever's ESEF annual filing: all five years of management discussion, risk-factor disclosures, and strategic commentary failed to extract from the structured filing format. Every qualitative driver assessment in the latest re-validation therefore rests on structured financial data (extracted from the filing), proxy data from quarterly trading statements, and cross-referenced analysis files — not on management's own words in the annual report. To close this gap, the FY2025 annual report PDF is available at Unilever's investor relations website.

Capital expenditure is not tagged in Unilever's ESEF taxonomy, meaning the maintenance capital expenditure estimate of EUR 1.5bn (approximately 3% of revenue) used in the earnings calculation is a proxy, not a filed figure. This introduces some imprecision into the core valuation metric; the proxy has been applied consistently across three years and is consistent with the company's physical asset base.

Net debt is also a derived figure: the long-term borrowings tag is absent from the ESEF taxonomy, so the USD 23.3bn estimate is sourced from Market-Data rather than directly from the annual filing's balance sheet.

The Rules

If the thesis is confirmed — all exit conditions are intact and the earnings yield sits between 5.0% and 8.0% — the rules call for holding the position. The dividend yield of approximately 3.71% is the primary expected return in the base case; the holding is carried for income and for optionality on the portfolio simplification re-rating.

If the thesis weakens — a specific driver deteriorates without yet triggering an exit condition — the rules call for not adding to the position, running a fresh re-validation focused on the deteriorating driver, and monitoring for one additional quarter before reassessing position size. Key dates are H1 2026 results (expected August 2026) for Power Brand underlying sales growth and operating margin, and continuing monitoring of McCormick transaction regulatory clearance status.

If the thesis breaks — any one of the seven exit conditions is confirmed from filed financial data or press releases — the rules call for exiting the full position unconditionally. Exit is unconditional on conditions 1, 2, and 7 (operating margin below 14%, return on invested capital below 10%, Power Brand growth below 1.5% — all three signal permanent moat erosion). No averaging down after an exit condition fires.

If the share price falls to the buy edge (owner earnings yield rises to 8.0% or above — approximately GBX 3,470 or below), the rules call for adding in two tranches over one to two quarters, subject to confirming that all thesis conditions are intact and that operating margin has not fallen below 16% in the most recent reported period.

If the share price rises to the trim edge (owner earnings yield falls below 5.0% — approximately GBX 5,600 or above at current owner earnings), the rules call for trimming 25–33% of the position. The trim is mechanical and requires no thesis deterioration; it reflects the yield no longer compensating for equity risk at the equity premium over the German Bund.

If the share price rises to the exit edge (owner earnings yield falls below 4.5% — approximately GBX 6,800–7,000, near the bull-case price), the rules call for closing the full position. At that yield the market is pricing the bull case as near-certain; risk/reward asymmetry is eliminated. No re-entry within 90 days.

Scenario Definitions

A scenario is a joint assumption about two independent variables: operating trajectory (how the holding's underlying cash flows grow relative to their long-run trend) and exit multiple (what the market pays for those cash flows at the horizon, relative to a fair-value anchor).

Scenario Operating trajectory Exit multiple
Bear Below trend Below fair value
Base At trend At fair value
Bull Above trend At or above fair value

Non-negotiable rule: In the bear case, both dimensions must be adverse simultaneously — a scenario where only the multiple moves is a re-rating scenario, not a bear.

Bear cases are comparable in structure across all holding types (company, ETF, bond), but not in magnitude. A company bear reflects idiosyncratic execution failure; an ETF bear reflects a broad market drawdown. Different mitigations, different diversification implications.
