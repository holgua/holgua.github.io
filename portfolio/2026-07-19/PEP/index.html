PepsiCo: The Bet Is Wobbling

The Bottom Line

Data as of 2026-07-19 · Latest filing 2026-07-09.

The bet is wobbling. PepsiCo's brand moat is intact at the gross-profit level — gross margin held at 54.7% in the first half of 2026, well above the 52% floor that would signal structural erosion — but the North American beverage business has now declined in unit volume for at least nine consecutive quarterly reporting periods, and the improvement signal that was written into the thesis has failed to materialise. As detailed in The Share Price section, the current price sits in the middle of the hold band, at fair value. The long-run expected return is about 8% a year — a modest equity premium — but the three-year scenario outlook is negative in both the bear and base cases, which puts that long-run figure in context: it requires a long enough horizon and enough patience to outlast the current pressure. The one condition to watch is whether PBNA beverage volume turns positive at the three months ending in September 2026; that is the first data point that would begin to confirm the base case rather than extend the wobble.

Recent Development

The latest quarterly filing — covering the 24 weeks ending 2026-06-13 — confirmed the central tension in the PepsiCo story: the business's structural defences are holding, but the beverages recovery is not arriving.

Gross margin came in at 54.7% for the first half and 54.2% for the most recent quarter, both solidly within the 53–55% band that has characterised the past five years. That figure is the operative test of whether the brand portfolio can absorb cost pressure — and it passed again, including a period of active commodity-cost headwinds and tariff escalation. Free cash generation improved sharply: the first half produced $1,170M versus a negative $342M in the year-earlier period, driven by better operating profit and lower capital spending. The 54th consecutive annual dividend increase — 4% to $5.92 per share annualised — was declared and paid without interruption.

What is not yet arriving is the beverage-volume recovery. The PepsiCo Beverages North America unit saw volumes decline 4% in the most recent quarter and 3% in the first half, extending an unbroken streak of quarterly volume declines to at least nine consecutive periods. The thesis had noted a tentative first improvement signal at the prior quarter; that signal was contradicted by the actual result. A separate wobble: the fair value of the contingent consideration tied to the poppi acquisition — the prebiotic cola brand acquired for $1.95 billion — fell from $278 million at year-end to $117 million by mid-year, reflecting lower probability that the brand will meet its near-term revenue milestones. On the positive side, the North America snacks business posted 1% unit-volume growth in the first half — the first positive snack-volume reading in the monitoring window.

Not one of these developments triggers a written exit condition. The compound trigger that matters most — beverage-volume decline sustained for three or more consecutive quarters and operating margin falling below 11.5% — remains unactivated because operating margin stands at 16.6%. The assessment in The Bottom Line stands: wobbling, not broken.

About PepsiCo

PepsiCo is, in its simplest description, two businesses inside one company. The first is Frito-Lay, the dominant maker of savoury snacks in North America: Lay's, Doritos, Cheetos, Tostitos, SunChips, and roughly two dozen other brands collectively hold approximately 60% of the US crisps-and-snacks market — a share that no competitor comes close to replicating. The second is the beverages business: Pepsi, Gatorade, Mountain Dew, Lipton iced tea, and a range of water and sports-drink brands commanding roughly 16% of the US liquid-refreshment market, behind Coca-Cola's 20%. The two businesses share a distribution backbone — a direct-delivery network that routes PepsiCo products from its own trucks to shop shelves, bypassing the warehouse and giving the company unusual control over shelf placement and on-floor merchandising.

Beyond North America, PepsiCo operates large beverage and snack businesses across Latin America, Europe, Africa, and Asia, selling in more than 200 countries. Those international businesses have become relatively more important as organic growth in mature North American categories has slowed. The company's revenue is roughly $94 billion a year. It has increased its annual dividend in each of the past 54 consecutive years — a streak that spans every recession, every oil shock, and every major geopolitical disruption since 1972, and that management treats as a binding commitment to owners.

The Case for the Bet

The bet rests on a simple proposition: PepsiCo's combination of brand strength and distribution infrastructure creates a cash-generating machine durable enough to compound at a low-to-mid-single-digit rate for many years — and the key question at any given moment is whether the price paid for that machine offers a fair return. Five conditions define what "durable" means in practice:

  1. Gross margin held above 52% in any two consecutive quarters. Gross margin (the share of each sales dollar left after the direct cost of producing and delivering the product) is the clearest signal that brand pricing power is absorbing commodity cost rather than surrendering it. The 52% threshold is the floor below which brand power can no longer be considered structurally intact.

Latest reading: 54.7% for the first half of 2026 and 54.2% for the most recent quarter (as of 2026-06-13), both well above the exit floor and within the long-run 53–55% band. This condition is not at risk.

  1. Operating margin stays above 11.5% for any two consecutive quarters. The operating margin (profit after production, distribution, and selling costs, as a share of revenue) must stay above 11.5% to confirm that the FY2025 compression was temporary rather than structural. Below this level, in combination with persistent beverage-volume decline, the base-case thesis collapses.

Latest reading: 16.6% for both the most recent quarter and the first half of 2026 (as of 2026-06-13). This condition is unambiguously clear of its floor; the operating margin recovery that the thesis projected is visible in the reported figures.

  1. PBNA beverage volumes do not decline for more than three consecutive quarters while operating margin is also below 11.5%. This is a compound exit condition — both legs must fire simultaneously. Beverage-volume decline alone, without margin deterioration, is a wobble, not a break.

Latest reading: PBNA beverage-unit volume declined 4% in the most recent quarter and 3% in the first half of 2026 (as of 2026-06-13), extending the unbroken decline streak to at least nine consecutive reporting periods. The volume leg is active and deepening. The margin leg is not triggered. The compound exit condition has not fired.

  1. The annual dividend per share continues to increase year on year. The 54-year consecutive-increase streak functions as management's own public commitment to the durability of cash generation — it is the one signal that no financial engineering can sustain if the underlying business is deteriorating. A freeze or cut ends the streak and signals a loss of confidence in cash-flow durability.

Latest reading: The annual dividend was raised 4% to $5.92 per share annualised (up from $5.69), declared 2026-02-03 and paid in June 2026. The streak is intact. Exit condition not triggered.

  1. Net debt does not exceed three times operating earnings before depreciation for two consecutive annual reporting periods. At that leverage level, the debt load constrains the dividend programme and signals that acquisition financing has permanently stretched the balance sheet beyond the company's organic deleveraging capacity.

Latest reading: Net debt was approximately $42.9 billion as of 2026-06-13, implying a leverage ratio of approximately 2.7–2.9 times on a mid-year basis — above the 2.48 times reported for the full year 2025, but below the 3.0 times exit threshold. This figure can only be confirmed definitively from the annual report. Exit condition not triggered.

The Share Price

At $139 today, PepsiCo's share price sits at fair value on an earnings-yield basis — the 3-year average owner earnings of approximately $8.96 billion divided by the current market value of roughly $190 billion gives a yield of 4.7%, which is exactly the fair-value anchor. The share price implies that the market expects the business to compound at a low-to-mid-single-digit rate indefinitely — consistent with the thesis base case but leaving no cushion for any of the three live uncertainties: tariff trajectory, beverage-volume recovery, or integration execution.

The buy edge (at which a meaningful margin of safety opens up) sits at $101. The trim edge (at which the market's optimism becomes stretched) sits at $205. In plain terms: at today's price of $139, the share is priced as though the base case will play out without material setbacks. That is a reasonable but not conservative assessment given the nine-quarter beverage-volume decline still in progress.

What's Wobbling

The primary wobble is the PBNA beverage-volume decline, now in at least its ninth consecutive quarterly reporting period — a streak that directly contradicts the thesis's expectation of a "first credible improvement signal" at the time the thesis was written. The most recent quarter showed a 4% decline in beverage units (carbonated soft drinks down 3%, non-carbonated beverages down 4%), which was worse than the preceding periods, not better. The exit condition this wobble tests — a compound trigger requiring both volume decline and margin deterioration — has not fired because operating margin is at 16.6%, far above its 11.5% floor. But the volume leg of that compound condition is active and has been for at least nine periods. If operating margin were to deteriorate while volumes remain negative, the compound trigger would be close.

A secondary wobble is poppi. The contingent consideration embedded in the acquisition — a future payment tied to the brand meeting near-term revenue milestones — fell from $278 million at year-end 2025 to $117 million by mid-June 2026. That decline is an internal accounting signal, not a public revenue disclosure, but it implies that the brand is not tracking to its own acquisition business plan. The premiumisation thesis (acquiring health-oriented brands to offset GLP-1 headwinds in the snack and beverage categories) depends in part on poppi executing. A $161 million decline in contingent consideration in a single half-year is not a confirmation of that execution.

A third wobble, smaller in scale: the North America snacks business reported a 2% revenue decline in the most recent quarter despite 1% unit-volume growth — meaning the pricing concessions required to stabilise snack volumes are coming at a cost to revenue per unit. Volume stability is being purchased through softer pricing.

Not one of these individually crosses a written exit line. Together they describe a business under genuine pressure at the franchise level, even as the structural brand moat (visible in the gross-margin number) holds.

What Could Still Go Wrong

  • GLP-1 drug adoption accelerates faster than anticipated, compressing North America snack volumes by 3% or more annually for several consecutive years. The premiumisation acquisitions (poppi, Siete) are too small to offset the scale of volume loss in the core Frito-Lay portfolio in a three-year horizon.
  • Aluminium tariffs and other input-cost headwinds prove structural rather than reversible, eroding the gross margin below 52% over two or more consecutive quarters — the signal that brand pricing power is no longer absorbing commodity cost at the gross level.
  • The new leadership team — the chief financial officer, the chief executive for North America, and the chief executive for the beverage business all have less than 18 months of tenure — fails to sustain execution quality across a complex portfolio restructuring during the integration of two major acquisitions.
  • Leverage rises above three times operating earnings for two consecutive annual reporting periods without a credible deleveraging plan, constraining the dividend programme and the capital-allocation flexibility the business has historically relied upon.
  • A goodwill impairment charge related to Siete, poppi, or SodaStream exceeds $300 million in any single year within the FY2026–FY2028 window, repeating the Rockstar Energy precedent and confirming that the acquisition capital is destroying rather than creating value.

At that level the market is pricing in approximately 33 years of base-case compounding instantly; redeploy proceeds at the next cheap-edge crossing or into a higher-conviction position. No re-entry within 90 days.

Scenario Definitions

A scenario is a joint assumption about two independent variables: operating trajectory (how the holding's underlying cash flows grow relative to their long-run trend) and exit multiple (what the market pays for those cash flows at the horizon, relative to a fair-value anchor).

Scenario Operating trajectory Exit multiple
Bear Below trend Below fair value
Base At trend At fair value
Bull Above trend At or above fair value

Non-negotiable rule: In the bear case, both dimensions must be adverse simultaneously — a scenario where only the multiple moves is a re-rating scenario, not a bear.

Bear cases are comparable in structure across all holding types (company, ETF, bond), but not in magnitude. A company bear reflects idiosyncratic execution failure; an ETF bear reflects a broad market drawdown. Different mitigations, different diversification implications.
