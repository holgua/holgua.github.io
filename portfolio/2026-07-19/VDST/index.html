About US Treasury 0-1y

US Treasury 0-1y is a fund that holds US government short-term debt — instruments issued by the federal government that mature within one year, collectively known as Treasury bills, or T-bills. The fund reinvests the income those bills generate rather than paying it out as a cash dividend, so the value of a share accumulates over time rather than throwing off a separate income stream. It is listed on the London Stock Exchange in US dollars and is structured as a UCITS fund, an Irish-domiciled vehicle that meets European regulatory standards for retail investment products.

The fund's purpose in this portfolio is not to generate equity-like returns. It serves as a cash-equivalent placeholder — a home for capital that is waiting for a more attractively priced opportunity elsewhere. Holding T-bills through this structure costs 0.07% of the holding's value per year (the fund's ongoing charge), which covers the cost of the daily-liquid, regulated packaging. No tracking-difference or tracking-error figures were available in this snapshot; no securities lending revenue is configured.

Tax profile. The fund is an accumulating UCITS domiciled in Ireland and registered as a reporting fund for Austrian tax purposes (OeKB Meldefonds), which means the annual deemed-distribution is reported and taxable each year in Austria even though no cash changes hands. The annual reportable income figure (Ausschüttungsgleiche Erträge) has not yet been retrieved from OeKB; a provisional estimate of 4.5% is used in scoring until the exact figure is confirmed with a tax adviser.

Net cost. Annual cost: 0.07% expense ratio. No securities lending revenue is configured.

The Case for the Bet

The bet on US Treasury 0-1y is a structural one, not a directional one: T-bills maturing within a year will pay back par plus the prevailing short-term rate, making them the closest available proxy for a risk-free USD return. Holding this fund is justified so long as three conditions remain intact:

  1. The ongoing charges figure (expressed as a percentage of assets per year) stays below 0.10%. At 0.07%, the fund is cheaper than its most common peers. If the cost rises above 0.10% for two consecutive quarterly reviews, equivalent ultra-short-duration Treasury funds (SHV, BIL, and IBTS are named in the thesis) are available at lower cost and the fee advantage would be lost.

Latest reading: 0.07% as of the most recent carry-forward, as of 2026-07-15 (exact current figure unavailable from the automated feed — manual re-verification against the Vanguard factsheet required quarterly). The exit condition is inactive; headroom is approximately 3 basis points.

  1. Fund size stays above one billion US dollars. A fund holding short-dated T-bills derives much of its value from instant liquidity — the ability to redeem at close to the current market price on short notice. If assets under management fall below one billion dollars, the redemption risk and potential for wider bid-offer spreads becomes material.

Latest reading: approximately 2.4 billion dollars as of the most recent carry-forward (exact current figure unavailable from the automated feed — this figure is unverified in current project files and must be re-confirmed manually against the Vanguard factsheet quarterly). The exit condition is inactive.

  1. US sovereign credit remains at AA- or above with at least two of the three major rating agencies. The fund holds obligations of the US federal government; if that government's creditworthiness is structurally impaired — as evidenced by a downgrade below AA- by Fitch, Moody's, and S&P (any two of the three) — the cash-equivalent premise would need reassessment.

Latest reading: No downgrade action detected as of 2026-07-19. The US maintains ratings above the trigger threshold at all three agencies. This condition is monitored externally; no automated detection is implemented in the pipeline.

What Could Still Go Wrong

  • Expense ratio creep. If Vanguard raises the ongoing charge above 0.10% — and the carry-forward mechanism means the pipeline would not detect the change automatically — the fee advantage over peer funds evaporates. The risk is low given Vanguard's cooperative ownership structure, but it is not zero, and it requires an active human check each quarter.
  • Fund-size decline. A sustained redemption trend could push assets under management below the one-billion-dollar floor, at which point liquidity in the secondary market and the fund's ability to trade efficiently in its own basket could be impaired. The current level is well above the floor, but the figure is not automatically tracked.
  • Mandate drift. An index methodology change — maturity-band widening, index provider switch, or shift to a broader Treasury category — would alter the risk profile of the underlying basket. Such changes require a material UCITS Key Information Document update and are monitored externally.
  • US sovereign downgrade. A downgrade of US federal government debt below AA- by two of the three major agencies would require reassessment of the entire cash-equivalent premise. Current ratings are above the trigger. (For this fund, exit fires when any exit condition is tripped, when vehicle integrity is breached, or when the sovereign rating trigger fires — any of three paths.)

The Rules

The holding operates under six pre-committed rules. Exactly one applies at any point, depending on what the monitoring cycle finds.

  1. If all three thesis drivers confirm and vehicle integrity holds: hold the position. Vehicle integrity is intact — the expense ratio is within its budget, fund size is above its floor, and the mandate is unchanged.

  2. If any driver weakens but no exit condition is triggered: do not add. Re-assess at the next quarterly review, monitoring expense-ratio and fund-size trends before increasing the allocation.

  3. If one or more exit conditions fire (the expense ratio exceeds 0.10% for two consecutive quarters, fund size falls below one billion dollars, or US sovereign credit is downgraded below AA- by two agencies): exit the position. Reinvest the proceeds in EUR cash or short nominal German government bonds.

  4. If the basket yield rises to or above 5.50% (the buy-band threshold, corresponding to a share price at or below $41): add at the margin if cash is available, recognising that T-bill rates are unusually attractive versus EUR alternatives. Vehicle-integrity checks still gate any large addition.

  5. If the basket yield falls to or below 3.50% (the trim-band threshold, corresponding to a share price at or above $65): trim at the margin, recognising that the USD T-bill carry versus EUR cash has materially weakened.

  6. If the basket yield falls to or below 3.00% (the exit-band threshold, corresponding to a share price at or above $76): exit per the applicable written exit condition.

Scenario Definitions

A scenario is a joint assumption about two independent variables: operating trajectory (how the holding's underlying cash flows grow relative to their long-run trend) and exit multiple (what the market pays for those cash flows at the horizon, relative to a fair-value anchor).

Scenario Operating trajectory Exit multiple
Bear Below trend Below fair value
Base At trend At fair value
Bull Above trend At or above fair value

Non-negotiable rule: In the bear case, both dimensions must be adverse simultaneously — a scenario where only the multiple moves is a re-rating scenario, not a bear.

Bear cases are comparable in structure across all holding types (company, ETF, bond), but not in magnitude. A company bear reflects idiosyncratic execution failure; an ETF bear reflects a broad market drawdown. Different mitigations, different diversification implications.
