About the Fund

The SPDR S&P 500 ETF Trust, run by State Street Investment Management, tracks the S&P 500 Index on a cap-weighted basis — meaning the index weights each company by its total market value. The fund holds all 500 companies in the index in proportion to their size, making it the broadest single-vehicle exposure to large US equities available. Launched in January 1993, it is the oldest and most heavily traded equity fund in the United States, with roughly $781 billion in assets as of 2026-07-10. Each share of the fund represents a fractional claim on the earnings and dividends of those 500 companies.

The fund uses an older legal wrapper called a Unit Investment Trust rather than the more common open-end fund structure used by rivals such as iShares and Vanguard. The practical difference is minor for most holders, but the wrapper prevents the fund from reinvesting dividends between quarterly distribution dates and from lending out its securities for additional income. Annual running cost is 0.09%, among the lowest in the market. Tracking difference and tracking error versus the index are unavailable from this data pull; the State Street factsheet carries those figures. No capital-gain distributions are expected, consistent with the in-kind redemption mechanism typical of US equity funds of this kind.

The Case for the Bet

The position is held while the basket's earnings yield exceeds the risk-free rate plus at least two percentage points of equity risk premium, and while the fund continues to operate as a clean, low-cost vehicle for broad US equity exposure. Six numbered conditions define when to exit.

  1. The basket's earnings yield must exceed the 10-year Treasury yield. The equity risk premium — the amount by which the basket's earnings yield exceeds the risk-free rate — should be positive. If it turns negative for two consecutive quarterly readings, the risk-premium case has failed and the position closes, regardless of the absolute level of the earnings yield.

Latest reading: Earnings yield 3.70% vs. 10-year Treasury 4.55% → equity risk premium −0.85% as of 2026-07-10. Nine consecutive negative weekly readings recorded since mid-June 2026. The quarterly-persistence threshold (two consecutive quarter-end readings) has not yet been met. Exit condition not yet triggered.

  1. The annual expense ratio must remain at or below 0.15%. State Street's pricing discipline is part of the vehicle thesis. An expense ratio rise above 0.15% with no fee response from a rival fund would end the rationale for this specific vehicle.

Latest reading: Expense ratio 0.0945% as of 2026-07-10, 0.055 percentage points below the exit threshold. No early-warning flag configured. Exit condition inactive.

  1. Assets under management must remain above $100 billion. An extraordinary liquidity event that shrinks the fund below this floor would indicate a structural break in the vehicle's viability.

Latest reading: Assets under management $781.2 billion as of 2026-07-10, approximately 7.8 times the floor. Exit condition inactive.

  1. The largest single holding must not exceed 13% of the fund. A single company breaching this weight would signal a regime change in S&P 500 concentration, not normal drift.

Latest reading: NVIDIA holds the largest position at 7.50% as of 2026-07-10, well below the 13.0% exit ceiling. Exit condition inactive.

  1. The S&P 500 index methodology must remain broadly intact. A material restructuring of the index's constituent rules by S&P Dow Jones Indices, in a way that undermines the broad-market mandate, would break the vehicle thesis.

Latest reading: No automated signal available. No restructuring announced. Requires external monitoring of S&P Dow Jones Indices publications and the State Street annual report.

  1. The median return on equity of the top-ten holdings must remain above 15%. If the basket's underlying businesses lose their quality — in aggregate, as measured by this metric — the long-run compounding case weakens.

Latest reading: Median return on equity of the deduped top-ten holdings is 37.28% as of 2026-07-10, well above the 15% exit floor. The weighted-mean figure (70.38%) is materially higher, largely due to the inflated book-value denominators of NVIDIA and Apple from share buybacks; the median is the conservative anchor. Exit condition inactive.

What Could Still Go Wrong

  • Multiple contraction in a higher-rate environment. Even in the base scenario — 6% annual earnings growth over five years and a terminal price-to-earnings multiple of 20 times — the annualised forward return works out to only 1.18%. The bear scenario (3% earnings growth, terminal multiple of 15 times) produces −7.09% a year: a meaningful loss in nominal terms before inflation.
  • Exit condition (1) triggers next quarter. If the end-of-Q3 2026 reading also shows a negative equity risk premium, the written rule calls for closing the full position. Quarterly snapshots of a declining premium tracking toward exit territory are not a tail risk — they are the current reading.
  • Technology concentration. The fund's technology sector exposure stands at 38.55%, well above a 30% single-sector threshold often used as a concentration flag. This is a structural feature of the cap-weighted S&P 500 and not a fund-level anomaly, but it means the basket's returns are more sensitive to technology earnings and valuation shifts than a broad-market label might suggest.
  • UIT structural limitations. The Unit Investment Trust wrapper cannot reinvest dividends between distribution dates and cannot lend securities for incremental income, unlike the open-end fund wrapper used by lower-cost S&P 500 alternatives (iShares Core S&P 500, Vanguard S&P 500). If a rival fund narrows or closes the cost gap while offering a more flexible structure, the vehicle thesis weakens.
  • Index-methodology change. S&P Dow Jones Indices retains the right to change the S&P 500's constituent rules. A material restructuring of the eligibility criteria, float-weighting methodology, or sector classification system could change what the fund actually tracks.

The Rules

If the basket is reasonably priced and the vehicle is sound — hold. If the equity risk premium is negative or basket quality is declining but no exit condition has formally fired — do not add; re-validate after the next quarter, and if the condition persists, move to a rival fund or to cash. If vehicle integrity is breached or an exit condition triggers — exit the full position and move proceeds to risk-free assets or to a holding that meets the value test. If the share price falls to or below the buy edge — add to the position; the basket is meaningfully cheap relative to risk-free. If the share price rises above the trim edge — trim 25 to 33%; the basket is meaningfully expensive relative to risk-free. If the share price reaches or exceeds the exit edge — exit; the basket's earnings yield is at or below 3.0%, fully priced against risk-free with no equity risk premium remaining.
