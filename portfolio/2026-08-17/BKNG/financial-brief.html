PART I — INVESTMENT THESIS

1.1 One-Paragraph Thesis

Booking Holdings is the dominant global online travel agency, operating Booking.com, Priceline, Agoda, KAYAK, and OpenTable across 220+ countries on an asset-light model that generates $9.1B of free cash flow at a 33.8% FCF margin (FY2025). The business earns ROIC of 45% against an ~8% cost of capital — a decisive wide-moat signal — driven by a two-sided network of 4.4 million properties, a growing direct-booking channel (mid-50s% of room nights), and a Transformation Program delivering $550M+ in run-rate cost savings. The central debate is whether EU DMA/DSA regulatory obligations and AI-disrupted search distribution erode marketing efficiency and pricing power, or whether Connected Trip platform economics compound into structurally superior profitability; EDGAR data — Beneish M-Score -3.91 (no manipulation), accruals ratio persistently -0.23 to -0.28, and buyback-driven per-share accretion of ~6%/year — supports the thesis that capital discipline is intact.

PART III — COMPETITIVE POSITION (DISTILLED)

3.3 Moat — Type, Evidence, Trajectory

  • Network effects: 4.4M properties on Booking.com create self-reinforcing two-sided flywheel (supply grew 4.0M→4.4M in FY2025); parent §3.3
  • Cost advantages: Direct-booking mix mid-50s% provides structural marketing cost advantage vs. paid channels (4.4% of gross bookings); parent §3.3
  • Switching costs (mild): Genius loyalty, saved itineraries, payment credentials, and Connected Trip ecosystem create moderate lock-in; parent §3.3
  • Moat width: Wide — ROIC 45.0% vs. ~8% CoC; range 7.6% (FY2021 COVID) to 45% (FY2025) confirms widening trajectory; parent §3.3
  • Trajectory: Widening (cautiously) — EU DMA parity prohibition is a structural headwind; KAYAK impairment ($457M FY2025) = AI eroding meta-search
