About the Fund

US Treasury 0-1y is a fund managed by Vanguard Group (Ireland) Limited and listed on the London Stock Exchange in US dollars (ticker VDST, Irish ISIN IE00BLRPPV00). It tracks a basket of US government bonds — specifically Treasury bills — with remaining maturity of between zero and one year. Income from those bills is reinvested inside the fund rather than paid out as a cash distribution, so the fund's net asset value drifts gradually upward over time as coupons accumulate.

The economic substance of the position is straightforward: owning this fund is economically similar to holding a rolling portfolio of very short-term US government debt. The weighted-average time to maturity of the basket is approximately 4.5 months, which means the portfolio is continuously rolling its holdings into new Treasury bills as old ones expire. At an annual charge of 0.07%, the cost of this convenience is low — the fund's entire analytical case rests on whether that charge and the vehicle itself remain sound, not on any assessment of the underlying US government's creditworthiness beyond the three configured exit conditions.

Net cost: 0.07% annual charge (gross expense ratio; no lending-revenue offset available in this snapshot).

The Case for the Bet

The fund is held as a near-cash placeholder inside a portfolio denominated primarily in euros. The question at every quarterly review is not whether US Treasuries are a good investment in the abstract, but whether this vehicle is the right way to hold that cash-equivalent exposure at acceptable cost, size, and mandate stability. Three conditions govern that judgment:

  1. Annual fund charge stays below 0.10% for any two consecutive quarterly reviews. An exit is warranted if the charge rises above this level for two quarters in a row, because lower-cost alternatives exist — comparable ultra-short-duration US Treasury funds listed in Europe are available at similar or smaller charge levels. The exit condition requires two consecutive breaches rather than one to avoid acting on a temporary rounding or data error.

What Could Still Go Wrong

  • Yield compresses through the trim threshold. A further 23 basis points of compression — plausible in a single Federal Open Market Committee meeting — would move the basket yield to 3.5% or below and trigger the trim signal. The fund would continue to function; this is a portfolio-rebalancing outcome, not a vehicle failure.
  • Fund cost rises. If Vanguard raises the annual charge above 0.10% for two consecutive quarters, exit condition (1) fires. The current 0.07% figure is carried forward and unconfirmed this cycle; if the live Vanguard factsheet shows a higher number, the clock on the two-quarter condition would start.
  • Fund size falls. Prolonged net outflows could push assets below the $1 billion floor. At $2.395 billion the headroom is substantial, but the figure is not freshly confirmed this cycle.
  • US sovereign credit is downgraded. Two simultaneous downgrades below AA- by any combination of Fitch, Moody's, and S&P would trigger exit condition (3) and require reassessment of the cash-equivalent premise. This is the lowest-probability scenario in the near term, but the condition is monitored externally, not automatically.
  • Vehicle-integrity breach undetected. Because yfinance does not return expense-ratio or total-assets figures for this UCITS-listed fund, any adverse change would not surface automatically in the pipeline; it requires a quarterly manual check against the Vanguard product page. A missed quarterly check is the main operational risk here.

The Rules

The pre-committed rules that govern what happens next:

  • If all three vehicle-integrity conditions remain intact (charge within budget, fund size above floor, mandate unchanged): hold. The position continues to serve its function as a near-cash USD placeholder.
  • If any driver weakens but no exit condition has fired: do not add. Re-assess at the next quarterly review, monitoring the charge and fund size trends before increasing the allocation.
  • If one or more of the three numbered exit conditions fires (charge above 0.10% for two consecutive quarters, fund size below USD 1 billion, or US sovereign downgrade below AA- by any two of the three major agencies): exit the full position. Reinvest in EUR cash or short nominal German government bonds.
  • If the basket yield rises to the buy-band level (5.5%, corresponding to a share price at or below $40): add at the margin, if cash is available. T-bill rates at that level mark an unusually attractive yield relative to EUR alternatives. Vehicle-integrity checks still govern any larger addition.
  • If the basket yield compresses to the trim-band level (3.5%, corresponding to a share price at or above $63): trim at the margin. USD T-bill carry relative to EUR cash has weakened materially at that reading. Vehicle-integrity conditions still govern any full exit.
  • If the basket yield compresses to the exit level (3.0%, corresponding to a share price at or above $74): exit per the applicable exit condition.
