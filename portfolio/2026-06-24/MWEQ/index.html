About the Fund

World Equal Weight holds roughly 1,500 companies from the MSCI World universe — developed-market equities spanning North America, Europe, Japan, and a number of smaller markets — with each company allocated approximately the same share of the portfolio at each quarterly rebalance. The target weight is around 0.07% per holding. Between rebalances, holdings drift; the largest drifted position at the most recent snapshot is Dell Technologies at 0.27%, a negligible weight by any conventional measure.

The logic is a deliberate departure from cap-weighted alternatives. In a standard global index fund, the ten largest companies consume 25–30% of the portfolio. Equal weighting gives each of the roughly 1,500 businesses essentially the same share of capital, which tilts the basket toward smaller names, toward non-US markets, and toward a somewhat higher earnings yield than cap-weighted counterparts. The structural cost is roughly 30% annual turnover from the quarterly rebalance, which is why the expense ratio runs a little higher than plain cap-weighted alternatives.

Annual cost: 0.20% (Invesco factsheet; not reported by the data provider for the Xetra listing).

Tax and legal structure. Irish-domiciled UCITS accumulating share class: capital gains are retained within the fund rather than distributed, dividend income from underlying holdings is reinvested, and no K-1 form is issued.

The Case for the Bet

The bet is that equal-weight global developed-market exposure delivers a structurally higher earnings yield than cap-weighted alternatives — because it avoids concentrating capital in the most expensively valued mega-caps — and that this yield advantage, compounded through the quarterly rebalance, more than covers the higher turnover costs over the long run.

Six conditions must keep holding for the bet to work:

  1. Equity risk premium stays positive for two consecutive measurement periods. The basket's earnings yield must stay above the 10-year risk-free rate for at least two consecutive quarterly checks. A single negative reading is not an exit; two consecutive negatives mean the core risk-premium case has failed. Current reading: 0.31%.

  2. Expense ratio stays below 0.30% (early-warning flag at 0.27%). Equal-weight funds run higher rebalance costs than cap-weighted peers. Above 0.30%, switching to a cheaper cap-weighted alternative becomes warranted. Current reading: 0.20%.

  3. Assets under management stay above €500 million (early-warning flag below €800 million). The fund is just over two years old. UCITS products below this threshold face elevated closure risk; a sustained drawdown here would be a vehicle-survival flag, not a market signal. Current reading: approximately €1.48 billion.

  4. Top-1 holding weight stays below 1.0% (early-warning flag at 0.5%). Equal weighting pins every constituent near 0.07% at rebalance. Drift above 1.0% — roughly fourteen times the target weight — signals either a rebalance failure, a methodology change, or extreme intra-quarter price drift the fund has not corrected. Current reading: Dell at 0.27%.

  5. MSCI Equal Weighted index methodology remains materially unchanged. A shift away from the quarterly rebalance, a change of universe, or a move toward factor-tilted weighting would alter what this vehicle actually delivers. Monitored externally; no change has been signalled.

  6. Median look-through return on equity of the top-10 holdings stays above 12%. Set below the 15% confirmation threshold used for cap-weighted funds, because equal weighting includes smaller businesses with structurally lower returns on equity. Exit trigger at 12%. Current reading: 14.1%.

The Rules

If the basket's earnings yield falls below the risk-free rate for two consecutive quarters — or if any vehicle-integrity condition fires — close the position and move the proceeds to a cap-weighted global core fund or to risk-free assets.

If the equal-weight earnings yield premium over the cap-weighted alternative compresses below 0.5 percentage points for two consecutive quarters, do not add to the position. Re-validate after the next quarter; if the premium fails to recover, the diversification benefit no longer justifies the higher expense ratio.

If all conditions confirm or sit in the borderline zone and no vehicle breach has occurred, hold. The basket is reasonably priced, the fund is sound, and the earnings yield premium over cap-weighted alternatives is intact.

If the basket's earnings yield rises above 6.25% — share price around €4.4 — add to the position. This entry should be rare; when it arrives, it is decisive.

If the basket's earnings yield falls below 4.00% — share price around €6.9 — trim 25–33% of the position and re-deploy at the next rebalance opportunity.

What's Wobbling

Two conditions sit in the borderline zone; neither is close to an exit trigger.

The equity risk premium is thin. At 0.31%, the basket earns only 31 basis points over the risk-free rate — a fraction of the 2.0% confirmed premium the thesis requires. The trend is mildly downward over recent checks. This does not trigger exit condition (1), which requires two consecutive negative readings; the historical sequence has stayed positive. But it is the central tension: equal weighting does deliver a structurally higher earnings yield than cap-weighted alternatives, and at current rates that advantage is being largely consumed by an elevated risk-free benchmark. The EUR-based caveat noted in Gaps in the Picture softens this considerably.

Look-through quality is adequate but unconfirmed. The ten most heavily drifted holdings — dominated by semiconductor-adjacent names including Kioxia, Astera Labs, AMD, and Micron — have a median return on equity of 14.1%. Exit condition (6) at 12% is 2 percentage points away. The cohort's quality is pulled in opposite directions: Kioxia and Micron are peak-cycle memory producers whose returns on equity are likely to revert; Intel's negative figure drags the median down structurally.

Together, these two MIXED readings reflect the designed trade-off of equal weighting: a broader, more representative spread of business quality, at the cost of a thinner equity premium than a concentrated portfolio of quality compounders.

What Could Break the Fund

  • Assets under management shrink toward €500 million. A sustained drawdown below the UCITS survival floor would trigger an exit for vehicle-survival reasons, not because the thesis is wrong.
  • Invesco raises the expense ratio above 0.30%. Equal-weight strategies cost more to run than cap-weighted peers because of quarterly rebalance turnover. Above that ceiling, the cost disadvantage would outweigh the structural earnings-yield premium.
  • Top-1 concentration spikes above 1.0%. Under normal operation, no single holding drifts far above ~0.3% between rebalances. A spike above 1.0% would signal a rebalance failure, a methodology drift, or extreme intra-quarter price movement the fund has not corrected.
  • Two consecutive negative equity risk premium readings. If the basket's earnings yield falls below the risk-free rate for two consecutive quarterly checks, the core economic case for equity over bonds has failed.
  • MSCI restructures the index methodology. A shift to factor-tilted weighting, removal of the quarterly rebalance, or a change in the parent universe would be a de facto product change even if the fund name stayed the same.
  • Median look-through quality collapses below 12%. A sustained deterioration to the exit floor would remove the quality anchor underpinning the thesis.
