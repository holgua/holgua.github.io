PART I — INVESTMENT THESIS

1.1 One-Paragraph Thesis

Microsoft is a platform-layer monopolist whose primary moat is enterprise identity lock-in via Azure Active Directory / Entra ID — an all-or-nothing switching dynamic requiring simultaneous re-provisioning of every application, device, and conditional-access policy. This switching-cost moat is reinforced by 50,000+ patents, Azure Government FedRAMP authorisation, and 400+ datacenters across 70 regions. ROIC has held 21–27% over FY2022–FY2026, confirming durable excess returns 2–3× WACC. The central investment debate is whether the AI infrastructure CapEx surge ($115.9B in FY2026, 34.9% of revenue) permanently compresses FCF per share — FCF/NI compressed from 0.84 (FY2024) to 0.50 (FY2026) — or builds a wider structural moat as Azure monetises AI at scale. Azure grew 41% FY2026 (43% Q4), commercial RPO surged 84% to $678B, and AI business reached $37B ARR (+123% YoY per Q3 FY2026 8-K), confirming that AI revenue is present reality, not projection; resolution of the CapEx monetisation question is the primary catalyst.

PART III — COMPETITIVE POSITION (DISTILLED)

3.3 Moat — Type, Evidence, Trajectory

  • Wide switching-cost moat: Azure Entra ID all-or-nothing migration barrier; M365 Commercial seat growth +6% YoY on 400M+ seat base. (§3.3)
  • Intangibles / IP: 50,000+ patents; OpenAI perpetual cross-licence; FedRAMP High — structural AI IP not fully in patent count. (§3.3)
  • Narrow network effects: LinkedIn professional network; GitHub Copilot developer ecosystem; Azure AI Foundry developer lock-in. (§3.3)
  • Moat width: Wide. ROIC 21.2% FY2026 vs. ~8–10% WACC — excess returns sustained over 5 years. (§3.3)
  • Trajectory: Stable to Widening. Azure AI capabilities deepen enterprise lock-in; new switching-cost layer above identity. (§3.3)
