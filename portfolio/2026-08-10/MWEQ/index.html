About World Equal Weight

World Equal Weight is an exchange-traded fund listed on the Frankfurt stock exchange that tracks the MSCI World Equal Weighted index — a variant of the standard global developed-market index that gives every constituent roughly the same weight, rather than proportional weight to each company's market size. In practice, the index holds approximately 1,500 companies across developed markets in North America, Europe, and Asia Pacific, with each company pinned to roughly 0.07% of the fund on each quarterly rebalance date. Between rebalances, individual shares drift from that target as their prices move; the quarterly reset pulls them back. The deliberate effect is to reduce the dominance of the handful of very large technology companies that drive the standard cap-weighted global index — where the ten largest names can account for more than 20% of the total.

The fund is issued and managed by Invesco Investment Management Limited under an Irish-domiciled structure known as a UCITS exchange-traded fund — a common European fund format. It is an accumulating fund, meaning any income generated by the underlying companies is retained inside the fund rather than paid out to shareholders. Capital gains also remain within the fund. No K-1 tax form is issued. The annual cost as a percentage of assets held is 0.20% per the fund's own factsheet, though this figure cannot currently be verified through automatic data feeds for this particular fund listing. Neither 1-year tracking difference nor tracking error data is available in the current snapshot.

The Case for the Bet

The bet is structural: equal weighting should deliver a higher earnings yield than the cap-weighted alternative by diluting the most expensive mega-cap names. The basket's earnings yield must exceed the 10-year government bond yield by a margin large enough to justify the additional risk of owning equities. Six conditions define when that bet is working and when it has failed:

  1. Equity risk premium remains positive for consecutive quarters. The basket's earnings yield must exceed the 10-year US Treasury yield. If that gap turns negative for two consecutive quarters — meaning government bonds pay more than the basket earns — exit condition (1) fires, regardless of where the absolute yield sits.

Latest reading: +0.27% as of 2026-07-30. The premium is positive. Prior snapshot (2026-07-19) was +0.33%. Both readings are positive — the two-consecutive-quarters test has not been triggered. The premium is thin, however, and sits 173 basis points below the 2.00% level that would constitute a clear positive reading.

  1. Annual cost as a percentage of assets stays at or below 0.30%. Equal-weight funds incur higher running costs than cap-weighted alternatives because the quarterly rebalancing generates trading activity. The ceiling of 0.30% is the point at which the cost disadvantage outweighs the equal-weight diversification benefit.

Latest reading: 0.20% per Invesco factsheet as of 2026-07-30. That is 10 basis points below the ceiling. The automated data feed cannot verify this figure for this fund's listing; the exit condition has not been triggered, but the guardrail cannot be enforced automatically until the data gap is resolved.

  1. Total fund assets stay above USD 500 million. Funds below a certain size face a higher risk of being shut down by their manager, as they become uneconomical to operate. USD 500 million is the survival floor below which the fund's continued operation cannot be assumed.

Latest reading: USD 1.64 billion as of 2026-07-30, which is 3.28 times the floor. Average daily traded volume was USD 1.07 million. The fund is approximately 22 months old; asset growth should continue to be monitored quarterly.

  1. Top-1 holding weight stays below 1.00%. By construction, equal weighting targets roughly 0.07% per company at each rebalance. Any drift above 1.00% — approximately 14 times the target weight — signals either a methodology change, a rebalance failure, or price drift the fund's sampling approach has not corrected. This ceiling is deliberately tight because the equal-weight design is the core proposition.

Latest reading: The largest single holding (Astera Labs, a US semiconductor firm) stood at 0.15% as of 2026-07-30, which is 85 basis points below the 1.00% ceiling. Equal-weight integrity is confirmed.

  1. MSCI does not materially restructure the Equal Weighted index methodology. The bet depends on MSCI Inc. maintaining quarterly rebalancing, a broad universe, and equal weighting as the core construction rule. A shift to factor-tilted weighting, removal of the quarterly rebalance, or a narrowing of the underlying universe would alter the product materially.

Latest reading: No signal of methodology change as of 2026-07-30. Monitoring is manual — no automated data feed tracks MSCI index methodology announcements.

  1. Median return on equity of the top-10 visible holdings stays above 12%. The equal-weight methodology pulls in smaller, lower-quality names by design, so the quality bar is set lower than for a cap-weighted equivalent. Below 12%, even the drift-winner cohort at the top of the basket has lost sufficient quality to warrant a review.

Latest reading: 29.27% median return on equity (the fraction of shareholder capital turned into profit annually) across the nine top-10 holdings where data is available as of 2026-07-30, comfortably above the 12% exit threshold and the 15% level that would constitute a full green-light reading.

What Could Still Go Wrong

Four fund-break scenarios are the relevant risks for a broadly diversified equal-weight basket:

  • The annual cost rises above 0.30%. Equal-weight funds carry permanently higher running costs than cap-weighted alternatives because of the quarterly rebalancing activity. Invesco has shuttered sub-scale UCITS products before; if this fund's cost base were to rise — due to falling assets, increased rebalance costs, or deliberate repricing — the cost advantage over simply holding a cap-weighted equivalent disappears.
  • Total fund assets fall below USD 500 million. The fund is 22 months old. A sustained market drawdown, or a reversal of inflows, could reduce assets toward the survival floor. Below that level, the risk of fund closure rises materially. This is a vehicle risk, not a market-price signal — the underlying index would continue to exist, but the delivery mechanism would not.
  • MSCI restructures the Equal Weighted index methodology. The entire rationale for holding this fund rather than a standard cap-weighted alternative rests on the quarterly rebalance and broad-universe equal weighting. A change by MSCI — shifting to factor tilts, reducing the rebalance frequency, or narrowing the universe — would alter what the fund is delivering, not merely how it delivers it.
  • Single-name concentration drifts above 1.00%. The top-1 ceiling is set at 14 times the target weight precisely because any reading at that level signals the equal-weight construction has broken down. The current reading of 0.15% is well clear, but extreme intra-quarter price moves in any one constituent could close the gap before the next quarterly rebalance.

The Rules

The rules translate the six conditions and the share-price band position into a single pre-committed response. Six situations are defined:

  1. If all conditions are working and the share price is within the hold band, hold. The equal-weight basket is reasonably priced, the vehicle is sound, and the earnings yield premium over the cap-weighted alternative remains intact.
  2. If any condition weakens but no exit line is crossed, do not add. Re-examine after the next quarter. If the earnings yield premium of the equal-weight basket over the cap-weighted alternative compresses below 0.5 percentage points for two consecutive quarters, the diversification benefit no longer justifies the higher running cost compared with the cap-weighted alternative; in that case, consider consolidating into a cap-weighted global fund.
  3. If a vehicle-integrity condition breaches (total assets fall below the floor, the annual cost rises above the ceiling, or the top-1 concentration exceeds the ceiling) or the value test fails (equity risk premium negative for two consecutive quarters), exit. Move proceeds to a cap-weighted global fund or to a risk-free instrument.
  4. If the share price falls to or below €4.6 (basket earnings yield at or above 6.25%), add to the position. The equal-weight basket is meaningfully cheap versus the risk-free rate; this situation should be rare and is intended to prompt a decisive response.
  5. If the share price rises to or above €7.2 (basket earnings yield at or below 4.00%), trim 25–33%. The equal-weight basket is meaningfully expensive versus the risk-free rate; harvest the premium and redeploy at the next rebalancing opportunity.
  6. If the share price rises to or above €9.6 (basket earnings yield at or below 3.00%), exit. The basket is fully priced versus the risk-free rate; redeploy proceeds to a risk-free instrument or to another holding that clears the value threshold.

Scenario Definitions

A scenario is a joint assumption about two independent variables: operating trajectory (how the holding's underlying cash flows grow relative to their long-run trend) and exit multiple (what the market pays for those cash flows at the horizon, relative to a fair-value anchor).

Scenario Operating trajectory Exit multiple
Bear Below trend Below fair value
Base At trend At fair value
Bull Above trend At or above fair value

Non-negotiable rule: In the bear case, both dimensions must be adverse simultaneously — a scenario where only the multiple moves is a re-rating scenario, not a bear.

Bear cases are comparable in structure across all holding types (company, ETF, bond), but not in magnitude. A company bear reflects idiosyncratic execution failure; an ETF bear reflects a broad market drawdown. Different mitigations, different diversification implications.
