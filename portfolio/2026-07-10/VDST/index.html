About the Fund

The Vanguard U.S. Treasury 0-1 Year Bond UCITS ETF (Acc) — listed on the London Stock Exchange under the ticker VDST — holds a rolling basket of US Treasury bills maturing within one year. It is domiciled in Ireland (ISIN IE00BLRPPV00) and structured as an accumulating fund: no cash is paid out. Instead, coupon income is reinvested back into the net asset value, so the listed price drifts upward over time as interest accrues. As of 2026-07-10 shares traded at $59.21, against a net asset value of $59.18. The fund's average holding matures in roughly 4.5 months.

The fund is managed by Vanguard Group (Ireland) Limited, the Irish subsidiary of Vanguard — one of the world's largest fund managers, organised as a cooperative owned by the investors in its own funds rather than by outside shareholders. That structure removes the usual commercial pressure to raise fees or alter mandates for revenue reasons, which is one of the reasons the fund was chosen for this role.

The purpose of this holding in the portfolio is not to generate high returns. It is to park USD cash in a near-risk-free instrument while keeping the option to redeploy that cash into equities when a better opportunity appears. The relevant comparison is not the stock market — it is what EUR cash earns at European short-term rates. Annual cost: 0.07% (known Vanguard-published charge; the automated data feed did not return a figure this cycle).

The Case for the Bet

The bet is that a basket of US Treasury bills maturing within one year is the most disciplined way to hold USD cash in a EUR-denominated portfolio — near-zero credit risk, instant liquidity, and a cost of 0.07% per year. Three conditions must hold for the fund to remain the right vehicle:

  1. Ongoing charges stay below 0.10% for two consecutive quarters. The fee advantage over comparable ultra-short-duration US Treasury funds is the chief reason to use this vehicle rather than a bank deposit or a competing fund. If costs rise above the ceiling for two consecutive quarterly reviews, cheaper alternatives (other short-duration Treasury funds available in Europe) should be sought.

What Could Still Go Wrong

  • Yield compression past the trim edge. If the Federal Reserve cuts rates aggressively and the basket yield falls below 3.5%, the position reaches the trim signal. If it falls below 3.0%, the exit signal fires. This is the central downside scenario the thesis was designed around.
  • Cost creep. If Vanguard raises the fund's charges above 0.10% for two consecutive quarters, the cost advantage that justifies using this vehicle over a bank deposit disappears. With the current fee at 0.07%, there is 3 basis points of headroom.
  • Fund size erosion. If outflows reduce the fund below USD 1 billion, the fund's liquidity profile and its ability to track the T-bill index efficiently could be impaired. At USD 2.4 billion (last known), the margin is substantial, but this figure requires manual quarterly verification.
  • Mandate drift. A change in the underlying index — for example, a widening of the maturity band beyond one year, or a switch to a different index provider — would alter the fund's duration profile without notice. UCITS regulations require disclosure via the Key Investor Information Document; Vanguard regulatory filings and KIID updates must be monitored externally.
  • US sovereign credit downgrade. A downgrade below AA− by two of three agencies (Fitch, Moody's, S&P) would call into question the credit-equivalent premise of the holding. Current ratings are above the trigger. (For this fund, exit fires when the share price reaches the exit edge, when a vehicle-integrity condition breaches, or when the named exit-condition trigger fires — any of three paths.)

The Rules

If the thesis is weakened (a condition is deteriorating but no exit threshold has been crossed), do not add. Re-assess at the next quarterly review. Monitor cost and fund size trends before increasing the allocation.

If the thesis is broken (any exit condition fires — cost above 0.10% for two quarters, fund size below USD 1 billion, or sovereign credit downgraded below AA− by two agencies), exit. Reinvest the proceeds in EUR cash or short nominal German government bonds.
