About the Fund

The fund holds a rolling basket of US Treasury bills maturing in zero to one year — the shortest-duration segment of the US government debt market. When a bill reaches maturity, the principal is reinvested into the next tranche of short-term paper. Income is accumulated internally rather than paid out as a distribution, so the fund's net asset value drifts upward slowly as interest accrues. The fund is structured as an exchange-traded fund domiciled in Ireland (UCITS), listed on the London Stock Exchange in US dollars.

Because all holdings are US sovereign obligations, there is no diversification across credit counterparties in the conventional sense — concentration risk does not apply. The relevant credit consideration is US government creditworthiness itself. The fund's weighted-average maturity as of the last snapshot was 0.375 years, approximately four and a half months, which implies near-zero sensitivity to changes in longer-term interest rates. Annual cost: 0.07% (Ongoing Charges Figure per Vanguard published data; the market-data feed does not return this figure for UCITS-listed funds).

Tax profile. The fund is registered as a reporting fund for Austrian tax purposes (OeKB Meldefonds). As an accumulating share class, income is taxed annually based on a deemed distribution figure, not on actual cash received; no US partnership K-1 is issued.

What Could Still Go Wrong

  • Yield compression through the trim band and beyond. If the Federal Reserve cuts rates aggressively — the bear-case scenario — the basket yield could compress through 3.5% and eventually through 3.0%, activating first the trim order and then the exit order in succession. The fund would still return the prevailing rate; the case for holding it weakens relative to alternatives.
  • Fund fee increase above threshold. If Vanguard raises the Ongoing Charges Figure to 0.10% or above for two consecutive quarterly reviews, the cost advantage over comparable ultra-short-duration US Treasury funds disappears and exit condition (1) fires regardless of yield level.
  • Asset base decline below the liquidity floor. If total fund assets fall below USD 1 billion — whether through market outflows, NAV decline, or fund restructuring — exit condition (2) fires. Daily liquidity could be impaired at smaller asset levels.
  • US sovereign credit downgrade. If two of the three major agencies downgrade US sovereign credit below AA−, exit condition (3) fires and the cash-equivalent premise requires reassessment from first principles.

(For this fund, exit fires when the share price reaches the exit edge, when a vehicle-integrity condition breaches, or when the named exit-condition trigger fires — any of three paths.)
