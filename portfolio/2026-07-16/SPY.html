About SP500

The fund tracks the S&P 500 index, a cap-weighted basket of 500 large US-listed companies chosen by S&P Dow Jones Indices based on market size, liquidity, and earnings history. Cap-weighting means each company's share of the fund mirrors its share of the total market value of all 500 members; NVIDIA and Apple, the two largest, together account for roughly 14% of the basket. Diversification is structural rather than deliberate — the basket holds everything in proportion to what the market has priced it at, from technology (38.6% of the total) down through financial services, communications, healthcare, and industrials. The fund was launched in January 1993 as the first US-listed exchange-traded fund and has run without interruption since.

Annual cost: 0.0945% (total expense ratio). Tracking difference and tracking error figures are not available from this snapshot; pull from the State Street SPDR factsheet when needed. The fund is structured as a Unit Investment Trust — an older legal wrapper that pre-dates the open-end ETF format used by competitors such as iShares and Vanguard. The UIT structure means dividends accumulate in the fund and are paid out quarterly rather than being reinvested continuously. No K-1 tax form is issued.

The Case for the Bet

The bet is that the S&P 500 basket will, over the holding period, yield more in earnings than can be earned risk-free — and that the vehicle carrying that exposure remains sound:

  1. Equity risk premium above zero. The basket's earnings yield (total earnings divided by total market value) must exceed the ten-year Treasury yield by a meaningful margin. The thesis defines "meaningful" as at least two percentage points. A negative spread means equity holders are being paid less than government lenders for bearing additional risk — at which point the thesis fails.

Latest reading: 2026-07-15. Earnings yield 3.69%, Treasury 4.545%, spread −0.86 percentage points. The spread has been negative since 2026-06-16 — twelve daily readings. Exit condition (1) requires the negative spread to persist for two consecutive quarters; that bar has not yet been crossed.

  1. Look-through quality above 15% median return on equity. The basket's businesses must collectively earn a decent return on the capital invested in them. The measure used is the median return on equity among the deduped top-ten constituents — the return is median (not average) to avoid distortion from companies whose book equity has been reduced to near-zero by buybacks.

Latest reading: 2026-07-15. Median return on equity of the deduped top nine: 37.28%, anchored by Alphabet, Broadcom, Microsoft, and Meta. The weighted average is 70.38%, heavily influenced by NVIDIA (114%) and Apple (141%) whose book equity has been compressed by aggressive capital returns — the median is the load-bearing figure. Comfortably above the 15% exit floor; condition (6) not triggered.

  1. Annual cost below 0.15%. The total expense ratio must stay below 0.15%. A rise above that level with no fee response from a competing fund would indicate that State Street has repriced the product in a way that erodes the value proposition.

What's Wobbling

The equity risk premium is the primary wobble. For 29 days, the basket has yielded less in earnings than the ten-year Treasury — a spread of −0.86 percentage points on the most recent reading. The thesis requires that this premium be positive and at least two percentage points wide; both conditions are failing. The wobble does not yet cross into an exit condition because exit condition (1) requires two consecutive quarters of negative readings, not two snapshots — the quarterly bar has not been crossed.

The second wobble is the position in the valuation band. At 3.69%, the earnings yield sits below the 4.00% trim-band edge that was set precisely to flag expensive territory. The trim-band breach and the negative equity risk premium are correlated — both reflect the same underlying fact that the basket is priced at 27 times earnings when the risk-free rate is 4.5% — but they are tracked separately because they operate on different persistence rules. The trim-band breach acts immediately; the equity risk premium breach requires quarters to confirm.

Neither wobble crosses a line from the Case for the Bet today. The vehicle-integrity conditions (cost, fund size, concentration) are all clean. What is active is the valuation signal: the basket is priced in a way that currently fails the minimum equity risk premium test, and another two quarters of the same will require action.

  • A material restructuring of the S&P 500 index methodology by S&P Dow Jones Indices — for instance, a change to sector classification rules or constituent-selection criteria — that undermines the broad-market mandate and fires exit condition (5).

The Rules

If the basket is reasonably priced and the vehicle is sound, hold.

If the thesis is weakened — the equity risk premium is negative or look-through quality is declining — do not add. Re-validate after the next quarter. If the negative equity risk premium persists or the median look-through return on equity keeps falling, the rules call for swapping to a comparable fund or moving to risk-free assets.

If the thesis breaks — any vehicle-integrity condition breaches or exit condition (1) is formally triggered after two consecutive quarters — exit. Move proceeds to risk-free assets or to a holding that clears the earnings-yield test.

If the basket falls to the cheap edge (share price at or below $445), add to the position. The basket is meaningfully cheap relative to the risk-free rate at that level.

If the basket rises to the trim edge (share price at or above $695), trim 25% to 33% of the position. The basket is meaningfully expensive relative to the risk-free rate; harvest the premium.

If the basket reaches the exit band (share price at or above $927), exit. At that level, the basket earnings yield is at or below 3.00% — fully priced against the risk-free rate. Redeploy proceeds to risk-free assets or to a holding that meets the earnings-yield test.

Scenario Definitions

A scenario is a joint assumption about two independent variables: operating trajectory (how the holding's underlying cash flows grow relative to their long-run trend) and exit multiple (what the market pays for those cash flows at the horizon, relative to a fair-value anchor).

Scenario Operating trajectory Exit multiple
Bear Below trend Below fair value
Base At trend At fair value
Bull Above trend At or above fair value

Non-negotiable rule: In the bear case, both dimensions must be adverse simultaneously — a scenario where only the multiple moves is a re-rating scenario, not a bear.

Bear cases are comparable in structure across all holding types (company, ETF, bond), but not in magnitude. A company bear reflects idiosyncratic execution failure; an ETF bear reflects a broad market drawdown. Different mitigations, different diversification implications.
