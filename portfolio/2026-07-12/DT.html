About Dynatrace

Dynatrace makes software that watches software. Large enterprises running applications across many cloud environments simultaneously — Amazon Web Services, Microsoft Azure, Google Cloud, private data centres — need a single system that detects problems automatically, traces them to their root cause, and increasingly handles routine remediation without human intervention. Dynatrace provides that, in real time, across the full stack: applications, infrastructure, databases, user sessions, and the AI models companies are now layering on top.

The core product is OneAgent, a sensor that instruments an enterprise's entire environment automatically on installation. Removing it and switching to a competitor means re-instrumenting everything from scratch — a multi-year project that makes the switch economically painful even when an alternative looks cheaper on a per-licence basis. Ninety per cent of Dynatrace's revenue is annual subscriptions; 70% of that base has adopted the Dynatrace Platform Subscription model, where customers commit a minimum annual spend and draw it down across whichever modules they use. This drives natural expansion without new procurement cycles and is the primary driver of the 110% net revenue retention rate — the same cohort of customers spends 10% more each year.

The Case for the Bet

Dynatrace is a long-duration bet on two propositions: that the complexity of running large enterprise technology stacks continues to grow faster than engineering headcount, and that Dynatrace's combination of switching costs and proprietary data-processing technology keeps it as the preferred provider as that complexity scales.

For the bet to keep working, the following conditions must hold:

  1. Annual recurring revenue growth stays above 13% on a constant-currency basis. Exit fires if growth falls below 13% for two consecutive quarters.

Latest reading: Annual recurring revenue grew 18% in the year to 2026-03-31, reversing a mild deceleration from 15% the year before. The annual report does not separately disclose a constant-currency figure, but the trajectory and net revenue retention context both point well above the 13% floor.

  1. Net revenue retention stays at or above 107%. This measures whether existing customers are spending more or less on Dynatrace each year. One quarter below 107% triggers a watch; two consecutive quarters triggers exit.

Latest reading: Net revenue retention was 110% for FY2026 (year ended 2026-03-31), matching each of the prior two years. Three consecutive years at 110%; the 107% threshold has not been approached.

  1. Free cash flow margin stays above 20% in any full fiscal year. Below 20%, the business is no longer converting revenue to cash at the rate the thesis requires.

Latest reading: Free cash flow margin was 26.2% in FY2026 — $530 million on $2,018 million of revenue, up from 25.3% the year before. The 20% floor is not in question.

  1. The chief executive and chief financial officer do not execute open-market sales of $5 million or more in any 90-day window. Large discretionary sales signal that those closest to the business do not share the long-run thesis.

Latest reading: All 62 Form 4 dispositions in the past twelve months are compulsory tax-withholding events on vesting shares. Zero open-market sales as of 2026-05-20.

  1. The Dynatrace Platform Subscription penetration rate does not stall below 72% of annual recurring revenue for three consecutive quarters. The platform subscription is the mechanism for natural customer expansion; a stall signals the flywheel is losing momentum.

Latest reading: The annual report does not disclose the DPS percentage numerically. The last disclosed figure — 70% — comes from quarterly earnings commentary. Exit condition five cannot be evaluated from the annual filing alone; quarterly earnings calls provide the tracking metric.

  1. Cisco or Palo Alto do not publicly announce named Dynatrace enterprise customer wins confirming active displacement. The trigger is evidence of displacement, not the acquisition of a competing product.

Latest reading: The annual report confirms Palo Alto Networks acquired Chronosphere in 2026 — the preparatory signal. No named Dynatrace customer displacement has been publicly disclosed as of 2026-05-20. Net revenue retention at 110% is the primary counter-signal. Generic large-language-model tools and "vibe coding" are flagged for the first time in management's own risk disclosures.

  1. Net cash stays above $600 million without a disclosed acquisition rationale. Below that level, the flexibility that funds buybacks and cushions a slowdown is materially reduced.

Latest reading: Net cash at 2026-03-31 was $823 million — $274 million of long-term debt against approximately $1.1 billion in cash and short-term investments.

The Share Price

The valuation framework used here treats the share price as a function of the cash the business generates relative to its market value — a yield measure (cash generated divided by the price of the entire company). At today's price of $45, that yield works out to approximately 4.0%. The fair-value anchor is 4.4%, which corresponds to a share price of about $41. The share currently trades at a 9% premium to that anchor, placing it modestly above fair value.

The buy edge — where the framework calls for adding — sits at $31, where the yield would reach 5.9%. The trim edge sits at $55, where the yield would fall to 3.3%. Both edges are informed by the underlying business quality and the required rate of return, not by short-term price movements or analyst consensus.

At $45, the share sits in the middle of the hold band — not cheap enough to trigger the buy rule, not expensive enough to trigger the trim.

What's Wobbling

The Chronosphere acquisition by Palo Alto Networks is not an exit trigger, but it shortens the pathway to one — the point at which a named Dynatrace customer is claimed publicly. That has not happened; net revenue retention at 110% argues it has not happened at scale. But the distance from "preparatory signal" to "exit condition fired" is shorter than a year ago.

Subscription gross margin compressed one percentage point — 86% to 85% — on higher cloud hosting costs. Overall gross margin expanded from 81% to 82%, which points the right direction. Worth tracking, but not a wobble that changes the verdict.

The DPS penetration metric was not disclosed in the annual report. The 70% figure comes from quarterly earnings commentary. One quarter of silence does not trigger exit condition five; that requires three consecutive quarters stalled below 72%. The quarterly earnings call is the tracking venue.

What Could Still Go Wrong

  • Net revenue retention falls below 107%. The bear case assigns 25% likelihood to retention compressing to 104% as Datadog and open-source tools erode pricing. Two consecutive quarters below 107% fires exit condition one.
  • Palo Alto or Cisco announces a named enterprise displacement. If Chronosphere or the Cisco/Splunk platform wins a named Dynatrace account publicly, exit condition six fires immediately.
  • Large-language-model tools commoditise the category. Management flags "vibe coding" and generic AI agents as substitutes for the first time; the speed of AI adoption could bring this within the three-year window.
  • Free cash flow margin slips toward 20%. The subscription gross margin compression in FY2026 is a leading indicator of what happens if hosting costs grow faster than revenue.
  • The buyback is deployed at elevated prices. $849 million remains available; buying at $45 rather than below $31 deploys capital at a smaller margin of safety than the thesis implies.

The Rules

If the thesis is confirmed — all drivers reading green or mixed, no exit condition crossed — maintain the full position. Continue monitoring net revenue retention, annual recurring revenue growth, free cash flow margin, and DPS penetration each quarter. Adding modestly below $38 is within the rules while retention holds above 107%.

If the thesis is weakened — one or more drivers showing early deterioration, no exit condition yet confirmed — reduce to half position within 30 days of the trigger. Monitor for two additional quarters before deciding between full exit and rebuilding.

If the thesis is broken — an exit condition is confirmed — exit fully within one quarter. Do not average down. Broken conditions include: net revenue retention below 107% for two consecutive quarters; constant-currency annual recurring revenue growth below 13% for two consecutive quarters; free cash flow margin below 20% for a full fiscal year; chief executive or chief financial officer open-market sales of $5 million or more in any 90-day window; documented Dynatrace enterprise customer displacement by Cisco or Palo Alto.

If the price falls below the buy edge — the free cash flow yield exceeds 5.9%, implying a price at or below approximately $31 — accumulate toward full position size in tranches. The company's own buyback at $38 in FY2026 sets a practical reference; below $35 the yield exceeds 6.2%.

If the price rises above the trim edge — the free cash flow yield falls below 3.3%, implying a price at or above approximately $55 — trim toward a half-to-two-thirds position to harvest the premium. Do not exit fully unless the thesis is simultaneously breaking.
