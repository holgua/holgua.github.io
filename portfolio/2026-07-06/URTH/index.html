About the Fund

The iShares MSCI World fund tracks the MSCI World Index — a float-adjusted, market-capitalisation-weighted index covering large- and mid-sized companies across 23 developed markets. The basket holds roughly 1,300 companies, split approximately 70% United States and 30% the rest of the developed world: Japan, the United Kingdom, France, Switzerland, Germany, Canada, and seventeen others. Emerging markets such as India, Brazil, or China are not included — this is a developed-world-only mandate, which is what the MSCI World label means.

The fund is issued by iShares, the exchange-traded-fund arm of BlackRock, the world's largest asset manager. It is structured as a Delaware statutory trust and does not issue K-1 tax forms. The annual charge is 0.24%, which puts it at the higher end of iShares' own global-equity lineup — the functionally similar IWDA product listed in London charges 0.20%. One-year tracking difference and tracking error are not available in this snapshot. The proportion of dividends qualifying for preferential US tax treatment is unconfirmed; capital-gain distributions are likely zero or minimal given that exchange-traded funds in this structure routinely use in-kind redemptions to avoid realising gains.

The Case for the Bet

The bet is that over the full holding period, the basket's earnings yield — the share of the basket's collective profits per share, as a proportion of the price paid — will exceed the risk-free rate on ten-year US government bonds plus a margin of at least two percentage points. That margin is the equity risk premium: the extra return required for bearing equity risk rather than holding government debt.

The bet rests on the following conditions:

  1. The basket's earnings yield exceeds the risk-free rate by at least two percentage points — the equity risk premium is not negative for two consecutive quarters. If it is, the value argument has failed.

Latest reading: The basket earnings yield stands at 4.152%, against the ten-year Treasury yield of 4.485%. The equity risk premium is therefore −0.333 percentage points — negative, meaning Treasuries outyield the basket. This has been negative across all eight recorded snapshots from 2026-06-16 through 2026-07-06. Exit condition (1) requires two consecutive quarterly negative readings; these are daily and weekly snapshots. The next quarterly gate closes at the end of the third calendar quarter of 2026.

  1. The annual charge stays below 0.35%, with no change in the fee without a corresponding reduction offered by the functionally identical UCITS alternative (0.20%).

Latest reading: Annual charge 0.24%, confirmed. Below the 0.30% early-warning level and the 0.35% hard ceiling. Exit condition (2) is inactive.

  1. Assets under management remain above $2.0 billion. Below that level, bid-ask spreads on a global-blend fund typically widen materially, making trading costly.

Latest reading: Fund size $8.1 billion — 4.1 times the floor. The early-warning threshold of $4.0 billion is also comfortably cleared. Exit condition (3) is inactive.

  1. The largest single holding stays below 10% of the basket — a threshold that would signal one company has become dominant enough to alter the character of the diversification.

Latest reading: Nvidia at 5.42%, below the 8.0% early-warning band and the 10.0% ceiling. Exit condition (4) is inactive.

  1. The MSCI World index methodology is not materially restructured — no country reclassification, removal of major developed-market constituents, or shift away from float-adjusted market-capitalisation weighting.

Latest reading: No automated signal available in this pipeline. The fund family and legal type are unchanged (iShares, Exchange Traded Fund). No reported methodology change.

  1. The median return-on-equity of the nine largest holdings stays above 15% — meaning the basket still holds businesses earning meaningfully above their cost of capital.

Latest reading: Median return-on-equity 37.28%, well above the 15% exit floor. The weighted-average figure (69.42%) is inflated by Nvidia and Apple, whose aggressive buyback programmes cause the accounting measure to exceed 100%; the median is the more conservative read. Exit condition (6) is inactive.

The Share Price

The basket earnings yield of 4.152% sits above the trim threshold of 4.0% — so no automatic trim signal has fired — but below the fair-value yield of 5.0%. Put plainly, the basket is trading in the expensive half of the hold zone, at a price that would need to fall roughly 17% before it reaches what the framework considers a neutral valuation. At $280, the basket would be so expensive (earnings yield down to 3.0%) that the rules call for a full exit.

Today's price of $203 is close to the trim line of $210 — just $7 short of the automatic trim trigger. That proximity is what the trim-edge description in the Bottom Line captures in shorthand.

Standing orders (actionable).

  • A standing buy order at $135. The rules call for adding once the share price closes at or below this line and the conditions are still intact. The basket would be priced at a genuine discount to the risk-free alternative.
  • A standing trim order at $210. The rules call for trimming a quarter to a third of the position when the share price closes at or above this line, to harvest the premium while maintaining the core holding.
  • A standing exit order at $280. The rules call for closing the full position when the share price closes at or above this line. At that price the basket earnings yield would reach 3.0% — fully priced relative to government bonds regardless of the quality of the underlying businesses.

Vehicle-integrity breaches and the named exit-condition trigger — two consecutive quarterly negative-equity-premium readings — are independent additional paths to exit, separate from the price-level orders above.

What's Wobbling

The one genuinely wobbling condition is the equity risk premium. The basket's earnings yield has been below the US ten-year Treasury rate continuously since the analysis began tracking it in mid-June 2026. At −0.333 percentage points on 2026-07-06, the premium is not only negative but has stayed negative across all eight available readings without a single positive print.

That much is settled: on a yield basis, Treasuries are currently the better deal. What is not yet settled is whether this constitutes a structural failure of the value case or a temporary phenomenon. The written condition requires negativity across two consecutive quarterly snapshots — a higher bar than a run of daily or weekly readings, however long. The first full quarterly test closes at the end of the third quarter of 2026. If the equity risk premium is still negative then, exit condition (1) fires and the rules call for exiting the position.

Nothing else is wobbling materially. The fund is operationally sound, the largest holding is nowhere near the concentration ceiling, and the basket's top companies are still earning well above their cost of capital.

What Could Still Go Wrong

  • Multiple contraction. The basket currently trades at a trailing price-to-earnings ratio of 24.1 — well above the long-run developed-market average of around 16. If markets normalise to historical averages over five years, the annual return in the bear case works out to roughly −5.9%, even with 3% annual earnings growth. Starting valuation is the dominant risk.
  • Persistent negative equity premium. If the ten-year Treasury rate stays above the basket earnings yield for two full consecutive quarters, exit condition (1) fires. The streak stands at eight weeks and counting.
  • Fee drift. A rise in the annual charge above 0.35% with no response from the cheaper UCITS alternative would trigger exit condition (2). URTH already runs four basis points above the functionally equivalent IWDA product.
  • Single-name concentration. Nvidia's weight stands at 5.42%, below the 10% ceiling, but cap-weighted indices drift upward with their winners. Continued outperformance could push Nvidia past the 8.0% early-warning level and eventually the 10.0% hard limit.
  • Index methodology change. MSCI periodically reviews country classifications and index construction rules. A material change — reclassifying a major market or shifting away from float-adjusted weighting — would undermine the mandate. No automated monitoring is in place for this condition.

The Rules

  • If the basket's conditions are all working and the price is within the hold band: hold — basket is reasonably priced and the vehicle is sound.
  • If any condition is wobbling but no exit trigger has fired: do not add. Re-validate after the next quarter. If the negative equity premium persists or the median quality reading keeps falling, switch to the cheaper UCITS alternative or move to cash per the position-sizing rule.
  • If a vehicle-integrity breach fires or an exit condition is met: exit. Move proceeds to the risk-free instrument or to a holding that meets the value test.
  • If the share price falls to or below the buy edge at $135: add to the position. The basket is meaningfully cheap relative to the risk-free alternative.
  • If the share price rises to or above the trim edge at $210: trim a quarter to a third of the position. The basket is meaningfully expensive; harvest the premium.
  • If the share price rises to or above the exit edge at $280: exit. The basket earnings yield at or below 3.0% — fully priced relative to the risk-free alternative. Redeploy proceeds to the risk-free instrument or to a holding that meets the value test.
