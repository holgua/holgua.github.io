5. Administration Connectivity & Current Political Climate

US Administration:

Ferrari's most material single political exposure is US trade policy. The tariff timeline is:

  • April 2025: US imposes 25% Section 232 tariff on automotive imports (including EU-origin vehicles). Ferrari responds with up to 10% price surcharges on certain models (Purosangue, 12Cilindri, etc.); legacy orders on 296, SF90, Roma families frozen at prior commercial terms. Ferrari guidance: potential 50 basis point risk to EBITDA/EBIT margins. (Source: CNBC, March 2025; ainvest.com; Confirmed)
  • May 2026: US threatens further tariff hike to 25% on EU autos, citing EU non-compliance with prior trade agreement terms. (Source: Al Jazeera, May 2026; Forbes.com, May 2026; Confirmed)
  • July 1, 2026 (effective): EU-US trade deal takes effect. Ferrari vehicles manufactured in Italy now enter the US at a 15% all-inclusive tariff ceiling with no stacking of Section 232 + reciprocal levies. This resolves the 25% threat scenario for the current period. (Source: tariffstool.com; politics-government.news-articles.net; Confirmed)

Outcome: Ferrari's current US tariff exposure is 15% (as of July 1, 2026) — below the 25% threshold identified in config.yaml Exit Condition (5) as the critical pass-through test. The 2025 operating data confirmed that Ferrari maintained its 2025 EBITDA guidance at ≥38.3% (actual: 38.8%) despite the tariff uncertainty — demonstrating partial pricing power pass-through at the 10% surcharge level. The 50 bps margin risk was not fully materialised in FY2025 results. Exit Condition (5) — which specifies "US tariff 25% AND Ferrari guidance citing EBITDA margin below 38%" — was not triggered and, at 15%, is not currently in the trigger zone. (Source: Ferrari FY2025 results press release; ainvest.com; Confirmed)

EU Administration:

Ferrari's relationship with EU regulatory policy is shaped primarily by:
- EU CO2 fleet targets: Low-volume manufacturer derogation exempts Ferrari from intermediate 2030 targets; 0g CO2/km applies from 2036 (one year grace vs. large manufacturers' 2035). The 2023 e-fuels carve-out (secured via ACEA lobbying) preserves a path for ICE/hybrid models post-2035 if run on certified synthetic fuels. (Source: Forbes.com CO2 exemption; TopGear.com; Confirmed)
- EU EV policy (AFIR mandate, 2035 BEV transition): Ferrari's Elettrica launch aligns with the EU mandate direction; the BEV transition is a regulatory tailwind for the Elettrica product strategy, not a headwind. However, Ferrari scaled back its 2030 electrification target: in 2022 it guided 40% of lineup as BEV; the October 2025 Capital Markets Day revised this to 20% BEV + 40% hybrid + 40% ICE (including e-fuel compatible). (Source: ainvest.com Elettrica 2025; barchart.com; Confirmed)
- Italian government co-investment in Maranello electrification: Aligns Italian government's industrial policy (preserving Emilia-Romagna automotive employment) with Ferrari's capex programme. This is a structural positive that is likely to continue regardless of Italian coalition government composition. (Source: investing.com Reuters; Confirmed)

6. Trade Policy & Tariff Exposure Update

Current US tariff position:

Ferrari vehicles imported from Maranello (Italy) to the US are subject to a 15% all-inclusive tariff ceiling under the EU-US trade agreement effective July 1, 2026. This represents:
- An improvement from the April 2025 25% Section 232 threat scenario
- A manageable pass-through level: Ferrari raised prices up to 10% on certain models in 2025 and maintained ≥38.3% EBITDA guidance, confirming that pricing power is sufficient at sub-15% tariff levels
- A remaining risk: the 15% tariff adds approximately EUR 50,000–75,000 to the theoretical import cost on a EUR 350,000–500,000 Ferrari — a surcharge that is manageable for Ferrari's UHNWI clientele at the current all-in prices

EU tariff on Chinese EVs:

The EU imposed tariffs on Chinese-manufactured EVs in 2024–2025 (ranging 18–35% additional duties on BYD, Geely, SAIC). This is not a direct Ferrari exposure (Ferrari does not manufacture in China and does not compete in the Chinese mass EV market) but affects Ferrari's competitive context in China — a key luxury automotive market. Ferrari's ultra-luxury segment is largely insulated from Chinese EV competition. (Source: EU regulation, Likely)

Key tariff risk remaining:

The July 1, 2026 EU-US deal is a deal, not permanent legislation — it could be renegotiated or revoked by future US administrations. The Forbes May 2026 article titled "Trump Backs Off On E.U. Auto Tariffs But Risks Remain" underscores that the tariff risk is not permanently closed, even at 15%. (Source: Forbes.com May 2026; Confirmed)

Narrative Assessment:

Ferrari's political exposure is low and manageable — the business model's extraordinary pricing power means that the primary political risk (US import tariffs) is tested and partially resolved. The April 2025 tariff shock (25% Section 232 rate) triggered Ferrari's pre-stated surcharge mechanism (up to 10% price increases on selected models), and FY2025 EBITDA margins of 38.8% confirmed that the pass-through held: the 50 bps margin risk flagged in guidance was contained below its feared level. The subsequent July 2026 EU-US trade agreement at 15% removes the immediate threat, though the instability of US trade policy means this is a continuously monitored risk rather than a closed issue. On the European regulatory side, Ferrari's low-volume manufacturer status (≤14,000 units pa) provides a structural buffer against mandatory BEV-only mandates until 2036 — one year of additional runway vs. mass-market peers — and the Italian government MOU co-investing over €106M in the Maranello e-building aligns government interests with the Elettrica launch. The Agnelli/Exor controlling shareholder structure means that Ferrari's management has no incentive to court political relationships that might pressure volume expansion (the scarcity model's primary moat-defender); political engagement is therefore minimal, focused on trade policy and regulatory compliance, and unlikely to introduce political tail risks from the supply side. For the investor, the political picture is genuinely immaterial to the investment thesis except for the US tariff tail risk, which is a known and monitored scenario with a pre-defined exit condition.
