About the Fund

World Equal Weight tracks the MSCI World Equal Weighted Index: roughly 1,500 companies drawn
from developed markets worldwide, each receiving the same weight — about 0.07% — on every
quarterly rebalance date. The index covers the same universe as the MSCI World Index, but
instead of weighting companies by their total market value (which concentrates position in
the largest names), it pins every holding near equal footing. The practical effect is a basket
that is less US-heavy than cap-weighted MSCI World (where the United States represents roughly
70% of the weight), less tilted toward mega-cap technology, and more evenly spread across
sectors — Industrials at 18%, Financial Services at 17%, and Technology at 16% are the three
largest groupings, each modest by comparison with the 25–30% technology concentration typical
of cap-weighted developed-market indices.

The fund is managed by Invesco and is domiciled in Ireland as a UCITS-regulated, accumulating
share class — meaning dividends and income are reinvested automatically rather than paid out.
Capital gains are not distributed. No K-1 form is issued (not applicable under UCITS rules).
Annual cost is 0.20%, sourced from the Invesco factsheet; securities-lending income is not
disclosed, so a net-cost figure cannot be computed.

The Case for the Bet

The core bet is that spreading weight equally across developed-market companies — rather than
concentrating it in the largest — produces a structural earnings-yield advantage over
cap-weighted indices, compensating for the higher annual turnover and slightly lower average
business quality that come with the broader exposure.

For the bet to work, six conditions must hold:

  1. Equity risk premium positive on a two-quarter rolling basis — basket earnings yield
    above the 10-year risk-free rate for at least one of the last two snapshots. Two consecutive
    negative readings mean the risk-premium case for equities over bonds has failed.
    (Early-warning: any single negative reading.)

  2. Annual expense ratio below 0.30% (current 0.20%; early-warning above 0.27%). Equal-weight
    funds carry higher turnover costs than cap-weighted peers; above 0.30% erodes the structural
    yield advantage.

  3. Assets under management above €500 million (current ~€1.48 billion; early-warning below
    €800 million). UCITS funds below the €300–500 million range face elevated closure risk. A
    drawdown to this floor is a vehicle-survival signal, not a market signal.

  4. Top-1 holding concentration below 1.0% (current: Dell Technologies 0.27%;
    early-warning above 0.50%). Any drift above 1.0% — roughly 14× the rebalance target
    weight — signals a methodology failure or extreme price drift not yet corrected.

  5. MSCI Equal Weighted methodology unchanged — no shift to factor-tilted weighting,
    no removal of the quarterly rebalance, no change of the parent universe.

  6. Median return-on-equity of the visible top-10 above 12% (current 14.1%; early-warning
    below 15%; exit below 12%). Set lower than the 15% used for cap-weighted funds because
    equal weighting structurally includes smaller, lower-profitability businesses.

The Rules

Five pre-committed rules determine what follows from each combination of conditions and price:

  1. If all conditions hold and the vehicle is sound — hold. The basket is reasonably priced,
    the vehicle is sound, the earnings-yield premium over cap-weighted peers is intact.

  2. If one or more conditions are weakening but no hard exit has fired — do not add. Re-validate
    after the next quarter. If the earnings-yield premium over a cap-weighted alternative compresses
    below 0.5 percentage points for two consecutive quarters, consider consolidating into a
    cap-weighted global fund.

  3. If any exit condition fires or the vehicle is structurally breached — exit. Move proceeds
    to a cap-weighted global equity fund or to risk-free.

  4. If the basket's earnings yield rises above 6.25% (roughly €4.44 per share at current
    earnings) — add in tranches. This is the buy zone; it should be rare.

  5. If the basket's earnings yield falls below 4.0% (roughly €6.94 per share at current
    earnings) — trim 25–33%. Harvest the premium and redeploy on the next rebalance.

The Share Price

The basket's earnings yield of 4.78% corresponds to a share price of roughly €5.80 on the
Xetra exchange (snapshot: 2026-06-23). The framework's fair-value anchor is a basket earnings
yield of 5.0% — a level that corresponds to roughly €5.55 per share at current earnings.

At €5.80, the share price is about 4.5% above the fair-value share-price anchor of €5.55:
mathematically at fair value, sitting within the hold band. The distance to the buy edge
(€4.44, where the earnings yield would be 6.25%) is about 30 percentage points away — the
basket would need to cheapen materially before a buy signal fires. The distance to the trim
edge (€6.94, where the earnings yield would be 4.0%) is about 16 percentage points away.

In plain terms: neither cheap enough to add on valuation grounds, nor expensive enough to
trim. The share price is doing what the thesis predicts for a broadly diversified developed-
market basket at current interest rates.

The Standing Orders

The rules translate the share-price bands into two standing orders and two price alerts. Each
is the operationalisation of a pre-committed rule.

Price alerts (informational).
- A first alert at €5.55, the fair-value anchor. A crossing means the share price has returned
to a neutral reading; no action follows, but a fresh read of the conditions is warranted.
- A second alert at €4.44, the cheap edge. A crossing arms the buy order below; before adding,
the latest re-validation should confirm the conditions are still working.

Standing orders (actionable).
- A standing buy order at €4.44. The rules call for adding in tranches once the share price
closes at or below this line and the conditions are still working. This would correspond to
a basket earnings yield of 6.25% — meaningfully above the risk-free rate.
- A standing trim order at €6.94. The rules call for trimming 25–33% of the position when
the share price closes at or above this line. At that level the basket earnings yield would
fall to 4.0% — a thin premium over the current risk-free rate.

The sell side of this fund is governed by vehicle-integrity breaches (fund size, annual cost,
concentration, methodology change), not by the share price reaching an exit level. No exit
price has been authored; the trim order is the most aggressive price-driven action.

What's Wobbling

Two of the six conditions sit in mixed territory rather than confirming cleanly.

The equity risk premium — 0.31 percentage points against the US 10-year Treasury — is
technically positive but thin. Recent history is slightly downward (0.35% on 2026-06-17,
0.33% on 2026-06-21, 0.31% on 2026-06-23), though not yet close to the negative territory
that would arm the exit condition. As noted in the conditions section above,
the USD benchmark is conservative for EUR holders; the German Bund equivalent would sit near
2.2%, well above the 2.0% confirm line.

The look-through quality metric — median return-on-equity of 14.1% across the visible top-10 —
sits in mixed territory by design. The current top-10 is semiconductor-heavy due to intra-quarter
price drift, not deliberate overweighting; the full basket's sector mix is far more balanced.
The 12% exit threshold is not under pressure.

Neither wobble changes the call. Equal weighting accepts mixed quality and thin spread in
exchange for genuine diversification away from mega-cap concentration.

What Could Break the Fund

  • Earnings yield falls to the trim line. Below 4.0% earnings yield (above roughly €6.94
    per share), the trim order activates — the basket would be priced at roughly 25 times
    earnings, expensive by historical standards.

  • Equity risk premium turns negative for two quarters. At current levels (0.31 percentage
    points of spread), a 35-basis-point rise in the Treasury yield without a matching rise in
    earnings would flip the sign and start the two-quarter exit clock.

  • Fund closure from asset-base decline. A sustained drawdown toward the €500 million floor
    triggers the vehicle-survival exit. The current €1.48 billion provides meaningful but not
    unlimited cushion.

  • Invesco raises the annual cost above 0.30%. The current 0.20% is competitive; above
    0.30% erodes the structural yield advantage over cap-weighted alternatives.

  • MSCI restructures the methodology. A shift away from equal weighting, removal of the
    quarterly rebalance, or a change of the parent universe would invalidate the structural
    thesis. This risk is qualitative and externally monitored.
