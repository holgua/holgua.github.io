About the Bond

The Bundesrepublik Deutschland Inflationsindexierte Anleihe 2021/2033 is a bond issued by the Federal Republic of Germany and maturing on 2033-04-15. It pays a nominal coupon of 0.10% per year, but both the coupon and the principal repayment are scaled by the German Harmonised Index of Consumer Prices (HICP) excluding tobacco — a Eurostat inflation gauge — meaning the cash flows rise with inflation over the bond's life. In simple terms: if prices in the euro area are 30% higher in 2033 than at issuance in 2021, the bond repays 30% more than the original face value.

The instrument is denominated in euros. Settlement is on the dirty price — the amount that changes hands includes accrued interest and the inflation adjustment. A buyer pays the dirty price of €120.23 per €100 original face value at settlement, not €95.14. The €95.14 is the quoted clean price in real terms (same purchasing-power units as the issuance date). To get the actual euros that change hands, multiply by the index ratio (1.26331), then add accrued interest.

The Case for the Bet

The bet is structural, not tactical. Germany's government is effectively unable to default on a euro-denominated bond, and the HICP linkage means the bond's real purchasing power cannot be eroded by inflation in the ordinary course. The case rests entirely on three conditions that must keep holding:

  1. The compound exit trigger stays inactive. The real yield at the current market price must not fall below −1.0% for two consecutive quarters while EUR cash simultaneously offers a better after-tax real return. A sustained negative real yield combined with a superior EUR cash alternative would mean the inflation-hedge function is no longer additive — the protection would cost more than buying it elsewhere.

  2. German sovereign credit stays above AA−. If any two of the three major agencies (Fitch, Moody's, Standard & Poor's) were to downgrade Germany below AA−, the near-zero credit-risk assumption that underpins the position would no longer hold.

  3. The HICP ex-tobacco index methodology is not materially restructured. If Eurostat were to replace or substantially alter the reference index in a way that severs the link to actual consumer-price inflation, the inflation-hedge function would be broken regardless of the bond's price.

What Could Still Go Wrong

  • Real yield slides toward the exit floor. If ECB policy rates were cut sharply while inflation expectations held firm, the linker's real yield could fall toward −1.0%. The compound trigger requires both a sustained yield below −1.0% and EUR cash offering a better real return — but falling yields alone would erode the margin.
  • German fiscal position deteriorates sharply. A sustained increase in German sovereign debt, combined with a loss of political consensus on fiscal discipline, could eventually put pressure on the AAA rating. A two-agency downgrade below AA− triggers exit condition (2).
  • Eurostat redefines or replaces the HICP ex-tobacco index. If the reference index were restructured in a way that understates actual consumer-price inflation, the inflation-hedge function would be impaired regardless of market price. This is a low-probability but non-zero regulatory risk.
  • Duration risk on the way out. With a modified duration of approximately 6.7 years, the bond is sensitive to real-yield movements: a 1 percentage-point rise in real yields would reduce the clean price by roughly €6.70 per €100 face. This matters if the position needs to be unwound quickly rather than held to maturity.
  • Inflation undershoots persistently. If HICP ex-tobacco averages well below 2% for an extended period, the index-linked principal accrual will be modest, and the bond's total return may fall short of conventional Bunds. The thesis accepts this as the cost of insurance — but it is still a cost.

The Rules

If the thesis holds — real yield within band, German credit intact, HICP linkage operating — hold. The inflation-hedge function is working as intended.

If the thesis weakens — a driver raises a concern without triggering an exit condition, or real yield drifts below 0% without reaching −1.0% — do not add. Re-assess the real after-tax return against EUR cash and nominal Bunds before taking any action.

If the thesis breaks — any of the three numbered exit conditions fires (real yield below −1.0% persistently, a two-agency German downgrade, or a material HICP methodology change) — exit. Move proceeds to nominal short-duration Bunds or EUR cash.

If the real yield rises to 1.50% or above (clean price at or below €91) and no driver challenges — add to the position. A 7-year AAA sovereign linker at 1.50% real yield is highly attractive; scale in proportionally to the inflation-hedge allocation target.

If the real yield falls to −0.50% or below (clean price at or above €104) — trim 25% on first crossing. If the real yield remains below −0.50% at the next quarterly review (at least 90 days later), trim an additional 25%. Park proceeds in EUR cash or short conventional Bunds. The two-step ladder is bounded — further crossings do not trigger additional trims until exit condition (1) fires.

If the compound trigger fires — real yield below −1.0% for at least 90 days and EUR cash simultaneously offering a superior real return — exit the full position. Switch proceeds to EUR cash or short-duration nominal Bunds; the inflation-hedge function is no longer additive versus the alternative.
