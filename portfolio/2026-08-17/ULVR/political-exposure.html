Narrative Assessment

Unilever's political picture for an investor is dominated by two near-term issues: the MAHA food ingredient regulatory movement and the McCormick antitrust review. MAHA is a structural headwind that has migrated from federal executive action to state law across more than a dozen US states, requiring the company to reformulate or lobby against ingredient bans — the Q1 2026 lobbying acceleration to $540,000 in a single quarter signals management is taking this seriously. The paradox is that the McCormick transaction, Unilever's primary strategic action, disposes of the Hellmann's and Knorr brands most exposed to MAHA — meaning that a successful deal close in mid-2027 would substantially reduce this political liability. The antitrust review of that same transaction by the UK CMA, EU DG COMP, and US DOJ is therefore simultaneously the principal deal risk and the primary path to political risk reduction. US tariff exposure is genuinely limited by Unilever's local-production model and has been confirmed as non-material by management. The absence of any government-background personnel on the board means there is no revolving-door advantage, but equally no conflict-of-interest liability. Unilever's political risk is not structural to the core business — it is a transitional risk tied to the Nutrition portfolio being exited and to regulatory trend-lines that run against processed food regardless of which party is in power.
