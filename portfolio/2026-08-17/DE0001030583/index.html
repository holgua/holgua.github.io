About Bund Linker 2033

The Federal Republic of Germany issued this bond in 2021 with a stated coupon of 0.10% a year and a maturity date of 2033-04-15. The coupon and the repayment of principal are not fixed in euro terms — they are linked to the German consumer price index excluding tobacco products, as computed by Eurostat. When prices rise, the bond's cash flows rise with them, meaning purchasing power is preserved in a way that a conventional fixed-coupon bond cannot match. Credit risk is effectively zero: Germany has never defaulted on a euro-denominated obligation and carries the highest possible credit rating.

The bond's quoted clean price of €94.64 refers to the price in real terms — the same purchasing-power units as the original issuance date. To establish what actually changes hands at settlement, the clean price is multiplied by the indexation coefficient (1.26347 as of 2026-08-14), then accrued interest is added. The result is the dirty price of €119.61 per €100 original face value. A buyer paying €94.64 in clean-price terms is paying €119.61 in actual euros; the gap is entirely the accumulated inflation adjustment, not a hidden cost.

The Case for the Bet

The bond is held as a structural inflation hedge — not as a rates trade. Three conditions determine whether the position should continue:

  1. Real yield at current market price stays above −1.00% or EUR cash does not offer a clearly superior real return. If the bond's real yield falls persistently below −1.00% and EUR cash offers better after-inflation returns, holding an instrument with a guaranteed real loss embedded in its price is no longer justified. Both legs of this compound condition must be satisfied simultaneously, for at least 90 calendar days.

Latest reading: 0.93% as of 2026-08-14. The yield sits comfortably above the exit floor of −1.00%. EUR cash yields roughly +0.21% in real terms; the linker leads by +72 basis points. The compound exit trigger has not activated; 0 of the required 90 days have been observed below the exit floor across 24 recorded ledger entries.

  1. German sovereign credit remains above AA−. If two of the three major rating agencies (Fitch, Moody's, and Standard & Poor's) downgrade Germany below AA−, the structural assumption of near-zero credit risk no longer holds and the basis for holding a government bond in preference to other EUR instruments is undermined.

Latest reading: All three agencies confirm AAA or equivalent as of 2026-06-15. Fitch: AAA, Moody's: Aaa, Standard & Poor's: AAA. The exit condition is not triggered.

  1. HICP ex-tobacco index methodology is unchanged. If Eurostat materially restructures the German consumer price index in a way that severs the link between the index and realised consumer-price inflation, the bond's inflation-hedge function is impaired regardless of its quoted yield.

Latest reading: No pending restructuring of the German HICP methodology as of 2026-06-15. Exit condition is not triggered.

What's Wobbling

Nothing is wobbling in a way that threatens any of the three conditions. All four thesis drivers — real yield, break-even inflation, German sovereign credit, and HICP methodology stability — were assessed as confirming the thesis at the 2026-08-14 review.

The one thing worth keeping an eye on is the direction of the ECB's deposit facility rate relative to break-even inflation. The ECB rate has been held at 2.25% since 2026-06-17, and break-even inflation at 2.04% leaves EUR cash with a real yield of only +0.21%. If the ECB were to cut rates materially — say, to 1.50% or below — while break-even inflation remained near 2%, EUR cash real yields would turn negative, which would widen the linker's lead and push the compound exit trigger further from firing. Conversely, if the ECB tightened and break-even fell, the linker's yield advantage could erode. Neither direction breaks any condition at current readings; it is a figure to revisit at each quarterly update.

What Could Still Go Wrong

  • Real yield falls to −1.00% and stays there while EUR cash outperforms. If a surge in linker demand (for example, a flight to inflation-linked assets during a period of elevated inflation uncertainty) pushes the bond's price above €108, the yield breaches the exit floor. If that persists for 90 days while ECB rates provide a better real return, exit condition (1) fires. Both legs must be satisfied simultaneously — a transient price spike alone does not trigger the rule.
  • German sovereign downgrade. A downgrade of Germany below AA− by any two of the three major agencies would fire exit condition (2). While no agency has signalled a negative outlook, this is not impossible over a seven-year horizon if fiscal stress in the euro area were to escalate significantly.
  • HICP methodology change. A decision by Eurostat to alter the German consumer price index ex-tobacco in ways that reduce its correlation with realised inflation would undermine the bond's hedging function. This is historically rare and institutionally resistant to change, but remains a tail risk.

  • If any driver weakens but the thesis is not broken: do not add. Re-assess when the real yield approaches 0% but has not yet triggered exit condition (1); compare real after-tax returns against EUR cash and nominal German government bonds before adding further.
  • If one or more exit conditions trigger: exit the position. Scenario Definitions

A scenario is a joint assumption about two independent variables: operating trajectory (how the holding's underlying cash flows grow relative to their long-run trend) and exit multiple (what the market pays for those cash flows at the horizon, relative to a fair-value anchor).

Scenario Operating trajectory Exit multiple
Bear Below trend Below fair value
Base At trend At fair value
Bull Above trend At or above fair value

Non-negotiable rule: In the bear case, both dimensions must be adverse simultaneously — a scenario where only the multiple moves is a re-rating scenario, not a bear.

Bear cases are comparable in structure across all holding types (company, ETF, bond), but not in magnitude. A company bear reflects idiosyncratic execution failure; an ETF bear reflects a broad market drawdown. Different mitigations, different diversification implications.
