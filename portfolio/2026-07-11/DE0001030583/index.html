About the Bond

The Federal Republic of Germany is the issuer — the sovereign borrower of the world's fourth-largest economy, rated AAA by Fitch and S&P and Aaa by Moody's. Germany has never defaulted on a Euro-denominated obligation. The bond matures on 2033-04-15 and was first issued in 2021-02-09.

What makes it a linker is the HICP ex-tobacco index: the principal and coupon payments on this bond are scaled up each year in line with the Harmonised Index of Consumer Prices excluding tobacco as published by Eurostat. That linkage means the bond's real purchasing power is preserved regardless of how much consumer prices rise between now and maturity. The coupon rate is 0.10% annually, which appears tiny in isolation; the economic return comes from the index-linked uplift on the principal, not from the coupon cash flow. The next coupon payment is due on 2027-04-15.

A buyer pays the dirty price of €119.97 per €100 original face value at settlement, not €94.91. The €94.91 is the quoted clean price in real terms (same purchasing-power units as the issuance date). To get the actual euros that change hands, multiply by the index ratio (1.26356), then add accrued interest.

The Case for the Bet

The bet is structural rather than tactical: the bond is held as a portfolio inflation hedge, sized to protect purchasing power, not to trade on rate movements. Three conditions, if broken, would disqualify that purpose entirely:

  1. Real yield falls below −1.0% for at least 90 consecutive days while EUR cash simultaneously offers a superior real return. A real yield that stays deeply negative for an extended period means the bondholder is locking in a guaranteed real loss relative to simply holding cash. The compound structure of this trigger — both the yield threshold and the cash-comparison leg must fire simultaneously — is deliberate: a brief dip below −1.0% in a volatile rate environment should not trigger an exit.

Latest reading: Real yield 0.88% as of 2026-07-10. Zero days observed below the −1.0% exit threshold. EUR cash real yield is approximately 0.25% (ECB deposit rate 2.25% minus break-even inflation 2.00%), leaving the linker 63 basis points ahead. Neither leg of the compound trigger is satisfied.

  1. Germany's sovereign credit rating is downgraded below AA- by any two of the three major rating agencies. A two-agency downgrade below AA- would signal a structural deterioration in Germany's creditworthiness, undermining the near-zero-default-risk premise on which the inflation-hedge logic rests.

Latest reading: As of 2026-06-15, Fitch rates Germany AAA, Moody's rates it Aaa, and S&P rates it AAA. All three sit well above the AA- trigger level. Exit condition (2) is not triggered.

  1. Eurostat materially restructures the HICP ex-tobacco index methodology in a way that severs the link between the index and actual consumer-price inflation. The inflation-hedge function depends entirely on the reference index tracking real consumer prices. A wholesale methodology change — substituting a different index series or altering the basket in a way that decouples it from experienced inflation — would hollow out the bond's protective role.

Latest reading: No pending HICP methodology restructuring has been recorded in the holdings sidecar as of 2026-06-15. Exit condition (3) is not triggered.

What Could Still Go Wrong

  • Sustained low real yield combined with superior EUR cash return. If the European Central Bank cuts rates sharply while break-even inflation stays elevated, the linker's real yield could fall toward or below zero. If EUR cash simultaneously offers a better after-inflation return — which can happen when nominal cash rates fall more slowly than break-even inflation rises — the compound trigger for exit condition (1) begins to accumulate days. Ninety days of that combination triggers an exit.
  • German sovereign downgrade. A credit event severe enough to push two of the three major agencies to downgrade Germany below AA- would not just trigger exit condition (2) — it would also likely widen the linker's real-yield spread well above its current near-fair-value level, repricing the bond sharply lower before an exit could be executed cleanly.
  • HICP index methodology change. Eurostat has the authority to revise the basket and methodology of the HICP ex-tobacco index. A change that materially alters the relationship between the index and experienced consumer-price inflation — for example, a significant methodological dampening of housing costs — would reduce the bond's effectiveness as a purchasing-power hedge and trigger exit condition (3).
  • Duration risk on an early-exit scenario. The bond carries a modified duration of approximately 6.7 years. A 100-basis-point rise in real yields would move the clean price by roughly €6.70 per €100 face. For a holder who exits before maturity, this is the relevant market-price sensitivity — the real-yield protection only fully materialises if the bond is held to 2033-04-15 maturity.

The Rules

If the thesis is intact and no band edge is approached, hold. Real yield is within band, German credit is intact, and HICP linkage provides inflation protection as intended.

If the thesis shows signs of weakening — real yield moves below 0% but has not yet triggered exit condition (1) — do not add. Re-assess the real after-tax return against EUR cash and nominal Bunds before adding to the position.

If any of the three exit conditions fires, exit the entire position. One or more numbered exit conditions triggered means the inflation-hedge rationale no longer holds. Proceeds move to nominal short-duration Euro Bunds or EUR cash.

If the real yield rises above 1.5% (price falls to or below €91), add to the position. A real yield above 1.5% on a seven-year AAA Euro sovereign linker is highly attractive relative to any EUR risk-free alternative; scale in proportionally to the inflation-hedge allocation target.

If the real yield falls below −0.50% (price rises to or above €104), trim 25% on the first crossing. If the real yield remains below −0.50% at the next quarterly review (at least 90 calendar days later), trim a further 25%. The two-step ladder is bounded — further crossings do not trigger additional trims until exit condition (1) fires.

If the compound exit trigger fires — real yield below −1.0% for at least 90 days and EUR cash offering a superior real return — exit the full position. Switch proceeds to EUR cash or short-duration nominal Bunds; the inflation-hedge function is no longer additive relative to the alternative.
