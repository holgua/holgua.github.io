The Bottom Line

Data as of 2026-08-12 · Latest filing 2025-12-31.

The share price sits below the buy edge, meaningfully below fair value. Three of the seven thesis conditions are delivering firmly; two are mixed; two cannot be assessed from structured data alone.

That letter is not law, but it is a credible leading indicator for what happens to OMV's earnings in the bear scenario.

Recent Development

The FY2025 annual report confirmed that OMV's operating cash flow engine remains resilient at €5,215 million — the fifth consecutive year above the €4.5 billion threshold that constitutes a bull signal for the first thesis condition. That consistency matters because it has come through a period of dramatic revenue compression: sales fell 61% from the FY2022 energy-crisis peak of €62.3 billion to €24.3 billion in FY2025, and yet the cash generation held. The accruals ratio of −0.08 — a metric comparing reported profit to actual cash earnings — confirms the cash is genuine rather than an accounting artefact.

The load-bearing development in the latest period, however, is not the OCF figure. It is the effective tax rate's proximity to its exit trigger. At 54.7% of pre-tax profits for FY2025, it stands just 0.3 percentage points below the 55% threshold that — if crossed in any year after 2026 — would trigger a mandatory full exit from the position. The FY2023 solidarity levy drove the rate to 58.4%; the subsequent two years have shown partial normalisation (51.7% in FY2024, then a rebound to 54.7% in FY2025), suggesting the structural floor for the rate is materially higher than the pre-windfall-tax era of approximately 42%. Five EU finance ministers signed an April 2026 letter calling for a new energy windfall levy; the next annual filing will resolve whether the exit condition has been crossed. For a fuller read of where the valuation stands today, see the Share Price.

The March 2026 formation of Borouge Group International — combining Borealis, Borouge, and NOVA Chemicals into a single entity in which OMV holds a 46.94% equity-method stake — did not yet appear in FY2025 numbers, which predate the transaction. The FY2025 book value for the predecessor Borealis stake stood at €5,255 million, 31% above the €4.0 billion floor that would trigger exit condition two. That figure gives some comfort but is the last data point under the old entity structure; the FY2026 annual filing will be the first test of whether the combined BGI entity sustains that book value.

About OMV

OMV is Austria's largest oil, gas and chemicals company, operating across three segments. The upstream business extracts crude oil and natural gas in Central and Eastern Europe — primarily Austria, where it has held licences for decades — as well as Norway and the United Arab Emirates. The downstream business runs three European refineries (the largest at Schwechat, near Vienna) and a retail fuel network with dominant positions in Austria and Romania, selling around 16 million tonnes of fuel annually across eight countries. The third leg is a 46.94% equity stake in Borouge Group International, a newly consolidated chemicals producer with manufacturing in Abu Dhabi and North America; BGI makes polyolefins — the plastics used in packaging, pipes, and automotive components — and is ranked fourth in the world by production volume.

The Austrian state, through its investment vehicle ÖBAG, holds 31.5% of OMV. The Abu Dhabi state energy company, XRG (formerly known as ADNOC), holds another 24.9%. Together, state actors control more than 56% of the voting rights. That ownership structure simultaneously protects OMV from hostile takeovers, provides the government backing needed to renew E&P licences domestically, and creates a governance tension: the same Austrian government that provides OMV its social licence also co-signed the April 2026 letter calling for a new EU windfall profit tax — a levy that, if enacted, would again hit OMV's earnings in the way the FY2023 solidarity levy did (€552 million that year alone). Emma Delaney, previously an executive at bp, takes over as chief executive in September 2026; her stated priority is capital expenditure discipline and integration of the BGI chemicals venture.

The Case for the Bet

The central argument is that OMV's combination of state-backed E&P licences in Central and Eastern Europe and a scaled, low-cost chemicals business in Abu Dhabi can sustain operating cash flows well above the level needed to pay its dividend and fund its investment programme — and that the current share price, below the 7.5% earnings-yield threshold, offers a margin of safety on that cash generation. Six specific conditions must keep holding:

  1. Annual operating cash flow stays above €4.5 billion at prevailing oil prices. The signal level is €4.5 billion when Brent crude is above approximately €53 per barrel; a reading below €3.5 billion at those oil prices would be a bear signal.

Latest reading: €5,215 million for the year ending 2025-12-31, the fifth consecutive year above the threshold. The accruals ratio of −0.08 confirms that cash earnings meaningfully exceed reported accounting profits.

  1. Capital expenditure does not absorb more than 90% of operating cash flow for two consecutive years. At that level, the business is consuming nearly all its cash generation in investment, leaving no buffer for debt repayment or dividends.

Latest reading: The broader capital expenditure estimate for FY2025 is approximately 73.8% of operating cash flow (using a definition that includes exploration and BGI-related spending). The investing cash flow alone implies a lower ratio of approximately 53%. Both readings remain below the 90% trigger, though the expected peak in capital expenditure intensity has not yet materialised. Two consecutive years above 90% are required before this exit condition fires.

  1. The Borouge Group International equity-method book value stays above €4.0 billion for two consecutive years. Below that level, the chemicals venture would be demonstrably destroying value rather than creating it.

Latest reading: €5,255 million as of 2025-12-31, 31% above the exit floor. This is the last reading under the predecessor Borealis entity label before the March 2026 BGI consolidation; the FY2026 annual filing will be the first under the new combined structure.

  1. The effective tax rate does not exceed 55% in any year after 2026. A sustained rate above 55% would confirm that EU windfall profit levies are recurring rather than one-off, structurally impairing earning power beyond the FY2023 solidarity levy precedent.

Latest reading: 54.7% for FY2025, which is 0.3 percentage points below the trigger level. The condition does not apply to FY2025 itself; it fires only for years after FY2026. The partial decline from the FY2023 peak of 58.4% is encouraging, but the structural floor appears materially higher than the pre-windfall era (approximately 42% in FY2021). The April 2026 EU finance ministers' letter is the most credible near-term risk indicator.

  1. Interest coverage stays above 4.0 times for two consecutive years. Coverage measures how many times operating profit covers the annual interest bill; below 4.0 times for two consecutive years would confirm leverage stress is threatening the investment programme.

Latest reading: 8.0 times for FY2025, down from 10.8 times in FY2024 and 12.6 times in FY2023 as operating profit has compressed with lower commodity prices. The decline is notable but the current level sits well above the exit threshold; two consecutive years below 4.0 times are required before this condition fires.

  1. Annual fuel sales volume in Austria and Romania does not decline more than 10% over any two-year period. A decline of that magnitude, confirmed in the annual report's segment disclosure, would signal structural erosion of the Central and Eastern European retail moat by competitors.

Latest reading: Not available for FY2025. The machine-readable structured data in OMV's regulatory filings does not include a tagged metric for segment fuel sales volume, and narrative text from the filings could not be extracted. The most recent available figure is 16.2 million tonnes across eight European countries for FY2024. This condition can only be assessed from the annual report text; the FY2025 annual report PDF has not been reviewed.

The Share Price

OMV's shares trade at approximately €62, measured against a fair value of approximately €84 — a share price that is meaningfully below fair value on the earnings-yield method used in this analysis. The earnings yield at this price (roughly 7.6% — the ratio of the company's owner earnings to its market value) exceeds the 7.5% level that marks the buy edge of the valuation band. In other words, the price is not merely cheap in the abstract; it is below the specific threshold that the pre-committed rules define as the entry trigger.

That said, the same share price looks materially different through a different lens. A peer-multiple valuation — which applies the price-to-earnings and enterprise-value-to-profit multiples that comparable European oil and gas companies trade at to OMV's projected financials — produces a base-case fair value of approximately €42 per share at current exchange rates. On that measure, €62 represents a 55% premium to the peer-anchored base case, with no margin of safety. The two methods reach such different conclusions because the earnings-yield approach anchors to OMV's current cash generation (which is strong) while the peer-multiple approach reflects where the market prices future earnings on a sector-comparable basis (which is more cautious). The warnings box above flags this conflict explicitly; it is the most structurally important caveat in this analysis.

The current dividend yield of approximately 4.8% per year provides real income during a period when the share price is near the buy edge. That yield is funded by an 86% payout ratio — a share of profits paid as dividends that leaves very little buffer if earnings fall. In the bear scenario (Brent below approximately €48 per barrel plus a windfall levy), that dividend would almost certainly be cut, removing the income cushion precisely when the capital loss risk is highest.

What's Wobbling

Three wobbles are present in the latest filing period, and not one of them changes the overall verdict from working to wobbling.

The first is the effective tax rate's proximity to its exit trigger. At 54.7% for FY2025, the rate is the closest it has been to the 55% exit level since the FY2023 solidarity levy year, when it spiked to 58.4%. The subsequent two years show partial normalisation (51.7% in FY2024, then 54.7% in FY2025), but the structural floor remains well above the pre-windfall era. The exit condition for this driver applies to any year after FY2026 — FY2025 at 54.7% does not trigger it — but the April 2026 EU finance ministers' letter (co-signed by Austria) elevates the probability of a FY2026 breach. This is the highest-urgency item entering the next filing cycle.

The second wobble is capital expenditure intensity. The thesis estimate puts the capital expenditure-to-operating-cash-flow ratio at approximately 73.8% for FY2025, broadly flat from FY2024. The "CapEx peak" that the thesis anticipated arriving in FY2025 has not yet been confirmed by observable data: investing cash outflows were €2,754 million in FY2025, little changed from €3,152 million in FY2024. The exit condition requires two consecutive years above 90%; the current level is 16 percentage points below that threshold. Emma Delaney's first capital markets communication as chief executive is the next catalyst to watch for guidance on whether the peak is approaching. The two consecutive years requirement means this condition cannot fire before the FY2026 annual report.

The third wobble is informational rather than operational. The fair-value anchor used in the earnings-yield model (set at 5.6%) diverges significantly from both the current earnings yield (7.6%) and the fair-value implied by the peer-multiple scenario bands (8.75%). The divergence means the buy-edge trigger is more conservative than a recalibrated model would produce — the pre-committed rules are calling for adding at a price that is already below what an updated fair-value would consider cheap. This does not change the verdict today, but it underscores that the valuation framework needs reviewing when the next full annual filing is processed.

What Could Still Go Wrong

  • EU windfall profit tax re-enacted for FY2026. The FY2023 solidarity levy drove OMV's effective tax rate to 58.4% and produced a one-off charge of €552 million. A recurrence — supported by the April 2026 EU finance ministers' letter — would simultaneously trigger exit condition three (if the rate exceeds 55% in any year after FY2026) and structurally compress owner earnings by 30–40% on a sustained basis.
  • Brent crude sustained below approximately €57 per barrel. OMV's operating cash flow model depends on oil prices in the roughly €62–70 range. At approximately €48 per barrel — a historically plausible scenario in a demand-destruction or OPEC+ coordination breakdown — free cash flow turns negative, the 86% dividend payout ratio becomes arithmetically unsustainable, and the share price would likely fall toward the peer book-value anchor of approximately €34. That is a potential decline of over 45% from today's price.
  • BGI chemicals book value deteriorates below €4.5 billion. The Borouge Group International (BGI) stake — OMV's primary long-term value creation bet — sits at €5,255 million in the last annual report before the entity consolidation. A sustained polyolefin market trough or capital allocation mis-step at BGI could compress this figure; below €4.5 billion it triggers a watch signal, and below €4.0 billion for two consecutive years it triggers a mandatory exit.
  • Capital expenditure remains above 80% of operating cash flow. At 73.8% for FY2025, the ratio is below the exit level but not declining. If Emma Delaney's incoming capital expenditure review fails to produce a downward revision, or if the BGI integration requires additional cash, two consecutive years above 90% would trigger a mandatory exit and indicate that the business is consuming all its cash generation without meaningful residual owner earnings.
  • PKN Orlen's Central and Eastern European expansion erodes fuel retail volumes. The Polish state energy champion has been expanding across Central Europe following its mergers with Lotos, PGNiG, and Energa. OMV's Austrian and Romanian retail network is the moat that protects the downstream segment; a sustained volume decline of more than 10% over two years would trigger exit condition six. No FY2025 volume data is available from structured sources — this condition requires manual review of the annual report text.

Gaps in the Picture

The dominant data gap in this analysis is the absence of capital expenditure from OMV's machine-readable regulatory filings. OMV reports under European standards (ESEF/IFRS) and does not tag its capital expenditure in a structured, computable format — a limitation common among European oil and gas filers. This means the capital expenditure-to-operating-cash-flow ratio central to exit condition one cannot be directly computed from structured data; it is estimated using investing cash flows and a broader definition from the thesis. The exit condition monitor therefore carries a material uncertainty band.

Similarly, OMV's annual report narratives — management commentary, segment volume disclosures, governance sections — are filed primarily in German and have not been extractable from the structured filing data. This means exit condition six (fuel sales volume) and the ÖBAG governance risk driver cannot be assessed from structured data alone. Both require manual review of the annual report PDF.

The fair-value anchor (5.6% earnings-yield target) is flagged as stale: it diverges from the current earnings yield by 35% and from the peer-implied fair-value yield by 56%. Updating the fair-value anchor would shift the price alerts and standing orders meaningfully; the current rules are more conservative than a recalibrated model would produce.

Free cash flow is not tagged in OMV's machine-readable annual filings, preventing direct computation of the dividend cover ratio needed to monitor exit condition five. The cover ratio is instead estimated from operating and investing cash flows, which conflates growth capital expenditure with maintenance spending.

The Rules

The pre-committed rules for this position define exactly what to do under each combination of conditions and price:

  • If all six thesis conditions are holding — operating cash flow above €4.5 billion, capital expenditure below 90% of OCF, BGI book value above €4.0 billion, effective tax rate below 55% after FY2026, interest coverage above 4.0 times, and no material CEE fuel volume decline — hold the current allocation and collect the dividend yield. No new capital is added at or above the current price, because the probability-weighted three-year expected return is near zero from this level; the thesis requires a lower entry point for additional capital.
  • If the thesis shows signs of weakening — capital expenditure above 80% of operating cash flow for one year, BGI book value declining toward €4.5 billion, effective tax rate sustained above 53%, Brent below approximately €53 per barrel for two consecutive quarters, or PKN Orlen capturing material CEE retail volume — do not add. Reduce the review cadence to annual and re-evaluate after the next annual filing.
  • If any one exit condition has been triggered — capital expenditure above 90% of operating cash flow for two consecutive years; BGI equity book value below €4.0 billion for two consecutive years; effective tax rate above 55% in any year after FY2026; interest coverage below 4.0 times for two consecutive years; dividend formally cut or cash flow cover of dividends below 1.0 times for two consecutive years; or CEE fuel sales volume declining more than 10% over two years — exit the full position. Move proceeds to a European diversified energy fund or to EUR cash depending on portfolio concentration at the time.
  • If the share price falls to or below the buy edge (an earnings yield at or above 7.5%, implying a price of approximately €62–63 per share) and no driver is at the challenged level, add to the position in two tranches, confirming that the operating cash flow trend is not deteriorating before committing the second tranche. The maximum addition per tranche is 50% of the existing allocation.
  • If the share price rises above the trim edge (an earnings yield below 4.5%, implying a price above approximately €94 per share), trim 25–33% of the position to harvest the premium above the normalised "stretched" valuation. Re-establish if the price subsequently returns to the buy zone.

Scenario Definitions

A scenario is a joint assumption about two independent variables: operating trajectory (how the holding's underlying cash flows grow relative to their long-run trend) and exit multiple (what the market pays for those cash flows at the horizon, relative to a fair-value anchor).

Scenario Operating trajectory Exit multiple
Bear Below trend Below fair value
Base At trend At fair value
Bull Above trend At or above fair value

Non-negotiable rule: In the bear case, both dimensions must be adverse simultaneously — a scenario where only the multiple moves is a re-rating scenario, not a bear.

Bear cases are comparable in structure across all holding types (company, ETF, bond), but not in magnitude. A company bear reflects idiosyncratic execution failure; an ETF bear reflects a broad market drawdown. Different mitigations, different diversification implications.
