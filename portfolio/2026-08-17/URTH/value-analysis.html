2. Equity Risk Premium

Metric Value
Basket Earnings Yield 4.16%
Risk-free rate (^TNX) 4.60%
Equity Risk Premium −0.44%

Judgment: CHALLENGES — ERP is negative (below 0.0%).

A negative ERP is Buffett's "Cash is a better deal" condition in its plainest form: the 10-year Treasury at 4.60% pays more per dollar invested than URTH's look-through earnings at 4.16%. The −44 bp gap means the market is pricing zero equity risk premium into a globally diversified basket of 1,300+ companies. Historically, sustained negative ERP periods have resolved either through price compression (falling P/E) or earnings acceleration. The thesis exit condition (1) — two consecutive quarters of negative ERP — is tracking toward confirmation; the driver_states context records 15 consecutive negative ERP snapshots since 2026-06-16 with Q3 2026 still open.

5. Owner Perspective (Buffett Frame)

"Would Berkshire deploy cash into this basket today?"

No. The basket's earnings yield of 4.16% is lower than the risk-free 10-year Treasury at 4.60%, so a dollar deployed into URTH today earns less in look-through earnings than the same dollar in government paper with zero equity risk. Buffett's canonical test — the equity should yield meaningfully more than the risk-free alternative — is failing by 44 basis points. Compounding the problem, the basket is priced at 24.03× trailing earnings, a ~20% premium to the analyst's 20× fair-value anchor, meaning the entry multiple itself is expensive. The saving grace is basket quality: median top-10 ROE of 37.28% confirms the underlying businesses are high-quality compounders, so the hold case is not about deteriorating business quality — it is about price discipline. At current prices, Berkshire would hold cash and wait; it would not add to this position.
