PART I — INVESTMENT THESIS

1.1 One-Paragraph Thesis

PepsiCo is a wide-moat consumer staples compounder with durable pricing power across iconic global brands (Lay's, Pepsi, Gatorade, Quaker, Mountain Dew), generating $93.9B in revenue across six reportable segments with ~44% international exposure. The central return mechanism is pricing-led growth layered on a direct-store-delivery distribution network that is structurally difficult to replicate, with ROIC of 12.5–15.3% consistently above estimated WACC. The central debate is whether GLP-1 medications and private-label competition structurally impair convenient foods demand, and whether the 2.48x Net Debt/EBITDA leverage trajectory can reverse without sacrificing the 54-year dividend growth streak. EDGAR data through Q2 2026 supports the base-case recovery: organic volume grew at the highest rate since 2022, gross margins recovered to 55.2% in Q1 2026, and management reaffirmed guidance of +2–4% organic revenue and +4–6% Core CC EPS for full-year 2026 (§12.3; Q2 2026 earnings call July 9, 2026).

6.2 Key Segment Observations

PFNA (North America Foods): Volume pressure from consumer trading-down through 2024. Recovery underway in 2026. GLP-1 medication headwind is concentrated here — 18% US adult usage as of spring 2026, 31% lower snack spending in GLP-1 households. Pricing power intact; gross margin structure superior. Mars-Kellanova (closed December 2025, $36B+ scaled snack entity) introduces a credible competitor, but Frito-Lay's ~60% US salty snacks share and DSD network provide a durable lead (§10.2).

PBNA (North America Beverages): FY2025 operating profit fell 53% on $1.86B Rockstar impairment. Core PBNA operating profit was +6% — the write-down was one-time. Tariff headwind of 6pp on commodity costs (aluminum packaging) is ongoing. Gatorade and Mountain Dew remain strong franchise assets. PBNA organic volume showed lowest decline in 7 consecutive quarters as of Q2 2026 — the first credible improvement signal (§10.2).

International (IB Franchise + EMEA + LatAm + APAC): Management disclosed positive organic revenue across all six segments in Q2 2026 — the broadest simultaneous growth profile in several quarters. IB Franchise (licensing model) is capital-light and provides exposure to international beverage growth without ownership capital requirements (§10.3).

PART VII — MARGIN TRENDS

7.1 Gross Margin — Pricing Power Confirmation

The 54% gross margin corridor is the primary evidence of brand moat durability. A 53.0–54.6% range across five years — spanning acute commodity inflation (FY2022), pricing recovery (FY2023–FY2024), tariff escalation (FY2025), and volume pressure — confirms pricing power is absorbing cost pressure at the gross level (§3.2).

  • Q1 2026 gross margin: 55.2% — highest quarterly reading in the five-year window, suggesting commodity cost relief is materializing.
  • Exit condition watch: gross margin below 52.0% for two consecutive quarters would signal brand pricing power is no longer sufficient (config.yaml exit condition 4).

7.2 Operating Margin — Compression and Recovery Path

FY2025 compression is attributable to non-recurring Rockstar impairment and tariff-driven SG&A. Gross margin held at 54.1%, confirming the compression is entirely below gross profit and partly non-recurring. Recovery toward 13.0–13.5% operating margin by FY2027 is the base case (§3.3, §6.4).

7.3 DuPont Interest Burden — Leverage Signal

The 5-factor DuPont Interest Burden declined from 0.946 (FY2023) to 0.886 (FY2025) — rising debt costs consuming a growing share of EBIT. This is the primary structural risk from elevated leverage, directly linkable to net debt rising from $31.8B (FY2023) to $37.1B (FY2025). Interest coverage remains well above distress (10.26x vs. 3.0x threshold), but the direction matters for credit quality (§6.2).
