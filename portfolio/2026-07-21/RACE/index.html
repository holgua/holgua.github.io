Ferrari: The Bet Still Works

The Bottom Line

Data as of 2026-07-21 · Latest filing 2025-12-31.

The bet is working. Ferrari's scarcity franchise — a deliberate policy of keeping annual production flat while the world's wealthiest buyers queue for allocation — is doing exactly what it promised: margins are expanding, earnings are growing, and all six written exit conditions remain untriggered. The share price sits in the middle of the hold band, at fair value by the earnings-yield measure. At 12% a year, the long-run expected return represents a healthy equity premium over the risk-free rate. The one condition to watch is whether the Elettrica, Ferrari's first battery-electric car, oversubscribes its order book when it opens for commercial orders — that event starts the clock on exit condition six, and it has not yet happened.

Recent Development

Ferrari's full-year 2025 results, drawn from its annual filing, confirm that the scarcity thesis is doing what it says on the label. Operating income grew 11.76% year on year to roughly EUR 2,110 million, while revenue rose 7.03% to EUR 7,146 million on essentially unchanged delivery volumes — 13,640 cars in the year, compared with 13,752 the year before. The implied average selling price per car crossed EUR 524,000, a figure that makes clear the revenue growth was driven entirely by pricing and model mix rather than by selling more cars. That is the thesis: hold volume flat, charge more, and watch margins widen.

The earnings-quality check — operating cash flow divided by net income — came in at 1.47 times, comfortably above the 1.0 times exit threshold. The operating profit margin before depreciation landed at 38.8%, 380 basis points above the 35% floor that would trigger an exit review, and management has guided 2026 to at least 39%.

One genuine wobble deserves attention. The US tariff environment is unsettled: a July 2026 trade agreement between the EU and the US set Ferrari's import duty at 15% all-inclusive, below the 25% level that would (if combined with a margin guidance cut) trigger exit condition five. Ferrari absorbed the earlier 25% tariff shock in 2025 by applying selective price surcharges of up to 10% on certain models — and still delivered 38.8% operating margin before depreciation. That is direct empirical evidence of pricing power. A May 2026 threat to re-escalate to 25% emerged in press coverage but remained unconfirmed as of the analysis date. The 2026 guidance of at least 39% operating margin before depreciation does not assume a 25% tariff scenario, so exit condition five currently has neither of its two required legs in place. The detail is worth monitoring at the Share Price if the tariff picture changes.

About Ferrari

Ferrari makes its money by selling approximately 13,500 to 14,000 ultra-luxury sports cars and grand tourers per year at average prices that no other automotive manufacturer approaches — over half a million euros per car in the most recent fiscal year. The company is structured deliberately to prevent volume from growing beyond that range. Demand from the world's wealthiest buyers is permanently higher than supply; that gap is maintained by design, not by capacity constraint. The result is a business that expands its profit margin while keeping its factory output essentially flat — a combination that is essentially unique among manufacturing companies.

The revenue base is reinforced by a loyalty staircase: access to Ferrari's most sought-after and historically appreciating limited-edition models is gated by a documented prior ownership history. Leaving the brand means losing a place in the allocation queue. This is not a formal contract but it functions as one, and it gives existing owners a strong economic reason to stay. The brand itself — anchored in a seventy-year Formula 1 racing heritage and in the mythology of Enzo Ferrari — underpins the pricing power that the scarcity architecture monetises.

Governance is structurally aligned with long-term ownership. The Agnelli family, through their holding company Exor, controls roughly 24% of the economic interest and 36% of the votes. Piero Ferrari, Enzo's son, holds approximately 10%. Together, the family bloc has both the economic stake and the voting power to block any activist campaign to "unlock value" by expanding production volumes — which would be the precise action that destroys the moat. The dual-class share structure that limits minority influence primarily serves to protect the scarcity model from short-term pressure.

The Case for the Bet

The central bet is that a business producing roughly 14,000 cars per year, with permanent demand exceeding supply, can sustain and expand its margin structure indefinitely because the brand heritage, the scarcity architecture, and the loyalty staircase reinforce each other in ways no competitor can replicate through capital investment. Six specific conditions define what must keep holding for that bet to remain intact:

  1. Annual production volume held below 16,000 units. The moat is the scarcity model. If Ferrari delivers more than 16,000 cars in any year — or if management signals that growth beyond that level is a strategic objective — the fundamental mechanism that sustains the premium is gone. That is the kill-switch condition, and it admits no ambiguity or averaging-down.

Latest reading: 13,640 units delivered in the year ending 2025-12-31, confirmed from Ferrari's Annual Report. Volume was broadly flat year on year (13,752 in the prior year), well below the 14,500-unit discipline threshold and far below the 16,000-unit exit line. Exit condition (1) not triggered.

  1. Operating profit margin before depreciation held above 35% in every fiscal year. A sustained margin below 35% would signal either that the scarcity premium has been eroded, or that the costs of the Elettrica battery-electric programme have permanently compressed the profit structure. The exit trigger is a single full fiscal year below the floor — not a brief dip.

Latest reading: The operating margin before depreciation was confirmed at 38.8% for the year ending 2025-12-31, representing 380 basis points of headroom above the exit floor and an expansion of 400 basis points over the three-year period 2022–2025. Management guidance for 2026 is at least 39%. Exit condition (2) not triggered.

  1. Operating cash flow divided by net income stays above 1.0 in every fiscal year. When a company generates consistently more cash than it reports in profit, the earnings are real. When the ratio drops below 1.0, it warrants immediate investigation for earnings management or deteriorating working capital. Ferrari has sustained this ratio above 1.25 in every year of the observable five-year record.

Latest reading: Net income EUR 1,596.9 million; operating cash flow EUR 2,349.3 million from structured filing data, giving a ratio of 1.47 times for the year ending 2025-12-31. The accruals ratio — a related measure of how much earnings are backed by cash — was negative every year from 2021 through 2025. Exit condition (3) not triggered.

  1. Average price per car (revenue divided by units delivered) holds flat or rises on the standard model lineup for two consecutive fiscal years. A price decline sustained across two years would signal that demand has softened enough to affect pricing on models that are not artificially restricted to a handful of owners — the most direct evidence that the pricing power argument is weakening.

Latest reading: Implied average selling price EUR 524,000 for the year ending 2025-12-31 (EUR 7,146 million revenue ÷ 13,640 units), up from the prior year. Pricing-led growth at flat volume. Exit condition (4) not triggered.

  1. US import tariffs on Ferrari vehicles stay below 25%, OR Ferrari's annual guidance does not embed a margin cut attributable to tariff absorption. This is a two-leg condition. A 25% tariff alone does not break the thesis if Ferrari can price through it — as it demonstrated in 2025. A margin guidance cut alone does not trigger the exit if the tariff is below 25%. Both legs must be present simultaneously.

Latest reading: The July 2026 EU-US trade agreement set the effective rate at 15% all-inclusive. Ferrari's 2026 operating margin before depreciation guidance is at least 39%, with no margin cut attributed to tariff pressure. A threat of re-escalation to 25% surfaced in May 2026 press coverage but was unconfirmed as of the analysis date. Exit condition (5) not triggered; the tariff tail risk should be monitored at each annual result.

  1. The Elettrica order book oversubscribes within 12 months of formally opening. The battery-electric Ferrari is the thesis's central test over the next several years. If the order book opens and shows slow uptake, price concessions, or delivery deferrals, it signals that the BEV transition requires pricing compromise — turning a potential margin tailwind into a headwind, and undermining exit condition (6).

Latest reading: The Elettrica was revealed at Ferrari's Capital Markets Day in October 2025 — over 1,000 horsepower, 122-kilowatt-hour battery, deliveries expected from late 2026. The commercial order book had not opened as of the analysis date. Exit condition (6) assessment clock has not started. Monitor from the point at which Ferrari formally opens the order book.

The Share Price

At $377, Ferrari sits at fair value by the earnings-yield measure. The net income as a proportion of the market capitalisation stands at 2.75%, which matches the fair-value threshold almost exactly (fair value is calibrated at 2.75%). That means the share price neither presents a discount to buy into nor a premium that triggers a trim.

The buy edge sits at $244, corresponding to a yield of 4.25% on current owner earnings. The trim edge sits at $829, corresponding to a yield of 1.25%. The current price of $377 is in the middle of the band between those two levels, with meaningful headroom on both sides.

The valuation also warrants a note of perspective from the discounted-cash-flow analysis. At a 7.3% discount rate and 9% growth, the base-case intrinsic value works out to roughly $368 per share — essentially at parity with today's price. The share price today is paying for the base case with no cushion. Under more conservative assumptions (8.3% discount rate), intrinsic value falls to around $299. The business is exceptional; the price is fair rather than cheap.

No valuation exit edge has been defined — the Rules do not call for a full position exit on price alone above the trim level; that is governed by the Standing Orders and the trim threshold.

Standing orders (actionable).

No exit-tier price order has been defined. The Rules do not call for closing the full position on price alone; the full-exit condition is tied to a written exit condition being triggered, not to the share price reaching any specific level above the trim edge.

What's Wobbling

The US tariff regime. The current tariff on Ferrari vehicles imported into the United States is 15% under the July 2026 EU-US trade agreement — below the 25% level in exit condition five. Ferrari empirically tested its pricing power at the 25% level in 2025 by applying selective surcharges of up to 10% on certain models, and delivered full-year operating margin before depreciation of 38.8%, above its own guidance floor. That is direct confirmation of pass-through capability. The wobble is a reported but unconfirmed May 2026 threat by the US side to re-escalate tariffs back to 25%. As of the analysis date, neither leg of exit condition five is in place: the current tariff is 15%, and management's 2026 guidance does not embed a margin cut from tariff pressure. This is not a trigger — it is a risk to monitor at the next annual result, particularly if the trade framework is renegotiated.

The Elettrica order book. The commercial order book for Ferrari's battery-electric car had not opened as of the analysis date. Exit condition six — the order book fails to oversubscribe within 12 months of opening — has not started its clock. The evidence here is neutral — there is nothing to confirm or challenge yet. The risk is real (BEV adoption at these price points is untested at scale), but it belongs in What Could Still Go Wrong rather than in a current wobble. Monitor from the commercial reveal, expected in the second half of 2026.

Neither wobble crosses any of the six lines in the Case for the Bet. Neither changes the hold call.

What Could Still Go Wrong

  • Elettrica order-book disappointment. If Ferrari's first battery-electric car requires price concessions, delivery deferrals, or shows slow uptake when the order book opens, it signals that BEV demand at these price points requires pricing compromise. That would turn development costs from a temporary burden into a structural margin headwind, and exit condition six would fire.
  • Production cap breach. Ferrari's scarcity model depends entirely on management discipline to keep annual deliveries below 16,000 units. The Agnelli family's controlling stake structurally resists activist pressure to "grow volumes," but the risk is governance-dependent rather than structural. A change in shareholder composition or a management decision to "unlock value" through volume expansion would destroy the moat immediately and irreversibly.
  • US tariff re-escalation with incomplete pass-through. The 25% tariff scenario was survived with a price surcharge in 2025 — but a sustained 25% rate combined with a management guidance cut in operating margin before depreciation attributable to tariff absorption would trigger exit condition five. The risk is tail-level but real while US trade policy remains unpredictable.
  • Luxury macro downturn compressing operating margin before depreciation below 35%. Revenue growth has decelerated from 19% in 2022 to 7% in 2025. A meaningful contraction in demand from ultra-high-net-worth individuals — whether driven by broader wealth effects, geopolitical disruption, or a sharp global recession — combined with the fixed costs of the Elettrica programme could push margins toward the 35% exit floor. The scarcity model provides insulation, but it is not a guarantee.
  • Multiple contraction without earnings growth. Two-thirds of the current price represents growth expectations, not current earnings power. A zero-growth scenario produces an intrinsic value around $128 per share by the earnings-power-value method. If growth stalls and the market reprices Ferrari as an automotive company rather than a luxury-goods franchise, the multiple contraction alone could deliver a loss of 50 per cent or more from today's price without any fundamental business deterioration.

The Rules

The pre-committed rules that govern what action follows from each combination of thesis condition and share price are stated here directly as conditions and consequences.

  • If the thesis is confirmed — all measurable conditions check out and no exit condition is triggered — hold without action. The moat is intact; the scarcity architecture, earnings margins at or above 38%, and operating cash flow above 1.20 times net income are all confirmed. Continue compounding.
  • If the thesis is weakened — one driver shows a deteriorating reading but no exit condition has fired — do not add to the position. Re-run a thesis validation against the specific weakening driver, most likely the operating margin before depreciation margin trend or Elettrica uptake. Monitor for one full fiscal year before reassessing position size.
  • If the thesis is broken — any single exit condition fires — exit the full position within 30 days. In particular, exit condition one (production above 16,000 units) destroys the moat irreversibly; the rule calls for immediate exit with no averaging down. Document the triggered condition.
  • If the share price falls to the buy edge ($244) and the thesis is confirmed intact — add in tranches over one to two quarters. A yield of more than 4.25% on Ferrari's owner earnings is a rare event and the rules call for acting on it decisively.
  • If the share price rises to the trim edge ($829) — trim 25 to 33 per cent of the position. At that price, the implied market capitalisation exceeds $145 billion on current owner earnings; the price is discounting decades of compounding, and disciplined position reduction preserves the ability to re-enter at a better yield.
  • If the share price rises above the exit band — exit the full position. At a yield below 1.0% on owner earnings, the market is pricing in an implausibly long runway of compounding; the rules call for closing the position and redeploying proceeds, with no re-entry within 90 days to avoid anchoring to the exit price.

Scenario Definitions

A scenario is a joint assumption about two independent variables: operating trajectory (how the holding's underlying cash flows grow relative to their long-run trend) and exit multiple (what the market pays for those cash flows at the horizon, relative to a fair-value anchor).

Scenario Operating trajectory Exit multiple
Bear Below trend Below fair value
Base At trend At fair value
Bull Above trend At or above fair value

Non-negotiable rule: In the bear case, both dimensions must be adverse simultaneously — a scenario where only the multiple moves is a re-rating scenario, not a bear.

Bear cases are comparable in structure across all holding types (company, ETF, bond), but not in magnitude. A company bear reflects idiosyncratic execution failure; an ETF bear reflects a broad market drawdown. Different mitigations, different diversification implications.
