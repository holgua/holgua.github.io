About All World

All World tracks the MSCI World Index — a float-adjusted, market-capitalisation-weighted benchmark spanning 23 developed-market countries and roughly 1,300 large- and mid-cap companies. The geographic split is approximately 70% United States and 30% other developed markets (Japan, the United Kingdom, France, Switzerland, Germany, Canada, and others). This combination gives a broader geographic footprint than a pure US large-cap index fund while remaining within developed-market credit and governance standards.

The fund is managed by iShares, the exchange-traded fund arm of BlackRock, and is structured as a Delaware statutory trust — a US-domiciled vehicle, with no K-1 tax form issued to shareholders. The annual cost is 0.24% of assets per year. Tracking difference and tracking error against the MSCI World Index are not available in the current data set (see Gaps in the Picture).

The Case for the Bet

The thesis is that over a long holding period, the basket's look-through earnings yield — the ratio of aggregate earnings to market price across all 1,300-plus constituent companies — will comfortably exceed the risk-free rate by at least a 2% equity risk premium. Holding this fund is a bet on that structural gap, not on any single quarter's price movement. Six conditions must stay intact for the bet to remain valid:

  1. The equity risk premium stays above zero for no more than one consecutive quarter at a time. The basket's earnings yield must exceed the 10-year US Treasury yield; sustained failure — defined as two complete calendar quarters of negative premium — triggers exit condition (1). A negative premium means the market offers no compensation above a government bond for bearing equity risk across 1,300 companies.

  1. The annual expense ratio stays at or below 0.35%. A rise above this ceiling with no fee response signals structural fee drift incompatible with the holding's cost discipline (iShares' comparable UCITS vehicle charges 0.20%).

What's Wobbling

The equity risk premium driver is the sole source of stress, and it is material. Sixteen consecutive negative readings since 2026-06-16 represent an unbroken string across more than four months of daily snapshots. The premium deepened from −0.437% on 2026-07-30 to −0.509% on 2026-08-10, moving in the wrong direction. The combination of a rising Treasury yield (4.696%) and a basket price-to-earnings multiple that has not compressed far enough to compensate is the mechanical source of the gap.

The gap to the trim edge is narrow — 19 basis points. A further rise in Treasury yields, or a modest upward re-rating of the basket's price-to-earnings multiple, would push the basket earnings yield below 4.0% and trigger the trim threshold. Neither of those moves requires an unusual event; either could occur at the next quarterly snapshot.

The two-quarter calendar gate for exit condition (1) does not cross into The Case for the Bet's exit territory until 2026-09-30. That date is the next hard checkpoint. If the third quarter of 2026 closes with the premium still negative, exit condition (1) fires and the action plan calls for full exit.

What Could Still Go Wrong

  • Equity risk premium confirmation. If the 10-year Treasury yield holds above the basket earnings yield through 2026-09-30, exit condition (1) triggers. A move to exit would require selling the full position and redeploying proceeds. This is the highest-probability near-term risk.
  • Multiple expansion into trim territory. If the basket's price-to-earnings multiple rises further from its current 23.9× without a corresponding increase in earnings, the earnings yield falls below 4.0%, triggering the trim threshold at a basket price of roughly $212.
  • Fund cost increase. A fee rise above 0.35% with no offsetting response would trigger exit condition (2). The comparable UCITS structure already charges 0.20%; the current 0.24% sits at the high end of the iShares global product range.
  • Single-name dominance. If a single constituent — currently NVIDIA at 5.16% — were to reach 10% of the basket through continued price appreciation, exit condition (4) would trigger even without any deterioration in underlying basket quality.
  • Bear-case return. The five-year scenario analysis shows a bear-case annualised return of −19.7%: this requires both earnings contracting (−10.8% per year, derived from the basket's cyclical sector composition) and the market de-rating from today's 23.9× trailing earnings to 14×. Both conditions must hold simultaneously. For this fund, exit fires when the share price reaches the exit edge, when a vehicle-integrity condition breaches, or when the named exit-condition trigger fires — any of three paths.

The Rules

If all drivers confirm and the basket is within the hold band, the rules call for holding — the basket is reasonably priced and the vehicle is sound.

If any driver challenges the thesis (as equity risk premium does now), the rules call for holding without adding. The prescribed action is to re-validate after the next quarter. If the equity risk premium stays negative or the median look-through return on equity of the top holdings keeps falling, the rules call for switching to the equivalent UCITS structure at a lower cost (0.20% expense ratio) or moving to cash per the position-sizing rule.

If the thesis breaks — either because two consecutive quarters of negative equity risk premium are confirmed (exit condition (1) on 2026-09-30) or because any vehicle-integrity condition breaches — the rules call for full exit. Proceeds move to risk-free instruments or to a holding that meets the value test.

If the basket becomes meaningfully cheap (earnings yield at or above 6.25%, implying a price-to-earnings multiple of approximately 16×), the rules call for adding to the position. At that level, the basket is priced at its long-run historical average multiple and offers a meaningful premium above the risk-free rate.

If the basket becomes meaningfully expensive (earnings yield at or below 4.0%, which corresponds to a price-to-earnings multiple of 25× or above), the rules call for trimming 25–33% of the position to harvest the premium.

If the basket earnings yield falls to or below 3.0% (a price-to-earnings multiple of approximately 33× or above), the rules call for full exit — the basket is fully priced against the risk-free rate and redeployment is warranted.

Scenario Definitions

A scenario is a joint assumption about two independent variables: operating trajectory (how the holding's underlying cash flows grow relative to their long-run trend) and exit multiple (what the market pays for those cash flows at the horizon, relative to a fair-value anchor).

Scenario Operating trajectory Exit multiple
Bear Below trend Below fair value
Base At trend At fair value
Bull Above trend At or above fair value

Non-negotiable rule: In the bear case, both dimensions must be adverse simultaneously — a scenario where only the multiple moves is a re-rating scenario, not a bear.

Bear cases are comparable in structure across all holding types (company, ETF, bond), but not in magnitude. A company bear reflects idiosyncratic execution failure; an ETF bear reflects a broad market drawdown. Different mitigations, different diversification implications.
