The basket earnings yield of 4.59% versus the 10-year Treasury at 4.622% means investors accepting a diversified basket of clean-energy equities are receiving less income than from a risk-free US government bond — the regime Buffett describes as "cash is a better deal." Two important caveats: (1) the benchmark is ^TNX (USD 10-year), while IQQH is EUR-denominated; substituting the Bund (approximately 2.5% as of July 2026) would produce an ERP of approximately +2.1%, which shifts the judgment from CHALLENGES to CONFIRM under the config thresholds — this USD/EUR mismatch is documented in the thesis as a known, unresolved gap; (2) the primary investment rationale for IQQH is thematic, not a value-vs-risk-free trade, so the ERP signal carries lower decision weight here than it would for SPY or URTH. Exit condition (1) — EY above 8.0% for four consecutive quarters coinciding with persistent capital losses — is not engaged; the basket yield is well below the 8.0% trap threshold.

5. Owner Perspective (Buffett Frame)

"Would Berkshire deploy cash into this basket today?"

Berkshire would not deploy fresh capital into IQQH at the current price. The basket earnings yield of 4.5935% is fractionally below the risk-free 10-year Treasury at 4.622% (ERP = −0.0285%), meaning the equity premium over risk-free is negative on the USD-benchmarked frame. Even granting the Bund-adjusted ERP of approximately +2.1%, the margin is thin for a thematic basket with meaningful single-stock concentration: the top constituent (Bloom Energy) represents 14.76% of the basket at a 1.29% ROE, and two loss-making constituents (PLUG, SEDG) are material enough to depress the weighted-mean ROE to 3.28%. Buffett's framework requires both an adequate yield premium over risk-free and a high-quality underlying business base; the basket does not meet either test cleanly on USD benchmarks. The median ROE of 14.87% tells a better story for the basket's core names but is still below typical Berkshire quality bars. The correct holding rationale for IQQH is not value-vs-risk-free but thematic conviction in the decarbonisation capital-expenditure cycle sustained by regulatory mandates (EU Green Deal, US IRA) and the structural cost advantage of renewables over a ~10-year horizon.
