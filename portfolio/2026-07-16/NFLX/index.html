Last Known Thesis (trimmed for the stub)

Netflix is held as the only at-scale global subscription streaming platform that has converted its content-spend lead into durable operating leverage — ~325M paid households across 190+ countries, FY25 revenue $45.2B at 29.5% operating margin, ROIC climbing from 15.0% (FY23) to 25.8% (FY25), and net leverage collapsing from 1.35× to 0.32× EBITDA. The central bet is that the layered monetisation stack (ad tier, paid sharing, multi-tier pricing) compounds on a largely fixed content cost base such that the operating-leverage flywheel continues turning toward management's FY26 31.5% OM guide and beyond, while content amortisation grows but lags revenue. The supporting bet is capital discipline: $9.1B FY25 buybacks against an $8B remaining authorisation, and the April 2026 decision to walk away from the $82.7B WBD bid (swallowing a $2.8B termination fee rather than overpay) — an explicit refusal of value-destructive M&A at a high-stakes moment. The biggest single risk is not direct competition but the speed of ad-revenue ramp versus content-amortisation creep: if ad revenue tops out below $4B by FY28 while content amortisation grows ~10% per year, the operating-leverage flywheel stalls and the multiple compresses sharply. Exit conditions include: (1) quarterly operating margin falls below 28% for two consecutive quarters without a one-off cause; (2) FY26 ad revenue trajectory tracks below $2.5B annualised by year-end; (3) ROIC falls below 20% for two consecutive years, signalling the moat is leaking…
