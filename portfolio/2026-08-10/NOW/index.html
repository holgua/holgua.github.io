The margin fell to 70.7% in the quarter, down from 77.5% a year earlier, driven by $153 million in additional amortisation charges in the quarter (and $194 million across the first half of the year) arising from three acquisitions completed in rapid succession over the prior seven months: Armis in April 2026, Veza in March 2026, and Moveworks in December 2025. Management has guided for further compression through the remainder of 2026. The 70% level matters because it is the floor written into the rules for this position — two consecutive quarters below it would trigger a formal exit review. At 70.7%, the margin sits approximately 68 basis points (just under three-quarters of a percentage point) above that floor.

The underlying business metrics all held. Subscription revenues grew 24.6% in the quarter, the net customer renewal rate stayed at 98% for the seventh consecutive year, and the contracted revenue backlog grew 21% to $29 billion. Every core growth and retention indicator stayed comfortably above its written threshold.

Two additional developments inform the verdict at today's price. The Department of Justice investigation into the company's government sales practices — centred on the subsequent employment of a former army technology officer — continues without resolution. The single largest government reseller through whom the company routes its government contracts now accounts for 13% of total revenues, up from 11% a year earlier, concentrating the potential impact if that investigation were to escalate.

About ServiceNow

ServiceNow sells annual subscriptions to a cloud software platform that automates the flow of work inside large organisations. The platform began in information-technology departments — handling requests, problems, and incidents across corporate computer networks — and has expanded over the past decade to take on workflows in human resources, finance, legal, procurement, and cybersecurity. A customer that starts with one module typically purchases others over time: the platform's shared data model and single interface make expansion cheaper than deploying a separate specialist tool, and the accumulated customisations and integrations become harder to replicate elsewhere with each passing year.

Revenue is almost entirely subscription-based, billed on contracts that typically run three years. Professional services — installing and configuring the platform for new customers — account for a small and declining share. The business does not sell advertising, does not manufacture hardware, and holds no inventory. In government markets, a portion of revenue flows through a small number of large channel partners rather than directly to end customers.

The Case for the Bet

The bet on ServiceNow rests on seven conditions, each verifiable in the company's periodic filings:

  1. Subscription revenue growing faster than 15% a year. The thesis requires 18–20% annual growth sustained through the platform's upsell and multi-pillar cross-sell; if growth falls below 15% for two consecutive quarters without a clear macroeconomic cause, the AI commoditisation scenario is confirmed and the growth case breaks.

Latest reading: 24.6% year-on-year growth in the three months ending in June 2026 ($3,877 million versus $3,113 million in the same quarter of 2025). Growth was driven by increased purchases from both new and existing customers, well above the 15% threshold.

  1. Net customer renewal rate at or above 97%. Seven years at 98% is the empirical proof of the switching-cost moat; any reading below 97% in a reported period — even one — signals that customers are finding it easier to leave than they once did, and the thesis's central claim is in doubt.

Latest reading: 98% for both the three and six months ending in June 2026. Unchanged from the comparable 2025 periods, maintaining the seven-year consecutive streak.

  1. Contracted near-term backlog growing faster than 18% a year. The portion of committed revenue expected to be recognised within 12 months is the clearest forward indicator of near-term subscription growth; two consecutive quarters below 18% would mean the pipeline is shrinking in ways the revenue line has not yet reflected.

Latest reading: 21% year-on-year growth to approximately $13.3 billion as of June 2026 (46% of a total backlog of $29 billion). Both the near-term and total backlog grew 21%.

  1. Now Assist annual contract value reaching $2.5 billion by the fourth quarter of 2026. Now Assist is the AI tool built on top of the core platform and priced separately; $2.5 billion in contracted value by the end of 2026 would confirm that AI is a genuine revenue layer rather than a feature bundled into existing contracts.

Latest reading: Not separately disclosed in the quarterly filing for the three months ending in June 2026. Management commentary outside the audited document indicated annual contract value crossed $1 billion in the same period. The $2.5 billion target cannot be verified from the publicly available filing alone.

  1. Reported gross profit margin staying above 70%. The total GAAP gross profit margin has compressed from 77.5% a year ago to 70.7% today, driven by amortisation of intangible assets acquired in three recent deals. Two consecutive quarters with the margin below 70% would indicate that acquisition costs are outpacing the efficiency gains from the enlarged platform.

Latest reading: 70.7% in the three months ending in June 2026, down from 77.5% in the same period of 2025. Approximately 68 basis points above the 70% floor. Management has guided for continued compression through 2026; the next quarterly filing will be the decisive test.

  1. No debarment notice or formal exclusion proceeding from the Department of Justice. A debarment order targeting the company or its primary government reseller would directly threaten the roughly 13% of total revenues flowing through the government channel and undermine the federal AI governance contract signed in September 2025.

Latest reading: As of the filing date (2026-07-23), no debarment proceedings had been filed. The investigation is ongoing and the company continues cooperating with document requests. The government reseller's share of total revenues rose to 13% from 11% a year earlier.

  1. Non-standard operating margin (stripping out share-based compensation and amortisation) staying above 28%. This non-standard metric tracks whether the underlying software economics are holding up while acquisition and compensation costs are temporarily set aside; two consecutive quarters below 28% would indicate those costs are consuming the business's economics faster than new revenues can replace them.

Latest reading: 29.4% in the three months ending in June 2026; 30.6% in the first half of 2026. Both above the 28% threshold, though compressing year-on-year as share-based compensation rose 31% and acquisition-related costs added further drag.

The Share Price

At $125, the share is expensive by the measure used here. That measure is an earnings yield calculated net of share-based compensation: the cash the business generates for shareholders each year after subtracting the dilutive compensation paid in shares, expressed as a percentage of the company's total market value. At $125, that yield stands at approximately 2.0%. The rules say a yield above 5.37% (a price near $47) signals an attractive entry; below 2.37% (a price above approximately $107) is the signal to reduce. At 2.0%, the share is already below the trim threshold — it has been since the current price was established. The fair-value estimate implied by the same method works out to $66: the price at which the business would yield its long-run expected rate of return. At $125, the stock trades at roughly 90% above that estimate.

The Standing Orders

Two price alerts are in place: one at $66, the fair-value estimate, and a second at $47 — the price representing a 25% cushion below fair value, which marks the boundary for adding to the position.

Three standing orders govern what happens at each price level.

If the price falls to $47, the signal is to add to the position in stages across one to two quarters — but only after confirming that all seven conditions above remain unbroken. At $47, that earnings yield would cross 5.37%, the first genuinely attractive entry point given the quality of the underlying business.

If the price rises above $107, the signal is to trim 25–33% of the position: not a full exit, but a reduction to harvest the premium while retaining the core. At $107, the yield drops below 2.37%, meaning the market is paying a price that leaves almost no margin for any deterioration in the business.

If the price reaches $291, the signal is to exit the position entirely. At that level, the market would be pricing in approximately seven years of base-case earnings growth delivered instantly, leaving no return for any buyer entering at that point.

What's Wobbling

Gross margin is approaching the exit threshold. The reported total GAAP gross profit margin fell from 77.5% a year ago to 70.7% in the three months ending in June 2026. The compression is entirely driven by amortisation of intangible assets acquired across three deals closed between December 2025 and April 2026; that amortisation will run through the income statement for five to six years at roughly $750 million a year. The 70% floor in exit condition five has not been crossed — the count of consecutive quarters below it has not started — but management has guided for further decline through the rest of 2026. The fourth-quarter filing will be the decisive test: one quarter below 70% would start the clock; a second would trigger the exit review. The margin decline does not cross any written condition today, but it narrows the headroom to almost nothing.

The government-channel concentration has increased. The Department of Justice investigation into the company's government sales practices (centred on the employment path of a former army technology officer) remains unresolved, as it has since mid-2024. No debarment proceedings have been filed, exit condition six has not been crossed, and the company has continued winning new government contracts. What has changed is the channel concentration: the single government reseller through whom a large share of government revenue flows now accounts for 13% of total revenues, up from 11% when the position was sized. The absolute probability of exit condition six firing has not changed, but the dollar impact would be roughly 18% larger than it was a year ago, because more revenue now runs through the channel most at risk.

What Could Still Go Wrong

  • AI tools built into Microsoft's corporate productivity suite and Salesforce's customer-management platform succeed in bundling basic workflow automation into licences that large customers already pay for, making ServiceNow's core service redundant for a share of the customer base and pushing the annual renewal rate below 97%.
  • The Department of Justice investigation results in a debarment notice or formal exclusion proceeding against the company or its primary government reseller, cutting an estimated 13% of total revenues and dismantling the federal AI governance contract that the thesis treats as a structural moat for the next two to four years.
  • The three acquisitions completed in rapid succession between December 2025 and April 2026 prove harder to integrate simultaneously than expected: key engineers depart, development timelines slip, and the $9.8 billion of goodwill on the balance sheet is partially written down, signalling that the acquisition prices reflected expectations that the business cannot deliver.
  • Now Assist fails to graduate from a free feature to a separately priced product at meaningful scale, and subscription growth decelerates toward the low-teens before the AI pricing layer has a chance to replace the revenue contribution that organic platform expansion once provided.

Gaps in the Picture

The owner-earnings figure used to compute the yield and fair-value estimate is calculated by subtracting total capital expenditure from operating cash flow, then subtracting share-based compensation. The filing does not separately disclose how much of the capital expenditure is maintenance spending (replacing existing infrastructure) versus growth spending (building new capacity). Using total capital expenditure is the conservative approach; the true figure available to shareholders could be modestly higher if growth investment is being treated as maintenance. This does not alter the directional conclusion — the stock is expensive by this measure — but it means the precise fair-value estimate carries a margin of uncertainty.

Now Assist annual contract value — the primary test for whether the AI pricing tier is commercially viable at scale — is not disclosed as a separate audited line in the quarterly filing. Exit condition four ($2.5 billion by the fourth quarter of 2026) depends on management commentary outside the audited document. The condition cannot be verified from publicly available filing data alone until a future annual filing.

The Rules

If all seven conditions are tracking and the share price sits between $47 and $107, hold the position at current size and wait for a price near $47–66 before adding.

If one condition is deteriorating but has not yet crossed its written threshold — the renewal rate sliding toward but not below 97%, the gross margin compressing toward but not sustaining below 70% — hold at reduced conviction and give the business two further quarterly filings to determine whether the deterioration is reversing before acting.

If any one of the seven written conditions fires, exit the full position within 30 days. At a price 90% above the base-case fair-value estimate, there is no cushion to ride out a broken thesis.

If the price falls to approximately $47 — where that earnings yield crosses 5.37% — and all seven conditions remain unbroken, add to the position in tranches across one to two quarters.

If the price rises above approximately $107 — where that earnings yield drops below 2.37% — trim 25–33% of the position to capture the premium while retaining the core holding.

If the price reaches $291 — where that earnings yield drops to approximately 0.34% — exit the full position; the market is pricing in roughly seven years of base-case earnings compounding delivered instantly, leaving no return for any buyer entering at that point.

Scenario Definitions

A scenario is a joint assumption about two independent variables: operating trajectory (how the holding's underlying cash flows grow relative to their long-run trend) and exit multiple (what the market pays for those cash flows at the horizon, relative to a fair-value anchor).

Scenario Operating trajectory Exit multiple
Bear Below trend Below fair value
Base At trend At fair value
Bull Above trend At or above fair value

Non-negotiable rule: In the bear case, both dimensions must be adverse simultaneously — a scenario where only the multiple moves is a re-rating scenario, not a bear.

Bear cases are comparable in structure across all holding types (company, ETF, bond), but not in magnitude. A company bear reflects idiosyncratic execution failure; an ETF bear reflects a broad market drawdown. Different mitigations, different diversification implications.
