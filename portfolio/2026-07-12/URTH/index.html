About the Fund

The iShares MSCI World ETF (ticker: URTH) tracks the MSCI World Index — a float-adjusted, market-capitalisation-weighted index covering the largest and mid-sized listed companies across 23 developed countries. In practice that means roughly 1,300 holdings weighted by size, with the United States accounting for around 70% of the basket and the remaining 30% spread across Japan, the United Kingdom, France, Switzerland, Germany, Canada, and a dozen other developed markets. The fund holds no exposure to emerging markets. When the basket's biggest names grow faster than the rest, they gradually take up a larger share; when they shrink, they shrink back. No active judgement is involved — the index rebalances mechanically, twice a year for full reconstitutions and twice more for interim reviews.

At an annual cost of 0.24%, the fund is competitively priced for a global large-cap blend vehicle, though it runs 4 basis points above the functionally equivalent IWDA UCITS structure (0.20%). A raise above 0.35% with no fee response is an explicit exit condition. Tracking difference and tracking error for the most recent year are not available in this snapshot; they should be verified from the iShares factsheet to confirm how precisely the fund replicates the index. The fund is a US-domiciled Delaware statutory trust and does not issue partnership tax forms (K-1s). Capital-gain distribution history is unverified — in-kind redemption, the standard ETF mechanism, often results in zero distributions — but this should be confirmed from the iShares annual report. Annual cost: 0.24% expense ratio.

The Case for the Bet

The bet is not that this basket is always worth buying. The bet is that over the holding period, the basket's underlying look-through earnings yield will exceed the risk-free rate plus a reasonable equity risk premium of at least 2 percentage points, and that the vehicle delivering those earnings will remain sound. Six conditions must keep holding for that bet to work:

  1. The equity risk premium stays positive across consecutive calendar quarters. The basket's earnings yield (what look-through profits amount to as a fraction of today's price) must exceed the 10-year US Treasury yield by at least 2 percentage points to confirm the bet. A negative premium for two consecutive calendar quarters triggers the first exit.

Latest reading: 2026-07-12. The basket earnings yield of 4.09% minus the 10-year Treasury at 4.57% gives a premium of −0.48%. This is negative and has been negative for all 12 readings on record, from 2026-06-16 to 2026-07-12. Exit condition (1) requires two consecutive calendar-quarter readings — not yet formally confirmed.

  1. The expense ratio stays at or below 0.35%. The annual management fee is the one structural cost a passive holder cannot avoid. A raise above 0.35% with no fee response signals that the fund has drifted above what the cheaper UCITS structure requires.

Latest reading: 2026-07-12. Expense ratio 0.24%, 11 basis points below the 0.35% ceiling. Exit condition (2) not triggered.

  1. Assets under management stay above $2 billion. Below roughly $2 billion in a global-blend exchange-traded fund, bid-ask spreads typically widen to a point that erodes returns on entry and exit.

Latest reading: 2026-07-12. Assets under management $8.06 billion — approximately four times the $2 billion floor. Exit condition (3) not triggered.

  1. The largest single holding stays below 10% of the basket. At 10% in a global cap-weighted index, a single name represents regime-change concentration rather than normal market drift.

Latest reading: 2026-07-12. Largest holding: NVIDIA at 5.17%, 4.83 percentage points below the 10% ceiling. Exit condition (4) not triggered.

  1. The median return-on-equity of the top ten holdings stays above 15%. Return on equity (the profit generated per unit of shareholders' capital) measures whether the basket's biggest companies are genuine compounders. Below 15%, the basket has lost its quality character.

What Could Still Go Wrong

  • The basket earnings yield falls further as the share price rises toward $209, triggering the trim order before the equity risk premium recovers. The trim would remove 25 to 33% of the position.
  • The equity risk premium remains negative through the end of two consecutive calendar quarters (the September and December 2026 quarter-ends, for example), formally firing exit condition (1). The action plan then calls for swapping to the IWDA UCITS structure (at 0.20% rather than 0.24%) or moving to cash.
  • A sharp multiple compression in global equities — the five-year bear case in the value analysis runs at −6.2% a year under a reversion to 14× earnings — inflicts significant mark-to-market losses without any of the named exit conditions firing first.
  • BlackRock raises the URTH expense ratio above 0.35% without a corresponding fee response, triggering exit condition (2).
  • NVIDIA or another top holding runs above 10% of the basket, signalling the kind of mega-cap concentration that exit condition (4) is designed to catch.

(For this fund, exit fires when the share price reaches the exit edge, when a vehicle-integrity condition breaches, or when the named exit-condition trigger fires — any of three paths.)

The Rules

The pre-committed rules governing action for each outcome are:

  • If the thesis is confirmed (all conditions hold, price within band): hold — basket is reasonably priced and the vehicle is sound.
  • If the thesis is weakened (any condition is under pressure but no exit trigger has fired): do not add. Re-validate after the next quarter. If the equity risk premium stays negative or the median look-through return on equity keeps falling, swap to the IWDA UCITS structure (0.20% expense ratio) or move to cash per the position-sizing rule.
  • If the thesis is broken (vehicle integrity breached or an exit condition formally triggered): exit. Move proceeds to the risk-free rate or to a holding that meets the value test.
  • If the share price falls to or below $134 (the buy edge — basket earnings yield at 6.25%): add to the position. The basket is meaningfully cheap relative to the risk-free rate.
  • If the share price rises to or above $209 (the trim edge — basket earnings yield at 4.00%): trim 25–33%. The basket is meaningfully expensive relative to the risk-free rate; harvest the premium.
  • If the share price rises to or above $279 (the exit edge — basket earnings yield at 3.00%): exit. Basket earnings yield at or below 3.0% means it is fully priced against the risk-free rate. Redeploy proceeds to the risk-free rate or to a holding that meets the value test.
