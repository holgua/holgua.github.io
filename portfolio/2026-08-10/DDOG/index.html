About Datadog

Datadog makes its money by watching what happens inside other companies' computer systems. Every time a cloud application runs — a website serves a page, a payment processes, a data pipeline executes — it generates enormous volumes of operational data: metrics measuring performance, logs recording events, traces tracking the path of individual requests through dozens of interconnected services. Datadog collects all of that, correlates it in real time across a unified platform, and presents it to the engineering and operations teams responsible for keeping the systems running. It charges for this on a consumption basis: the more infrastructure a customer runs through Datadog's platform, the more they pay.

The business model compounds in a distinctive way. Customers typically start with one or two monitoring modules — infrastructure metrics, perhaps application tracing — and expand organically as their teams find more uses for the platform. As of mid-2026, 85% of customers use two or more products, 58% use four or more, and 13% use ten or more, up from 7% a year earlier. Each module a customer adds deepens the cost and effort of switching to a competitor: moving away from Datadog requires re-instrumenting servers, rewriting dashboards, retraining staff, and rebuilding alert policies — a project that typically takes six to eighteen months of parallel operation. That friction is the engine of a retention rate that has held above 120% for four consecutive years, meaning the existing customer base grows its spending by more than 20% a year even before a single new customer is added.

The Case for the Bet

The core bet is that Datadog's instrumentation agents are deeply enough embedded in its customers' infrastructure that the company can compound revenue from its installed base for many years, while the switching cost protects that base from hyperscaler competition. Six specific conditions must hold for that bet to remain viable:

  1. The rate at which existing customers expand their spending stays above 112%. The dollar-based net revenue retention rate — the share of revenue from existing customers retained and expanded year-on-year — is the single most important signal of moat health. Below 112% for two consecutive quarters would indicate that the land-and-expand flywheel has structurally stalled, most likely from cloud-native tools substituting Datadog in single-cloud deployments or from AI-native customers optimising telemetry spend downward.

Latest reading: "in the low-120%s" as of 2026-06-30, up from approximately 120% a year earlier. Above the 115% watch level and well above the 112% floor. A forward risk has been disclosed: the largest customer began reducing usage at the start of the third quarter of 2026. The next filing will be the first data point on whether that is idiosyncratic or systemic.

  1. Gross margin stays above 77%. Datadog delivers its platform via third-party cloud infrastructure. If the cost of that infrastructure rises faster than Datadog can pass it through to customers via usage pricing, the unit economics of each customer relationship erode. A gross margin below 77% for two consecutive quarters would indicate that cost absorption is structurally impairing the business model.

Latest reading: 79% in both the three months and the six months ending 2026-06-30, down one percentage point year-on-year from 80%, driven by increased spending with the company's primary cloud infrastructure provider. Two points above the exit-condition floor, but the direction warrants attention — the first notable year-on-year compression in the gross margin in several quarters.

  1. Stock-based compensation stays below 25% of revenue. Datadog pays a large share of its employee costs in newly issued shares rather than cash. This is common in growth-stage software companies and does not show up as a cash outflow, but it dilutes existing shareholders by expanding the share count. When stock-based compensation runs above 25% of revenue with no credible plan to normalise, the gap between the cash profit reported and the true per-share value created becomes too wide to model reliably.

Latest reading: 20.3% in the three months ending 2026-06-30, and 20.4% in the six months ending 2026-06-30 — below both the 25% exit threshold and the 21.9% watch level. The trend is stable: absolute stock-based compensation is rising with headcount, but revenue growth is keeping the ratio in check.

  1. Revenue growth stays above 20%. A deceleration below 20% year-on-year for two consecutive quarters would indicate that the growth engine has shifted structurally — either from competitive displacement in the mid-market or from a macro environment that has caused enterprises to defer cloud spending. At current valuation levels, a sustained deceleration of this magnitude would make the implied return from holding the stock deeply negative even in an optimistic scenario.

Latest reading: 36% year-on-year in the three months ending 2026-06-30; 34% in the six months ending 2026-06-30. Comfortably above the 20% floor. Management's guidance implies full-year 2026 revenue of $4.45–$4.47 billion, representing approximately 30% growth year-on-year — still well above the threshold, though the largest-customer disclosure introduces uncertainty for the second half.

  1. No single acquisition above $2 billion at premium valuations. Datadog has historically made disciplined tuck-in acquisitions — small bolt-ons in the $10–$220 million range that add product capability without integration risk. A single acquisition above $2 billion at a high revenue multiple would introduce execution complexity inconsistent with the current earnings base and would divert management attention from the core platform expansion.

Latest reading: Three tuck-in acquisitions completed in the six months ending 2026-06-30, totalling $191.5 million. No large acquisition has been announced or disclosed. The pattern remains consistent with the thesis.

  1. No material regulatory enforcement action related to cross-border data transfers or AI model training. Datadog's platform processes operational telemetry data from customer infrastructure across borders and is beginning to incorporate AI model training components. A material fine under Europe's privacy regulation or a compliance shutdown affecting more than 5% of revenue under the European Union's artificial intelligence rules — or equivalent enforcement in Asia-Pacific data-sovereignty law — would materially impair the addressable market in regulated sectors.

Latest reading: No enforcement action or material fine disclosed as of 2026-06-30. The filing added new language noting that the European Union's artificial intelligence rules came into effect in August 2024, carry significant monetary fines, and apply beyond European borders. This is a new risk-factor addition, not a signal of any pending proceeding. The threshold for the exit condition has not been approached.

The Share Price

At $261, the share price sits above the trim line — the yield on the company's owner earnings (free cash flow less the stock-based compensation that dilutes shareholders) is 0.27%, against a fair-value yield of 2.5% and a trim threshold of 1.5%. Put plainly: the market is willing to accept a return well below what the business's underlying cash generation can support at a neutral price, because it is pricing in many more years of compounding before growth normalises.

The implied prices at each band give a sense of the distance. The trim line corresponds to a share price of approximately $46 (the level at which a 1.5% earnings yield would be reached). The fair-value midpoint corresponds to approximately $28. Both are well below today's $261 — the current price is nearly ten times the earnings-based fair value. This is not a close call; the stock is in full avoid territory on a yield basis, and the Standing Orders reflect that.

Two drift warnings from the valuation model deserve a mention here, as they affect how to interpret the fair-value figure. The yield-based fair value (2.5% target, implying approximately $28 per share) diverges substantially from the earnings yield currently being produced by the business (0.27%). A separate scenario analysis using peer and historical multiples arrives at a central estimate of approximately $132 — a very different number from $28. Both figures reflect the genuine difficulty of anchoring fair value for a business growing at 30%+ per year. The divergence is documented in Gaps in the Picture and has no effect on the framework verdict, which is driven by the yield-based position.

What's Wobbling

Two wobbles are present, and neither crosses a written exit condition as of the filing dated 2026-08-06.

The largest customer is reducing usage. Management disclosed explicitly that the company's largest customer — understood to be a large AI workload operator — began reducing usage at the start of the third quarter of 2026. This customer is part of the AI-native cohort that contributed high single-digit percentage points to year-on-year revenue growth in the quarter ending 2026-06-30. The financial magnitude of the reduction has not been quantified in dollar terms. This wobble does not trigger exit condition (1) in the Case for the Bet — that condition requires the trailing twelve-month retention rate to fall below 112% for two consecutive quarters, and the current reading is in the low-120% range. The risk is that it becomes systemic rather than idiosyncratic: if multiple AI-native customers begin to optimise usage simultaneously, the retention rate could decline faster than the quarterly reporting cadence reveals. The next quarterly filing is the first evidence point.

Gross margin compressed by one percentage point. In the three months ending 2026-06-30, gross margin was 79%, down from 80% in the same period a year earlier. The driver is higher spending with the company's primary cloud infrastructure provider — costs that Datadog internalises to serve growing AI and GPU workloads. This does not trigger exit condition (2) in the Case for the Bet — that condition requires a sustained fall below 77%. But it is the first notable year-on-year compression in the gross margin in several quarters, and if the infrastructure cost trajectory continues upward while revenue growth decelerates, the combination could erode the cash-generation profile that underpins the thesis.

What Could Still Go Wrong

  • The largest-customer headwind spreads. If the usage reduction that began at the start of the third quarter of 2026 is not idiosyncratic to one customer but reflects a broader pattern of AI-native companies optimising telemetry spend, the retention rate could fall toward the 115% watch level within two quarters and approach the 112% exit floor within four. At current valuation, any meaningful deceleration in retention destroys a large portion of the implied return.
  • Hyperscaler native tools win the mid-market. AWS CloudWatch, Azure Monitor, and Google Cloud Operations are embedded in enterprise discount programmes at near-zero incremental cost. For companies running on a single cloud, the economics of choosing a third-party monitoring platform are weakening. If mid-market customers begin migrating in volume, new-customer growth slows before the enterprise base can compensate.
  • Stock-based compensation fails to normalise. In the year ending 2026-01-31, the company paid $750.7 million in newly issued shares as employee compensation — against free cash flow of $1,000.6 million. The gap between the cash profit and the shareholder-dilution-adjusted profit is wide. If headcount continues growing and revenue growth decelerates, the ratio of stock-based compensation to revenue could rise above the 25% exit threshold without a credible management plan to reverse it.
  • A large acquisition disrupts the model. Datadog's tuck-in acquisition track record is strong, but growth pressure could tempt management toward a transformative deal. A single acquisition above $2 billion at a high multiple would introduce integration complexity, divert engineering resources from the core platform, and impair the free cash flow trajectory that the valuation implicitly assumes will compound.
  • Regulatory action on data or AI. European data-transfer rules and the artificial intelligence regulation framework create enforcement risk for a platform that processes cross-border operational telemetry and incorporates machine-learning models into its products. A material fine or compliance shutdown would reduce the addressable market in regulated sectors and could trigger exit condition (6).

Gaps in the Picture

Two fair-value anchors coexist and diverge materially. The owner-earnings yield method used in the valuation framework implies a fair value of approximately $28 per share (at a 2.5% yield target on trailing free cash flow less stock-based compensation). A separate discounted cash flow and peer-multiple analysis produces a central estimate of approximately $132. The difference arises because the yield method applies a static threshold to current cash generation without explicit credit for future growth, while the scenario analysis models the revenue trajectory forward. Both figures are internally consistent with their own assumptions; the divergence reflects genuine uncertainty about the appropriate method for valuing a business at this growth rate. A future update to the valuation methodology will resolve which anchor is canonical. Until then, both numbers appear in the report and neither should be read as more authoritative than the other.

The earnings yield currently produced by the business (0.27%) also diverges substantially from the yield-based fair-value target (2.5%) — a gap of 89%. This drift warning does not change any framework verdict but signals that the fair-value calibration was set at a different point in the business's development and may need revisiting.

The Rules

The following rules govern what action follows from each combination of thesis state and share-price position. They are pre-committed — set when calm — and apply automatically when the corresponding condition is met.

If the thesis is confirmed and all conditions are working: hold any existing position and continue monitoring. No new entry at today's price; a yield of 0.27% on owner earnings is already below the 1.5% level at which the trim instruction fires. Revisit the buy decision only if the free cash flow yield rises to or above the 1.5% level (approximately $200 per share at current cash generation) with the thesis intact.

If the thesis is weakening but not broken: do not initiate a new position. If the retention rate declines toward 115% or revenue growth falls below 20%, reassess within one earnings cycle before any sizing decision. A wobbling-but-intact thesis at elevated valuation has a strictly negative risk-reward for new money.

If the thesis breaks: do not enter. If holding, exit the full position within 30 days. The broken condition fires when the retention rate falls to 112% or below for two consecutive quarters, gross margin falls below 77% for two consecutive quarters, or stock-based compensation rises above 25% of revenue with no credible normalisation path. No averaging down once a condition has fired.

If the share price falls to or below the buy edge of $17: the rules call for initiating a position in two tranches over one quarter, contingent on the retention rate being at or above 115% and the free cash flow margin at or above 25% — both confirming the moat is intact before committing capital. The buy entry corresponds to a 4% owner-earnings yield, the value-investor margin-of-safety zone.

If the share price rises to or above the trim edge of $46: do not add. If holding, trim 25–33% of the position. At today's price of $261, the stock is already well above this level; this instruction is most relevant for any holder who entered at a significantly lower price.

If the share price rises to or above any exit-tier level: no exit-tier rule has been authored for this holding. The trim instruction is the operative upper-band standing order; a full exit at a specific price target has not been pre-committed.

Scenario Definitions

A scenario is a joint assumption about two independent variables: operating trajectory (how the holding's underlying cash flows grow relative to their long-run trend) and exit multiple (what the market pays for those cash flows at the horizon, relative to a fair-value anchor).

Scenario Operating trajectory Exit multiple
Bear Below trend Below fair value
Base At trend At fair value
Bull Above trend At or above fair value

Non-negotiable rule: In the bear case, both dimensions must be adverse simultaneously — a scenario where only the multiple moves is a re-rating scenario, not a bear.

Bear cases are comparable in structure across all holding types (company, ETF, bond), but not in magnitude. A company bear reflects idiosyncratic execution failure; an ETF bear reflects a broad market drawdown. Different mitigations, different diversification implications.
