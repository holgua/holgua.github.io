About Clean Energy

The iShares Global Clean Energy Transition UCITS ETF tracks the S&P Global Clean Energy Transition Index, a basket of roughly 100 companies across solar, wind, grid infrastructure, electrification hardware, and clean-energy enabling technology. The mandate is explicitly thematic — the index is not built to be diversified across sectors, and it is not a proxy for the broad market. Three sectors — Technology, Utilities, and Industrials — together account for about 98.5% of the portfolio. That concentration is intentional: the index is designed to capture the companies most exposed to the global shift in capital expenditure toward low-carbon energy systems, so sector concentration comes with the territory.

The fund is sponsored by BlackRock (iShares) and domiciled in Ireland under the UCITS structure, meaning it is regulated by the Irish central bank and subject to EU investor-protection rules including daily liquidity and segregated assets. It is listed on the Frankfurt Stock Exchange (Xetra), trades in euros, and is registered as an OeKB Meldefonds for Austrian tax purposes. Annual cost: 0.65% of assets per year (the total expense ratio). No tracking difference or tracking error figures are available for this cycle; the fund's historical replication fidelity cannot be formally assessed until those figures are pulled from the iShares factsheet.

The Case for the Bet

The position rests on a structural thesis — that the global capital-expenditure cycle is being redirected toward decarbonisation over a ten-year horizon — and on a set of specific conditions that must hold for the vehicle to remain worth owning:

  1. Basket earnings yield stays above 4.0% (the trim threshold). The fund's earnings yield is the aggregate share of earnings the basket delivers per euro of market price. Below 4.0%, the basket is expensive enough relative to the risk-free rate that the rules call for trimming. Above 6.25%, it is cheap enough to add to. Between those levels, the position is within the hold band.

Latest reading: 4.59% as of 2026-07-19 (from iShares factsheet trailing multiple of 21.77, dated 2026-05-31). The basket sits inside the hold band, 59 basis points above the trim threshold and 41 basis points above the fair-value yield of 5.0%. No trim or exit signal.

  1. Equity risk premium remains positive (basket earnings yield exceeds the 10-year Treasury yield). The premium is the spread between what the basket earns per unit of price and what a risk-free government bond pays. A negative spread for any sustained period means equity holders are no longer compensated for equity risk, which is a structural signal, not merely a valuation observation.

Latest reading: +0.05% as of 2026-07-19 (basket earnings yield 4.59% minus US 10-year Treasury 4.54%). Positive but thin — 195 basis points below the 2.0% confirmation threshold. Monitoring flag; not an exit trigger. The 10-entry history shows all readings positive; exit condition (1)'s persistence requirement is not engaged.

  1. Median return on equity of the top-ten constituents stays above 10%. The basket's quality screen uses the median return on equity (the annual profit a company earns relative to the capital shareholders have contributed) rather than the weighted average, because two structurally loss-making names in the top ten would otherwise distort the average. The custom threshold of 10% is deliberately lower than the broad-market default, reflecting the capital-intensive economics of utilities and clean-energy equipment makers.

Latest reading: 14.87% median as of 2026-07-12. The confirmation threshold of 10% is cleared by 4.87 percentage points. The weighted mean of 3.34% is depressed by Plug Power (−128.8%) and SolarEdge (−72.5%), but this is a persistent basket feature rather than a new development.

  1. Annual cost (total expense ratio) stays below 0.80%. This is a hard vehicle-integrity ceiling. If the expense ratio breaches 0.80%, the cost drag makes the basket structurally uncompetitive against lower-cost alternatives, and the rules call for an exit regardless of the thematic thesis.

Latest reading: approximately $4.17 billion as of 2026-07-19, roughly 21 times the floor. Closure risk negligible.

What's Wobbling

The equity risk premium is the one signal in an otherwise clean picture. The basket's earnings yield of 4.59% exceeds the 10-year US Treasury of 4.54% by just 0.05 percentage points — five basis points. That is positive, which means exit condition (1)'s inverted-yield leg has not fired, but the margin is thin enough that a modest Treasury yield decline or basket price rise would push it to zero.

This does not cross any of the lines in The Case for the Bet. The confirmation threshold for the equity-risk-premium driver requires a spread of at least 2.0 percentage points; the current reading of 0.05% falls between the confirmation floor and the zero line, classifying the driver as mixed rather than challenged. The rules do not call for any action at a mixed reading.

One calibration note adds texture: the risk-free rate used in the calculation is the US 10-year Treasury yield, not a euro-area equivalent. If the German 10-year Bund yield (approximately 2.5% as of mid-2026) were used instead, the equity risk premium would widen to roughly 2.1 percentage points — clearing the confirmation threshold. Whether the US or euro-area rate is the right benchmark for a euro-denominated fund is an unresolved calibration question noted in Gaps in the Picture.

The other structural feature worth naming — the gap between the median and weighted-mean return on equity in the top ten — is a watching brief, not a wobble. At 14.87% median versus 3.34% weighted mean, the 11-point gap is driven by two persistently distressed names (Plug Power and SolarEdge) that have been basket features for multiple quarters. The median comfortably clears the 10% confirmation threshold; the weighted mean has not crossed the 6% floor that would register as a challenge.

What Could Still Go Wrong

  • Earnings yield trap. If the basket's earnings yield climbs above 8% while the share price is falling — meaning constituent fundamentals are deteriorating, not that the basket is cheap — exit condition (1) fires. At a current earnings yield of 4.59%, this requires a doubling of the yield from here, which implies either a roughly 40% fall in the trailing earnings multiple or a severe basket-wide earnings recession. Remote today, but the condition is stated precisely so it can be monitored each quarter.
  • Vehicle-integrity breach. Four separate triggers: annual cost rising above 0.80%, fund assets falling below €200 million, any single name exceeding 20% of the basket, or a BlackRock wind-down notice. The first three all have meaningful headroom today; the fourth requires external monitoring of iShares product-page announcements.
  • Index methodology change. The S&P Global Clean Energy Transition Index is maintained by S&P Dow Jones Indices, not BlackRock. A material change to the index's inclusion criteria, sector scope, or weighting scheme — exit condition (3) — would change the economic exposure the position was designed to own. This is not detectable by the automated pipeline; it requires monitoring S&P's index governance communications.
  • Policy reversal. A legislated repeal of the US Inflation Reduction Act's clean-energy tax credits or the EU Green Deal's binding targets — exit condition (4) — would remove the regulatory tailwind underwriting the capital-expenditure cycle thesis. A temporary policy pause does not trigger this condition; it requires a durable, legislated rollback.
  • Bear scenario. If the basket's constituent earnings contract by roughly 17% (reflecting the sector mix's cyclical and concentration characteristics) and the market applies a 15-times earnings multiple at the exit horizon — both dimensions adverse simultaneously — the five-year annualised return works out to about −23.8% a year. The wide spread between bear and bull (−23.8% to +17.4%) reflects thematic concentration risk and argues for sizing the position in accordance with structural conviction, not solely on the base-case return. (For this fund, exit fires when the share price reaches the exit edge, when a vehicle-integrity condition breaches, or when the named exit-condition trigger fires — any of three paths.)

The Rules

If the thesis is confirmed (all drivers working, vehicle intact): hold. The thematic thesis is intact and the vehicle is sound. Do not add solely because the earnings yield is elevated; add only if there is active evidence that the decarbonisation capital-expenditure case is strengthening.

If the thesis is weakened (one or more drivers challenged but no exit condition triggered): do not add. Re-validate after the next quarter and after any material policy development affecting the US Inflation Reduction Act or the EU Green Deal. If exit conditions (3) through (5) are approaching, accelerate the review timeline.

If the thesis is broken (vehicle-integrity breach or a written exit condition fires): exit the full position. Move proceeds to a risk-free instrument or into a broad-market fund as replacement.

If the share price falls below the buy edge (earnings yield rises above 6.25%): add to the position, provided the basket earnings yield is below 8% and the thematic thesis is intact. A basket earnings yield above 8% coinciding with persistent capital losses is exit condition (1) — verify the nature of the yield widening before adding.

If the share price rises above the trim edge (earnings yield falls below 4.0%): trim 25–33% of the position. The basket is meaningfully expensive versus the risk-free rate. Move proceeds to a risk-free instrument or to a broad-market fund rather than holding cash within the thematic position.

If the share price rises above the exit edge (earnings yield falls below 3.0%): exit the full position. The basket is fully priced versus the risk-free rate. Redeploy proceeds to a risk-free instrument or to a holding that meets the value test.

Scenario Definitions

A scenario is a joint assumption about two independent variables: operating trajectory (how the holding's underlying cash flows grow relative to their long-run trend) and exit multiple (what the market pays for those cash flows at the horizon, relative to a fair-value anchor).

Scenario Operating trajectory Exit multiple
Bear Below trend Below fair value
Base At trend At fair value
Bull Above trend At or above fair value

Non-negotiable rule: In the bear case, both dimensions must be adverse simultaneously — a scenario where only the multiple moves is a re-rating scenario, not a bear.

Bear cases are comparable in structure across all holding types (company, ETF, bond), but not in magnitude. A company bear reflects idiosyncratic execution failure; an ETF bear reflects a broad market drawdown. Different mitigations, different diversification implications.
