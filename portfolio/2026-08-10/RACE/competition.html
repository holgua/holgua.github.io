Overall Competitive Position Leader

Overall Competitive Position derivation: Ferrari qualifies as Leader: unambiguous revenue and margin leadership in the ultra-luxury sports car segment, widest moat in the cohort across all four pillars, share stable by design (scarcity model), demonstrably superior pricing power under tariff stress, and primary competitors either financially distressed (Aston Martin), undergoing major restructuring (Porsche), or constrained by volume strategy (Lamborghini's Urus-centric model diverges from Ferrari's aspirational positioning). The sole qualifier is the Elettrica BEV execution risk — but this is an internal strategy execution question, not a competitive threat from an external entrant.

Narrative Assessment:

Ferrari's competitive moat is verifiably wider than any peer's based on the available evidence, and it widened further in 2025: the 38.8% EBITDA margin, sustained through US tariff headwinds that visibly compressed Lamborghini's margins by 3pp and required Porsche to take €3.9bn in extraordinary charges, is the clearest single-year demonstration in the five-year observable period that the scarcity model's economics are not merely cyclical but structurally superior. The closest credible competitive threat is Lamborghini: VW/Audi Group's R&D backing provides R&D resources and scale that Ferrari cannot match on an absolute spend basis, and the Urus's volume (75% of Lamborghini deliveries) funds an innovation pipeline for future sports cars. However, Lamborghini's increasing dependence on SUV volume (high units at lower ASP and margin) is moving the brand in a directionally opposite trajectory to Ferrari — toward volume premium, away from hyper-scarcity. This divergence is strategically beneficial for Ferrari: as Lamborghini's ASP and exclusivity per unit decline relative to Ferrari's (Purosangue at 2× Urus ASP; Revuelto at comparable levels to SF90 but lower brand stature), Ferrari reinforces its position as the singular ultra-luxury benchmark. The most important competitive question over the next 3 years is not whether Ferrari can defend its moat against existing peers — the evidence strongly says yes — but whether the Elettrica BEV, launching late 2026, can sustain or improve the scarcity premium in the context of an EV powertrain that alters the acoustic and performance character of the Ferrari experience. That is not a competitive threat from outside Ferrari; it is a self-imposed test of whether the brand can extend its moat into a new propulsion era. Every external competitor faces the same challenge, but Ferrari faces it first and at the highest ASP — which means it is also the best positioned to set the luxury BEV price benchmark if the Elettrica lands well.
