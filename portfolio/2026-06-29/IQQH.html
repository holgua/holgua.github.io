Clean Energy: The Bet Still Works

The Bottom Line

The bet is working. The iShares Global Clean Energy Transition fund tracks a basket of roughly 100 companies spanning solar, wind, grid infrastructure, and electrification — the structural argument that global decarbonisation mandates and the falling cost of renewables will redirect trillions in capital toward these businesses is intact. All five vehicle-integrity checks pass cleanly, and the basket is priced in the upper half of the hold band, modestly above fair value. The one condition to watch is the equity risk premium — the yield the basket offers above government bonds is thin, at only 19 basis points, and another small rise in Treasury yields would push it negative, which would trigger a re-examination of the holding even though no exit condition has yet fired. That risk is covered in full below.

About the Fund

Clean Energy is the iShares Global Clean Energy Transition UCITS ETF (listed on Xetra under the ticker IQQH.DE), managed by BlackRock Asset Management Ireland. The fund tracks the S&P Global Clean Energy Transition Index, which holds roughly 100 companies whose revenues or operations are tied to the shift away from fossil-fuel energy. The basket tilts heavily toward three sectors: Technology (37%), Utilities (32%), and Industrials (29%) — together accounting for 98% of the fund. In plain terms, the largest constituents are companies that make solar panels and inverters, that own and operate wind and solar power plants, and that manufacture the grid equipment and electrification hardware that a decarbonising economy requires. The fund holds global exposure: the top ten names span the United States, China, Denmark, India, and Brazil.

The index is not a market-cap-weighted snapshot of the energy sector. It is a thematic selection of companies the S&P methodology classifies as directly benefiting from the clean-energy transition — which means its sector composition and constituent roster can change materially if the index methodology is revised or if constituent eligibility rules are updated. That methodological risk is one of the named exit conditions.

Tracking metrics. The 1-year tracking difference and tracking error are not yet available from the fund's published materials; see the Gaps section below.

Tax profile. The fund is an Irish-domiciled UCITS (Undertakings for Collective Investment in Transferable Securities), not a US exchange-traded trust. No K-1 tax form is issued. Whether the share class distributes income or accumulates it — and the fund's UK Reporting Fund status — have not been confirmed from the iShares product page; these matter primarily for investors subject to UK or Austrian tax treatment.

Annual cost. Annual cost: 0.65% per year.

The Case for the Bet

The underlying argument is that a multi-decade shift in global energy capital expenditure is underway, driven by regulatory mandates and the economics of renewable generation, and that the companies in this basket are among the primary beneficiaries. The earnings-yield bands used for portfolio consistency are a secondary monitoring tool; the primary holding rationale is thematic conviction over a roughly ten-year horizon.

For the bet to remain in force, six conditions must continue to hold:

  1. Earnings yield trap absent. The basket's earnings yield does not stay above 8.0% for four consecutive quarters while the index simultaneously posts persistent capital losses — a combination that would signal constituent-fundamentals collapse rather than a temporary cheapness.
  2. Vehicle integrity intact. The expense ratio stays below 0.80%, assets under management stay above €200 million, no single constituent exceeds 20.0% of the basket, and iShares has not issued a product wind-down notice for IQQH; current reading: expense ratio 0.65% (floor at 0.80%: 15 bps headroom; early-warning at 0.70%: 5 bps headroom), assets €5.1 billion (25.6× the floor), top constituent Bloom Energy at 12.1% (early-warning at 17.0%, hard ceiling at 20.0%).
  3. Index methodology stable. S&P has not materially altered the Clean Energy Transition Index's inclusion criteria, sector scope, or weighting scheme in a way that changes what economic exposure this position was intended to own.
  4. Policy tailwinds intact. No durable, legislated repeal (not a temporary pause) of US Inflation Reduction Act clean-energy tax credits or EU Green Deal binding targets — the regulatory underwriting of the capex cycle.
  5. Structural cost advantage of renewables persists. Solar and wind levelised cost of energy continues to undercut fossil incumbents in the majority of geographies, or if the advantage is temporarily lost, capacity additions have not yet flatlined for two consecutive years.
  6. No superior thematic vehicle available. A lower-fee, broader, or higher-quality clean-energy fund with equivalent regulatory-jurisdiction exposure has not emerged to a degree that justifies switching.

What's Wobbling

The equity risk premium is the only driver not fully cleared. At 0.19%, the basket offers almost no yield above the risk-free rate — positive, but below the 2.0% confirmation floor set for this holding's low-return-on-equity, long-duration basket.

The reason the 2.0% confirmation floor is set where it is matters: Utilities and Industrials businesses structurally earn lower returns on equity than broad-market averages, which means investors in this basket implicitly accept a thinner yield premium in exchange for exposure to the capex-cycle story. A slim but positive equity risk premium is therefore less alarming for this fund than it would be for a broad-market index fund. The history of equity risk premium readings covers only two observations (2026-06-23 at +0.11% and 2026-06-24 at +0.19%), both positive — not enough to determine a trend.

The metric would need to post two consecutive negative readings before the persistence check flags an elevated concern. At today's reading, it is one directional move of roughly 20 basis points away from turning negative.

No other driver wobbles. All vehicle-integrity conditions pass. No policy reversal has been logged. No index methodology change has been signalled.

What Could Still Go Wrong

  • Rate shock. A sharp rise in government bond yields pushes the equity risk premium negative, and the basket's earnings yield remains depressed because the basket re-rates to a lower earnings multiple at the same time. This combination — low yield, negative risk premium — approaches exit condition 1 faster than the current trajectory suggests.
  • Constituent-fundamentals collapse. A prolonged period of earnings deterioration among the basket's largest constituents (particularly Bloom Energy, which already earns only 1.3% on equity, and the two loss-making positions, Plug Power and SolarEdge Technologies) drives the basket earnings yield above 8.0% while the index declines — the textbook pattern for exit condition 1.
  • Policy reversal. A durable repeal or substantial rewriting of the Inflation Reduction Act's clean-energy provisions, or a binding EU Green Deal rollback, removes the regulatory underwriting of the capex cycle. The impact on constituent earnings could be immediate and steep for companies whose project pipelines depend on tax credits.
  • Index methodology change. S&P alters the Clean Energy Transition Index's eligibility criteria in a way that brings in companies with substantially different economic exposure — for example, shifting weight toward oil-and-gas transition plays rather than pure-play renewables. This would change the economic bet without a change in the fund's name or listed price.
  • Vehicle exit. The expense ratio drifts above the early-warning threshold of 0.70% (5 basis points of headroom exists today), ultimately crossing the 0.80% ceiling, or assets under management fall sharply on a sustained thematic outflow. At €5.1 billion, an assets floor breach is implausible today, but the expense-ratio headroom is narrow.

(For this fund, an exit fires when the share price reaches the exit edge at €16, when a vehicle-integrity condition breaches, or when one of the six named exit-condition triggers fires — any of three paths.)

The Rules

The pre-committed action plan translates into six if-then rules, stated directly as states of the evaluation:

  1. If the thematic thesis is confirmed — all five vehicle-integrity conditions pass, no exit condition is triggered, and drivers are at worst partially passing: hold. Do not add on the basis of the earnings yield signal alone; add only if the decarbonisation capex case is actively strengthening.

  2. If the thesis is weakening — any driver is failing to pass its threshold but no exit condition has fired: do not add. Re-validate after the next quarter and after any material policy development affecting the Inflation Reduction Act or EU Green Deal. If exit conditions 3–5 are approaching, accelerate the review timeline.

  3. If the thesis is broken — a vehicle-integrity condition has breached or a thematic exit condition has fired: exit the full position. Move proceeds to risk-free assets or into the World Equal Weight or All World broad-market fund as a replacement.

  4. If the share price is below the buy band — the basket earnings yield has risen above 6.25%: add to the position, provided the basket earnings yield is below 8.0% and the thematic thesis is intact. A basket earnings yield above 8.0% with persistent capital losses is exit condition 1 — verify before adding.

  5. If the share price is above the trim band — the basket earnings yield has fallen below 4.0%: trim 25–33% of the position. The basket is meaningfully expensive relative to the risk-free rate. Move proceeds to risk-free assets or to the broad-market funds rather than holding cash within the thematic position.

  6. If the share price is above the exit band — the basket earnings yield has fallen to or below 3.0%: exit the full position. The basket is fully priced against the risk-free rate. Redeploy proceeds to risk-free assets or to a holding that still meets the value test.
