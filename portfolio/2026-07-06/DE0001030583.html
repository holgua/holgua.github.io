About the Bond

The Federal Republic of Germany (ISIN DE0001030583, issued February 2021, maturing 2033-04-15) is a sovereign inflation-linked bond — a class of instrument in which both the principal repaid at maturity and the annual coupon are adjusted upward with realised consumer-price inflation as measured by the German Harmonised Index of Consumer Prices excluding tobacco (a European price index compiled by Eurostat that covers a broad basket of goods and services). Unlike a conventional bond, which pays a fixed nominal amount regardless of what prices do in the intervening years, this instrument guarantees that every euro returned at maturity has the same purchasing power as the euros invested at issuance — and then pays a small coupon of 0.10% per year on top of that inflation-adjusted principal.

Credit risk is effectively absent. The Federal Republic of Germany carries the highest available ratings from Fitch (AAA), Moody's (Aaa), and S&P (AAA) and has never defaulted on a euro-denominated obligation. The bond was issued with a total outstanding volume of €10.65 billion, making it one of the larger individual lines in the German government's inflation-linked programme.

The bond's index ratio currently stands at 1.2634, reflecting the cumulative rise in the reference price index since the bond's base period. A buyer pays the dirty price of €120.04 per €100 original face value at settlement, not €94.99. The €94.99 is the quoted clean price in real terms (same purchasing-power units as the issuance date). To get the actual euros that change hands, multiply by the index ratio (1.26340), then add accrued interest.

The Case for the Bet

The holding is sized as a portfolio inflation-hedge allocation, not as a rates trade. The contractual structure means the bond does what it promises regardless of whether inflation runs hot or cold: the real purchasing power of the position is preserved. The single most important signal governing whether to hold, add, trim, or exit is the real yield at the current market price — a number that moves with the bond's price and states exactly what annual return above inflation is on offer if the bond is held to the 2033-04-15 maturity.

Three specific conditions must keep holding for the bet to keep working:

  1. Real yield at current market price does not fall below −1.0% for two consecutive quarters while EUR cash simultaneously offers a superior after-tax real return. The exit fires only when both legs are true at once — the yield is deeply negative and cash is a better deal. The threshold exists because a negative real yield means the bond's price is high enough to embed a guaranteed real loss, and the bet is only defensible as long as the alternative (EUR cash) does not offer more.

Latest reading: The real yield currently stands at 0.86%, approximately 187 basis points above the −1.0% exit threshold. EUR cash earns approximately 0.35% in real terms (ECB deposit rate of 2.25% less break-even inflation of 1.90%). The linker leads cash by 51 basis points. Neither leg of the exit trigger is active.

  1. German sovereign credit is not downgraded below AA− by any two of the three major rating agencies (Fitch, Moody's, S&P). The threshold is set at AA− rather than a lower level because a two-agency downgrade below AA− would represent a structural shift in how global capital markets price German sovereign risk — and would likely depress the bond's price independently of the inflation adjustment.

Latest reading: Germany holds AAA/Aaa at all three agencies. No agency has placed the rating on watch or negative outlook. Exit condition (2) is inactive.

  1. The HICP ex-tobacco index methodology is not materially restructured by Eurostat in a way that severs the link between the index and realised consumer-price inflation. The risk is remote but real: if the reference index were redefined or replaced, the bond's inflation-protection function could be impaired even before any formal default.

Latest reading: No pending HICP methodology restructuring has been announced by Eurostat. Exit condition (3) is inactive.

The Share Price

The real yield on an inflation-linked bond moves inversely with the bond's price: when the price rises, the yield falls, and the bond becomes more expensive. The bond currently trades at €94.99, which equates to a real yield of 0.86% — placing it at the buy edge of the hold band (buy threshold 1.50%, fair value 0.50%, trim threshold −0.50%).

In absolute terms, 0.86% is a modest positive real yield: the bond is offering meaningful but not exceptional real compensation above inflation for a seven-year AAA-rated sovereign instrument. The fair-value estimate (the yield the bond would offer if priced at the intersection of the ECB deposit rate, break-even inflation, and a 30-basis-point sovereign spread) implies a price of €97.34 — meaning today's €94.99 sits modestly below that anchor, at fair value on a gap basis (−2.4%).

The relevant comparison for assessing the opportunity cost is EUR cash. The European Central Bank's deposit facility rate stands at 2.25%. This comparison is pre-tax — German income tax applies to both legs (Abgeltungssteuer on cash interest and on the linker's coupon plus index accrual); the after-tax direction is not modelled here.

What Could Still Go Wrong

  • Real yield turns persistently negative. If the European Central Bank cuts rates aggressively while the bond's price is bid up by demand for safe inflation protection, the real yield could slide below −1.0% for an extended period. If EUR cash simultaneously offers a better real return, the compound exit trigger fires and the position should be closed.
  • Germany is downgraded. A severe fiscal shock — an extended conflict, a disorderly energy transition, or sustained banking-sector stress — could prompt two major rating agencies to cut Germany below AA−. The event would be unprecedented in the post-war period but is not impossible.
  • HICP methodology is restructured. Eurostat has periodically updated the HICP basket and methodology in ways that affect measured inflation. A structural redefinition — such as replacing HICP ex-tobacco with a narrower index or capping the inflation adjustment — would impair the bond's hedging function even without a formal default.
  • ECB deposit rate rises sharply. If the ECB tightens policy materially beyond current market expectations, EUR cash real yields could converge on or exceed the linker's real yield, eliminating the spread advantage that currently favours the linker. This would not trigger an exit condition directly but would narrow the margin of safety.

The Rules

  • If the thesis is confirmed (real yield inside the hold band, German credit intact, HICP linkage undisturbed): hold the position. The inflation-protection function is operating as intended.
  • If the thesis weakens (real yield moves below 0% but the compound exit trigger has not yet fired): do not add. Re-assess the after-tax real return on the linker against EUR cash and conventional Bunds before taking any action; the bar for adding at a negative real yield is high.
  • If the thesis breaks (one or more of the three numbered exit conditions fires): exit the full position. Move the proceeds to short-duration nominal Bunds or EUR cash.
  • If the price falls to the buy edge (real yield rises above 1.50%): add to the position in proportion to the inflation-hedge allocation target. A 7-year AAA EUR sovereign linker at 1.50% real yield is highly attractive relative to EUR alternatives.
  • If the price rises to the trim edge (real yield falls below −0.50%): trim 25% on the first crossing. If the real yield remains below −0.50% at the next quarterly review (at least 90 days later), trim a further 25%. The ladder stops there and does not escalate further until the exit trigger fires.
  • If the compound exit trigger fires (real yield has been below −1.0% for at least 90 days and EUR cash offers a superior real return): exit the full position. Switch proceeds to EUR cash or short-duration nominal Bunds; the inflation-hedge function is no longer additive relative to the alternative.
