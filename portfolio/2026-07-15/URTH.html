About the Fund

All World — formally the iShares MSCI World ETF, ticker URTH — holds shares in roughly 1,300 large- and mid-cap companies across 23 developed markets. The underlying index is the MSCI World, a float-adjusted, market-cap-weighted benchmark that includes the United States at around 70% of the weight, with the remaining 30% split across Europe (the United Kingdom, France, Germany, Switzerland), Japan, Canada, and other developed markets. There is no exposure to emerging markets. The basket is purely passive: it does not make active stock selections or tilt toward any factor; it simply owns the market, at the market's own weights.

The fund's most concentrated position at the latest snapshot is NVIDIA at 5.17% of the portfolio, followed by Apple at 4.76% and Alphabet at 4.16%. Together the top nine available holdings account for roughly one quarter of net assets. Technology stocks represent 30.85% of the basket — a structural feature of how float-adjusted cap weighting treats US mega-cap technology companies, not an active overweight. Financial services (15.66%), industrials (11.39%), and healthcare (9.08%) round out the next-largest sectors. The fund is 99.72% in equities, with the remainder in routine operational cash.

The annual cost is 0.24%. Tracking difference and tracking error figures for the most recent twelve months are not available in this snapshot; they are worth verifying from the iShares URTH factsheet, though the structural expectation for a fund of this size is tight replication. The wrapper type is a Delaware statutory trust; no K-1 is issued.

The Case for the Bet

The bet is that over the full holding period, the basket's look-through earnings yield will exceed the risk-free rate plus a reasonable equity risk premium of at least 2 percentage points. URTH's role is distinct from that of an all-US index fund: the roughly 30% allocation to non-US developed markets (Japan, the United Kingdom, France, Switzerland, Germany, Canada, and others) narrows the regime-risk of a purely American exposure. The bet is not that the basket is always a buy; it is that the compounding power of the underlying businesses, held at a reasonable entry price, will beat the risk-free alternative over time.

Six conditions keep that case alive:

  1. Equity risk premium stays positive for two consecutive quarters. The basket's earnings yield must exceed the ten-year Treasury yield over any two-quarter window. A sustained negative premium means equities are not compensating for the extra risk over government paper.

Latest reading: Equity risk premium −0.46% as of 2026-07-15 (basket earnings yield 4.09% vs. ten-year Treasury 4.545%). Thirteen consecutive negative daily snapshots since 2026-06-16. The quarterly persistence clock is running; the two-quarter bar has not elapsed. Exit condition (1) is not yet triggered.

  1. Annual expense ratio remains at or below 0.35%. Any fee increase above 0.35% by BlackRock/iShares, with no fee response, signals cost drift the functionally equivalent UCITS structure does not require.

Latest reading: Expense ratio 0.24% as of 2026-07-15 — 11 basis points below the hard ceiling. Status: clear.

  1. Assets under management remain above $2.0 billion. Below approximately $2 billion, bid-ask spreads for a global blend fund of this type typically widen materially, raising transaction costs.

Latest reading: Assets under management $8.06 billion as of 2026-07-15, roughly four times the floor. Average daily dollar volume approximately $226 million. Status: clear.

  1. Top single-name concentration stays below 10%. A weight above 10% in any one name in a nominally diversified global index signals regime-change concentration — normal drift does not reach that level.

Latest reading: NVIDIA at 5.17% as of 2026-07-15, 4.83 percentage points below the ceiling. Status: clear.

  1. MSCI World index methodology remains intact. A material restructuring of the index — country reclassification, removal of major developed-market constituents, or a fundamental shift away from float-adjusted cap weighting — would undermine the mandate this position is meant to fill.

Latest reading: No automated signal for methodology change or sponsor restructuring as of 2026-07-15. Monitored externally; no exit signal.

  1. Median look-through return on equity of the top-ten holdings stays above 15%. Return on equity (the profit a company generates relative to the shareholders' capital it has deployed) below 15% across the basket's largest names would indicate the underlying businesses have lost their quality as compounders.

Latest reading: Median return on equity of the nine available top holdings 37.28% as of 2026-07-15, well above the 15% floor. Status: clear.

Put plainly, the basket is not cheap. The share price will have to drop — or earnings will have to rise — before the valuation case turns constructive.

What's Wobbling

One driver is in question: the equity risk premium. The basket's earnings yield has been below the ten-year Treasury yield for thirteen consecutive daily snapshots since 2026-06-16. That makes the risk-premium case weaker than the original thesis assumed at entry — the bet was that the yield would exceed the risk-free rate plus 2 percentage points; right now it falls 46 basis points short of the risk-free rate alone, meaning it misses the hurdle by more than 2.4 percentage points.

What keeps the exit clock from firing is the rule's design: the written condition requires two consecutive quarters of negative equity risk premium, not two snapshots. The clock started around 2026-06-16; the next quarterly check falls approximately around 2026-09-16. If the premium is still negative at that reading, the two-quarter bar is crossed and the exit condition triggers.

The trim-band margin is a related watch item. At an earnings yield of 4.09% against a trim threshold of 4.00%, only a 9-basis-point yield compression separates the current reading from the trim trigger. Any combination of price appreciation and flat earnings could close that gap quickly.

All five vehicle-integrity conditions remain clear. The hold call is correct for now; the monitoring is active.

What Could Still Go Wrong

If the basket's earnings yield remains below the ten-year Treasury for two full consecutive quarters, exit condition (1) fires and the Rules call for closing the position. With 13 daily snapshots already negative, the quarterly clock is running.
  • Basket price rises further before the exit signal arrives. The trim trigger at $209 is 9 basis points away in yield terms. A price move through $209 fires the trim order before any exit condition is formally confirmed.
  • Earnings contract in a broad market downturn. The bear scenario in the value analysis assumes a ten-year-Treasury-anchored multiple compression from 24.46× to 14× simultaneously with an earnings decline of roughly 11%. That combination produces a cumulative five-year return of approximately −20% — not a tail scenario, but one that requires both the multiple and the earnings to move adversely at the same time.
  • US mega-cap technology concentration intensifies. Technology already accounts for 30.85% of the basket, driven by float-adjusted weighting of US companies. If NVIDIA or another top name reaches 10% of the index, exit condition (4) fires. At the current 5.17%, that would require roughly doubling of the relative market-cap weight.
  • Fee drift without a response. If BlackRock/iShares raises the expense ratio above 0.35% without a fee reduction, exit condition (2) fires. At 0.24%, there is 11 basis points of headroom — a small but not trivial buffer.

(For this fund, exit fires when the share price reaches the exit edge, when a vehicle-integrity condition breaches, or when the named exit-condition trigger fires — any of three paths.)

Scenario Definitions

A scenario is a joint assumption about two independent variables: operating trajectory (how the holding's underlying cash flows grow relative to their long-run trend) and exit multiple (what the market pays for those cash flows at the horizon, relative to a fair-value anchor).

Scenario Operating trajectory Exit multiple
Bear Below trend Below fair value
Base At trend At fair value
Bull Above trend At or above fair value

Non-negotiable rule: In the bear case, both dimensions must be adverse simultaneously — a scenario where only the multiple moves is a re-rating scenario, not a bear.

Bear cases are comparable in structure across all holding types (company, ETF, bond), but not in magnitude. A company bear reflects idiosyncratic execution failure; an ETF bear reflects a broad market drawdown. Different mitigations, different diversification implications.
