About Unilever

Unilever makes the everyday products that households in more than 190 countries buy without much deliberation — soap, shampoo, conditioner, laundry detergent, mayonnaise, stock cubes, ice cream, deodorant. The business divides into four broad categories: Beauty & Wellbeing (Dove, TRESemmé, Dermalogica), Personal Care (Axe/Lynx, Rexona, Domestos), Home Care (Comfort, Surf, Seventh Generation), and Nutrition (Hellmann's, Knorr, Marmite, Horlicks). The Ice Cream segment — Magnum, Ben & Jerry's, Cornetto, Wall's — was separated into an independent company in December 2025. The Nutrition segment's core brands are being sold to McCormick & Company, with the deal expected to close around mid-2027.

The economic engine is straightforward: Unilever's brands command a price premium over own-label alternatives, and the company's distribution infrastructure — built over decades in markets where competing retailers are fragmented or unreliable — allows it to reach customers that competitors cannot serve efficiently. Roughly 60% of revenue comes from emerging markets, where a growing middle class is trading up from local alternatives to the familiar international brands. The combination of brand equity and distribution depth has sustained operating margins of 16.6–17.9% through five consecutive years that included the worst commodity-price inflation episode in a decade, confirming that the franchise can pass cost increases to consumers without losing volume.

The Case for the Bet

The central idea is that Unilever's brand equity and last-mile distribution network in emerging markets will keep generating excess returns on capital for the foreseeable future, making it a business worth holding at fair value for the dividend income and the optionality from the portfolio-simplification cycle. The conditions that must keep holding:

  1. Operating margin stays above 14%. An operating margin (the share of each euro of revenue that becomes profit before interest and tax) held above 14% for every fiscal year signals that the brand premium is intact. Below 14% for two consecutive years is the tripwire.

Latest reading: 17.9% for the year ending 2025-12-31. At the five-year high, up from 16.8% the prior year; the 14% threshold has not been approached in the observable five-year history.

  1. Return on invested capital stays above 10%. Return on invested capital (the cash the business generates from each euro of capital tied up in it) staying above 10% confirms the brand franchise is creating economic value — earning more than the estimated cost of the capital it employs. Below 10% for two consecutive years would signal the spread has collapsed.

Latest reading: 14.7% for 2025. Recovered from a trough of 11.5% in 2024 that was driven by goodwill impairment charges, not by operating deterioration; the 10% threshold is not in reach.

  1. Owner earnings stay above EUR 5.5bn per year. Owner earnings — operating cash flow minus estimated maintenance capital spending — represent the cash the business generates after keeping the physical assets in working order. Falling below EUR 5.5bn for two consecutive years would signal the bear-case floor has been breached.

Latest reading: Approximately EUR 6.85bn for 2025 (operating cash flow of EUR 8.35bn minus EUR 1.5bn estimated maintenance capital spending). EUR 1.35bn above the exit floor.

  1. No large goodwill write-down without a disposal rationale. A goodwill impairment charge exceeding EUR 3bn in any single year, without a concurrently announced disposal, would signal that past acquisitions are being acknowledged as failures under pressure — a management-integrity concern.

Latest reading: Goodwill declined by EUR 4.6bn in 2025, from EUR 22.3bn to EUR 17.7bn. This decline is directly attributable to the Ice Cream demerger completed in December 2025 — a deliberate disposal, not a surprise write-down. The exit-condition carve-out applies.

  1. Earnings quality remains sound. The accruals ratio — a measure of how closely reported earnings track actual cash generation — staying below +0.10 for two consecutive years signals that management is not inflating earnings with accounting entries rather than cash.

Latest reading: +0.016 for 2025. Near zero, consistent with three consecutive years of clean earnings quality (2024: −0.047; 2023: −0.039). The +0.10 threshold is not approached.

  1. McCormick Foods transaction completes without major deterioration. The planned disposal of the Hellmann's, Knorr, and Marmite brands to McCormick & Company at USD 44.8bn is a load-bearing element of the portfolio-simplification thesis. A deal collapse or a reprice downward by more than 20% would strand capital in lower-quality portfolio and signal execution failure.

Latest reading: Transaction signed March 2026 at USD 44.8bn; antitrust reviews ongoing across three jurisdictions as of 2026-07-31; no enforcement action or blockage signalled. Deal remains on track.

  1. Power Brand organic sales growth stays above 1.5%. Power Brand underlying sales growth (the volume-and-price combined measure of Unilever's branded top line, excluding currency and portfolio changes) falling below 1.5% for two consecutive trading periods would signal that private-label competition is eroding the pricing premium faster than growth in emerging markets can offset.

Latest reading: 5.0% in the three months ending March 2026, with the volume component running at 4.0%. The 1.5% exit threshold has not been approached in any recorded period.

  1. US regulatory and tariff risk remains manageable. The MAHA (Make America Healthy Again) regulatory movement and US tariff policy affecting imported consumer goods represent policy risks that do not appear in the financial statements but can affect the Nutrition brands and supply-chain economics.

Latest reading: Management confirmed tariff impact as "limited and manageable" as of the 2025 results; MAHA's September 2025 Commission report issued no company-specific enforcement recommendations; the McCormick transaction, if completed, disposes of the brands most exposed to MAHA-style food-ingredient regulation.

What's Wobbling

Net leverage is the sole driver reading as mixed. Net debt stands at approximately USD 23.3bn, implying a debt-to-equity ratio of around 161% — elevated by any conventional measure. This is not a surprise, and it is not new deterioration: the thesis explicitly acknowledged the leverage level when it was written, identifying the McCormick Foods transaction's expected USD 44.8bn in proceeds as the mechanism for bringing debt down. The watch threshold — operating cash flow as a share of revenue falling below 12% — has not been approached: the ratio was 16.5% in the most recent full year.

The leverage figure will be worth revisiting after the McCormick transaction closes. Until then, the scenario in which elevated debt becomes a genuine constraint requires operating cash flow to deteriorate materially and simultaneously — the bear case, in other words, not the base case. Under base-case economics, McCormick proceeds substantially eliminate the leverage concern.

No other driver is weakening. The seven remaining conditions are all reading as confirming.

What Could Still Go Wrong

  • Emerging-market currencies depreciate sharply against the euro. Unilever earns roughly 60% of revenue in currencies that are more volatile than European ones; a 15% basket depreciation would compress reported earnings and owner earnings materially, as it does in the bear case.
  • Private-label products take sustained market share in Unilever's core developed-market food and home-care categories. The brand premium is habitual loyalty, not contractual lock-in; if private-label quality improves and price gaps widen in a cost-of-living squeeze, Power Brand organic growth could dip below the exit threshold.
  • The McCormick Foods transaction fails regulatory clearance or is substantially repriced. Deal collapse would strand the Nutrition brands inside the portfolio, reverse the portfolio-simplification thesis, and eliminate the deleveraging path.
  • Capital allocation reverts to large-scale acquisitions at expensive multiples. Unilever came close to acquiring GlaxoSmithKline's consumer business in 2022 at a price that would have been deeply value-destructive. That instinct can reappear.
  • Commodity re-inflation or a sustained cost shock compresses margins faster than pricing power allows recovery. The FY2022 evidence suggests Unilever can pass through commodity costs, but that ability depends on consumers accepting price increases — possible in a growth environment, less certain in a prolonged recessionary period in developed markets.

The Rules

  • If any driver weakens without triggering an exit condition: do not add to the position. Monitor for one additional quarter before reassessing size. Key monitoring dates are the quarterly trading statements (Power Brand organic growth, India separately, operating margin) and the next annual filing (return on invested capital, accruals ratio, goodwill carrying value). Scenario Definitions

A scenario is a joint assumption about two independent variables: operating trajectory (how the holding's underlying cash flows grow relative to their long-run trend) and exit multiple (what the market pays for those cash flows at the horizon, relative to a fair-value anchor).

Scenario Operating trajectory Exit multiple
Bear Below trend Below fair value
Base At trend At fair value
Bull Above trend At or above fair value

Non-negotiable rule: In the bear case, both dimensions must be adverse simultaneously — a scenario where only the multiple moves is a re-rating scenario, not a bear.

Bear cases are comparable in structure across all holding types (company, ETF, bond), but not in magnitude. A company bear reflects idiosyncratic execution failure; an ETF bear reflects a broad market drawdown. Different mitigations, different diversification implications.
