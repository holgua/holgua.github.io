About the Fund

Clean Energy is iShares' exchange-traded fund tracking the S&P Global Clean Energy Transition Index, an index of roughly 100 companies whose revenues are materially tied to the build-out of solar, wind, electricity grids, electrification infrastructure, and the enabling technology behind all of them. The fund is listed on Xetra in euros (ISIN IE00B1XNHC34) and issued by BlackRock's Irish UCITS arm — the same legal wrapper used by most iShares funds sold in Europe.

The basket's three dominant sectors at the 2026-07-09 snapshot are Technology (37%), Utilities (32%), and Industrials (29%), leaving less than 2% in anything else. That concentration is by design: the index was built to track clean-energy exposure, not to represent the economy broadly. The ten largest holdings account for just over half the basket, with Bloom Energy (12%) and First Solar (10%) the top two. Annual cost is 0.65% of assets under management, charged as an ongoing total expense ratio.

Tax profile. The fund is structured as a distributing Irish UCITS (OeKB Meldefonds: yes), which means distributions are paid out rather than reinvested automatically. No K-1 is issued. Tax treatment on distributions and capital gains depends on the country of residence and applicable tax rules; the after-tax picture is not modelled in the pipeline.

The Case for the Bet

The fund is held as a long-duration bet on the global decarbonisation capital-expenditure cycle — the thesis being that regulatory mandates, falling renewable-energy costs, and sustained industrial investment will together create a structural multi-year tailwind for the companies in this basket. The valuation framework (earnings-yield bands and equity risk premium) is tracked for consistency with the broader portfolio, but it carries less decision-making weight here than it does for a broad-market position: the primary frame is thematic conviction over roughly a decade.

Six conditions underpin the thesis:

  1. Equity risk premium above zero. The basket's earnings yield should exceed the risk-free rate, confirming that the market is not simply paying a risk-free return in equity wrapper. The confirm threshold is a spread of at least 2.0 percentage points; a reading between 0.0% and 2.0% is classified as mixed, not a challenge. A sustained negative reading would constitute a challenge to the thesis.

Latest reading: basket earnings yield 4.59%, ten-year US Treasury 4.53%, spread +0.06 percentage points as of 2026-07-09. Mixed — positive but below the 2.0-point confirm floor. The risk-premium history across the six most recent data points (2026-06-23 through 2026-07-09) has been consistently positive, ranging from +0.06 to +0.19 percentage points.

  1. Constituent quality: median return-on-equity above 10%. The underlying companies should be earning a return on their book capital above the custom floor set for clean-energy utilities and equipment makers (which structurally run below the 20% floor applied to broad-market holdings). A weighted-mean reading below 6% would signal a fundamentals collapse in the tail, not a basket-wide quality issue.

Latest reading: median return-on-equity across the top ten deduped constituents is 14.87% as of 2026-07-09, clearing the 10% confirm floor. Weighted-mean return-on-equity is 1.53% — sharply lower than the median, dragged by Plug Power (return-on-equity −129%) and SolarEdge (return-on-equity −73%); this tail drag is a known structural feature of the thematic basket, not a sign of pervasive deterioration. Driver confirms.

  1. Annual expense ratio below 0.80%. The fund's ongoing charge should remain within the vehicle-integrity ceiling. At 0.65%, the current charge leaves 15 basis points (hundredths of a percentage point) of headroom.

Latest reading: expense ratio 0.65% as of 2026-07-09; 15 basis points below the 0.80% ceiling. Driver confirms.

  1. No single constituent above 20% of the basket. Concentrated single-name risk above this threshold would signal index fragility or unusual price divergence from the S&P's quarterly rebalance cadence.

Latest reading: top holding is Bloom Energy at 12.13%, leaving 7.87 percentage points of headroom to the 20% ceiling. Driver confirms.

  1. Assets under management above €200 million. The fund must remain viable on the exchange: iShares has historically wound down thematic UCITS ETFs aggressively once assets fall below roughly this level.

Latest reading: assets under management €4.48 billion (approximately €4.28 billion above the floor) as of 2026-07-09. Average daily trading volume on Xetra roughly €2.97 million per day. Driver confirms.

What's Wobbling

The only driver not on a firm confirm reading is the equity risk premium. At +0.06 percentage points over the US ten-year Treasury rate, the basket is not offering the minimum 2.0-point spread the framework requires to call the premium confirmed. The premium has been positive but thin across all six data points recorded since late June 2026, drifting gradually lower as Treasury yields have edged up.

This is not yet a challenge to the thesis. Exit condition (1) — the earnings-yield trap — would require the basket earnings yield to remain above 8.0% for four consecutive quarters while the index is also suffering sustained capital losses. At 4.59%, the yield is less than half the 8.0% threshold, and capital losses of that magnitude are not in evidence. That reading is worth watching because a further narrowing of the spread (or a further rise in Treasury yields compressing it to zero or negative) would move the driver from mixed to challenge.

The second wobble is structural and qualitative: four of the six exit conditions — policy reversal, theme exhaustion, index methodology restructure, and the availability of a better thematic vehicle — are user-monitored externally, with no automated pipeline signal. That is by design, but it means any material development on those fronts requires active attention between quarterly refreshes.

What Could Still Go Wrong

  • Multiple compression. The bear case in the Value Analysis delivers −1.4% a year over five years, driven entirely by the market re-rating the basket from its current 21.77 times earnings to 15 times — a plausible outcome if clean-energy policy tailwinds weaken or interest rates remain high. Eight per cent annual earnings growth is not enough to overcome that valuation headwind.
  • Policy reversal. A legislated rollback of the US Inflation Reduction Act's clean-energy tax credits or the EU Green Deal's binding targets would remove one of the primary structural supports for the decarbonisation capex cycle. A temporary policy pause (executive order, delay in implementation) does not trigger the exit; only a durable statutory repeal does.
  • Theme exhaustion. If solar and wind levelised costs of energy cease to undercut fossil-fuel incumbents in the majority of geographies AND new capacity additions flatline for two consecutive years, the structural cost-advantage driver is gone.
  • Index methodology shift. S&P quarterly reconstitutions have historically shifted the basket composition significantly in a thematic index of this type. A restructuring that materially changes the economic exposure — for example, a significant broadening to include nuclear or fossil-adjacent names — would trigger exit condition (3).
  • Tail-name implosion. Two of the top-ten holdings (Plug Power, SolarEdge) are currently loss-making with deeply negative return-on-equity. A further deterioration in their fundamentals could push the weighted-mean return-on-equity below the 6% distress floor, triggering the look-through quality challenge signal. Neither constituent would trigger a vehicle-integrity breach individually (both are well below the 20% concentration ceiling), but a broad clean-energy equipment sector rout could move multiple constituents into distress territory simultaneously.

For this fund, exit fires when the share price reaches the exit edge, when a vehicle-integrity condition breaches, or when a named exit condition triggers — any of three independent paths.

The Rules

The action plan was set in advance. Applied to the current reading:

  • If the thematic thesis is confirmed (all drivers confirm or mixed, no exit condition fired, vehicle intact): hold — do not size up on the earnings-yield signal alone; add only if the decarbonisation capex case is actively strengthening.
  • If the thesis is weakened (any driver moves to challenge, or a named exit condition approaches but has not fired): do not add. Re-validate after the next quarter and after any material policy development touching the Inflation Reduction Act or EU Green Deal. If exit conditions (3) through (5) are approaching, accelerate the review timeline.
  • If the thesis is broken (a vehicle-integrity breach fires, or a thematic exit condition triggers): exit the full position and move proceeds to risk-free or into a broad-market replacement such as URTH or MWEQ.
  • If the share price falls to the buy edge (basket earnings yield reaches 6.25% or higher): add to the position, provided the basket earnings yield is below 8.0% and the thematic thesis remains intact. A basket earnings yield above 8.0% coinciding with persistent capital losses is exit condition (1) — verify before adding.
  • If the share price rises to the trim edge (basket earnings yield at or below 4.0%): trim 25–33% of the position. Move proceeds to risk-free or to URTH or MWEQ rather than holding cash within the thematic position.
  • If the share price reaches the exit edge (basket earnings yield at or below 3.0%): exit the full position. The basket is fully priced relative to risk-free; redeploy proceeds to a holding that meets the value test.
