Recent Development

The headline from the three months ending 2026-03-31 is the reversal of a significant PacifiCorp wildfire liability verdict. In April 2026, an Oregon appellate court overturned the jury finding that had assigned liability for wildfire damages to Berkshire's energy business. The ruling materially reduces the worst-case legal exposure that had been an overhang on the energy subsidiary for several years. Separately, the energy business agreed to sell its Washington utility operations to a neighbouring utility for $1.9 billion in cash, expected to close in the first half of 2027.

On the operating side, the railroad business had its best recent quarter: revenues up 4.8% and pre-tax earnings up 13.5% to $1.82 billion, driven by better pricing per car and lower fuel costs. Book value per share rose to approximately $337, continuing the steady compounding that sits at the centre of the Case for the Bet.

The one note of caution is the car-insurance business, Berkshire's GEICO subsidiary. The ratio of costs to premiums collected widened to 87.3% in the first quarter from 79.8% a year earlier, as claims came in more frequently and at higher severity. The prior-year comparison was unusually favourable — the first quarter of 2025 had near-zero catastrophe losses — so the headline deterioration overstates the underlying trend. GEICO is still profitably underwriting, and no written exit condition has been triggered. For a fuller account of what wobbled and why, see What's Wobbling. The share-price stance is covered in the Share Price.

About Berkshire

Berkshire Hathaway makes its money through three reinforcing engines. The first is insurance: GEICO sells car insurance directly to consumers; the reinsurance arm takes on catastrophe and specialty risks that most carriers cannot absorb; and smaller commercial-insurance subsidiaries write a variety of lines. All three collect premiums before paying claims, and the money held in the gap is called the float. Berkshire's float stood at roughly $176.9 billion at the end of the first quarter of 2026. The company is currently paid, rather than charged, to hold it — meaning underwriting profits offset the cost of the float entirely, leaving Berkshire's $1 trillion investment portfolio as free leverage.

The second engine is BNSF Railway, which moves coal, grain, consumer goods, and industrial freight across the western United States and Canada. No new large railroad has been built in decades; the franchise cannot be replicated. The third engine is Berkshire Hathaway Energy, a regulated utility owning electricity generation and transmission assets across several western states. Alongside these, Berkshire holds roughly $298 billion of publicly traded equities and a cash reserve of approximately $369 billion. It has never paid a dividend.

Greg Abel became sole chief executive on 2026-01-01, after Warren Buffett stepped back from day-to-day operations while retaining the chairman role. Abel built the energy business from a small Iowa utility into one of Berkshire's largest subsidiaries. His capital allocation record at the parent level is new.

The Case for the Bet

The central bet is that the management culture Buffett built — decentralised decision-making, owner-operator incentives at each subsidiary, patient capital allocation at the parent — will continue to compound retained earnings and float at rates a diversified index cannot replicate. Four specific conditions must keep holding:

  1. Book value per share keeps growing faster than a broad market index over any rolling five-year period. The written exit condition fires if book value per share trails the total return of the MSCI World equity index for five or more consecutive years. That would signal the compounding edge has eroded permanently.

Latest reading: Shareholders' equity grew +10.5% in the year to 2025-12-31, reaching $717 billion, with a further $9.8 billion added in the three months ending 2026-03-31. No exit signal has fired.

  1. Each major acquisition demonstrates that the capital discipline Buffett practised is intact under Abel. The written exit condition fires if Berkshire completes a transaction exceeding $20 billion at a reported price above 1.5 times the book value of the target, or if any acquired business subsequently requires a goodwill write-down exceeding $5 billion.

Latest reading: The first Abel-era acquisition — OxyChem, completed 2026-01-02 for approximately $9.5 billion — is below both the size and price thresholds. No goodwill impairment has been flagged. The deal is too small and too recent to serve as a definitive signal, but the exit condition has not been triggered.

  1. No structural culture change abandons the decentralised owner-operator model. The written exit condition fires on the initiation of a regular cash dividend, a break-up of subsidiary businesses, or a capitulation to activist shareholders that changes the operating model.

Latest reading: No dividend has been declared. No break-up has been announced; the planned sale of the Washington utility operations is a disposal of a regulated asset within an ongoing segment, not a structural change. The exit condition has not been triggered.

  1. The price-to-book ratio stays below 2.0 times. The written exit condition fires if the ratio of Berkshire's share price to the book value of its equity per share exceeds 2.0, at which point the valuation fully prices in optimistic compounding and the opportunity cost against a low-cost index fund becomes clearly negative.

Latest reading: The price-to-book ratio at 2026-07-10 is approximately 1.52 using the 2025-12-31 equity base, or 1.50 against the more recent 2026-03-31 figure. Both are well inside the 2.0 trigger. The exit condition has not been triggered.

The Share Price

At $504 per B-share, the price-to-book ratio of approximately 1.52 places Berkshire modestly above its fair-value anchor of 1.45. That anchor is the level at which the business's compounding ability is priced in fully but conservatively — reflecting the high-quality franchise with a discount for the Abel transition. The buy-band edge is 1.25 times book, which corresponds to the historical level at which Buffett himself was willing to repurchase shares, treating that as a signal that the price is clearly below intrinsic value. The trim edge is 1.75 times book, a level at which the price implies aggressive compounding expectations that the base scenario does not support.

At today's price, no buy signal and no trim signal is active. The share is priced at fair value in terms of the book-value anchor, and in the upper half of the range between buy and trim. The gap between today's $504 and the buy edge at $415 is approximately 18%.

What's Wobbling

The most visible wobble is GEICO's combined ratio (the proportion of every premium dollar consumed by claims and expenses). It rose to 87.3% in the three months ending 2026-03-31, from 79.8% a year earlier, as claims came in more often and at higher cost per event. The prior-year quarter had near-zero catastrophe losses, making it an unusually clean comparison. A reading of 87.3% still means GEICO earns an underwriting profit; the written exit condition is framed around sustained market-share loss from data-driven competitors, not a single quarterly ratio. Across all insurance businesses combined, underwriting earnings improved year-on-year to $2.27 billion from $1.72 billion — the reinsurance and specialty arms more than offset GEICO's step-down.

The second wobble is the pace of buybacks. Berkshire repurchased approximately $235 million of its own shares in the first quarter of 2026 — described in the filing as "relatively minor amounts." At a price-to-book ratio of roughly 1.45 to 1.50 at the time, management's stated practice is to repurchase only when the price is clearly below intrinsic value; the modest pace is consistent with that standard. Neither wobble crosses any of the four written exit conditions.

What Could Still Go Wrong

  • Abel's capital allocation proves inferior. Expensive acquisitions in weak industries, or subsequent write-downs, erode the book value compounding thesis gradually until the five-year benchmark test fires. This is the primary long-run risk.
  • A compound catastrophe hits insurance and energy simultaneously. A major event producing $15 billion or more in net insurance losses, combined with an escalation of PacifiCorp's remaining wildfire liability, would hit earnings and book value in the same year. The $369 billion cash reserve provides absorption capacity, but not a complete shield.
  • Concentration in the equity portfolio. Roughly 65 per cent of the $298 billion portfolio is in five holdings. A sustained competitive challenge to any of them sends mark-to-market losses directly through book value, affecting both the compounding case and the exit trigger.
  • GEICO loses ground to data-driven competitors. Progressive prices risk in near-real-time using driving data. If GEICO cannot close that gap, it loses its best risks to more accurate pricers, and the combined ratio deteriorates structurally rather than cyclically.
  • Berkshire Hathaway Energy's renewable investment pipeline is compressed. Legislation enacted mid-2025 restricts production-tax-credit eligibility on new construction after the end of 2025. Existing projects are grandfathered, but future capital deployment in renewables earns less.

The Rules

The pre-committed rules that govern action on this holding are as follows.

If the thesis is confirmed — meaning no exit condition has fired and no driver is signalling deterioration — hold without action and let the compounding continue.

If the thesis is weakened — meaning one or more drivers are signalling concern but no written exit condition has fired — do not add to the position. Run a fresh evaluation on the specific deteriorating driver and decide after the next quarterly filing.

If the thesis is broken — meaning a written exit condition has fired — close the full position within 30 days. No averaging down.

If the price falls below the buy edge ($415) — confirm the thesis is intact first. If it is, add to the position.

If the price rises above the trim edge ($581) — trim 25 to 33 per cent of the position to harvest the premium while keeping the core holding.

If the price rises above the exit edge ($647) — close the full position.
