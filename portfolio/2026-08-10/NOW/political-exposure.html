1. Board & Executive Revolving-Door Map

EDGAR Baseline (§4.5)

The FY2025 Financial Analysis and Q2 2026 10-Q (Note 17) identified one confirmed revolving-door hire: a serving U.S. Army CIO was brought on as Head of Global Public Sector in March 2023, five months after the Army awarded a $432M contract to ServiceNow via Carahsoft. Both the individual (Raj Iyer) and President/COO CJ Desai departed in July 2024 following an internal investigation. The DOJ, DoD OIG, and Army Suspension and Debarment Office are cooperating with an ongoing investigation. The Q2 2026 10-Q (Note 17) confirms the investigation remains active and the company continues to cooperate with the DOJ's document production requests. No debarment proceedings have been disclosed and no changes to risk factor language were noted since the FY2025 10-K. No board-level revolving-door members were identified in EDGAR disclosures.

3. PAC & Executive Political Contributions

Corporate PAC

ServiceNow's corporate PAC (registered as a separate segregated fund) disbursed $293,862 in the 2024 election cycle. No outside spending (super PAC or dark money) reported. Partisan split data was not obtainable via direct FEC API access for this filing; historically, enterprise software PACs tend toward a bipartisan split favouring incumbents on relevant committees (Armed Services, Government Oversight, Technology).

CEO / CFO Individual Contributions

No confirmed individual donations from Bill McDermott (CEO) or Gina Mastantuono (President & CFO) to partisan committees were identified via FEC searches in this analysis. This is consistent with the pattern of large-enterprise CEOs who engage the administration through direct dialogue and product alignment rather than visible partisan donations.

Overall Assessment

ServiceNow's PAC is small relative to peers. Political contribution activity is not a primary channel of influence for this company — the GSA OneGov deal and direct CEO-to-administration dialogue represent the more substantive connection.

Q2 2026 Update: Armis Acquisition and Defense Intelligence Expansion

ServiceNow completed its acquisition of Armis Security Ltd. in April 2026 ($6.4B consideration, $6.3B goodwill added per Q2 2026 balance sheet). Armis provides cyber asset intelligence to over 2,700 enterprise and government customers globally, with known exposure to defense and intelligence agency sectors. This acquisition materially expands ServiceNow's addressable government market beyond traditional ITSM into cybersecurity asset management, which is a high-priority area under the current administration's national security AI framework and the DoD Cybersecurity Maturity Model Certification (CMMC) programme. The combined SecurityOps + Armis + Veza (identity governance) stack is now being positioned explicitly for intelligence community and defense agency customers requiring IL5/National Security Cloud certification.

Total Identifiable Federal Contract Value

Approximately $1.19B across three named contracts (Army + Air Force + HHS), with the GSA OneGov deal adding an undisclosed ceiling. The Air Force contract alone allocated ~$37.2M in FY2025 operations funds.

Revenue Concentration

Q2 2026 10-Q notes that direct sales represented 75–76% of revenues for H1 2026, down from 78% in H1 2025, indicating slightly higher partner/reseller channel mix. The FY2024 disclosure that one U.S. federal government reseller/customer accounted for approximately 11% of revenues has not been updated for FY2025 or H1 2026 in the Q2 filing. Federal revenue continues to be sold substantially through Carahsoft as the primary reseller; this creates a channel concentration risk distinct from end-customer diversification.

DOGE Risk Assessment

Despite broad DOGE cost-cutting rhetoric, federal IT contract spending reached ~$130B in FY2025 (vs. $126B in FY2024). ServiceNow's workflow automation, ITSM, and now cybersecurity products sit in the "government efficiency" narrative — they are more likely to be positioned as tools of DOGE-driven consolidation than targets of it. The GSA OneGov deal (September 2025) was explicitly framed as aligned with President Trump's executive order on procurement consolidation. The Q2 2026 10-Q notes that 12-month agreement cadence with the federal government peaks in Q3 (government fiscal year end), which creates an important seasonal tailwind for Q3 2026 reporting.

  2.   3. ServiceNow Federal Forum (February 2025): McDermott headlined a major federal government-focused industry event in National Harbor, MD, weeks after Trump's inauguration, reinforcing the company's positioning as the enterprise platform of choice for federal modernization.

  4. The One Big Beautiful Bill Act (H.R. 1, enacted July 4, 2025) introduced tax code amendments that the Q2 2026 10-Q notes had immaterial impact on NOW's Q2 effective tax rate (32%), confirming no adverse fiscal policy impact.

  5. Armis Acquisition Defense Angle (Q2 2026 update): The April 2026 acquisition of Armis (cyber asset intelligence, $6.4B) directly expands ServiceNow's addressable market into defense and intelligence cybersecurity — sectors favoured by current administration national security priorities. This deepens the structural alignment beyond ITSM into security/AI governance.

  6. Drag factor: The unresolved DOJ investigation (revolving-door hire of Army CIO) is a structural liability that constrains the relationship from reaching score 5. The Q2 2026 10-Q confirms the investigation remains active with ongoing document production. No resolution timeline is known.

6. Trade Policy & Tariff Exposure Update

EDGAR Baseline (§8.6)

ServiceNow's §8.6 Administration Sensitivity Score (from the Financial Analysis) is rated Medium, noting growing but unquantified federal revenue, an active DOJ investigation, and DOGE-era federal IT budget sensitivity as the primary risks.

Q2 2026 10-Q Macro Commentary

The Q2 2026 10-Q MD&A explicitly addresses macroeconomic risk including "interest rates, global inflation and tariffs" noting that the company "has not experienced any impact to our liquidity or to our current and projected business operations and financial condition due to recent macroeconomic events." Management explicitly states it "will continue to monitor the direct and indirect impact of macroeconomic events on our business and financial results."

Trade Policy Assessment — Software-First Business Model

ServiceNow is a software company with a SaaS delivery model; it does not manufacture hardware and imports no physical goods subject to tariffs. Tariff exposure is entirely indirect, operating through three channels:

Low-medium — committed equipment schedule but NOT a hyperscaler at scale; AWS/Azure/GCP cost pass-through is a secondary risk

One Big Beautiful Bill Act — Tax Update (Q2 2026 10-Q)

H.R. 1, enacted July 4, 2025, brought significant U.S. tax code amendments. The Q2 2026 10-Q confirms the enacted legislation had immaterial impact on NOW's effective tax rate for Q2 2026. Effective tax rate was 32% vs. 18% in Q2 2025, with the increase driven primarily by mix of earnings/losses across jurisdictions and SBC shortfalls, not by H.R. 1. This removes a regulatory policy risk that had been flagged as uncertain.

Trade Policy Net Assessment

Tariff exposure for ServiceNow is negligible to low at the direct level. The Q2 2026 management commentary explicitly confirms no observed impact from tariffs. Indirect data centre cost inflation is real industry-wide (industry-wide AI data centre tariff costs estimated at $6B in 2025) but represents a fraction of ServiceNow's cost structure. The more material trade risk is geopolitical — specifically, restrictions on selling AI-enabled software to certain countries (China, Russia) or regulations on cross-border data flows. ServiceNow's EU data residency investments and FedRAMP certifications are a structural hedge against data sovereignty risk.

Narrative Assessment

ServiceNow occupies an unusually advantageous position in the current US political environment: its workflow automation, AI governance, and — following the April 2026 Armis acquisition — cybersecurity asset management platforms are precisely the tools the Trump administration has chosen to associate with its government modernisation and national security AI agenda. The September 2025 GSA OneGov deal — offering discounts of up to 70% to all eligible federal agencies, endorsed by GSA acting administrator Rigas, and aligned with Trump's procurement consolidation executive order — represents a structural competitive moat against rivals in the federal ITSM and AI governance market. CEO Bill McDermott's publicly acknowledged "active, ongoing discussions with senior officials" signals a relationship that goes beyond transactional contracting to include strategic positioning. The Armis acquisition materially deepens this advantage by extending into defense and intelligence cybersecurity spending — a high-priority, budget-protected area under the current administration's national security framework.

The DOJ investigation into the Raj Iyer hire is the single largest political liability on the balance sheet. The Q2 2026 10-Q (Note 17, filed 2026-07-23) confirms the investigation remains active with ongoing document production to the DOJ, DoD OIG, and Army Suspension and Debarment Office. Two years have elapsed since the July 2024 departures and no debarment proceedings or criminal charges have been publicly announced; continued contract wins (Air Force, GSA OneGov, Armis cross-sell into government) suggest the practical risk remains contained. The Q2 2026 10-Q risk factor language is unchanged from the FY2025 10-K — no escalation signal. Nevertheless, the investigation remains unresolved and constitutes a lasting headline risk with a non-quantifiable debarment tail.

The federal revenue concentration risk is material. A single customer (almost certainly Carahsoft, the primary federal reseller) accounted for ~11% of FY2024 revenues. Carahsoft itself is under a separate DOJ/FBI investigation (October 2024 raid). ServiceNow's direct-to-government GSA OneGov deal may partly reflect a desire to reduce this channel concentration over time. The Q2 2026 10-Q notes that direct sales mix declined from 78% to 75–76% in H1 2026, partially reflecting expanded partner channel from Armis integration, which adds a new dimension of channel management risk.

Lobbying spend is modest relative to revenue and peers, consistent with a company that competes on product alignment and executive relationships rather than traditional Washington influence infrastructure. Trade and tariff exposure is negligible for a software company; the Q2 2026 management commentary explicitly confirms no observed impact. The One Big Beautiful Bill Act (H.R. 1, enacted July 4, 2025) had immaterial tax impact per the Q2 2026 10-Q. The more meaningful geopolitical risks relate to AI export controls and data sovereignty regulations, both of which ServiceNow is actively managing through FedRAMP certifications and EU data residency investments.

Overall Political Risk Rating: MEDIUM

The administration connectivity, GSA OneGov deal, and Armis defense expansion represent genuine revenue upside with clear policy tailwinds. The unresolved DOJ investigation, Carahsoft single-reseller concentration (itself under investigation), and the potential for DOGE priorities to shift after 2026 or for a future administration to alter the federal AI procurement landscape prevent a "Low" rating. Resolution of the DOJ investigation is the single most important political risk catalyst to monitor.
