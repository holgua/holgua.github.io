About the Bond

Germany's federal government — the Federal Republic of Germany, rated AAA by all three major credit-rating agencies — issues bonds to finance itself, just as any national government does. Most of those bonds pay a fixed nominal coupon: the annual payment is stated at issuance and does not change. This bond is different. It is an inflation-linked bond (sometimes called a "linker"): both the coupon payment and the final repayment of principal at maturity are adjusted upward in line with actual inflation. The reference measure is the Harmonised Index of Consumer Prices excluding tobacco, a Eurostat series that tracks how much the average basket of goods and services in the euro area costs over time. When consumer prices rise, the bond's payments rise proportionally.

The bond was issued in February 2021 with a real coupon of 0.10% per year — a nominal rate so low as to be almost trivial. What matters is the inflation adjustment. By 2026-06-16, consumer prices have risen enough since issuance that the indexation coefficient stands at 1.25675: every €100 of original face value now commands €125.68 in inflation-adjusted terms. The bond was issued at €10.65 billion and matures on 2033-04-15, roughly seven years from now.

A buyer pays the dirty price of €120.09 per €100 original face value at settlement, not €95.47. The €95.47 is the quoted clean price in real terms. To get the actual euros that change hands, multiply by the index ratio (1.25675), then add accrued interest.

The bond matures on 2033-04-15. At that point, the full inflation-adjusted principal is repaid in a single lump sum. A holder who reaches maturity and reinvests the annual coupon receives a real return equal — by construction — to the real yield embedded in the price paid at purchase.

The Case for the Bet

The case rests on one structural claim: inflation protection at near-zero credit risk, at a price that embeds a positive real return. Germany has never defaulted on a euro-denominated obligation. The HICP inflation index is a long-established Eurostat series. Together, those two facts mean the position carries essentially no issuer risk and no index-integrity risk under normal conditions. The real yield at market price — and how it drifts over time — is the primary signal for whether the position remains worth holding.

Three conditions must keep holding for the bet to work:

  1. The real yield must not stay persistently below −1.0%. Specifically, the real yield at current market price must not remain below −1.0% for two consecutive quarters while EUR cash or conventional Bunds offer a superior real return. At that point, the inflation-hedge benefit would be outweighed by a guaranteed real loss baked into the price.
  2. Germany's sovereign credit must remain strong. A downgrade below AA− by any two of the three major agencies (Fitch, Moody's, S&P) would signal a structural change in issuer creditworthiness and trigger an exit.
  3. The HICP inflation index must not be materially restructured. If Eurostat were to replace the reference index with a measure that no longer tracks realised consumer-price inflation, the bond's inflation-protection mechanism would be severed.

The Rules

The pre-committed rules for this holding translate each state of the bet and of the price into a specific action:

  • If the bet is working as intended: hold. Real yield is within band, German credit is intact, and HICP linkage provides inflation protection as intended.
  • If the bet is weakening but not broken: do not add. Re-assess if real yield moves below 0% but exit condition (1) has not yet fired; compare real after-tax return against EUR cash and conventional Bunds before adding.
  • If one or more exit conditions fires: exit the full position. Move proceeds to nominal short-duration EUR Bunds or EUR cash.
  • If real yield rises above 1.50%: add to the position. A real yield above 1.50% on a seven-year AAA EUR sovereign linker is highly attractive; scale in proportionally to the inflation-hedge allocation target.
  • If real yield falls below −0.50%: trim 25% on first crossing. If real yield remains below −0.50% at the next quarterly review (at least 90 calendar days later), trim an additional 25%. Park proceeds in EUR cash or short conventional Bunds. The 25-plus-25 ladder is bounded — further crossings do not trigger additional trims until exit condition (1) fires.
  • If the compound exit trigger fires — real yield below −1.0% for at least 90 days and EUR cash now offering a superior real return — exit the full position. Switch proceeds to EUR cash or short-duration nominal Bunds; the inflation-hedge function is no longer additive versus the available alternative.

The Conditions Today

Real yield. The real yield as of 2026-06-16 is 0.78%. This sits above the 0.50% fair-value anchor and within the holding band, which runs from −0.50% at the trim threshold to 1.50% at the add threshold. A real return of 0.78% above inflation represents fair compensation for the duration risk (roughly seven years to maturity) at current EUR interest rates. Exit condition (1) is not approached: the yield would need to fall from 0.78% to below −1.0% and stay there for two consecutive quarters to trigger that exit.

Break-even inflation. The break-even inflation rate — the market's implied average inflation expectation over the remaining holding period, derived by comparing this bond's real yield with the nominal yield on conventional German bonds of similar maturity — is 1.98%. Two conventional Bunds bracket this bond's 2033-04-15 maturity: one maturing in February 2033 (nominal yield 2.75%) and one in August 2033 (nominal yield 2.78%). Interpolating between them gives a nominal midpoint of approximately 2.76%, and subtracting the real yield of 0.78% yields a break-even of 1.98%. That figure sits almost exactly at the European Central Bank's stated 2% inflation target: the market prices in target-level inflation over the remaining roughly seven years, not a speculative overshoot. No bubble premium for inflation protection is baked into the price.

German sovereign credit. Germany is rated AAA by Fitch, Aaa by Moody's, and AAA by S&P. No rating watch or negative outlook is in effect at any agency. Exit condition (2) — a downgrade below AA− by two agencies — is far from triggered.

Inflation index integrity. No Eurostat announcement of any restructuring of the HICP ex-tobacco methodology is known as of 2026-06-15. The mechanism that links this bond's payments to realised consumer-price inflation remains intact. Exit condition (3) is inactive.

The Standing Orders

The rules translate the real-yield bands into three standing orders, anchored to specific clean prices as of 2026-06-16.

Standing add order: real yield ≥ 1.50% / clean price ≤ €91. If the bond's clean price falls to €91 or below, the embedded real return has risen to 1.50% or above. At that level, a seven-year AAA EUR sovereign linker offers an attractively priced inflation hedge. The rules call for adding in tranches once the clean price closes at or below this line and the conditions are still working.

Standing trim order: real yield ≤ −0.50% / clean price ≥ €104. If the bond's clean price rises to €104 or above, the embedded real return has fallen to −0.50% or below. A guaranteed real loss is now embedded in the purchase price. The rules call for trimming 25% on first crossing, with a potential second 25% trim after 90 days if the price has not recovered.

Standing exit order: compound trigger / clean price ≥ €108. A clean price of €108 corresponds to a real yield of approximately −1.0%. But price reaching €108 alone does not trigger an exit. The compound trigger requires both legs to fire simultaneously: real yield below −1.0% for at least 90 days, and EUR cash offering a superior real return. Both legs must be satisfied before exiting.

What Could Still Go Wrong

  • German credit deterioration. Germany's sovereign credit quality underpins the entire bet. A sustained fiscal deterioration — from prolonged defence spending obligations, demographic pension pressures, or economic underperformance — could eventually put the AAA rating under pressure. Exit condition (2) monitors this explicitly; the trigger is set at a downgrade below AA− by any two agencies.
  • HICP methodology change. The HICP ex-tobacco index has been stable for years, but Eurostat retains the ability to restructure its methodology. A material change that substituted a different inflation measure or significantly altered the index basket would weaken the bond's inflation-protection function even if German creditworthiness remained intact. Exit condition (3) monitors this.
  • Real yield falls sharply. Two scenarios could push the real yield into negative territory: the ECB cutting rates aggressively (which reduces the nominal yield on competing assets and typically compresses linker yields as money flows in), or an inflation spike (which pushes break-even inflation higher and, by the arithmetic of the real yield calculation, compresses the real yield). Either scenario could move the bond toward the trim or exit thresholds over time.
  • EUR/USD foreign exchange risk for non-EUR holders. The bond and all its payments are denominated in EUR. An investor whose reference currency is not EUR bears the additional risk that the EUR weakens against their home currency. At a EUR/USD rate of 1.1616, a 10% EUR depreciation would offset several years of the real yield advantage. This risk does not affect the thesis — which is expressed in EUR real terms — but it affects the total return for non-EUR investors.
