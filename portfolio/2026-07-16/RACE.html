Ferrari: The Bet Still Works

The Bottom Line

Data as of 2026-07-15 · Latest filing 2025-12-31.

The bet is working. Ferrari's scarcity franchise produced another year of margin expansion and earnings growth in FY2025, and no exit condition has been triggered. The share price sits in the middle of the hold band, at fair value — close enough to the intrinsic estimate that the current reading offers no margin of safety but gives no sell signal either. The one condition to watch is the Elettrica launch: the annual filing's structured data cannot confirm whether the battery-electric order book is on track to oversubscribe, and that answer will only come from the narrative sections of the FY2025 annual report. FY2025 brought another clean set of numbers from Ferrari's structured financial filing. Operating income rose 11.8% year on year to EUR 2,110 million, revenue grew 7.0% to EUR 7,146 million, and net income reached EUR 1,597 million — up 4.9% from the prior year. The operating cash flow figure of EUR 2,349 million was 1.47 times net income, a ratio that has stayed above 1.25 times in every year of the five-year filing history and is among the clearest signals that the reported earnings are real.

The valuation reading is the most notable feature of the update: the earnings yield — Ferrari's annual earnings divided by its market capitalisation — came in at 2.75%, exactly at the level defined as fair value in the analytical framework. That means the current share price of $376 implies that, if earnings keep compounding at the historical rate and the market eventually prices shares to reflect that, the long-run annual return is approximately 12%. That is not an unattractive number in isolation, but it contains no cushion. A small change in discount-rate assumptions reduces the intrinsic estimate materially, and there is no margin of safety to absorb that change. The standing guidance — hold, but do not add — reflects precisely this tension between a remarkable business and a fully priced entry point.

The gap in the data is significant and will matter more as the Elettrica launch approaches. Three of the six exit conditions cannot be verified from structured data alone, including the most important one: whether annual production volume has approached the 16,000-unit threshold that would signal the production cap is under pressure. About Ferrari

Ferrari N.V. makes its money by selling access to a myth. The company manufactures roughly 13,500 to 14,000 cars a year — a number set not by what the market can absorb but by what the production cap can defend — at prices beginning above EUR 250,000 for the entry range and rising well past EUR 2 million for limited-edition hypercars. The margin earned on each car (profit as a share of sales, before interest and tax, has averaged above 38% for the past three years) is not the product of manufacturing efficiency; it is the product of deliberate scarcity. When demand permanently exceeds supply, buyers queue rather than negotiate, residual values hold firm, and the brand's status compounds with each passing season. Ferrari has been present at every Formula One championship since 1950, and that seventy-five-year racing record is, in effect, a marketing engine with no direct cost equivalent.

The business has two revenue lines. The principal one is selling cars — the Prancing Horse range of sports cars and GT vehicles, the Purosangue (the first Ferrari with four doors, introduced in 2023 and now running at capacity), and the Icona and limited-series hypercar programmes that carry the highest average selling prices and generate the most concentrated mix enrichment. The secondary line — smaller but growing — is licensing the Ferrari brand to merchandise, theme parks, and co-branded goods. A first fully battery-electric model, the Elettrica, is targeted for delivery in 2026 at a price above EUR 500,000; the order book has not yet opened, making this the single most important variable in the next twelve months of monitoring.

The Case for the Bet

The bet on Ferrari rests on a single compounding mechanism: as long as annual production stays capped well below the number of people who want to buy a Ferrari, the company can raise prices faster than costs rise, earning margins that no conventional car manufacturer can approach. There are six conditions that must keep holding for the bet to remain intact:

  1. Annual production below 16,000 units. The scarcity architecture only functions if supply is permanently below demand. Any guidance or delivered figure above 16,000 units would signal that management is prioritising short-term revenue over the long-run pricing premium — the single event that would invalidate the thesis immediately.

Latest reading: Production volume data was not available from the FY2025 structured filing (2025-12-31). Historical guidance has pointed to roughly 13,500–14,000 units; no press release or investor communication as of 2026-07-15 has indicated a departure from that range. Verification of the exact FY2025 figure requires the annual report narrative.

  1. Profit margin above 35% on an earnings-before-interest-depreciation-tax basis. The margin level is the financial expression of the scarcity premium. A sustained fall toward 35% would indicate either that demand has weakened enough to require pricing concessions, or that the cost of developing the Elettrica is permanently compressing the earnings structure.

Latest reading: The FY2025 margin on an earnings-before-interest-depreciation-tax basis was 38.8%, an expansion of approximately 50 basis points on the prior year (38.3% in FY2024). The five-year trend is unambiguously upward: 35.9% in 2021, dipping to 34.8% in 2022, then recovering to 38.2%, 38.3%, and 38.8% in FY2023, FY2024, and FY2025 respectively. Exit condition not triggered.

  1. Operating cash flow above net income. When a business reports earnings that exceed the cash it actually collects from customers, the gap often signals that income is being manufactured through accounting rather than earned through trade. Ferrari's requirement is the opposite: its ratio of operating cash to reported income has exceeded 1.25 times in every year of the five-year filing history.

Latest reading: Net income for FY2025 was EUR 1,597 million; operating cash flow was EUR 2,349 million — a ratio of 1.47 times (2025-12-31). This is the highest ratio recorded in the five-year dataset. The structured filing does not separately disclose the cash flow statement in machine-readable form, so this figure is confirmed from the financial statements but cannot be decomposed further from the available data. Exit condition not triggered.

  1. Average selling price not declining for two consecutive years on standard models. If customers begin resisting Ferrari's asking prices on the core lineup — not just the limited-run models that are inherently lumpy — it would signal that the scarcity premium is softening at the point where it matters most for the underlying earnings trajectory.

Latest reading: Revenue per unit cannot be computed precisely from structured data because unit delivery counts are disclosed in narrative sections that were not extractable in this filing cycle. Revenue grew 7.0% year on year to EUR 7,146 million (FY2025 vs FY2024), which at flat-to-low volume growth implies average selling price expansion — consistent with exit condition (4) not being triggered. Confirmation of the exact unit figure requires the annual report narrative.

  1. US import tariff stays below 25%, or pricing power absorbs it without a margin guidance cut. Ferrari exports roughly 30% of deliveries to the Americas. A return to the 25% tariff rate imposed briefly in 2025, combined with a management statement that the margin cannot absorb it, would demonstrate that pricing power has a ceiling the thesis did not account for.

Latest reading: The tariff rate on EU automobile imports to the United States stands at 15% as of 2026-07-15, following the EU-US agreement that reduced the rate from 25%. When the 25% rate was in effect, Ferrari raised prices on selected models by up to 10% and reported a 50 basis-point earnings headwind — a headwind subsequently removed. The narrative filing sections that would confirm current tariff-related guidance were not available from structured data. Exit condition not triggered at current rate.

  1. Elettrica order book oversubscribes within 12 months of opening. The first battery-electric Ferrari is priced above EUR 500,000 and positioned as the opening of a new pricing tier, not a concession to electrification. If the order book requires price concessions or extended time to fill, it would signal that the luxury electrification thesis is harder than assumed and that the Elettrica is a margin headwind rather than a margin tailwind.

Latest reading: The Elettrica order book has not yet opened as of 2026-07-15; delivery is targeted for 2026. No uptake data exists to assess. This condition cannot be tested until the book opens. The relevant monitoring point is whether the opening is announced on schedule and whether press reports of allocation demand match the expectation of oversubscription.

The Share Price

At $376, the share price sits almost exactly at the analytical fair-value estimate — the level at which a 2.75% earnings yield (Ferrari's annual earnings divided by its market capitalisation) matches the rate judged consistent with a fair price for Ferrari's current earnings power plus a 9% annual compounding assumption. In practice, this means the share is neither cheap nor expensive by the methodology's own definition; it is priced to deliver the expected long-run compounding rate and nothing more.

To translate that into concrete terms: the price today implies roughly 37 times last year's earnings and roughly 27 times last year's earnings before interest, depreciation, and tax. Those are luxury-goods multiples. The nearest comparables are companies such as Hermès, where similar metrics apply and where the argument for premium pricing is structural rather than cyclical. The analytical framework assigns a buy signal at $244 — where the earnings yield rises to 4.25% and implies a long-run return of roughly 13% a year — and a trim signal at $829, where the yield falls to 1.25% and the market is in effect pricing in decades of compounding at once. The current reading sits in the middle of that range, with ample distance to both edges.

What's Wobbling

There is one material wobble in the FY2025 update, and it is structural rather than financial: four of the seven thesis drivers were recorded as silent because Ferrari's annual European filing format does not provide machine-readable access to the narrative portions of the report. This means that production volume discipline, Elettrica launch status, tariff response, and governance signals cannot be confirmed or denied from the structured data alone.

This is a data-source limitation, not a thesis signal. The three drivers that can be tested numerically — operating margin, revenue growth, and net income growth — all confirmed the thesis in FY2025. No exit condition was triggered by the structured data. But the silence on narrative drivers means that exit conditions (1), (5), and (6) — the production volume cap, the tariff absorption test, and the Elettrica order-book oversubscription — are unverified rather than confirmed. The practical implication is that a reading of the Ferrari FY2025 annual report PDF is an outstanding monitoring obligation. The financial numbers are clean; the narrative read is pending.

What Could Still Go Wrong

  • Volume discipline breaks. If Ferrari management decides that a one-time production increase — to 15,000 or 16,000 units — is a rational response to a demand opportunity, the scarcity premium begins to erode. The brand recovers slowly, if at all. This is the single highest-consequence risk.
  • Battery-electric execution disapppoints. The Elettrica is priced above EUR 500,000 and positioned as a new pricing tier. If the order book fills slowly, requires concessions, or sees early cancellations, the Elettrica shifts from margin tailwind to margin headwind, and the multi-year earnings trajectory changes.
  • Margin compression from development costs. Building the company's first battery-electric platform is capital-intensive. If research and development costs and factory investment run ahead of schedule, the earnings margin could compress toward 34–35% before the Elettrica revenue ramp covers the spend. The financial filing does not separately disclose capital expenditure, so the exact magnitude of this investment is not visible from structured data.
  • Luxury spending contracts. A deep recession among high-net-worth individuals — concentrated wealth destruction rather than broad economic weakness — could temporarily soften demand enough to slow the order backlog. Ferrari's multi-year order queue provides a buffer; it does not provide immunity.
  • Multiple compression without an earnings change. At 37 times earnings, Ferrari's share price leaves almost no room for a change in how the market classifies the business. A reassessment from luxury-goods to premium-automotive multiples — even with the earnings trajectory intact — would produce a significant price decline without any underlying deterioration in the business itself.

This is a recurring limitation of the data source, not an anomaly. This approach treats net income as a proxy for owner earnings on the grounds that depreciation and amortisation approximately equal maintenance capital expenditure. The actual maintenance capital expenditure figure is not available from the structured filing. If capital expenditure is materially higher than depreciation — which the implied figure of roughly EUR 944 million suggests — the override may overstate sustainable owner earnings.

The Rules

If the thesis is confirmed — all exit conditions intact, all measurable drivers positive — hold with no action. Ferrari's moat is working; the right response is to continue compounding.

If the thesis weakens — any driver shows deterioration without triggering a written exit condition — do not add to the position. Investigate the specific weakening driver, most likely the earnings margin trend or Elettrica uptake. Monitor for one full fiscal year before reassessing the position size.

If the thesis breaks — any single exit condition fires, in particular exit condition (1) where production guidance or delivered figures exceed 16,000 units — exit the full position within 30 days. The moat-breaking event destroys the compounding mechanism irreversibly. No averaging down.

If the share price falls to the buy edge ($244) — confirm the thesis is intact and no exit condition has been triggered, then add in tranches over one to two quarters. A price at that level, where the yield rises to 4.25%, is a rare entry into an exceptional franchise.

If the share price rises to the trim edge ($829) — trim 25–33% of the position to harvest the premium. At that yield level the market is discounting decades of compounding at once; disciplined reduction preserves the optionality to re-enter at a better price.

If the share price rises to the exit edge — exit the full position. The market would be pricing the business at a yield below 1.0%, implying bubble-territory multiples for a single equity. Redeploy the proceeds. No re-entry within 90 days.

Scenario Definitions

A scenario is a joint assumption about two independent variables: operating trajectory (how the holding's underlying cash flows grow relative to their long-run trend) and exit multiple (what the market pays for those cash flows at the horizon, relative to a fair-value anchor).

Scenario Operating trajectory Exit multiple
Bear Below trend Below fair value
Base At trend At fair value
Bull Above trend At or above fair value

Non-negotiable rule: In the bear case, both dimensions must be adverse simultaneously — a scenario where only the multiple moves is a re-rating scenario, not a bear.

Bear cases are comparable in structure across all holding types (company, ETF, bond), but not in magnitude. A company bear reflects idiosyncratic execution failure; an ETF bear reflects a broad market drawdown. Different mitigations, different diversification implications.
