About the Fund

World Equal Weight tracks the MSCI World Equal Weighted Index, which takes the same roughly 1,500 companies that make up the standard MSCI World (a widely followed benchmark of developed-market equities, covering about 23 countries) and assigns each one an identical share weight at the point of each quarterly rebalance. The result is a portfolio where a small Swiss pharmaceutical company and a large American technology firm begin each quarter with precisely the same 0.07% weight — a deliberate contrast to the standard cap-weighted index, where the largest companies by market value hold the most weight and the smallest hold the least.

The fund is managed by Invesco, an Irish-domiciled UCITS (a pan-European fund structure) vehicle under the ticker MWEQ on Frankfurt's Xetra exchange. It uses optimised or sampling replication, meaning it holds a representative subset of the index's roughly 1,500 constituents rather than every one individually. The fund is an accumulating share class, so dividends and income are reinvested rather than distributed. At 0.20% a year in annual charges, it is competitively priced for an equal-weight strategy, which incurs meaningfully more turnover than a cap-weighted fund due to the quarterly rebalancing required to restore equal weights after prices drift. The fund launched in September 2024 and had accumulated approximately €1.48 billion in assets as of mid-2026.

Annual cost: 0.20% total expense ratio. The fund is an Irish-domiciled UCITS, is not subject to US K-1 tax reporting, and retains capital gains within the fund rather than distributing them.

The Case for the Bet

The structural case for holding this fund rests on one idea: that concentrating a developed-market equity allocation in a handful of the world's largest companies by market value — as every standard cap-weighted index does — introduces a form of implicit momentum bet. By the time a company reaches the top of the cap-weighted rankings, its share price reflects a great deal of expected future growth, and the entry multiple is correspondingly high. Equal weighting removes that concentration by construction. The basket's earnings yield (total profits relative to total market value across ~1,500 companies) is expected to run structurally higher than a cap-weighted equivalent, because smaller and mid-sized companies, as well as non-US markets that are underrepresented in cap-weighted MSCI World, receive proportionally more weight.

The bet holds as long as six conditions remain in place:

  1. The basket earns more than the risk-free rate. Specifically, the equity risk premium (the basket's earnings yield minus the 10-year US government bond yield) must remain positive for two consecutive quarters. A negative premium — meaning government bonds pay more than the basket of 1,500 businesses earns collectively — signals that the risk of owning equities is not being compensated, and the holding becomes indefensible.

Five consecutive non-null quarterly readings are all positive; the persistence trigger for an exit requires two negative readings in a row.   1. The annual cost stays manageable. Invesco must not raise the fund's annual expense ratio above 0.30%. What's Wobbling

Two of the six drivers sit in a cautionary middle zone rather than a clean pass.

The equity risk premium is thin. At +0.38 percentage points above the 10-year government bond yield, the basket is barely earning more than what a lender to the US government receives risk-free. The thesis requires a minimum of 2.0 percentage points of excess return before the equity case is fully confirmed. The current reading is positive — exit condition (1) requires two consecutive negative readings, and the history shows five consecutive non-null positive readings — but the gap is narrow enough that a modest rise in government bond yields, or a slight compression in the basket's earnings yield, would push the reading toward zero. The risk-free rate used here is the US 10-year Treasury yield; the equivalent German government bond yield would likely produce a modestly higher equity risk premium, as noted in the holding's threshold calibration notes.

The basket's drift leaders show middling earning power. The top ten holdings by weight at the 2026-07-06 snapshot — dominated by semiconductor and technology-hardware names that have appreciated most since the last quarterly rebalance — show a median return-on-equity of 14.14%. That sits just below the 15.0% level required for a full confirmation. The important context is that this top-ten list is a snapshot of the quarter's price-momentum winners, not the full equal-weight basket of 1,500 companies; the broader basket necessarily includes many ordinary businesses in industrials, utilities, and materials that a focused quality investor would not touch. The exit condition requires the median to fall below 12.0%; at 14.14% there is meaningful distance from that line. Intel's negative return-on-equity (-2.9%) and several sub-10% readings pull the median down; none of these individually triggers the exit condition.

Neither wobble is close to a written exit line, and the vehicle-integrity checks — expense ratio, fund size, single-name concentration — all pass comfortably.

What Could Still Go Wrong

  • Multiple compression in a higher-rate environment. The bear case in the value analysis assumes a terminal earnings multiple of 12× against today's 20.5×, producing an estimated annual return of about -4.9% over five years. The basket's thin equity risk premium makes this scenario more plausible than it would be if the yield gap were wider: a sustained rise in risk-free rates, without a corresponding rise in basket earnings, would push the equity risk premium negative and fire exit condition (1) before the multiple compression fully plays out.

  • Fund closure from asset drain. If the basket underperforms cap-weighted alternatives persistently, net outflows could push assets below the €800 million early-warning level, then toward the €500 million survival floor. Invesco has closed sub-scale UCITS products before. The current €1.48 billion provides a meaningful cushion, but the fund is only approximately 22 months old and has not been tested through a prolonged period of equal-weight underperformance.

  • Rebalancing drag compounding. The quarterly rebalance that makes equal-weighting possible also generates about 30% annual portfolio turnover. In a market where the largest names continue to outperform — as they have for much of the past decade — the systematic selling of winners and buying of laggards creates a structural headwind. This drag is permanent and priced into the 0.20% annual cost, but its market-impact cost in conditions of concentrated momentum is not.

  • Index-methodology change. MSCI Inc. maintains the index methodology and can alter it. A shift away from equal weighting — for instance toward a factor-tilt or market-cap hybrid — would change the product materially. There is no automated early-warning signal for this risk; it requires ongoing monitoring.

The Rules

The rules below apply regardless of the cost at which shares were acquired. They are derived from the pre-committed conditions and price bands.

  • If the thesis remains intact and the price is within the hold band: hold — equal-weight basket is reasonably priced, vehicle is sound, equal-weight earnings-yield premium over the cap-weighted alternative remains intact.
  • If the thesis is weakened (any driver deteriorates but no exit condition fires): do not add. Re-validate after the next quarter. If the equal-weight earnings-yield premium over the cap-weighted global index compresses below 0.5 percentage points for two consecutive quarters, the diversification benefit no longer justifies the higher annual cost compared with a cap-weighted alternative; consider consolidating.
  • If the thesis is broken (any exit condition fires): exit. Vehicle integrity breached (fund size, annual cost, single-name concentration, or methodology change) or the value test failed. Move proceeds to a cap-weighted global equity fund or to risk-free assets.
  • If the price falls below the buy edge ($4.6): add to the position. The equal-weight basket is meaningfully cheap relative to risk-free; the structural earnings-yield premium over cap-weighted alternatives should make this signal rare and decisive.
  • If the price rises above the trim edge ($7.3): trim 25–33%. The equal-weight basket is meaningfully expensive relative to risk-free; harvest the premium and redeploy at the next rebalance.
  • If the price rises above the exit edge ($9.7): exit. The basket's earnings yield at or below 3.0% — fully priced relative to risk-free. Redeploy proceeds to risk-free assets or to a holding that still meets the value test.
