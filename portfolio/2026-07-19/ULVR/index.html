Unilever: The Bet Still Works

The Bottom Line

Data as of 2026-07-19 · Latest filing 2026-02-12.

The bet is working. Unilever's operating margin reached a five-year high of 17.9% in 2025, owner earnings remain well above the exit threshold, and emerging-market volumes are accelerating — all the conditions the thesis rests on are passing their most recent checks. The Share Price sits in the middle of the hold band, at fair value; neither cheap enough to add nor expensive enough to trim. The one condition to watch is the McCormick Foods transaction — a USD 44.8 billion deal that concentrates the portfolio in premium condiments but adds to an already sizeable debt load, and whose regulatory clearance is the single most consequential near-term event in the investment story.

TL;DR

Buy at p3,754. Trim at p6,006. Exit at p8,580.

At p3,754, the long-run total return is about 12% a year. At today's p4,671, about 10% a year. Both figures are steady-state estimates over a horizon of many years — they assume the business keeps compounding at today's pace and the market eventually prices shares to fair value.

Recent Development

The most recent filing covers the full year ending 31 December 2025 — the first annual report after the Magnum Ice Cream demerger completed in December 2025. The headline revenue figure fell 3.8%, from EUR 52.5 billion to EUR 50.5 billion, which looks concerning until one remembers that the Ice Cream division was removed from the consolidated group. On an organic basis, the underlying picture is the opposite: Power Brand underlying sales growth reached +4.3% in 2025 as a whole, accelerating to +5.0% in the three months ending March 2026, with volume driving +4.0% of that growth. Emerging-market organic growth hit +5.7% in the same quarter, with India contributing +7%.

Operating income rose from EUR 8.8 billion to EUR 9.0 billion despite the smaller revenue base, pushing the operating margin back to 17.9% — the top of the five-year range. Operating cash flow came in at EUR 8.4 billion, slightly below the prior two years (EUR 9.4–9.5 billion), which the analysis attributes to transition timing around the demerger rather than a structural shift. Owner earnings — operating cash flow minus an estimated EUR 1.5 billion in maintenance capital spending — settled at EUR 6.9 billion, well above the EUR 5.5 billion floor that would trigger an exit review.

Not one of the seven written exit conditions was triggered in this cycle. The full picture of conditions and current readings is in the Case for the Bet. For what the filing implies for the Share Price, see the valuation section below.

About Unilever

Unilever makes and sells everyday consumer goods — shampoo, soap, washing-up liquid, mayonnaise, skin cream, deodorant — under a portfolio of roughly 30 "Power Brands" that includes Dove, Hellmann's, Knorr, Domestos, Lifebuoy, and Axe. It earns its money the same way every major branded consumer-goods company does: consumers repurchase these products at regular intervals, brands sustain modest pricing premiums over own-label alternatives, and the company's sheer scale in procurement and logistics keeps costs lower than smaller rivals can match. The result is operating margins of 16–18% that have held steady through a decade of raw-material swings.

What sets Unilever apart from most of its peers is geography. Roughly 60% of its revenue comes from emerging markets — India, Southeast Asia, sub-Saharan Africa, Latin America — where a proprietary last-mile distribution network reaches local shopkeepers and small retailers in places where organised retail barely exists. That network took decades to build and would cost tens of billions for a competitor to replicate from scratch; it is the structural underpinning of the business's durable competitive position. The portfolio is in the middle of a deliberate simplification: the Ice Cream division (Magnum, Cornetto) was separated in December 2025 and now trades independently, and a further USD 44.8 billion sale of the Hellmann's and Knorr condiments business to McCormick Foods was signed in March 2026 with an expected close in mid-2027. After that transaction settles, Unilever will be concentrated almost entirely in Beauty and Wellbeing, Personal Care, and Home Care — the segments with the highest margins and the strongest volume growth.

The Case for the Bet

Unilever is held as a franchise whose structural advantages — brand pricing power and an irreplicable distribution network — generate returns on invested capital above the cost of capital across economic cycles. The specific conditions that must keep holding for the thesis to work are:

  1. Operating margin stays above 14% for each fiscal year, or recovers within one year if it dips below. An operating margin below 14% for two consecutive years would signal that the brand and distribution moat has begun to erode permanently — not just cyclically. The five-year floor in the recorded data is 16.6%, sustained even at the peak of the FY2022 commodity inflation cycle.

Latest reading: 17.9% in the year ending December 2025. The highest level in the five-year record; no proximity to the 14% floor.

  1. Return on invested capital stays above 10%. A sustained fall below 10% would mean the WACC spread has collapsed — the capital deployed in the business is no longer generating economic value above its cost. The watch threshold is set with a buffer above the estimated cost of capital of 8–9%, so that a single weak year does not trigger action.

Latest reading: 14.7% in FY2025, recovering from a trough of 11.5% in FY2024. The FY2024 trough was caused by goodwill impairment charges associated with the Ice Cream demerger, not by structural deterioration in the underlying business. Well above the 10% threshold.

  1. Owner earnings — operating cash flow minus estimated maintenance capital spending of EUR 1.5 billion — stay above EUR 5.5 billion per year. Below EUR 5.5 billion for two consecutive years would confirm the bear case scenario in which cash generation has fallen to the point where the dividend may be at risk and the debt load becomes harder to service.

Latest reading: EUR 6.9 billion in FY2025 (operating cash flow EUR 8.4 billion minus EUR 1.5 billion maintenance capital estimate). EUR 1.4 billion above the exit floor. The three-year average is approximately EUR 7.6 billion per year, which is the normalised baseline used for valuation.

  1. Goodwill impairment in any single year stays below EUR 3 billion unless a concurrent disposal explains it. A large unplanned impairment signals that management is recognising past acquisition failures under pressure, rather than executing deliberate portfolio rationalisation.

Latest reading: Goodwill fell from EUR 22.3 billion to EUR 17.7 billion in FY2025 — a reduction of EUR 4.6 billion. This falls outside the exit condition because it coincides with the Ice Cream demerger; the disposal fully explains the reduction. Exit condition 4 is not triggered.

  1. The accruals ratio stays below +0.10 for each fiscal year. The accruals ratio — net income minus operating cash flow, divided by total assets — measures whether reported earnings are running ahead of actual cash generation. A ratio above +0.10 sustained over two years would signal earnings quality deterioration.

Latest reading: +0.016 in FY2025. Well within bounds. The prior two years were negative (−0.047 in FY2024, −0.039 in FY2023), meaning cash earnings exceeded reported earnings — an above-average quality signal.

  1. The McCormick Foods transaction is not blocked or repriced by more than 20% below the agreed USD 44.8 billion value. A collapse or a forced large discount would confirm execution failure and leave capital stranded in lower-quality portfolio businesses that were earmarked for exit.

Latest reading: The transaction remains on track as of July 2026. Regulatory review by the US Federal Trade Commission is ongoing; no second request or enforcement action has been reported.

  1. Power Brand underlying sales growth stays above +1.5% across two consecutive trading-statement periods. A sustained fall below +1.5% would signal that the brand moat is losing pricing premium to private-label alternatives faster than emerging-market growth can offset.

Latest reading: +4.3% in FY2025 full year; +5.0% in the three months ending March 2026. Both well above the 1.5% exit threshold. Volume drove +4.0% of the Q1 2026 growth figure — a meaningful signal that consumers are choosing to buy more Unilever products, not merely accepting price increases.

The Share Price

Unilever's earnings yield — measuring annual owner cash flows against the current market price — stands at 6.43% at p4,671. The fair-value anchor, the yield at which the business is priced to return approximately what a diversified investor should expect over a long horizon, is 6.5%. At 6.43%, the share price is a rounding error away from that anchor: slightly above fair value in yield terms, or, equivalently, slightly below the implied fair-value price of p4,620.

In practical terms, the stock is trading at fair value. It is not cheap enough that the Rules would call for adding to the position, and not expensive enough that a trim is warranted. The buy edge requires the yield to rise to 8.0%, which corresponds to approximately p3,754 — a 20% decline from the current level. The trim edge requires the yield to compress to 5.0%, which corresponds to approximately p6,006 — a 29% rise.

One number puts this in context. An earnings yield of 6.43% already implies an annual return of approximately 10% when the expectation of ongoing business growth is added in — a level that sits ahead of the risk-free rate by what the Rules characterise as a modest equity premium. That is a reasonable, if not exceptional, return on a business with this quality profile. The share price is not signalling urgency in either direction.

The Standing Orders

The Rules translate the share-price bands into three standing orders and two price alerts. Each is the operationalisation of a pre-committed rule — not a new judgement made here.

Price alerts (informational).
- A first alert at p4,620, the fair-value anchor. The crossing means the share price has returned to neutral; no action follows, but a fresh read of the conditions is warranted.
- A second alert at p3,754, the cheap edge. The crossing arms the buy order below; before adding, the latest re-validation should confirm all conditions are still passing.

Standing orders (actionable).
- A standing buy order at p3,754. The Rules call for adding in two tranches over one to two quarters once the share price closes at or below this level and all conditions are still intact. The tranche approach is intended to avoid committing the full allocation into a falling price in the event that negative volume growth is the cause of the decline.
- A standing trim order at p6,006. The Rules call for trimming 25–33% of the position when the share price closes at or above this line, to harvest the premium while retaining the core holding. The trim is mechanical and does not require any deterioration in the underlying business.
- A standing exit order at p8,580. The Rules call for closing the full position at or above this level. At that price the yield compresses to approximately 3.4%, which means the market is pricing in growth running at full bull-case velocity indefinitely — a state where risk/reward asymmetry has been eliminated. No re-entry within 90 days after an exit at this level.

What's Wobbling

The most prominent wobble is leverage. Net debt stands at approximately USD 23.3 billion, at a debt-to-equity ratio of roughly 160%. The McCormick Foods transaction, expected to close in mid-2027, will require USD 44.8 billion in consideration — meaning gross debt will spike before the combined business begins generating cash that can repay it. No written exit condition covers leverage alone, and operating cash flow of EUR 8.4 billion means the current debt load is well-serviced. But the watch signal is simple: if operating cash flow as a proportion of revenue falls below 12% for two consecutive years, the leverage position becomes a structural constraint rather than a financial choice. The current reading of 16.5% is comfortably above that threshold.

A secondary wobble is the qualitative data gap. All five annual ESEF filings for Unilever failed to produce usable narrative text from the digital report format; the pipeline runs exclusively on structured financial data. This means no management commentary on strategy, risk factors, or business conditions has been directly read from any filing. The gap is partially closed by quarterly trading statements and press releases, which provided the Power Brand growth and emerging-market volume figures used in this cycle. The full annual report PDF, available from the company's investor relations website, contains the qualitative material that the structured-data pipeline cannot reach.

The third wobble is the competitive pressure on Home Care from retailer own-label products in European markets. Home Care was the weakest segment by underlying sales growth in 2025, at +1.9% over the first nine months of the year — above the +1.5% exit threshold but thin. L'Oréal remains a structural competitive threat in premium beauty, and a reported large-scale acquisition in the Kenvue hygiene and personal care space creates a new scaled rival in hygiene and skin care (the specific acquirer and deal terms require verification from current external sources). Neither has crossed any written threshold, and both are monitored.

What Could Still Go Wrong

  • EM currency collapse. Approximately 60% of Unilever's revenue comes from emerging markets. A sustained depreciation of 15% or more in the basket of those currencies — the Indian rupee, Indonesian rupiah, Brazilian real, and others — would reduce reported earnings in euros meaningfully, compress the dividend in sterling terms, and could push owner earnings below the EUR 5.5 billion exit floor if margins also compress simultaneously.
  • McCormick transaction failure or renegotiation. If the US Federal Trade Commission blocks the transaction or conditions approval on concessions that require a substantial price reduction, the strategic rationale for concentrating the portfolio in premium condiments unravels. Capital earmarked for the deal would need to be redeployed, and the goodwill written onto the balance sheet for Hellmann's, Knorr, and Marmite would need to be impaired — triggering exit condition 4.
  • Private-label substitution in European markets. European grocery retail is structurally shifting toward own-label; Aldi, Lidl, and Carrefour have expanded their private-label ranges aggressively. If that substitution accelerates and Power Brand underlying sales growth falls below +1.5% for two consecutive quarters, the brand moat is demonstrably narrowing faster than the exit condition tolerates.
  • Balance sheet stress post-McCormick close. If the combined entity's net debt rises above four times its annual earnings before interest and tax — a threshold not yet reached but plausible under an adverse scenario where McCormick integration costs run above plan — financial flexibility is materially constrained and a dividend cut becomes a possibility.
  • Management execution risk on portfolio simplification. The Growth Action Plan, the Ice Cream demerger, and the McCormick sale are three major portfolio moves in roughly three years. Simultaneous execution raises integration risk. A failure on any of the three — particularly McCormick — would damage management credibility and likely reprice the stock regardless of whether any hard exit condition is triggered.

Gaps in the Picture

Three data limitations are worth naming explicitly.

First, Unilever's earnings yield in the valuation rests on a normalised cash earnings figure of USD 7.6 billion per year. That figure requires subtracting an estimated EUR 1.5 billion in maintenance capital spending from operating cash flow — and capital expenditure is absent from Unilever's structured digital filings because the taxonomy used does not include the relevant tag. The EUR 1.5 billion estimate is based on approximately 3% of revenue, a reasonable proxy for an asset-light branded consumer goods business, but it is not a verified number. The valuation is sensitive to this assumption: if actual maintenance capital spending is materially higher, the earnings yield is lower than reported.

Second, Unilever's fair-value band cross-check — which would normally use historical trading multiples and peer multiples from companies like Procter & Gamble and Nestlé to verify the yield-based valuation — returned no usable data this cycle. The self-history band requires monthly price observations against the company's own data feed, and those observations were unavailable; Nestlé returned empty history. The yield method is therefore the sole valuation anchor, without a peer or history cross-check.

Third, no filing narrative has been extractable from any of the five Unilever annual reports processed to date. The annual reports are published in a digital format that the pipeline's text-extraction tool could not read. This means every qualitative driver — management commentary on emerging-market conditions, risk-factor discussion, strategy updates — is sourced from quarterly trading statements and press releases rather than from the filed annual document.

The Rules

The pre-committed rules govern what follows from each combination of thesis state and share price. There are six rules, each mapping a named condition to an action.

If the thesis is confirmed — all seven exit conditions passing, no deteriorating driver — then the position is held as long as the earnings yield remains between 5.0% and 8.0%, operating margin is above 14%, return on invested capital is above 10%, Power Brand underlying sales growth is above 1.5%, and the McCormick Foods transaction remains on track. The dividend yield of approximately 3.71% is the expected primary return in the base case.

If the thesis weakens — a driver deteriorating but no exit condition yet triggered — then no new capital is added. The specific deteriorating driver is reviewed against the next quarterly trading statement or annual filing, with a one-quarter observation window before any position-size decision. Monitoring tightens to quarterly for Power Brand growth and operating margin, and continuous for McCormick regulatory status and significant emerging-market currency moves.

If the thesis breaks — any single exit condition is confirmed from filed data or press releases — then the full position is exited. The exit is unconditional on conditions 1 (operating margin), 2 (return on capital), and 7 (Power Brand growth): these signal permanent moat erosion and admit no further monitoring period. No averaging down after an exit condition is confirmed.

If the share price falls to the buy edge — the earnings yield rises to 8.0% or above, corresponding to approximately p3,754 — then new capital may be added, in two tranches over one to two quarters, provided all thesis conditions are still passing and the operating margin in the most recent reported period is at or above 16%. The tranche approach is deliberate: it avoids committing the full allocation into a price that is still falling.

If the share price rises to the trim edge — the earnings yield compresses below 5.0%, corresponding to approximately p6,006 or above — then 25–33% of the position is trimmed. The trim is mechanical; it does not require any deterioration in the underlying business.

If the share price rises to the exit edge — the earnings yield compresses below 4.5%, corresponding to approximately p8,580 or above — then the full position is exited. At that yield the market is pricing the bull case as near-certain; risk/reward asymmetry is eliminated. No re-entry is permitted within 90 days.

Scenario Definitions

A scenario is a joint assumption about two independent variables: operating trajectory (how the holding's underlying cash flows grow relative to their long-run trend) and exit multiple (what the market pays for those cash flows at the horizon, relative to a fair-value anchor).

Scenario Operating trajectory Exit multiple
Bear Below trend Below fair value
Base At trend At fair value
Bull Above trend At or above fair value

Non-negotiable rule: In the bear case, both dimensions must be adverse simultaneously — a scenario where only the multiple moves is a re-rating scenario, not a bear.

Bear cases are comparable in structure across all holding types (company, ETF, bond), but not in magnitude. A company bear reflects idiosyncratic execution failure; an ETF bear reflects a broad market drawdown. Different mitigations, different diversification implications.
