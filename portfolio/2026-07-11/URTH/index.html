About the Fund

All World is a passively managed fund that tracks the MSCI World Index — a float-adjusted, market-capitalisation-weighted collection of roughly 1,500 of the largest companies across 23 developed markets. The geographic split is approximately 70% United States and 30% from the rest of the developed world: Japan, the United Kingdom, France, Germany, Switzerland, Canada, and others. The basket holds no emerging-market exposure. Its role in the portfolio is to provide broad global equity diversification beyond a purely US-listed position.

The fund is managed by iShares, a brand of BlackRock, under a Delaware statutory trust structure. No K-1 tax form is issued. The annual cost is 0.24%. Tracking difference and tracking error for the past year are not available in the current data pull; the iShares factsheet is the source to verify replication efficiency. Net cost after securities lending revenue is not quantified — iShares has not disclosed the lending programme for this fund in the current snapshot.

The Case for the Bet

The core idea is structural: by holding a basket of around 1,500 companies across 23 developed markets, the fund narrows the concentration risk of a purely US-focused position while staying anchored to the world's largest, most liquid equity markets. The bet holds as long as six conditions continue to hold:

  1. The basket's look-through earnings yield stays above the government bond yield, plus a two-percentage-point premium for equity risk. That gap — the equity risk premium — is the basic test of whether holding equities rather than government bonds makes financial sense. The minimum acceptable premium is 2%. If the premium turns negative for two consecutive calendar-quarter readings, exit condition (1) fires.

Latest reading: At 2026-07-11, the premium is −0.48%. The basket yields 4.09% in look-through earnings; the ten-year government bond yields 4.57%. This is the eleventh consecutive negative reading. The quarterly persistence test — two calendar-quarter readings both negative — has not yet formally confirmed, but the direction is unambiguous.

  1. The annual expense ratio stays below 0.35%. At 0.35%, the fund costs meaningfully more than the functionally equivalent IWDA UCITS fund (0.20%), with no compensating feature. A rise above that ceiling is exit condition (2).

Latest reading: At 2026-07-11, the annual cost is 0.24%, leaving 11 basis points of headroom to the ceiling. Exit condition (2) is not triggered.

  1. Total assets stay above $2 billion. Below that level, bid-ask spreads on a global blend fund tend to widen materially, increasing the hidden cost of transacting. That threshold is exit condition (3).

Latest reading: At 2026-07-11, total assets are $8.06 billion — 4.03 times the floor. Exit condition (3) is not triggered.

  1. The largest single holding stays below 10% of the basket. A single company crossing 10% of a global, cap-weighted index would signal an unusual degree of market concentration — not normal drift but a regime change. That threshold is exit condition (4).

Latest reading: At 2026-07-11, Nvidia holds 5.17% of the basket, 4.83 percentage points below the 10% ceiling. Exit condition (4) is not triggered.

  1. The MSCI World index methodology is not materially restructured. The value of a passive global fund rests on the stability of the index it tracks. A change that drops major developed-market countries, shifts from float-adjusted capitalisation weighting, or otherwise undermines the developed-markets mandate would break the structural premise. That is exit condition (5).

Latest reading: No signal of methodology change is available through automated monitoring. This condition is watched externally.

  1. The median return on equity of the basket's ten largest holdings stays above 15%. Return on equity (the profit a company earns relative to the capital it has retained) at the median of the top ten measures whether the basket's biggest positions are genuinely compounding vehicles. A collapse to 15% or below is exit condition (6).

Latest reading: At 2026-07-11, the median return on equity of the nine largest deduped holdings is 37.28% — well above the 15% floor. The weighted average (70.42%) is distorted upward by Nvidia and Apple, whose aggressive share buybacks have shrunk book equity to near-zero; the median is the more reliable read. Exit condition (6) is not triggered.

The current trajectory makes this the most probable near-term risk.
  • The share price reaches the trim edge before the conditions improve. A close at or above $209 triggers the trim order. The price today is $204.63. This is an independent, near-term event risk that does not require any change in underlying fundamentals.

If the earnings yield falls further — driven by rising prices rather than rising earnings — the exit threshold of 3.0% (a price of $279) comes into view. At that point the basket would offer a return well below the long-run equity premium on a yield basis alone.

The 10% hard limit is 4.83 percentage points away.

A BlackRock restructuring that transfers URTH's mandate, or an iShares fee increase above 0.35%, would trigger the operational exit conditions independently of valuation. Both are low-probability events given BlackRock's scale, but neither can be monitored automatically. (For this fund, exit fires when the share price reaches the exit edge, when a vehicle-integrity condition breaches, or when a named exit condition triggers — any of three paths.)

The Rules

The pre-committed rules that govern this position cover six situations:

  1. If all drivers are working — the vehicle is sound and the earnings yield is in the hold band without any driver flagging concern — hold. The basket is reasonably priced and the vehicle is sound.

  2. If any driver is under pressure but no exit condition has formally fired — the current situation, with the equity risk premium negative — do not add. Re-validate after the next quarter. If the premium stays negative or the median look-through return on equity keeps falling, swap to the IWDA UCITS alternative (which costs 0.20% annually rather than 0.24%) or move to cash per the position-sizing rule.

  3. If an exit condition fires — vehicle integrity breached, or an exit condition formally triggered — exit the full position. Move the proceeds to a risk-free instrument or to a holding that currently meets the value test.

  4. If the share price falls to or below the buy edge of $134 — the basket's earnings yield rising to 6.25% or above — add to the position. The basket is meaningfully cheap relative to the risk-free rate. Note: this rule only applies when no driver is simultaneously flagging concern; the current negative equity risk premium would need to have cleared first.

  5. If the share price rises to or above the trim edge of $209 — the basket's earnings yield at or below 4.0% — trim 25–33% of the position. The basket is meaningfully expensive relative to the risk-free rate; harvest the premium while keeping the core.

  6. If the share price rises to or above the exit edge of $279 — the basket's earnings yield at or below 3.0% — exit the full position. At that price the basket is fully priced versus the risk-free rate. Redeploy the proceeds to a risk-free instrument or to a holding that currently meets the value test.
