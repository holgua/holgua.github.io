Amazon: The Bet Still Works

The Bottom Line

The bet is working. The three-part compounding engine — cloud computing, advertising, and the international retail segment — all confirmed positive in Amazon's latest quarterly filing, covering the three months to 2026-03-31. The share price at $250 sits in the upper half of the hold band, well above fair value at $176; there is no margin of safety for a fresh entry at current prices, and no reason for an existing holder whose conditions are all working to take action today. The one condition worth watching over the next year is whether Amazon's massive data-centre buildout — roughly $147 billion in capital expenditure over the past twelve months — converts into revenue fast enough to justify its scale.

About Amazon

Amazon is three distinct businesses wrapped inside one corporate structure. The most visible is the retail marketplace — a North American operation generating $426 billion in annual sales and an international arm at $162 billion — which functions primarily as a cash-generating scale engine and a source of Prime subscription loyalty. Prime (subscription revenue of nearly $50 billion in 2025) bundles fast delivery, streaming video, music, and grocery delivery into a service that has proven difficult to cancel.

The second business, and by far the most profitable on a unit basis, is Amazon Web Services (AWS), the cloud computing and infrastructure division. AWS brought in $129 billion in revenue in 2025 — 18% of total sales — but contributed 57% of total operating income, at a margin above 35%. It provides data storage, processing power, databases, and now artificial intelligence infrastructure to companies around the world who prefer renting capacity to building their own. Customers sign multi-year contracts, which creates predictable revenue and switching inertia.

The third profit pool is advertising. Amazon's advertising business — primarily the sponsored listings that appear when someone searches for a product on the marketplace — reached $69 billion in annual revenue in 2025, growing at 22%. It benefits from a uniquely powerful combination: shopping intent signals that most other advertising platforms cannot match.

Amazon's founder Jeff Bezos remains the executive chairman, holding approximately 8.8% of shares outstanding. Day-to-day operations are run by Andy Jassy, who previously ran AWS for five years before becoming chief executive in 2021.

The Case for the Bet

The central idea is that Amazon is a compounding flywheel: the retail operation generates the cash and the customer relationships that subsidise AWS and advertising, both of which are growing faster than retail and at far higher profit margins. If that dynamic continues for another three to five years, operating income should compound at a rate well above revenue growth, pulling overall cash generation higher even as the initial capital spending on AI infrastructure tapers.

For the bet to keep working, five conditions must keep holding:

  1. AWS revenue growth stays at or above 18% a year. The exit line sits at below 15% for two consecutive quarters — two data points below that level, not one, because one soft quarter could be a timing artefact. The current reading is +28%, well above both thresholds.

  2. AWS operating margin stays above 28%. The logic is that Amazon is investing heavily in AI data-centre capacity; if that investment is converting into revenue, margins should recover toward prior levels. If margins compress sustainably below 28%, something has broken in the reinvestment economics. The current reading is 37.7%, nearly 10 percentage points above the exit line.

  3. Capital expenditure normalises by 2027–2028. Total capital spending over the past twelve months reached approximately $147 billion — nearly double the prior-year level. The concern is not current spending but persistence: if capital expenditure stays above $120 billion in both 2027 and 2028 without commensurate AWS revenue acceleration, the AI buildout has overshot demand. This condition is not triggered today, but it cannot be tested until 2027.

  4. Advertising revenue continues compounding. The exit trigger is advertising growth falling to single digits for two consecutive years. At +24% in the most recent quarter, that threshold is not in view.

  5. No structural legal remedy is imposed. The US Federal Trade Commission has an active antitrust proceeding against Amazon targeting its retail marketplace practices. A court order requiring structural separation — not a fine, but an enforced breakup — would be an immediate exit trigger. There is currently no indication that such a remedy is imminent.

The Rules

If the thesis is confirmed — all drivers working, no exit condition triggered — hold the position. Continue compounding the AWS and advertising flywheel without intervention.

If the thesis is weakening — at least one driver is deteriorating but no written exit condition has fired — do not add to the position. Re-examine the specific deteriorating driver after the next two quarterly filings before deciding. One soft quarter is not enough; the second data point confirms a trend.

If the thesis is broken — a written exit condition has been triggered — close the full position within 30 days. Do not average down into a broken thesis. Note the triggered condition for future reference. AWS revenue or margin exits should be confirmed across two consecutive quarters; an antitrust structural remedy or unexpected change of auditor is an immediate exit without a second-quarter confirmation.

If the share price falls below the buy edge of $140 — and the thesis is confirmed — add to the position in tranches over one to two quarters. Confirm the thesis is intact before adding; a cheap price is not an invitation to buy into a deteriorating business.

If the share price reaches the trim edge of $263 or above — trim 25 to 33% of the position to harvest the premium while keeping the core holding.

If the share price reaches the exit edge of $421 — close the full position. At that level the market is pricing in roughly eleven years of base-case compounding instantly; the implied forward return falls below the long-run equity premium, and redeployment into something cheaper is the better use of capital.

The Conditions Today

AWS revenue growth. In the three months to 2026-03-31, AWS revenue came in at $37.6 billion, 28% higher than the same quarter a year earlier. That is the highest growth rate recorded in at least six quarters and sits well above the 18% thesis target and far above the 15% exit threshold. Amazon disclosed $364 billion in contracted but not-yet-recognised AWS revenue as of 2026-03-31, with roughly 45% to be recognised within two years — a forward indicator of the demand pipeline. This condition confirms.

AWS operating margin. The three-months-to-2026-03-31 AWS operating income of $14.2 billion on $37.6 billion in revenue works out to a margin of 37.7%. That is down from 39.4% in the equivalent quarter a year earlier, as Amazon invested in additional data-centre and AI infrastructure. The margin remains nearly ten percentage points above the 28% exit threshold. The compression is attributable to planned investment, not to pricing pressure or demand softness; the same filing cites AWS growth acceleration as the primary explanation. Mixed, but not concerning.

Capital expenditure. Over the twelve months to 2026-03-31, total capital spending reached approximately $147 billion — nearly double the $88 billion in the prior trailing-twelve-month period. The exit condition targets the 2027 and 2028 fiscal years, not the current trailing figure, and it applies only if that spending persists without commensurate AWS revenue acceleration. With AWS growing at 28% and a $364 billion backlog confirming forward demand, the "without commensurate acceleration" clause is not met today. This is the highest-priority item to watch in annual guidance — not a current trigger, but an active watch.

Advertising revenue. Advertising services reached $17.2 billion in the quarter, 24% higher than the equivalent period a year earlier. Both the North American and international retail segments cited higher advertising sales as a primary driver of improved operating income — North America's operating income rose 41% in the same quarter, in part because of it. This condition confirms.

International segment. The international retail business generated $1.4 billion in operating income in the quarter (a 3.6% margin), up from $1.0 billion and 3.0% a year earlier. The segment has moved from unprofitable to profitably scaling, adding a third operating income stream compounding above revenue growth. This condition confirms.

FTC antitrust outcome. The latest quarterly report references ongoing litigation from state attorneys-general and the Federal Trade Commission, but no structural remedy has been imposed and no new trial schedule was disclosed in the filing. The exit condition — a court-ordered structural separation — has not been triggered.

Capital allocation and insider activity. Amazon's March 2022 authorisation to repurchase $10 billion of its own shares remains $6.1 billion untouched, with zero shares repurchased in either the most recent quarter or the equivalent quarter a year ago. In March 2026, the company raised $37 billion in US-dollar bonds and €14.5 billion in euro bonds to fund the AI data-centre buildout. No Bezos Form 144 filing exceeding 40 million shares in a single filing has appeared, meaning the insider-selling exit condition is not triggered.

The Share Price

The valuation framework measures value as a yield: owner earnings (cash profit from operations minus total capital spending, then minus stock-based compensation paid to employees, which is a real cost even if non-cash) divided by the total market value of the business. The result, at current prices, is a yield of 4.2%. The fair-value anchor — the yield that implies the business is reasonably priced for a patient holder — is 6.0%. The buy edge is at 7.5% yield, which corresponds to a share price of around $140. The trim edge is at 4.0% yield, which corresponds to around $263.

At $250, the yield of 4.2% sits between fair value (6.0%) and the trim threshold (4.0%), just $13 below the line where trimming would be warranted. The share price is well above the fair-value anchor of $176 — 42% above, to be precise — meaning the market is already pricing in a significant portion of future growth. The position is inside the hold band, closer to the top than the middle.

What's Wobbling

The capital expenditure surge. Over the past twelve months Amazon spent approximately $147 billion on property, equipment, and data-centre infrastructure — capital spending at a pace that consumed nearly all of the operating cash flow it generated ($149 billion). The $364 billion in committed AWS backlog provides the counter-argument: customers have already signed contracts for that capacity. But capital expenditure at this scale is a forward bet on AI demand absorbing the infrastructure before depreciation creates margin pressure. The wobble does not trigger any exit condition today; it becomes a real test in 2027 and 2028.

The tariff environment in retail. Amazon's own quarterly filing flags that China-based marketplace sellers account for a significant portion of its third-party seller revenues and advertising income, and that tariff policy changes could adversely affect those relationships. North American operating income still rose 41% in the quarter despite this headwind, suggesting the exposure is being managed — but it is real, and there is no written exit condition that maps directly to tariff drag unless advertising growth eventually slows to single digits.

The absence of share repurchases. Amazon has not repurchased a single share under its March 2022 buyback authorisation, leaving $6.1 billion unused while the share price fell materially in 2022 and 2023. The thesis characterises Amazon's capital allocation as "acceptable but not excellent" — this is the acceptable-but-not-excellent part. The rules do not require buybacks, and no exit condition is triggered by their absence; it is simply a drag on per-share compounding that a more shareholder-oriented management team might have avoided.

The fair-value anchor divergence. A separate bottom-up scenario analysis, built from peer multiples and Amazon's own historical valuation ranges, produces a base-case central estimate of $554 per share — more than three times the $176 anchor derived from the yield-based method. The two figures are not contradictory so much as they measure different things: the yield method asks what price produces a reasonable return from today's earnings; the scenario analysis asks what the market might pay for those earnings if Amazon keeps compounding. Neither is wrong, but the divergence is large enough to warrant attention before the next add or trim decision.

What Could Still Go Wrong

  • AWS market share erosion in AI workloads. Microsoft Azure, through its partnership with OpenAI, and Google Cloud, through its Gemini models, are competing aggressively for the AI training and inference work that is driving AWS's acceleration. If those competitors capture the majority of new AI workload migrations, AWS's 28% growth rate could slow faster than the contracted backlog implies — the backlog reflects where customers have already committed, not where they will commit next.

  • AI capital expenditure stranding. Amazon spent $128 billion on capital expenditure in 2025 and has indicated spending will be higher in 2026. If AI demand does not absorb data-centre capacity at the pace implied by the backlog, the assets will depreciate against a lower-than-expected revenue base, compressing margins. The server useful-life estimate was already shortened from six years to five in 2025, adding depreciation cost.

  • A structural antitrust remedy. The Federal Trade Commission's monopoly case against Amazon's retail practices is unresolved as of the most recent filing, with a trial expected in late 2026. A court-ordered requirement to spin out the marketplace, or to separate the Prime logistics network from third-party fulfilment, would fundamentally alter the flywheel economics. The probability is difficult to assess, but the binary character of the outcome makes it the largest single tail risk.

  • European regulatory costs. Amazon's appeal of a €746 million GDPR fine was dismissed by Luxembourg courts in early 2025. Active UK and EU antitrust class actions are proceeding. These are manageable at current scale but are a persistent drag and carry the risk of escalation.

  • Anthropic mark reversal. Amazon's 2025 net income of $77.7 billion includes roughly $15 billion in non-cash income from marking up its Anthropic AI stake. If Anthropic's private-market valuation contracts — as can happen when AI investment enthusiasm cools — those gains reverse, reducing reported earnings materially without affecting the operating business. The underlying business is solid; the reported number is distorted.

Gaps in the Picture

Owner earnings as measured here use the company's officially reported operating cash flow minus total capital expenditure as filed, then subtract stock-based compensation. This approach counts all capital spending as a cost regardless of how much is maintenance versus growth investment. Amazon's management team reports a different, and far lower, "free cash flow" definition in its own shareholder communications — one that excludes lease obligations and principal repayments. The number derived from the official filings and used in the valuation framework ($132.8 billion for 2025) is significantly higher than what management reports; the difference reflects real cash commitments that the framework treats as operating costs. Neither number is wrong, but they tell different stories, and the framework's number may overstate distributable cash flow.

The risk-free rate used to contextualise the expected return is drawn from the 10-year US government bond yield as reported in a separate portfolio holding's data file rather than from Amazon's own market data file, which does not carry the field. The rate used is 4.509%, as of 2026-06-23.

Two fair-value anchors coexist: the yield-method anchor of $176 and the bottom-up scenario estimate of $554 (peer and self-multiple analysis). A future schema migration will resolve the divergence. The yield method reflects the return implied at that price from today's earnings alone; the scenario method reflects what the market might pay for those earnings in the future. Both are documented; neither has been reconciled to a single number.

A FAIR-VALUE-DRIFT warning has been flagged in the analytical record: the scenario-based midpoint implies a yield of 1.84%, compared to the 6.0% used in the valuation framework. The divergence is 69 percentage points in yield space. The valuation band (buy at $140, trim at $263, exit at $421) is derived from the 6.0% yield anchor and is unaffected operationally — but the anchor warrants review before the next add or trim decision to confirm it still reflects current intent.
