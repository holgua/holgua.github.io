The Bottom Line

Data as of 2026-07-19 · Latest filing 2025-12-31.

The bet is working. The share price sits below the buy edge, meaningfully below fair value — the two most recent annual filings confirm that the oil-and-chemicals cash engine has kept running at better than €5 billion a year, interest cover sits at double the exit floor, and no exit condition has been triggered. Recent Development

The most significant development in the 2025 annual filing is confirmation that OMV's operating cash flow held above €5 billion for the fifth consecutive year — €5,215 million in 2025, down only 4.4% from the prior year's €5,456 million despite oil prices normalising well below their 2022 peak. That resilience matters because the thesis rests on the cash engine staying robust across the commodity cycle, not just at peak prices.

Two drivers moved in the wrong direction. The ratio of capital spending to operating cash flow rose to 73.8% in 2025 (up from roughly 35% in 2021), compressing free cash flow to €1,366 million — tight but still positive. Separately, the equity-method book value of the Borealis chemicals stake fell to €5,255 million from €6,661 million the year before, continuing a decline that began at a €7,294 million peak in 2022. Neither reading has crossed an exit threshold, but both are moving in the direction that would trigger an exit if they continue.

The effective tax rate of 54.7% is the sharpest monitoring item. It rose 3 percentage points in a single year and now sits within half a percentage point of the 55% level that, if exceeded in any year after 2026, would constitute exit condition three. A repeat of the 2023 EU solidarity contribution — which cost €552 million in that year — would make that crossing likely. The interest-coverage ratio, at 8.0 times (operating profit divided by interest expense), remains double the 4.0 times exit floor and reinforces the picture described in The Bottom Line: structurally intact, with specific lines worth watching.

About OMV

OMV is Austria's largest integrated energy and chemicals company. The business spans three activities: finding and extracting oil and gas (with operations in Austria, Romania, Norway, and the United Arab Emirates), refining crude oil and converting it into petrochemicals through three European refineries, and selling fuels and gas through a retail and commercial network concentrated in Austria and Central and Eastern Europe.

The chemicals side has grown more prominent. In March 2026 OMV consolidated its existing Borealis plastics business with two other large producers — Abu Dhabi's Borouge and Canada's NOVA Chemicals — into a combined entity called Borouge Group International, in which OMV holds a 46.94% stake. The resulting entity is the world's fourth-largest producer of polyolefins (the plastics used in packaging, pipes, and automotive components). Two large shareholders anchor the ownership: the Austrian state holding company ÖBAG with 31.5%, and the Abu Dhabi energy company XRG (formerly ADNOC) with 24.9% — together they account for 56.4% of the shares, giving the company an unusual degree of state-actor stability.

The Case for the Bet

The bet rests on one central idea: that OMV's dual position as a state-anchored domestic energy producer and a growing chemicals company allows it to generate enough cash, through a full commodity cycle, to justify its current share price while paying a 5.5% dividend yield. The specific conditions that need to hold are:

  1. Operating cash flow stays above €4.5 billion at prevailing oil prices. The cash engine is the load-bearing pillar. If operating cash flow falls below €3.5 billion while Brent crude trades above $60 per barrel, that would signal structural deterioration rather than a cyclical trough.

Latest reading: €5,215 million in the year ending 2025-12-31. The fifth consecutive year above €5 billion. Comfortable margin above both the bull signal (€4.5 billion) and the bear-watch threshold (€3.5 billion).

  1. The ratio of capital spending to operating cash flow stays below 90% for two consecutive years. Rising capital investment is one of the principal risks. If CapEx consumes more than 90% of operating cash flow for two years running, free cash flow turns negative and the dividend — which paid out 85.7% of trailing earnings in 2025 — comes under pressure.

Latest reading: 73.8% in the year ending 2025-12-31 (capital spending €3,849 million against operating cash flow €5,215 million). Below the exit threshold but rising sharply from roughly 35% in 2021. Free cash flow is compressed to €1,366 million — positive, but thin.

  1. The Borouge Group International equity-method book value stays above €4.0 billion. The chemicals pivot is the primary organic value-creation bet. If the book value of the equity stake falls below €4.0 billion for two consecutive years, that would signal that the chemicals transformation is destroying rather than creating value.

Latest reading: €5,255 million at 2025-12-31. Retains a 24% buffer above the €4.0 billion floor, but has fallen €2.0 billion since the 2022 peak. The entity label will change in the 2026 annual filing as the BGI combination completes — the threshold may need recalibrating when that filing is published.

  1. The effective tax rate stays below 55% in any year after 2026. EU windfall levies are the asymmetric tail risk. A 2023 solidarity contribution cost €552 million in a single year. If the effective tax rate exceeds 55% in any year from 2027 onwards, that would confirm the levy is a recurring structural cost rather than a one-off event.

Latest reading: 54.7% in the year ending 2025-12-31, up from 51.7% the prior year. Below the exit threshold, but the trend is upward and a repeat of the 2023 levy would likely push the rate above 55% in 2026 — which is the first year the exit condition can fire.

  1. Interest coverage stays above 4.0 times for two consecutive years. The coverage ratio (operating profit divided by interest expense) measures whether the balance sheet has enough room for the capital programme without straining the debt service.

Latest reading: 8.0 times at 2025-12-31 (operating profit €3,110 million, interest expense €388 million). Double the exit floor and accompanied by a clean earnings-quality indicator (the accruals ratio of −0.08 confirms that reported profit is backed by real cash).

  1. CEE fuel sales volume does not fall more than 10% over two years. The retail fuel network in Austria and Romania is the downstream moat. PKN Orlen has expanded aggressively across Central and Eastern Europe after its 2022–2023 mergers; if Orlen's expansion causes OMV's fuel volumes in those markets to fall more than 10% over two years, that signals structural moat erosion.

Latest reading: Not available from structured filing data as of 2025-12-31. OMV's filing uses a format that prevents extraction of narrative text; the retail volume numbers live in the narrative sections, not in the structured financial data. Manual review of the full annual report is required to assess this condition.

  1. State-actor governance does not shift toward value-destructive capital allocation. Two state-connected shareholders — ÖBAG and XRG — together hold 56.4% of shares and have board representation through the 1st and 2nd deputy chairwomen respectively. The watch signal is any disclosed capital allocation decision that deviates from the stated spending budget in favour of government energy-security objectives at the expense of minority shareholders.

Latest reading: Not available from structured filing data as of 2025-12-31. Governance disclosures are in the narrative sections of the annual report; no unusual related-party transactions were identified in the structured financial data.

The Share Price

At $62 per share (as of the latest data refresh), OMV sits at the buy edge of the valuation band — the point at which, on a normalised-earnings basis, the share price implies a yield of 7.52% on owner earnings (net profit, adjusted so that capital spending and depreciation are approximately equal). The threshold for adding to a position is a yield of 7.50%, so the current price is essentially on the line.

The fair-value anchor — the price at which the yield would equal the 5.60% long-run fair-value rate — sits at $83. The current price is 25% below that figure, which is the margin of safety threshold set in the thesis. Put plainly: at today's price, there is roughly a 25% discount to a conservative estimate of what the business is worth on normalised mid-cycle earnings. That gap is what the buy-threshold rule is designed to capture.

The trim trigger — an earnings-yield reading below 4.50%, implying a share price above roughly $94 — is 52% above today's price. It would require the market to price in full earnings recovery beyond even the optimistic scenario, which has not happened.

The Standing Orders

The rules translate the share-price bands into three standing orders and two price alerts. Each operationalises a pre-committed rule — not a new judgement introduced here.

Price alerts (informational).

- A second alert at $62, the buy edge. The crossing arms the buy order below; before adding, the latest re-validation should confirm no condition has deteriorated.

Standing orders (actionable).
- A standing buy order at $62. Per the pre-committed rules, adding occurs in two tranches once the share price closes at or below this line and the thesis conditions remain intact. The second tranche should be withheld until the operating cash flow trend is confirmed not to be deteriorating. Maximum addition: 50% of the existing allocation per tranche.
- A standing trim order at $104. Per the pre-committed rules, 25–33% of the position is trimmed when the share price closes at or above this line. At that level an earnings-yield reading below 4.50% implies the market is pricing in recovery beyond the base case; harvesting partial gains at that point is the rule.
- A standing exit order at $180. Per the pre-committed rules, the full position is closed if the share price reaches or exceeds this level. At $180 the market would be pricing in approximately 26 years of base-case compounding at current earnings — an implied forward return that falls below the long-run equity premium; reallocation would be warranted.

The three order levels do not contradict the cost-basis verdicts in The Bottom Line: those describe the current reading; the standing orders describe what the pre-committed instructions will execute if the share price moves to each line.

What's Wobbling

Three conditions are in a monitored state rather than a clean one.

Capital spending versus cash flow. The ratio of capital spending to operating cash flow has risen from around 35% in 2021 to 73.8% in 2025. Free cash flow is still positive — €1,366 million — but the direction of travel is toward the 90% threshold that would trigger exit condition two. One bad year (a sharp oil price fall combined with continued high investment) could bring the ratio close to the line. The trigger still requires two consecutive years above 90%, not one, so there is a single year of buffer before the exit condition fires — but that buffer is thinner than it was two years ago.

The chemicals pivot. The equity-method book value of the Borealis chemicals business has fallen from a peak of €7,294 million in 2022 to €5,255 million at the end of 2025 — a €2 billion decline over three years. The business has not crossed the €4 billion exit floor, and a 24% cushion remains. However, the trend has been consistently downward; a further decline of similar magnitude would bring the book value close to the exit threshold. The BGI entity transition — the 2026 merger of Borealis with Borouge and NOVA Chemicals — makes the 2026 annual filing the first test of whether the larger combined entity stabilises or continues to erode. See The Case for the Bet for the full exit condition.

The tax rate. At 54.7% — up from 51.7% the year before and 58.4% in 2023 — the effective tax rate has shown it can oscillate by several percentage points in a single year depending on whether a windfall levy is in place. The 2023 solidarity contribution of €552 million pushed the rate to 58.4%. If a similar levy were enacted in 2026, the rate would exceed the 55% threshold that constitutes exit condition three (which fires only from 2027 onward, but the 2026 filing would be the first year it could apply). This is the condition with the least buffer and the highest dependence on an external policy decision rather than on management action.

What Could Still Go Wrong

  • Oil price falls to $55 per barrel and stays there. At that level, operating cash flow contracts sharply, the capital spending ratio likely exceeds 90%, free cash flow turns negative, and the dividend — currently costing more than 85% of trailing earnings — becomes unsustainable. The bear scenario fair value under this combination is roughly $32 per share, a 46% loss from today's price.
  • The EU enacts a second windfall levy in 2026. A repeat of the 2023 solidarity contribution at the same scale (around €550 million) would push the effective tax rate above 55%, activating exit condition three in the first year it can fire. Because the tax is a recurring policy instrument rather than a one-off event, a second enactment would structurally impair earnings power beyond the cycle.
  • The Borouge chemicals integration disappoints. The 2026 filing will be the first to consolidate the combined Borealis–Borouge–NOVA entity. If the combination fails to arrest the book-value decline — or if integration costs are higher than expected — the chemicals pivot that underpins the long-run value case weakens materially.
  • PKN Orlen's Central European expansion accelerates. OMV's retail fuel network in Austria and Romania is the downstream moat, and Orlen now operates more than 3,000 filling stations across Central and Eastern Europe. Because the structured filing data cannot extract retail volume figures, this risk cannot currently be assessed from the annual filing alone; it requires manual review of the full annual report.
  • Dual state-actor ownership leads to value-destructive capital allocation. With ÖBAG (Austrian state) and XRG (UAE state) together holding 56.4%, capital decisions may favour energy security or geopolitical objectives over minority shareholder returns. The CapEx programme's rising cost is already the clearest manifestation; a further state-directed investment at sub-par returns would compound the dilution.

Gaps in the Picture

Two of the seven thesis conditions — the CEE fuel-sales volume driver and the governance quality driver — could not be assessed from the structured annual filing data. OMV files its annual report in a format that embeds the narrative sections as images rather than extractable text, so the retail volume figures and supervisory board disclosures are present in the document but not reachable through structured data extraction. Both conditions remain silent until a manual review of the full annual report is completed.

The owner earnings figure used in the valuation (€1,520 million, converted to approximately $1,740 million at the 2026 ECB rate) is an override taken directly from the 2025 net income rather than a computed free cash flow figure, because the structured filing data does not separately report depreciation, amortisation, and capital spending in a single extractable line. The resulting yield of 7.52% is arithmetically correct, but readers should note that it is anchored to net profit rather than to cash generation after maintenance investment.

The fair-value anchor (5.60% owner earnings yield, implying a share price of €83) has drifted meaningfully from the current yield reading: the current 7.52% yield implies a fair value higher than the 5.60% anchor, which means the anchor itself may be stale. Re-calibrating the fair-value yield to the current earnings base would widen the apparent discount — but that recalibration has not been performed in this cycle.

The Rules

Do not add at the current price; the probability-weighted three-year expected return from this level does not clear the threshold for adding new capital.

  2. Reduce the review cadence to annual and re-evaluate after the next annual filing.

  3. If the thesis breaks — any one of the six exit conditions fires (capital spending ratio above 90% for two consecutive years; BGI book value below €4.0 billion for two consecutive years; effective tax rate above 55% after 2026; interest coverage below 4.0 times for two consecutive years; dividend formally cut or free cash flow cover falls below 1.0 times for two consecutive years; CEE fuel sales volume declines more than 10% over two years) — then exit the full position. Move proceeds to a European diversified-energy fund or to euro cash, depending on portfolio concentration at the time of exit.

  4. If the share price falls below the buy threshold — owner earnings yield above 7.50%, implying a price at or below roughly $62 — then add to the position in two tranches, confirming that the operating cash flow trend is not deteriorating before committing the second tranche. Maximum addition: 50% of the existing allocation per tranche.

  5. If the share price rises above the trim threshold — owner earnings yield below 4.50%, implying a price above roughly $94 — then trim 25–33% of the position. At that level the market is pricing in structural earnings recovery beyond the optimistic scenario; harvesting partial gains at that point is the rule, with the option to re-establish at a lower yield if conditions warrant.

Scenario Definitions

A scenario is a joint assumption about two independent variables: operating trajectory (how the holding's underlying cash flows grow relative to their long-run trend) and exit multiple (what the market pays for those cash flows at the horizon, relative to a fair-value anchor).

Scenario Operating trajectory Exit multiple
Bear Below trend Below fair value
Base At trend At fair value
Bull Above trend At or above fair value

Non-negotiable rule: In the bear case, both dimensions must be adverse simultaneously — a scenario where only the multiple moves is a re-rating scenario, not a bear.

Bear cases are comparable in structure across all holding types (company, ETF, bond), but not in magnitude. A company bear reflects idiosyncratic execution failure; an ETF bear reflects a broad market drawdown. Different mitigations, different diversification implications.
