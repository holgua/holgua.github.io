Amazon: The Bet Still Works

The Bottom Line

The bet is working — Amazon's cloud-computing business grew 28% in the three months ending March 2026, well above the threshold that would signal trouble, and all written exit conditions remain untriggered. The share price sits in the upper half of the hold band, well above fair value. At today's price the long-run expected return is about 12% a year — a healthy equity premium over the risk-free rate. The one condition to watch is the capital-expenditure trajectory: spending has nearly doubled year on year and must keep converting into AWS revenue to justify the outlay.

About Amazon

Amazon earns money in three structurally different ways. The first is what most people recognise: an online marketplace and retail operation generating $426 billion in North America and $162 billion internationally in 2025, at thin margins (roughly 7% and 3%). These businesses fund the Prime membership base and generate the transaction data that powers the next engine.

The second is advertising. Every search on Amazon, every Prime Video stream, is an opportunity to show a sponsored result. That business produced $69 billion in revenue in 2025 and grew 24% in the three months ending March 2026.

The third, and the most important for understanding the current price, is Amazon Web Services — the cloud-computing infrastructure business. Companies and governments rent computing power and data storage from AWS rather than running their own facilities. AWS produced $129 billion in revenue in 2025, earned a 35% operating profit margin, and contributed 57% of Amazon's total operating profit on only 18% of total revenue. It is the profit engine that makes the rest of the business possible.

The Case for the Bet

The bet is that AWS retains enough growth momentum and margin discipline to compound the business's profits faster than its current price implies, while advertising extends the runway and the international operation gradually matures into a third profit stream.

For this to work, four conditions must hold:

  1. AWS quarterly revenue growth stays at or above 18% year on year. The exit fires if growth falls below 15% for two consecutive quarters — signalling that AI workloads have not absorbed the investment.
  2. AWS segment operating margin stays at or above 28%. A sustained compression below this line would signal that the reinvestment economics have broken.
  3. Advertising revenue growth stays above single digits. Two consecutive full years of single-digit growth would signal the third engine has saturated.
  4. No court-ordered structural remedy from the FTC monopoly case. A divestiture ruling would be an immediate exit regardless of financial metrics.

Two secondary conditions apply: capital expenditure must not remain above $120 billion in both 2027 and 2028 without corresponding AWS revenue acceleration; and no single Form 144 filing by founder Jeff Bezos should exceed 40 million shares.

The Rules

The pre-committed action plan, stated as standing conditions:

  • If the thesis is holding: Hold. Continue compounding.
  • If any driver is weakening but no threshold crossed: Do not add. Monitor across the next two quarterly filings before acting.
  • If any exit condition fires: Close the full position within 30 days. AWS and margin exits require two consecutive quarters of confirmation; an FTC remedy or auditor change is immediate.
  • If price falls to or below $140: Confirm no exit condition has fired and AWS growth is still above 18%, then add in tranches over one to two quarters.
  • If price rises to or above $263: Trim 25–33% of the position.
  • If price rises to or above $421: Close the full position. At that price roughly 11 years of base-case compounding is priced in instantly; the implied forward return falls below the long-term equity premium.

The Conditions Today

AWS revenue growth was +28% for the three months ending 31 March 2026 ($37.6 billion vs. $29.3 billion a year earlier) — well above the 18% target and far from the 15% exit threshold. Amazon's committed order book stood at $364 billion with a 5.5-year weighted-average remaining term. This driver is confirming.

AWS operating margin was 37.7% for the same period, down about 1.7 percentage points from a year earlier due to front-loaded infrastructure investment. At 37.7%, the margin remains nearly 10 percentage points above the 28% exit threshold. The compression is planned and disclosed — mixed, not alarming.

Advertising growth was +24% for the three months ending March 2026. The quarterly filing cites advertising as a primary driver of operating income improvement in both North America (up 41% to $8.3 billion) and the international segment. Confirming.

International operating margin was 3.6% in the three months ending March 2026, up from 3.0% a year earlier. The segment swung to sustained profitability recently and continues to scale. Confirming.

Capital expenditure reached approximately $147 billion in the trailing twelve months through March 2026, nearly double the $88 billion of a year earlier. Exit condition (4) targets 2027 and 2028 — not the trailing twelve months — and applies only if AWS growth fails to keep pace. Given 28% AWS growth and the $364 billion backlog, that clause is not met today. Mixed and the highest-priority forward watch item.

FTC proceedings are ongoing. No structural remedy has been imposed and no new trial timeline appeared in the latest quarterly filing. Silent — no trigger.

Capital allocation is unchanged from the thesis's expectation: no buybacks since March 2022, $6.1 billion authorisation untouched, $37 billion in new bonds issued to fund AI infrastructure. Bezos's latest Form 144 covers 25 million shares — below the 40 million threshold. Mixed, consistent with the thesis's "acceptable but not excellent" characterisation.

The Share Price

The valuation method uses owner earnings yield: annual owner earnings (operating cash flow minus capital expenditure and stock-based compensation) divided by market capitalisation. For full-year 2025, that was $113 billion against a $2.69 trillion market capitalisation — a yield of 4.2%.

Fair value was set at a 6.0% yield, corresponding to $176 per share. The buy edge of $140 corresponds to a 7.5% yield; the trim edge of $263 to a 4.0% yield.

At $250, the current yield is 4.2%, placing the price $13 below the trim edge and well above fair value. The share is in the upper half of the hold band — expensive enough that no new purchase is justified, not yet expensive enough to trim.

A separate scenario analysis using peer and historical earnings multiples produces a base-case central estimate of $564 per share, far above the yield-method figure of $176. The gap reflects two different questions: what the current year's cash generation justifies at a required yield, versus what three-year base-case earnings are worth at peer multiples. Gaps in the Picture explains the divergence.

What's Wobbling

Capital expenditure pace. The trailing-twelve-month capex of approximately $147 billion is nearly equal to the full year's operating cash flow of $149 billion, leaving free cash flow of only $1.2 billion on the cash-flow-statement measure — a sharp compression from $26 billion a year earlier. The bet is that AI infrastructure converts into AWS revenue within two to three years at the same economics as prior cycles. That conversion is on schedule so far, but the dependency on continued conversion is unusually high.

Retail tariff exposure. The quarterly filing is explicit: Chinese-based sellers account for a significant share of third-party seller services and advertising revenues. Advertising grew 24% and North America operating income grew 41% despite this environment, suggesting the exposure is being managed — but it is real.

No buybacks. Amazon has not repurchased a single share since March 2022, despite the stock being 60% lower at times in 2022 and 2023. This is not a thesis-breaker — the thesis explicitly called capital allocation "acceptable but not excellent" — but it is a factual signal that management prioritises infrastructure investment over returning capital at depressed prices.

What Could Still Go Wrong

  • AWS loses AI workload share to Azure or Google Cloud before the capex wave peaks — both competitors are growing faster in net-new AI bookings right now.
  • A court-ordered structural remedy in the FTC case forces a business separation, directly impairing the flywheel economics.
  • The AI capex cycle takes longer to normalise than expected: consolidated capital expenditure above $120 billion in both 2027 and 2028 would trigger exit condition (4).
  • Advertising growth decelerates into single digits for two consecutive full years, removing the third compounding engine from the thesis.
  • The Anthropic investment is written down — Amazon's 2025 reported net income includes approximately $15 billion of non-cash gains from its stake in this private artificial-intelligence company, which could reverse as happened with the Rivian stake in 2022.

Gaps in the Picture

Two fair-value anchors coexist. The earnings-yield method puts fair value at $176 per share (based on a 6.0% target yield on full-year 2025 cash earnings). A separate scenario analysis using peer and historical multiples produces a base-case central estimate of $564 per share. The gap — 221% — is not a calculation error. It reflects two different questions: what today's cash generation is worth at a required yield, and what three-year base-case earnings are worth at peer multiples. A future update of the valuation framework is expected to reconcile these anchors.

Maintenance capital expenditure is not separately disclosed. Amazon does not break out what portion of capital expenditure maintains existing assets versus what builds new ones. The valuation uses the narrow cash-flow-statement figure of $6.7 billion (full year 2025), excluding the larger infrastructure spend recorded through finance leases. The management-defined total of $128 billion is primarily growth-oriented but the split is not available in the filed figures.

AWS segment-level capital expenditure is not disclosed. The filing describes the investment directionally but does not give an AWS-specific figure. The capex conversion thesis rests on this allocation being efficient; it cannot be verified directly from public filings.
