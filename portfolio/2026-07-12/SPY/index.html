About the Fund

The SPDR S&P 500 exchange-traded fund, managed by State Street Investment Management, tracks the S&P 500 index — a benchmark of 500 large US-listed companies weighted by their market value. Technology companies such as Nvidia, Apple, Alphabet, and Microsoft make up the largest share (38.55% of the basket), followed by financial services (11.55%) and communication services (9.90%). By market value, the basket is dominated by the largest and most widely traded companies in the United States.

The fund launched in January 1993 as the first US-listed exchange-traded fund and operates under a Unit Investment Trust structure. The trust structure cannot reinvest dividends between distribution dates (creating a small 0.25% cash sleeve) and cannot lend its securities, making the annual expense ratio of 0.0945% the complete cost picture. The fund is US-domiciled, issues no tax partnership forms, and historically produces no capital gain distributions through in-kind redemptions — though the annual report is the primary source for confirmation.

The Case for the Bet

The fund is held on the basis that the S&P 500 basket, at the right price, offers a better long-run return than the risk-free rate plus a minimum 2 percentage-point equity premium. Six conditions must hold for the bet to stay valid:

  1. The equity risk premium does not go negative for two consecutive calendar quarters. The risk premium is calculated as the basket's earnings yield minus the ten-year Treasury yield. If Treasuries yield more than the basket's earnings for two successive quarter-end readings, the core return case has failed, and the position is unwound regardless of how individual vehicle checks look.

Latest reading: The equity risk premium stood at −0.89% on 2026-07-12, with eleven consecutive negative daily readings since 2026-06-16. The exit condition requires two calendar-quarter readings to confirm — the first quarter-end confirmation remains outstanding. The direction is unambiguous, but the formal trigger has not yet fired.

  1. State Street does not raise the expense ratio above 0.15% without a fee response. At 0.0945%, the annual running cost represents among the lowest charges available for large-cap US equity exposure. An increase above 0.15% with no competitive response from State Street would signal a deterioration in the cost case.

Latest reading: Expense ratio confirmed at 0.0945% as of 2026-07-12. Headroom of 0.0555 percentage points to the ceiling, or about 59%. Exit condition not triggered.

  1. Assets under management do not fall below $100B. The $100B floor is set at a level representing an extraordinary liquidity event — a shock so severe it would imply structural problems with the fund or the market it represents. An ETF of this scale cannot lose assets quietly.

Latest reading: Assets stand at approximately $781B as of 2026-07-12, or 7.8 times the floor. Exit condition not triggered.

  1. No single holding exceeds 13% of the fund. The 13% ceiling is calibrated to flag genuine regime-change concentration — not the normal drift of a market-cap-weighted index in which the largest company expands its share. At 13%, a single name represents a meaningful single-point-of-failure risk.

Latest reading: Nvidia holds 7.50% of the fund as of 2026-07-12, 5.5 percentage points below the ceiling. Exit condition not triggered.

  1. The S&P 500 index methodology is not materially restructured by S&P Dow Jones Indices. The broad-market mandate — 500 large US-listed companies, cap-weighted — is what the bet is on. A change to the selection criteria, float-weighting rules, or sector structure that fundamentally alters what the index represents would break the thesis.

Latest reading: No automated signal for index-methodology changes. State Street's fund operating normally per the 2026-07-12 snapshot. User monitors externally via S&P Dow Jones Indices announcements.

  1. Median return on equity of the deduped top-10 holdings does not fall below 15%. Return on equity (the profit a business earns relative to the capital shareholders have put in) for the top ten holdings is used as a proxy for the earning quality of the basket's most influential names. A collapse below 15% would indicate the basket has lost the quality base on which the earnings-yield framework rests.

Latest reading: Median return on equity across the deduped top-10 stands at 37.28% as of 2026-07-12 — 2.5 times the exit floor. The weighted average is considerably higher at 70.38%, but that figure is distorted by Apple's and Nvidia's large buyback programmes shrinking their book equity; the median is the load-bearing read. Exit condition not triggered.

What's Wobbling

The equity risk premium is the live wobble. At −0.89% as of 2026-07-12, Treasuries outpay the basket by nearly a full percentage point, and every daily snapshot since 2026-06-16 has recorded a negative reading. The trend has moved in the wrong direction: the premium was −0.59% in late June 2026 and has compressed further to −0.89% today, driven by a combination of rising Treasury yields and a falling basket earnings yield.

The written exit condition requires two consecutive negative calendar-quarter readings to formally fire. The first quarter-end test date is still ahead. Until then, the thesis is technically intact — the formal trigger has not fired — but the economic basis for holding at full weight has eroded. The current valuation band position (above the trim edge) already calls for a reduction in holding size under the pre-committed rules, independent of the equity risk premium.

What Could Still Go Wrong

The bear case for the basket has four main paths:

  • Multiple compression without earnings recovery. All three forward-return scenarios assume earnings continue growing at around 6% per year (the long-run S&P 500 consensus). If earnings growth stalls while the price-to-earnings multiple contracts from 27 times toward 15 times, the five-year annualised return works out to roughly −7% per year — a significant loss in nominal terms.
  • Treasury yields remain elevated. The negative equity risk premium is currently not a formal exit trigger, but if Treasury yields stay at or above 4.5% through the end of two consecutive calendar quarters, exit condition (1) fires. That would require closing the position regardless of price or vehicle quality.
  • Single-name shock in the largest holdings. Nvidia at 7.5% of the fund is the largest position. A severe disruption to Nvidia's earnings — regulatory, competitive, or demand-driven — would mechanically drag the basket's earnings yield higher (good, eventually, for valuation) but would create near-term price drawdown. At the 13% concentration ceiling, this risk becomes an exit trigger; today's 7.5% is well clear, but the ceiling exists precisely for this scenario.
  • Vehicle-integrity failure. Cost increases above 0.15%, an extraordinary asset-flight event pushing the fund below $100B, or a fundamental restructuring of the S&P 500 index methodology would each trigger exit regardless of price. None of these is elevated today, but all three exist as discrete, non-gradual failure modes. (For this fund, exit fires when the share price reaches the exit edge, when a vehicle-integrity condition breaches, or when the named exit-condition trigger fires — any of three paths.)

The Rules

The following rules govern what the pre-committed plan calls for in each situation. They are stated as conditions, not recommendations.

  • If the basket is reasonably priced and the vehicle is sound (all conditions holding, price between the buy edge and the trim edge): hold — basket is reasonably priced and the vehicle is sound.
  • If any written driver is showing strain but no formal exit condition has fired (thesis weakened): do not add. Re-validate after the next quarter. If the equity risk premium stays negative or the median look-through return on equity keeps falling, swap to a comparable fund or move to cash per the position-sizing rule.
  • If vehicle integrity has been breached or an exit condition has formally triggered (thesis broken): exit. Move proceeds to risk-free or to a holding that meets the value test.
  • If the price falls to or below the cheap edge ($445, basket earnings yield at or above 6.25%): add to position. Basket is meaningfully cheap relative to risk-free.
  • If the price rises to or above the trim edge ($695, basket earnings yield at or below 4.00%): trim 25–33%. Basket is meaningfully expensive relative to risk-free; harvest the premium.
  • If the price rises to or above the exit edge ($927, basket earnings yield at or below 3.00%): exit. Basket earnings yield fully priced relative to risk-free. Redeploy proceeds to risk-free or to a holding that meets the value test.
