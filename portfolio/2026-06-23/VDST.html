US Treasury 0-1y: An Update on the Bet

The Bottom Line

At this snapshot, what follows is an update on the bet rather than a clear verdict in one direction — the fund operates outside the standard equity-assessment frame. The fund holds US Treasury bills with maturities of zero to one year, accumulating income internally, and is designed as a near-cash position rather than as a yield-growth engine. All three vehicle-integrity guardrails are intact: the annual charge is 0.07%, well below the 0.10% ceiling that would trigger the first exit condition; assets under management stand at approximately $2.4 billion, more than double the $1 billion floor for the second; and no downgrade of US sovereign credit below AA- has been announced by two or more major rating agencies, leaving the third exit condition inactive. The basket's current weighted-average yield is approximately 3.73% a year, a touch below the 4.8% base-case level encoded in the thesis — a sign that the Federal Reserve has already cut rates from 2024 highs, but not into the 3.5% territory that would tip the fund into the bear scenario. The one thing to watch is whether rate cuts accelerate faster than the base case anticipates.

The Verdict

The fund is designed to perform one specific job: park cash at short-term US government rates, cheaply, in a liquid structure, while the rest of the portfolio waits for better opportunities in equities. On that measure it is doing its job. The underlying basket yield sits within the hold band — below the bull-case level of 5.5% that would represent unusually attractive T-bill rates, but above the bear-case floor of 3.5% where aggressive Fed cuts would make holding USD T-bills materially less attractive than EUR alternatives. Exit conditions are not triggered. The recommendation is to hold the position and verify the annual charge and fund size quarterly, since Vanguard's data is not returned by automated feeds for this structure.

About the Fund

The Vanguard U.S. Treasury 0-1 Year Bond UCITS ETF (Acc) is a fund domiciled in Ireland under the UCITS regulatory framework, listed on the London Stock Exchange. It tracks a basket of US government debt instruments with remaining maturities between zero and twelve months — in practice a rolling portfolio of Treasury bills, the shortest-dated sovereign debt the US government issues. The fund uses an accumulating share class, meaning any coupon income is reinvested automatically rather than distributed as cash. At 0.07% annual charge, it is one of the lower-cost vehicles in its category; comparable ultra-short-duration Treasury funds from other providers typically charge between 0.05% and 0.15%.

No tracking-difference or tracking-error figures are available from automated data sources for this fund. Vanguard does not surface these metrics via standard market-data feeds for its UCITS-listed share classes, so the quality of index replication cannot be assessed mechanically this cycle. The fund is managed by Vanguard Group (Ireland) Limited, part of one of the world's largest passive-management operations; the structural incentive to minimise tracking error is strong, even without a published figure.

The fund is structured as a UCITS Irish-domiciled accumulating vehicle. Income is internally reinvested; there are no cash distributions. The fund does not issue K-1 tax forms. Capital-gain distributions are not applicable in the accumulating structure. Annual cost: 0.07% expense ratio. No securities-lending revenue offset is reported for this share class.

The Case for the Bet

The bet is that short-term US Treasury bills represent a better use of a cash allocation than holding bank deposits or short nominal government bonds elsewhere, because the fee is low, the liquidity is high, and the underlying credit quality of the US sovereign is sufficient.

Three conditions must hold for the bet to remain valid:

  1. Annual charges stay contained. The fund's ongoing charges figure must remain at or below 0.10%. If it rises above that level for two consecutive quarterly reviews, comparable lower-cost alternatives (such as SHV, BIL, or IBTS) become preferable on cost grounds alone. The current figure is 0.07% — within budget with 3 basis points of headroom.

  2. Fund size stays above the liquidity floor. Assets under management must remain above $1 billion. Below that level, the fund's value proposition — instant T-bill-like liquidity at large scale — begins to erode, as redemption pressure at a smaller fund can cause wider spreads or forced selling. The current figure is approximately $2.4 billion, sourced manually from Trackinsight as of 2026-06-19. Automated data sources do not surface this figure for UCITS ETFs; manual quarterly verification is required.

  3. US sovereign credit remains above the minimum threshold. US government debt must not be downgraded below AA- by any two of the three major credit-rating agencies (Fitch, Moody's, S&P). Such a downgrade would require a fundamental reassessment of whether short-term US Treasury bills remain an appropriate cash-equivalent instrument. This condition is monitored externally; no automated detection is in place.

The Rules

The pre-committed rules are:

  1. If vehicle integrity is intact and no exit condition has fired: hold the position. Verify annual charges and fund size quarterly. No change to allocation is warranted unless conditions shift.

  2. If signs emerge that vehicle integrity is weakening — costs rising toward the ceiling, assets shrinking toward the floor: do not add to the position. Reassess at the next quarterly review. Monitor trends in both charges and fund size before considering any increase in allocation.

  3. If any exit condition fires — charges above 0.10% for two consecutive quarters, fund size below $1 billion, or US sovereign credit downgraded below AA- by two agencies: exit the position. Reinvest the proceeds in EUR cash or short-dated nominal German government bonds.

  4. If the basket yield rises above the 5.5% bull-case level: the rules treat this as a signal that T-bill rates are unusually attractive — the sentinel buy-band has been crossed. However, because vehicle-integrity rules govern all real add/trim decisions for this fund, price action alone does not trigger a mechanical add; the vehicle-integrity conditions above apply.

  5. If the basket yield falls below the 3.5% bear-case level: the rules treat this as a signal that T-bill rates have compressed to a level where the opportunity cost of holding USD T-bills versus EUR alternatives becomes significant. Again, vehicle-integrity rules govern; this is a signal to reassess rather than a mechanical trigger.

The relevant frame is the basket's yield-to-maturity, not the price-to-earnings ratio.

The weighted-average yield-to-maturity of the underlying basket is 3.73% a year, as of the May 2026 month-end snapshot sourced from Vanguard's portfolio data system. The fair-value sentinel for this fund is 4.8% — representing the base-case T-bill rate for mid-2026. At 3.73%, the basket yield is below that base case, reflecting rate cuts by the Federal Reserve since the highs of 2023-2024. The bear-case sentinel is 3.5%; the current yield sits 23 basis points above that level. The bull-case sentinel is 5.5%, which the current yield is more than 170 basis points below. In practical terms: the fund is delivering a positive nominal return, but at a rate that is closer to the cautious end of the expected range than the bullish one.

What's Wobbling

The one material wobble is the direction of Federal Reserve policy. The basket yield of 3.73% reflects a Fed that has already moved toward easier monetary conditions. If rate cuts continue at a faster pace than the base case assumes — converging toward the 3.5% bear-case level or below — the fund's yield advantage over EUR cash equivalents narrows. The Bund Linker 2033 real yield (the EUR risk-free benchmark in this portfolio) provides the natural comparison: if EUR real yields remain elevated while USD nominal yields compress, the opportunity-cost case for holding USD T-bills weakens.

There is no automated mechanism to track this cross-currency comparison in the current pipeline; it is a portfolio-level judgement, not a fund-level trigger. The fund itself does not wobble in a structural sense — vehicle integrity is intact.

What Could Break the Fund

  • Sustained rate compression. If the Federal Reserve cuts short-term rates aggressively toward or below 3.5% — the bear-case scenario — the fund's yield drops to levels where EUR cash or short German government bonds offer comparable or superior real returns, and the rationale for holding USD T-bills weakens.
  • Rising costs. If Vanguard raises the fund's ongoing charges figure above 0.10% — even briefly for two consecutive quarters — the cost advantage over alternatives disappears and the first exit condition fires.
  • Fund size erosion. If assets under management fall toward $1 billion, redemption pressure or liquidity constraints could impair the fund's ability to operate as a near-cash instrument at low cost.
  • US credit downgrade. A downgrade of US sovereign credit below AA- by two or more major rating agencies would require a fundamental reassessment of the cash-equivalent premise. This is a tail risk, but one that is explicitly named in the exit conditions.
  • Currency translation. The fund denominates in USD; the portfolio is EUR-denominated. A significant EUR/USD shift could alter the effective return in EUR terms, independent of T-bill rates. This is a portfolio-level exposure, not a fund-integrity concern.
