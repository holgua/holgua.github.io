About the Fund

This fund tracks a basket of US Treasury bills — short-term government borrowing instruments issued by the US federal government — that mature within one year. The fund is managed by Vanguard Group (Ireland) Limited and structured as a UCITS exchange-traded fund domiciled in Ireland, listed on the London Stock Exchange (ISIN IE00BLRPPV00). It operates under an accumulating structure: instead of paying dividends, all interest income earned by the basket is reinvested in the net asset value, so the fund's price drifts upward over time at approximately the current Treasury-bill yield rate.

The mandate is deliberately narrow. The fund holds only US government obligations maturing within twelve months, keeping the portfolio's average time to maturity at roughly four to five months. At that duration, the fund behaves like a cash equivalent rather than a bond fund: its value moves very little when interest rates shift, because the underlying holdings roll over quickly to the prevailing rate.

Tax profile. The fund is structured as an accumulating UCITS and is registered as a reporting fund for Austrian tax purposes (OeKB Meldefonds). No US partnership tax form (K-1) is issued. The taxable deemed distribution amount (Ausschüttungsgleiche Erträge) has not yet been formally confirmed; a provisional estimate of 4.5% per year is used as a stand-in. Annual cost: 0.07% per year (ongoing charges figure). No securities-lending revenue is configured.

The Case for the Bet

This fund is held as a USD cash equivalent within the portfolio — not as a directional bet on interest rates, but as a parking place for capital while waiting for equity opportunities at fair prices. The bet is that the fund delivers T-bill returns at minimal cost with daily liquidity, without the credit risk, term risk, or complexity of alternatives. Three conditions govern when that premise holds:

  1. The ongoing charges figure stays below 0.10% per year for two consecutive quarterly reviews. The economic case for this fund rests on its near-zero cost relative to alternatives such as other short-duration Treasury funds. If fees rise above the ceiling for two consecutive quarters, cheaper vehicles exist and the cost advantage is lost.

  1. Fund assets stay above USD 1 billion. The fund's value as a cash equivalent depends on its liquidity: the ability to redeem shares quickly and at scale. If assets fall below the floor, redemption risk and bid-ask spreads could erode the vehicle's usefulness as a daily-liquid placeholder.

  1. The index methodology and Vanguard fund structure remain unchanged, and US sovereign credit is not downgraded below AA− by two of the three major rating agencies. This condition monitors two linked risks: a change to the fund's mandate (such as a switch to a different index or a widening of the maturity band) or a structural deterioration in the credit quality of the underlying obligor (the US federal government). What Could Still Go Wrong

  • Aggressive rate cuts compress yield below the trim edge. If the Federal Reserve cuts rates sharply — the bear case assumes basket yield at 2.5%, which corresponds to a return well below the thesis base case — the fund continues to perform its role but at significantly reduced carry. Trim rules would eventually fire.
  • Fund assets fall below the floor. Without automated monitoring of this figure, a sustained outflow from the fund could go undetected between quarterly manual checks. A large redemption event, if undetected, could leave the position in a fund approaching the liquidity threshold.
  • Expense ratio increases above the ceiling. The 0.07% figure is a carry-forward, not a live feed. An unreported fee increase that goes undetected for two consecutive quarters would technically satisfy exit condition (1) without triggering the automated check.
  • US sovereign credit rating deteriorates. A downgrade below AA− by two of the three major agencies would fire exit condition (3). No automated detection is in place; this is monitored externally.
  • UCITS structure or index methodology changes. Vanguard could change the fund's reference index, widen the maturity band, or merge the fund into another vehicle. Any material update to the fund documentation must be monitored externally. (For this fund, exit fires when the basket yield reaches the exit edge, when a vehicle-integrity condition breaches, or when the named exit-condition trigger fires — any of three paths.)

Scenario Definitions

A scenario is a joint assumption about two independent variables: operating trajectory (how the holding's underlying cash flows grow relative to their long-run trend) and exit multiple (what the market pays for those cash flows at the horizon, relative to a fair-value anchor).

Scenario Operating trajectory Exit multiple
Bear Below trend Below fair value
Base At trend At fair value
Bull Above trend At or above fair value

Non-negotiable rule: In the bear case, both dimensions must be adverse simultaneously — a scenario where only the multiple moves is a re-rating scenario, not a bear.

Bear cases are comparable in structure across all holding types (company, ETF, bond), but not in magnitude. A company bear reflects idiosyncratic execution failure; an ETF bear reflects a broad market drawdown. Different mitigations, different diversification implications.
