About the Fund

World Equal Weight is a passively managed fund run by Invesco that tracks the MSCI World Equal Weighted Index. The index is the equal-weight sibling of the more familiar cap-weighted MSCI World: it starts from the same universe of roughly 1,500 companies across 23 developed markets, but instead of letting market capitalisation determine each company's slice, it resets every holding to the same weight — roughly 0.07% each — at the end of every quarter. A company three times the size of another gets the same allocation on the day the rebalance runs; only price drift between rebalances creates temporary differences in weight.

The practical consequence is a basket tilted away from US mega-caps and toward the broader mid-size developed-market world. Where a cap-weighted global index has roughly 70% in US companies and a large concentration in a handful of technology giants, this fund spreads exposure across industrials (18.2% of the basket), financial services (16.8%), technology (15.8%), healthcare (8.3%), consumer goods, materials, energy, and utilities. No single sector exceeds 30%.

The fund is Irish-domiciled under the UCITS framework and uses optimised sampling replication — it holds a representative subset of the index rather than every one of the roughly 1,500 constituents. It is an accumulating share class, meaning any income earned by the underlying companies is reinvested rather than paid out as a distribution. The fund charges 0.20% per year.

Annual cost: 0.20% (gross expense ratio). Tracking difference and tracking error figures are not yet available; the fund has only been operating for approximately 22 months.

The Case for the Bet

The structural argument is straightforward: concentrated cap-weighted indices have been driven by a relatively small number of large technology companies over the past decade, and equal weighting is a deliberate counterweight to that concentration. By spreading equal weight, the basket earns a higher share of its returns from the broader economy and is less dependent on the fate of any one sector or company.

For the thesis to hold, six conditions must stay intact:

  1. The basket's earnings yield must exceed the 10-year Treasury yield. When it does not for two consecutive quarters, the risk premium has failed and the exit fires regardless of absolute yield levels.

Latest reading: Basket earnings yield 4.87% vs. 10-year Treasury yield 4.49% as of 2026-07-06, giving a positive equity risk premium of 0.38 percentage points. Positive, but thin. Five consecutive quarterly readings have all been positive; the persistence leg of exit condition one is inactive.

  1. The annual fund charge must stay below 0.30%. Equal-weight strategies run higher annual turnover than cap-weighted peers — roughly 30% per year from the quarterly rebalance — which creates a permanently higher cost base. If Invesco raises the charge above 0.30%, the cost case for equal weight over a cheaper cap-weighted alternative breaks.

Latest reading: The current annual charge is 0.20% (sourced from the fund's known charge structure). This is 7 basis points of headroom below the 0.27% early-warning level. Status: clean.

  1. Assets under management must remain above €500 million. The fund is approximately 22 months old. UCITS funds below roughly €300–500 million in assets face materially elevated closure risk; Invesco has wound up sub-scale UCITS products before. A sustained decline to this floor is a vehicle-survival signal, not a valuation signal.

Latest reading: Assets under management of approximately €1.48 billion as of 2026-07-06 — roughly three times the floor. The early-warning level of €800 million has not been approached. Status: clean.

  1. No single holding may drift above 1.0% of the fund. By mandate, every holding targets approximately 0.07% at each quarterly rebalance. Any drift above 1.0% — roughly 14 times the target — signals methodology failure, a rebalance breakdown, or extreme intra-quarter price drift that sampling has not corrected. This threshold is deliberately tight: the equal-weight mandate is the product.

Latest reading: Dell Technologies sits at 0.27%, the largest single holding after price drift since the most recent rebalance. This is well inside both the 0.50% early-warning level and the 1.0% ceiling. Status: clean.

  1. MSCI must not materially restructure the Equal Weighted index methodology. A shift to factor-tilted weighting, removal of the quarterly rebalance, or a change in the underlying universe from MSCI World would alter what the fund is doing in ways that the structural thesis no longer justifies.

Latest reading: No automated signal exists for MSCI methodology changes. External monitoring is required. Status: silent — not a concern today, but not tracked by this pipeline.

  1. The median return on equity of the fund's ten most visible holdings must stay above 12%. Equal weighting deliberately includes smaller companies with structurally lower returns on capital than the top of cap-weighted MSCI World; the 12% floor (lower than the 15% used for cap-weighted counterparts) defines the point below which even the observable part of the basket has lost quality.

Latest reading: Median return on equity for the deduped top-10 holdings was 14.14% as of 2026-07-06, sitting in the mixed band (above the 12.0% level where concerns begin, below the 15.0% level where quality is confirmed). The top-10 at this snapshot is skewed toward semiconductor and technology names — the post-rebalance drift winners — including Kioxia and Micron, whose returns on equity reflect cyclical memory-chip earnings peaks rather than durable compounding quality. Status: mixed.

What's Wobbling

The equity risk premium is the single live concern. At 0.38 percentage points, the basket's earnings yield barely clears the 10-year Treasury yield. The arithmetic is plain: if the 10-year Treasury yield rises above the basket's current earnings yield of roughly 4.87%, the equity risk premium turns negative. That alone does not trigger the exit — the exit requires two consecutive negative quarters — but the gap is uncomfortably thin in a higher-for-longer rate environment.

There is a structural explanation, and it does not make the risk disappear. The equal-weight construction pulls in hundreds of smaller companies across industrials, materials, utilities, and consumer staples that are structurally lower-yielding than the concentrated top-10 of cap-weighted MSCI World. The basket was expected to deliver a higher earnings yield than cap-weighted counterparts; it does, but only marginally at current prices. The equal-weight premium over cap-weight has narrowed as equal-weight has partially re-rated in this cycle.

The median return on equity of 14.14% across the top-10 visible holdings adds a secondary wobble. It is in the mixed band by design — smaller equal-weight constituents carry lower returns on capital than mega-cap compounders — but it is structurally below the confirm threshold of 15.0%. That is a known trade-off, not a deterioration, and the 12.0% exit-trigger floor remains well below today's reading.

Neither wobble crosses any written exit-condition line as of 2026-07-06.

What Could Still Go Wrong

  • The risk-free rate rises further. A 10-year Treasury yield above roughly 4.87% — the current basket earnings yield — would immediately turn the equity risk premium negative. A sustained two-quarter stretch of negative premium fires exit condition one. The basket currently has zero basis points of buffer if yields rise even fractionally.
  • Multiple compression in a recession. The bear-case scenario projects a −4.9% annualised return over five years, driven almost entirely by the earnings multiple contracting from the current 20.5 times earnings to 12 times. This is severe but historically consistent with a sustained higher-for-longer rate environment or a global downturn. The downside gap between the bear and base case is approximately 12.7 percentage points, roughly twice the upside gap between base and bull.
  • Fund scale deteriorates. The fund is only 22 months old and not yet deeply embedded in institutional allocations. A prolonged period of underperformance versus cap-weighted MSCI World could trigger outflows toward the €800 million early-warning floor. At €1.48 billion in assets, the cushion exists — but it is not inexhaustible.
  • MSCI methodology change. A restructuring of the Equal Weighted index methodology — shift to factor weighting, removal of the quarterly rebalance, or narrowing of the constituent universe — would dissolve the product's structural rationale without triggering any price-based exit signal. This risk is externally monitored and not tracked automatically by this pipeline.

The Rules

The rules state what follows from each combination of thesis state and share-price position. These are the pre-committed instructions, not new judgements added here.

  • If the thesis is confirmed (all drivers mixed or better, no exit condition triggered, vehicle integrity intact): hold — the equal-weight basket is reasonably priced, the vehicle is sound, and the equal-weight earnings-yield premium over cap-weighted global peers remains intact.
  • If the thesis is weakened (a driver reads as a concern but no exit condition has fired): do not add. Re-validate after the next quarter. If the equal-weight earnings-yield premium over the cap-weighted MSCI World equivalent compresses below 0.5 percentage points for two consecutive quarters, the diversification benefit no longer justifies the higher annual charge versus a cap-weighted alternative — consider consolidating.
  • If the thesis is broken (vehicle integrity breached, or the two-consecutive-quarter negative-equity-risk-premium rule fires, or another written exit condition triggers): exit. Move proceeds to a cap-weighted global core fund or to risk-free.
  • If the share price falls below the buy edge at $4.6: add to the position. The basket's earnings yield would be meaningfully above the risk-free rate; the structural premium over cap-weight should make this rare and decisive.
  • If the share price rises above the trim edge at $7.2: trim 25–33%. The basket's earnings yield would have compressed to the point where harvesting the premium and redeploying on the next rebalance is the appropriate action.
  • If the share price rises above the exit edge at $9.6: exit. The basket would be fully priced against the risk-free rate. Redeploy proceeds to risk-free or to a holding that still meets the value test.
