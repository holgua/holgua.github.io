Last Known Thesis (trimmed for the stub)

Crocs, Inc. is held as a capital-light consumer brand franchise whose core asset — the Croslite™ proprietary material formulation and the globally recognised clog silhouette — generates approximately $1.1B in segment operating income on $3.3B in Crocs Brand revenue, sustaining gross margins above 60% in a tariff-impacted environment and delivering FCF of $659M in FY2025 despite $737M in non-cash HEYDUDE impairment charges. The primary moat is intangible: a trade-secret material formulation with no close manufacturing analogue, embedded in a product that has survived two cultural trend cycles and demonstrated cross-generational and cross-geographic durability. Capital return is a second-order driver: management has reduced shares by 18.6% over three years and cut total debt from $2.3B to $1.2B, compounding per-share value even as GAAP earnings are distorted. The HEYDUDE acquisition at $2.5B in 2022 was a capital misallocation — $737M in write-downs to date, with $1.1B in remaining trademark carrying value within 10% of fair value, creating a binary non-cash risk with every quarterly result. At current price ($118.39, near the 52-week high of $121.26), the FCF yield is 11.2% and forward adjusted P/E is approximately 8.8x, offering a narrow but present margin of safety if HEYDUDE stabilises; the base case (50% probability) targets 12–18% annualised returns over 3 years with HEYDUDE contributing modestly positive and Crocs Brand growing mid-single-digit internationally. The bull case (20% probability) — HEYDUDE revenues recover and the brand builds independent value — implies 25–35% annualised returns. The bear case (30% probability) — HEYDUDE triggers a second full impairment and tariff escalation beyond current rates — implies -10 to -20% annualised returns. The thesis is explicitly NOT a recovery story for HEYDUDE; it is a hold on the Crocs Brand franchise at a price that reflects the binary overhead without demanding HEYDUDE resolution. Exit conditions: (1) HEYDUDE trademark impairment recurs with a charge exceeding $200M on the remaining $1.1B trademark in any single fiscal year, indicating structural rather than cyclical failure; (2) consolidated gross margin (as reported) deteriorates below 55% for two or more consecutive quarters, indicating the Croslite moat is eroding or tariff pass-through is failing; (3) adjusted FCF yield at market price falls below 7.5%, pricing in perfection with binary risk outstanding; (4) net debt rises above $1.5B, signalling management is re-levering rather than continuing to deleverage; (5) Carretta securities class action results in adverse class certification or material settlement exceeding $150M.
