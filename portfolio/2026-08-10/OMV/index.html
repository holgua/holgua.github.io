The Bottom Line

Data as of 2026-08-10 · Latest filing 2025-12-31.

The bet is working. At €62, the share price sits below the buy edge — the threshold at which the company's earnings relative to its market value signal a meaningful margin of safety — and meaningfully below fair value. Owning the shares at this price is priced to deliver a healthy equity premium over the long run, assuming the cash-generation track record holds. The one condition to watch most closely is the effective tax rate, which at 54.7% for FY2025 sits just 0.3 percentage points below the level that would trigger an exit rule if it persists after FY2026.

How the Score Adds Up

OMV scores −1.32, ranking 16 of 20 active holdings — the expected return, once taxes and the required minimum are subtracted, falls just short of the hurdle that would make the position worth adding to at today's price.

score   = quality-weight × (expected return − fx/tax drag − hurdle rate)   = 1.00 × (5.47% − 1.88% − 4.91%)   = 1.00 × −1.32%   = -1.32

Quality weight: 1.00. OMV holds the middle moat tier — two credible but non-self-reinforcing competitive advantages (state-protected exploration licences in Central and Eastern Europe, and a cost-advantaged chemicals stake via Borouge Group International) that are real enough to count but not strong enough to earn the top multiplier. A wider moat would lift the weight above one; a holding with no defensible position would reduce it below one.

Expected return: 5.47%. This figure is the probability-weighted average of the three scenario returns — 25% weight on −34.8% (bear), 50% weight on +2.1% (base), and 25% weight on +52.5% (bull). The arithmetic produces 5.47% before deducting costs and the required minimum. The arithmetic: 0.25 × (−34.8) + 0.50 × 2.1 + 0.25 × 52.5 = +5.48%, rounded to 5.47% after probability normalisation.

Hurdle rate: 4.91%. The hurdle is the minimum annual return the position must be expected to deliver to be worth holding over an equivalent safe alternative. It is built from three components: a real risk-free yield of 0.91% (what inflation-linked government debt pays above inflation), an assumed long-run inflation rate of 2.00%, and an equity risk premium of 2.00% (the extra return demanded for owning a share in a single company rather than lending to a government). Together these set the bar at 4.91% a year.

The score of −1.32 means the expected return clears the hurdle by only a small margin before costs — and after the 1.88% tax-and-currency drag is applied, it falls slightly below it. OMV sits in the lower third of the portfolio's 20 holdings by this measure, above only SP500, Berkshire, Datadog, and Unilever in the current ranking.

Recent Development

The FY2025 annual filing confirmed that OMV's operating cash flow held above €5 billion for the fifth consecutive year despite revenue falling 61% from the 2022 energy-crisis peak. The €5,215 million figure for 2025 — down 4.4% from €5,456 million in 2024 — is the clearest evidence that the cash-generation engine is structurally resilient rather than commodity-cycle dependent. An accruals ratio of −0.080 (a measure of how much reported profit is backed by actual cash received) adds further confirmation: cash earnings are meaningfully exceeding accrual earnings.

The two watchpoints that emerged from the filing are tax and capital expenditure. The effective tax rate — what OMV actually pays as a share of pre-tax profit, after all levies and adjustments — came in at 54.7% for FY2025, declining from 58.4% at the 2023 solidarity-levy peak but still well above the pre-windfall-era rate of 42.4% in 2021. In April 2026, five EU finance ministers signed a joint letter calling for a new solidarity levy on oil and gas companies; should that levy be enacted for FY2026, it would likely push the rate above 55% and trigger an exit condition. Capital expenditure, proxied by investing cash flows of €2,754 million in 2025, remains broadly flat rather than showing the expected peak; the incoming chief executive, Emma Delaney (joining from bp in September 2026), has stated capital discipline as a priority, making her first public guidance the next material data point.

The valuation reading also shifted: at €62, an owner-earnings yield of 7.58% — a measure of what the business earns relative to its market price — sits above the 7.5% threshold that triggers the pre-committed add instruction. That puts the share price fractionally below the buy edge. For the full picture of what the price implies, see the Share Price.

About OMV

OMV is Austria's largest integrated oil, gas and chemicals company. It earns money in three main ways. The first is finding and extracting oil and natural gas — primarily in Austria and the surrounding Central and Eastern European region, in Norway, and in the United Arab Emirates. The second is refining: OMV runs three European refineries that convert crude oil into petrol, diesel, aviation fuel, and chemical feedstocks, and operates one of the dominant retail fuel networks in Austria and Romania. The third, and increasingly the most important strategically, is chemicals: since March 2026, OMV holds a 46.94% stake in Borouge Group International AG, a new combined entity formed from OMV's Borealis subsidiary, the Abu Dhabi–listed Borouge company, and Canada's NOVA Chemicals. The combined business is the world's fourth-largest producer of polyolefins — the plastics used in packaging, pipes, automotive parts, and a wide range of industrial goods.

The Austrian state is structurally present. ÖBAG, Austria's state holding company, owns 31.5% of OMV; XRG (formerly ADNOC, the Abu Dhabi national oil company's investment arm) owns a further 24.9%. Together these two state-linked actors hold 56.4% of the company, which protects OMV from hostile takeover and anchors its access to state-sanctioned exploration licences, but also introduces the risk that capital allocation decisions reflect political priorities rather than purely commercial ones.

The Case for the Bet

Six specific conditions must keep holding for the investment to deliver the returns implied by today's price:

  1. Operating cash flow remains at or above €4.5 billion per year at current oil prices. This is the foundation of the thesis — without it, the dividend, the capital expenditure programme, and the debt service all come under pressure simultaneously. The cash-flow resilience across the 2022–2025 commodity-price cycle is the primary evidence that OMV's position in the Central and Eastern European energy system is durable rather than purely price-sensitive.

Latest reading: €5,215 million for the year ending 2025-12-31. This is the fifth consecutive year above the €4.5 billion threshold, achieved despite a 61% revenue decline from the 2022 peak. The accruals ratio of −0.080 confirms the quality of these cash earnings.

  1. The combined chemicals business (Borouge Group International) retains its book value above €4.0 billion over any two-year period. This condition protects against the risk that the chemicals pivot — which consumed years of management attention and significant capital — destroys rather than creates value. A decline below €4.0 billion in the equity-method carrying value for two consecutive years would signal the stake is impaired beyond rescue.

Latest reading: €5,255 million at 2025-12-31, which is 31% above the exit floor and above the €5.0 billion stability threshold. FY2025 is the last annual filing under the Borealis entity label; from FY2026 onwards, the combined Borouge Group International entity will appear, and the exit threshold may warrant recalibration given the larger combined scale.

  1. The effective tax rate stays below 55% in any year after FY2026. This condition targets the risk of recurring EU windfall levies on oil and gas producers. The solidarity contribution levied in FY2023 added €552 million to OMV's tax bill and pushed the effective rate to 58.4%. If a comparable levy is enacted again for FY2026 or later years, exit condition (3) would trigger.

Latest reading: 54.7% for the year ending 2025-12-31 — declining from the 58.4% FY2023 peak and 0.3 percentage points below the exit threshold. The condition applies only to years after FY2026, so FY2025 does not trigger it. However, the April 2026 EU finance ministers' letter calling for a new levy raises the probability of a FY2026 breach materially. This is the highest-urgency watch item entering the next filing cycle.

  1. Interest coverage — operating profit divided by interest expense — stays above 4.0 times for any two consecutive years. This protects against the risk that rising debt costs and falling earnings combine to threaten the investment programme and the dividend.

Latest reading: 8.0 times at 2025-12-31, down from 10.8 times in 2024 and 12.6 times in 2023 as operating profit compressed with lower oil prices. The current level is well above the 4.0 times exit threshold; the declining trend is worth watching but does not yet approach the warning zone.

  1. The dividend is not formally cut, and cash available from operations covers the dividend by at least 1.0 times over any two-year period. At €62 per share, the dividend yield is approximately 5.6–6.8%; removing this income pillar removes the primary near-term reason to hold the shares at current prices.

Latest reading: The annual cash dividend of approximately €6.50 per share requires about €2.1 billion per year; with operating cash flow of €5,215 million for FY2025 and investing outflows of €2,754 million, the implied free cash flow position suggests the dividend is covered, though free cash flow cannot be precisely measured from ESEF data because capital expenditure is not tagged in OMV's public filings.

  1. OMV's annual fuel sales volume in Austria and Romania does not decline more than 10% over any two-year period. PKN Orlen — the Polish oil and gas major — has been expanding its retail fuel network into Austria and other Central and Eastern European markets, directly threatening OMV's dominant regional position.

Latest reading: 16.2 million tonnes sold across eight European countries in FY2024 (from Competition research). No FY2025 update is available from structured data — OMV's annual report filing does not tag fuel-sales volume in machine-readable form, so this figure must be read from the annual report's narrative section directly.

The Share Price

At €62, the share price is fractionally below the buy edge of €63 — the point at which an owner-earnings yield (what the business earns per share, expressed as a percentage of the current price) reaches 7.5%, a level that the pre-committed rules treat as representing a 25% margin of safety relative to the long-run fair-value estimate. In practical terms, at €62 per share the price implies roughly 13 times the business's normalised annual earnings, which is a modest multiple for a company with five consecutive years of cash generation above €5 billion.

The fair-value anchor is €84 per share — derived by asking what price would give an owner-earnings yield of 5.6%, the rate consistent with the discounted-cash-flow estimate of the business's intrinsic worth. At €62, there is a 35% gap between the current price and this anchor. Worth noting: there is a second fair-value estimate derived from peer-company profit multiples that implies a lower central value of approximately €48. The two methods disagree because they make different assumptions about what normalised earnings look like; the gap is discussed further in Gaps in the Picture.

The Standing Orders

Two price levels are worth setting as alerts. The first is €84 — the fair-value central estimate; a price at or above this level means the margin of safety has narrowed and the position is no longer attractively priced for new capital. The second is €63 — the buy edge; a price at or below this level signals the pre-committed add instruction is active, assuming no exit condition has been triggered.

Three standing orders follow from the valuation rules:

Buy at €63 or below. When the share price falls to €63, an owner-earnings yield of 7.5% is reached — the pre-committed entry signal. The instruction is to scale in over two tranches, confirming that the operating cash flow trend is not deteriorating before committing the second tranche. Maximum addition per tranche is 50% of the existing allocation.

Trim at €104 or above. When the share price rises to €104, an owner-earnings yield of 4.5% is reached — what the Rules treat as "stretched" — a price at which the market is pricing in structural earnings recovery beyond even the bull-case macro assumptions. At that level, the instruction is to trim 25–33% of the position and re-establish at a lower yield if warranted.

Exit at €180 or above. At €180, an owner-earnings yield of approximately 2.7% is implied, implying the market is pricing in approximately 26 years of continued earnings growth at the current pace — a level inconsistent with any plausible scenario. The instruction at this level is to exit the full position.

Note: no exit-tier rule is configured for valuation alone — the €180 level is the computed outer bound, but the primary exit signals are the six operating-condition triggers in the Case for the Bet, any one of which would prompt a full exit regardless of the share price.

What's Wobbling

Three things are wobbling. Not one of them changes the direction of the overall verdict at today's price.

The effective tax rate is uncomfortably close to the exit line. At 54.7% for FY2025, the effective tax rate is 0.3 percentage points below the level that would trigger exit condition (3) if it persists after FY2026. The exit condition applies from FY2026 onwards — FY2025 at 54.7% does not trigger it — and the rate has actually declined from its 58.4% peak in 2023. But the April 2026 EU finance ministers' letter calling for a new solidarity levy makes this the highest-urgency item entering the next filing cycle. If that levy is enacted, the FY2026 effective rate is likely to cross 55% and the exit instruction would activate.

The capital expenditure ratio has not peaked as expected. The broader measure of capital expenditure relative to operating cash flow is estimated at 73.8% for FY2025 — broadly flat from 2024 and not showing the decline that was anticipated when the thesis was written. The exit condition fires only if this ratio exceeds 90% for two consecutive years; the current level is 16 percentage points below that trigger. The incoming chief executive's first capital-markets guidance will be the next meaningful data point on whether the investment cycle is genuinely peaking.

The fair-value estimate carried in the system is stale. At today's price of €62, an owner-earnings yield of 7.58% is observed — 35% above the 5.6% yield used as the fair-value anchor. The fair-value-bands analysis implies the anchor should be closer to 7.5–8.75%. This means the buy instruction is actually more conservative than the stale anchor implies — the shares are cheaper relative to earnings than the system's own fair-value figure would suggest — but the discrepancy means the fair-value anchor should be updated. This does not change today's verdict.

What Could Still Go Wrong

  • Oil prices fall sharply and persistently — Brent crude drops toward 55 US dollars per barrel — which collapses operating margins across both the upstream exploration business and the refining segment simultaneously, turning cash flow from positive to negative and making the dividend unsustainable without debt.
  • The EU enacts a recurring windfall levy on oil and gas producers for FY2026 and beyond, pushing the effective tax rate above 55% and triggering the exit condition — reducing after-tax earnings by hundreds of millions of euros per year regardless of the oil price.
  • The Borouge Group International chemicals business delivers returns below the cost of capital as polyolefin prices remain depressed, eroding the equity-method carrying value toward the €4.0 billion exit floor over two consecutive years.
  • PKN Orlen's expansion into Austrian and Romanian retail fuel markets captures enough volume from OMV's network that the annual fuel sales figures in Austria and Romania decline by more than 10% over a two-year period, confirming structural erosion of the Central and Eastern European retail moat.
  • The incoming chief executive's capital allocation priorities diverge from the thesis — if OMV's state shareholders use their 56.4% combined stake to direct capital toward politically motivated investments rather than value-creating ones, the capital expenditure-to-cash-flow ratio could rise without generating the returns the thesis requires.

Gaps in the Picture

The ESEF annual filing does not tag capital expenditure, net debt, or free cash flow in machine-readable form. All three are critical inputs for independently verifying the dividend sustainability, the capital expenditure-to-cash-flow ratio, and the leverage picture. The figures used in the analysis — including the 73.8% capital expenditure ratio — are estimates based on broader investing cash flow categories rather than precisely tagged items.

The annual report narrative is not available in machine-readable form: OMV files in German, and the iXBRL extraction failed for all six annual filings from 2020 to 2025. This means two of the seven thesis conditions — fuel-sales volume in Central and Eastern Europe (condition 6) and governance quality (the ÖBAG ownership risk driver) — cannot be assessed from structured data alone. The Rules

Five rules govern what action the valuation framework calls for:

  1. If the thesis is confirmed — operating cash flow above €4.5 billion, the capital expenditure-to-cash-flow ratio declining toward 60%, the Borouge Group International book value stable or rising above €5.3 billion, the effective tax rate below 55%, and interest coverage above 6 times — hold the current allocation and collect the dividend yield. Do not add at the current price; the probability-weighted three-year return at €62 does not support new capital at this level.

  2. If the thesis weakens — the capital expenditure ratio rises above 80% for one year, the chemicals book value declines toward €4.5 billion, the effective tax rate is sustained above 53%, oil prices fall below 60 US dollars per barrel for two consecutive quarters, or PKN Orlen captures material Central and Eastern European retail volume — do not add, reduce the review cadence to annual, and re-evaluate after the next annual filing.

  3. If the thesis breaks — any one of the six exit conditions triggers: capital expenditure ratio above 90% for two consecutive years; chemicals book value below €4.0 billion for two consecutive years; effective tax rate above 55% after FY2026; interest coverage below 4.0 times for two consecutive years; dividend formally cut or cash dividend cover below 1.0 times for two consecutive years; or Central and Eastern European fuel sales declining more than 10% over two years — exit the full position and move proceeds to a European diversified-energy fund or euro-denominated cash, depending on portfolio concentration at the time.

  4. If the price falls below the buy edge — owner-earnings yield above 7.5%, implying a share price at or below approximately €62 — add to the position over two tranches, with a maximum addition of 50% of the existing allocation per tranche.

  5. If the price rises above the trim edge — owner-earnings yield below 4.5%, implying a share price above approximately €94 — trim 25–33% of the position; harvest partial gains and re-establish at a lower yield if warranted.

Scenario Definitions

A scenario is a joint assumption about two independent variables: operating trajectory (how the holding's underlying cash flows grow relative to their long-run trend) and exit multiple (what the market pays for those cash flows at the horizon, relative to a fair-value anchor).

Scenario Operating trajectory Exit multiple
Bear Below trend Below fair value
Base At trend At fair value
Bull Above trend At or above fair value

Non-negotiable rule: In the bear case, both dimensions must be adverse simultaneously — a scenario where only the multiple moves is a re-rating scenario, not a bear.

Bear cases are comparable in structure across all holding types (company, ETF, bond), but not in magnitude. A company bear reflects idiosyncratic execution failure; an ETF bear reflects a broad market drawdown. Different mitigations, different diversification implications.
