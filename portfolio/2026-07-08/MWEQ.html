About the Fund

World Equal Weight tracks the MSCI World Equal Weighted Index — a version of the standard MSCI World index of large and mid-cap companies in 23 developed markets, rebalanced quarterly so that each of the roughly 1,500 constituents holds an equal share of the basket (approximately 0.07% per name on each rebalance date). It is managed by Invesco Investment Management Limited and listed on Xetra in Frankfurt under the symbol MWEQ.DE. The fund uses optimised (sampling) replication rather than holding every constituent directly, and it is structured as an accumulating share class — income is reinvested inside the fund rather than paid out.

The practical effect of equal weighting is to give materially more exposure to smaller companies, non-US markets, and lower-valued names than a standard cap-weighted global index would. In a cap-weighted version of MSCI World, the ten largest companies — mostly large-cap US technology businesses — absorb roughly 20–25% of the portfolio. In this fund, those same companies each hold roughly 0.07%, and the total weight of every US company combined is proportionally smaller than in the cap-weighted alternative.

Annual cost: 0.20% of assets per year. The fund is structured as an Irish-domiciled UCITS exchange-traded fund — capital gains are retained within the fund and not distributed, and no K-1 tax form is issued. Tracking difference and tracking error figures are not yet available as the fund is approximately 22 months old.

The Case for the Bet

The bet is structural: equal weighting is held as a deliberate counterweight to the concentrated global funds also in the portfolio, which tilt heavily toward a small number of large US technology companies. The argument is that spreading weight evenly across 1,500 developed-market businesses produces a higher basket earnings yield, more diversified geographic exposure, and a natural tilt toward smaller and lower-valued companies — at the cost of higher annual turnover from quarterly rebalancing and a structurally lower return on equity for the median constituent.

Six specific conditions must keep holding for the bet to remain valid:

  1. The basket must earn more than safe government bonds. Specifically, the earnings yield must exceed the 10-year government bond yield, and that margin must stay positive for two consecutive quarters before an exit is required. A single negative reading is not enough to trigger action.

Latest reading: Basket earnings yield 4.87%, government bond yield 4.49% → margin +0.38 percentage points. Positive across all five available historical readings; no consecutive negative quarter recorded.

  1. The annual cost must stay below 0.30%. Equal-weight funds incur higher operating costs than standard index funds because the quarterly rebalance generates approximately 30% annual portfolio turnover. The ceiling is set at 0.30% to maintain a meaningful cost advantage over less disciplined alternatives.

Latest reading: 0.20% per year, well inside the 0.27% early-warning level and the 0.30% hard ceiling. No drift.

  1. The fund must stay above $500 million in total assets. A UCITS-structured exchange-traded fund of this age faces elevated closure risk if assets fall sharply — Invesco has wound up sub-scale UCITS funds before. The $500 million floor is a vehicle-survival threshold, not a valuation signal.

Latest reading: Approximately $1.48 billion. Early-warning level is $800 million; current assets are nearly twice that.

  1. No single holding may drift above 1.0% of the fund. By design, each of the roughly 1,500 names starts every quarter at approximately 0.07%. If any single name drifts above 1.0% between rebalances — roughly 14 times its intended weight — it signals either a methodology change, a rebalancing failure, or extreme price movement that the sampling strategy has not corrected. The threshold is deliberately tighter than what would apply to a concentrated fund, because even weight distribution is the product.

Latest reading: Dell Technologies, the current top holding, sits at 0.27%. Early-warning threshold is 0.50%; hard ceiling is 1.0%. No flag.

  1. The MSCI Equal Weighted methodology must remain materially unchanged. A shift to factor-tilted weighting, removal of the quarterly rebalance, or a change in the parent universe would alter what the fund delivers and would require a fundamental reassessment of whether the holding still serves its purpose.

Latest reading: No automated signal exists for methodology drift. External monitoring required; no change has been reported.

  1. The median return on equity of the ten most prominent holdings must stay above 12%. Return on equity measures how much profit a company generates for each pound of capital shareholders have invested. The threshold is set lower than the 15% used for the concentrated global funds because equal weighting deliberately includes smaller, lower-margin businesses.

Latest reading: Median 14.14%, sitting in the middle band above the 12% floor but below the 15% confirm level. Structurally expected for a broadly diversified equal-weight basket.

The trim line, by contrast, sits at a yield of 4.00% — equivalent to a share price near $7.20 — and the basket would need to reprice by more than 20% from today's level to reach it.

The exit level is set at a yield of 3.00%, equivalent to roughly $9.60. At that reading, the basket would be priced so expensively relative to government bonds that the equity case would effectively disappear.

What's Wobbling

The equity risk premium is the only reading that carries a note of caution. At 0.38 percentage points, it is positive and has never gone negative across the five available quarterly readings — which means exit condition one (two consecutive negative readings) is not close to triggering. But it is also far below the 2.0 point level that would confirm the basket offers a genuinely compelling return over safe money. Any further rise in 10-year government bond yields — from the current 4.49% toward 4.87% — would erase the premium entirely and begin the clock on exit condition one.

The look-through quality reading (median return on equity of 14.14%) sits in the middle band by design. This is not a deterioration signal — it is what equal weighting across 1,500 companies looks like. The notable outliers in the current top-10 drift snapshot are two memory-semiconductor companies, Kioxia and Micron, whose very high return-on-equity figures reflect a cyclical peak in that industry rather than durable compounding quality. Equally, Intel's negative return on equity (-2.91%) is a known cyclical trough. The median strips out these extremes; 14.14% is the load-bearing read.

What Could Still Go Wrong

  • Rate shock compresses the equity risk premium to zero or below. At today's earnings yield of 4.87%, any 10-year government bond yield above 4.87% turns the equity premium negative. A sustained period of negative readings would trigger exit condition one after two consecutive quarters.
  • Multiple contraction crushes the bear-case return. The bear scenario in the value analysis assumes the basket's price-to-earnings ratio falls from 20.5× to 12× over five years — driven by a sustained higher-for-longer rate environment or a global recession. That contraction alone produces a negative annualised return even before any earnings decline.
  • Cost rise above the 0.30% ceiling. Equal-weight funds have structurally higher operating costs due to quarterly rebalancing; if Invesco raises the annual charge above 0.30%, exit condition two fires.
  • Asset base falls toward the $500 million floor. A sustained market drawdown combined with redemptions could bring assets toward the fund-viability threshold. At $1.48 billion today the cushion is wide, but it is not unlimited.
  • Methodology drift. A change to the MSCI Equal Weighted index — dropping the quarterly rebalance, shifting to factor weighting, or changing the parent universe — would alter what the fund delivers and trigger exit condition five. No automated monitoring exists for this risk; it requires external attention.

The Rules

If the thesis is confirmed — all conditions hold and the vehicle is sound — hold. The equal-weight basket is reasonably priced, the vehicle is sound, and the earnings yield premium over the concentrated global fund remains intact.

If the thesis is weakened — one or more conditions read in the warning band — do not add. Re-evaluate after the next quarterly rebalance. If the earnings yield premium over the cap-weighted alternative compresses below 0.5 percentage points for two consecutive quarters, the diversification benefit no longer justifies the higher annual cost; consider consolidating into the cap-weighted alternative.

If the thesis is broken — a vehicle-integrity condition breaches, the value test fails, or a written exit condition fires — exit. Move proceeds to the cap-weighted global fund or to a risk-free instrument.

If the share price falls to the buy level — add to the position. The equal-weight basket at a meaningfully cheap earnings yield is a rare and decisive entry signal.

If the share price rises to the trim level — trim 25–33% of the position. The equal-weight basket is meaningfully expensive relative to risk-free; harvest the premium and redeploy on the next rebalance.

If the share price reaches the exit level — exit the position entirely. The basket earnings yield is at or below 3.0%, effectively at parity with risk-free money. Redeploy proceeds to a risk-free instrument or to a holding that still meets the value test.
