Recent Development

The three months ending in March 2026 delivered one of the cleanest quarterly confirmations the AWS thesis has produced. Cloud revenue reached $37.6 billion, a 28% increase on the same period a year earlier, with an operating margin of 37.7% — nine-and-a-half percentage points above the written exit threshold of 28%. The $364 billion in remaining performance obligations from long-term customer contracts, combined with the announcement of a $100 billion, eight-year capacity expansion with OpenAI, provided a contractual anchor for growth that extends the thesis well beyond the current filing period.

The advertising business continued its trajectory. Revenue from advertising services came in at $17.2 billion in the quarter, up 24% on the same quarter a year earlier — far above the single-digit threshold at which exit condition five would engage. The International segment delivered a 3.6% operating margin on $39.8 billion in revenue, which is consistent with the thesis assertion that it has turned sustainably profitable. No written exit condition was triggered.

The one development that warrants close watching is capital expenditure. At $43.2 billion in a single quarter — a 78% increase on the same quarter in 2025 — the annualised run-rate is well above $150 billion. Management guided that investment would continue to increase through 2026. Cash generation, measured as the difference between operating cash flows and total capital expenditures, compressed to $1.2 billion for the twelve months ending in March 2026, down from $25.9 billion in the prior-year period. The AI capital cycle is still accelerating, not normalising. For a detailed look at what this means for the Share Price, the next section sets out where the current price sits against the valuation framework.

About Amazon

Amazon makes its money across three reinforcing businesses that fund and depend on each other. The largest by revenue is the retail operation: a marketplace where Amazon sells goods directly and also hosts millions of third-party merchants, who pay fees for the right to list and, increasingly, for advertising to reach shoppers on the platform. The logistics network underneath — its own aircraft, delivery vans, and a chain of fulfilment centres — was built over two decades and now handles more than 13 billion same- or next-day deliveries annually in the United States alone.

The second, and more profitable, business is Amazon Web Services, the cloud computing arm. Enterprises and governments rent computing power, storage, and software services from Amazon's data centres rather than building and running their own. AWS brought in $45.6 billion in segment operating income in 2025, representing a margin of roughly 35% on its revenue, and it is growing faster than the retail operation. The third engine is advertising: Amazon sells sponsored placements to merchants who want their products shown first in search results. Because these placements are targeted using Amazon's purchase-intent data — what people actually search for and buy — they command pricing that has been rising steadily. Advertising revenue was $68.6 billion in 2025, growing at 22% year on year, and it is becoming an increasingly important contributor to the overall profit pool.

The Case for the Bet

The thesis is that Amazon's three business engines — cloud, retail, and advertising — will continue to compound earnings power faster than the price of shares for the next three to five years, and that the AWS capital expenditure cycle will generate returns above the cost of capital by 2027–28. The conditions that must hold for that to remain true are:

  1. AWS quarterly revenue growth stays above 15%. The exit condition fires if AWS revenue growth falls below 15% for two consecutive quarters, which would signal that the AI infrastructure investment has not absorbed demand at the expected pace. The exit requires two consecutive quarters to filter out one-off disruptions.

Latest reading: 28% growth in the quarter ending March 2026, measured against the same quarter one year earlier. The $364 billion in contractual backlog from long-term customer agreements provides a multi-year anchor well above the 15% floor.

  1. AWS segment operating margin stays above 28%. The exit condition fires if AWS margin falls below 28% for two consecutive quarters, absent explainable one-off charges, signalling that the reinvestment economics have broken. The 30% level is the target; the 28% line is the written floor.

Latest reading: 37.7% operating margin in the quarter ending March 2026 ($14.2 billion operating income on $37.6 billion revenue) — 9.7 percentage points above the exit threshold. The slight compression from the 39.5% level of the prior year is consistent with intentional infrastructure investment, not structural pricing pressure.

  1. Advertising revenue growth stays in double digits. The exit condition fires if advertising revenue growth slows to single digits for two consecutive years. At that point, the third compounding engine would have saturated, changing the long-run earnings trajectory materially.

Latest reading: +24% in the quarter ending March 2026, reaching $17.2 billion. The growth rate is far above single-digit territory.

  1. International segment remains profitably operating. The thesis asserts that the International segment has turned sustainably profitable after years of investment losses, extending the runway for operating income to compound faster than revenue.

Latest reading: 3.6% operating margin in the quarter ending March 2026, on $39.8 billion in revenue. Foreign exchange movements contributed $347 million of the $1.4 billion operating income, which is the monitoring item — underlying profitability without favourable exchange movements is somewhat thinner.

  1. Capital expenditure does not persistently overshoot without matching revenue acceleration. Exit condition four fires if consolidated capital expenditure remains above $120 billion in both 2027 and 2028 without commensurate AWS revenue acceleration. The current year's spend, annualised, is well above $150 billion — but AWS growing at 28% satisfies the "commensurate acceleration" qualifier that keeps the exit from triggering now.

Latest reading: $43.2 billion in the single quarter ending March 2026, with management guiding that investment will continue to increase in 2026. The exit condition is not currently triggered, but this is the highest-priority monitoring item for the 2027 annual filings.

  1. No structural regulatory remedy, auditor change, or owner selling signal. The FTC antitrust trial, expected in late 2026, carries binary risk: an adverse ruling requiring business separation would unbundle the capital-subsidy mechanism that allows AWS margins to fund retail investment. Exit condition three fires immediately upon any court-ordered structural remedy. Condition six fires on a material weakness in internal controls or unexpected auditor change; condition seven fires if Jeff Bezos's Form 144 filings exceed 40 million shares in a single filing.

Latest reading: The quarterly filing contains only standing litigation disclosure, with no substantive update on trial status or scheduling. No structural remedy has been imposed; no auditor change; Bezos's known 10b5-1 disposals have not approached the threshold.

The Share Price

The current share price of $231 sits in the upper half of the hold band. The owner-earnings yield — the ratio of net cash generation, after adjusting for stock-based compensation, to the total market value of the company — is 4.6% at the current price. The fair-value anchor is 6.0%, which corresponds to a share price of approximately $176. The trim edge is 4.0%, which corresponds to $263. Nothing in the current valuation triggers the trim or exit orders.

The picture is worth a moment of plain-language translation. At 4.6%, Amazon is generating less in owner earnings per dollar invested than it would cost to buy a broadly diversified fund at fair value — and slightly more than the written trim threshold would imply. The market is prepared to accept this lower current yield because it expects the capital expenditure cycle to translate into substantially higher earnings in future years. That expectation is reasonable given AWS's trajectory; it is also the central assumption that the exit conditions are designed to monitor.

There is a material methodological divergence between the earnings-yield anchor used in the valuation framework here (6.0% yield) and the sum-of-parts model in the detailed value analysis (which suggests a base-case fair value of approximately $454 per share). The gap exists because the earnings-yield anchor was calibrated before the AI capital expenditure surge reduced reported cash generation, and a sum-of-parts model projects earnings on a post-normalisation basis. Both methods are internally consistent; the earnings-yield anchor is the more conservative of the two. Readers interested in why the market sees it differently will find a fuller treatment in the next section.

Why the Market Sees It Differently

The price says the business will earn more over time than what the earnings-yield model currently implies. The market's view and the framework's fair-value anchor — $176 based on a 6.0% earnings yield — diverge by 31.5%.

The precise nature of that divergence is almost paradoxical. At $231, the market is implying an owner-earnings growth rate of about 7.8%, just 0.2 percentage points below the 8.0% assumed in the framework's base case. On Amazon's own historical valuation multiples, the current share price sits near the bottom of the trailing five-year range rather than at a premium to it. The disagreement is therefore not primarily about growth expectations — it is about method. The owner-earnings yield framework uses reported cash generation, which has been heavily suppressed by $128 billion of annual capital expenditure; the market appears to be valuing Amazon on a projection of earnings power once that investment cycle normalises, much as a sum-of-parts model would.

Three hypotheses explain what the market could be pricing. The first is structural demand acceleration: if AWS AI workloads absorb the capital expenditure and sustain growth above 25% through 2028, the suppressed current cash generation is a temporary byproduct of a high-return investment, not a permanent feature. The contractual backlog of $364 billion and the $100 billion, eight-year OpenAI agreement directly support this reading. The second is margin and quality expansion: AWS is already at 37.7% operating margin and the overall business generated $79.9 billion in operating income in 2025, up from $36.9 billion two years earlier — the direction of travel is consistent with the market's more optimistic valuation. The third hypothesis is the AI portfolio held on the balance sheet: Amazon's stakes in Anthropic (with carrying value exceeding $70 billion in convertible instruments and equity) and a $15 billion investment in OpenAI are not captured in owner-earnings calculations that measure operating cash generation. The sum of these positions adds roughly $8 per share.

At least two of the three hypotheses are directly supported by evidence in the latest quarterly filing. What would need to happen for the gap to close in the other direction — for the framework's anchor to be vindicated rather than the market — is that the AI infrastructure spend fails to generate commensurate workload revenue, and reported cash generation in 2027 and 2028 remains suppressed relative to the $176 implied fair value. That is the bear case, with a 25% assigned likelihood.

The Standing Orders

The Rules translate the share-price bands into three standing orders and two price alerts. Each is the operationalisation of a pre-committed rule — not a new judgement made here.

Price alerts (informational).
- A first alert at $176, the fair-value anchor. A crossing downward means the share price has returned to the valuation midpoint; no action follows automatically, but a fresh read of the conditions is warranted before acting.
- A second alert at $140, the cheap edge. The crossing arms the buy order; before adding, the latest re-validation must confirm no exit condition is triggered and AWS growth remains at or above 18%.

Standing orders (actionable).
- A standing buy order at $140. Pre-committed conditions call for adding to the position in tranches over one to two quarters once the share price closes at or below this line and the core conditions remain working. The buy order is not triggered at the current price.
- A standing trim order at $263. Pre-committed conditions call for trimming 25–33% of the position when the share price closes at or above this line, to harvest the premium while keeping the core holding. The trim is not triggered at the current price.
- A standing exit order at $421. Pre-committed conditions call for closing the full position when the share price closes at or above this line. At that reading, the market would be pricing in roughly eleven years of base-case compounding immediately; the implied forward return falls below the long-term equity premium, and redeploying the proceeds beats continuing to hold. The exit is not triggered at the current price.

The three order levels do not contradict the current hold reading: those terms describe where the price sits today; the standing orders describe what will execute if price moves to each line.

What's Wobbling

The capital expenditure trajectory is the only active wobble. At $43.2 billion in a single quarter — and with management explicitly guiding that investment will continue to rise through 2026 — the annualised run-rate is well above the $120 billion annual threshold in exit condition four. The condition does not trigger yet because AWS grew 28% in the same quarter, satisfying the "commensurate acceleration" qualifier written into the exit. But the qualifier is the thin thread holding the condition in check. If AWS growth decelerates below the 18–20% range while capital expenditure remains elevated, the condition could trigger by the 2027 annual filings.

Capital allocation more broadly has shifted materially in the past twelve months. There were no share repurchases in either the first quarter of 2025 or the first quarter of 2026, with $6.1 billion of buyback authority remaining unused. Instead, Amazon committed $15 billion to an OpenAI preferred stock investment plus an additional $35 billion under an equity commitment letter, announced a $10.9 billion acquisition of Globalstar for satellite connectivity, and issued $53.8 billion of new long-term debt. The thesis described capital allocation as "acceptable but not excellent"; these moves push the characterisation toward the aggressive end of that range. No value-destructive outcome has been confirmed, but the concentration of large AI bets on predetermined terms — particularly the $35 billion OpenAI commitment — obligates capital on a forward basis regardless of execution.

The FTC antitrust trial expected in late 2026 remains a silent condition: the quarterly filing contains only standing litigation disclosure, no new development. A silent driver is not a confirmed driver. The trial's outcome remains the highest-probability binary risk in the holding over the next 24 months, and the Standing Orders are designed to execute if it materialises.

What Could Still Go Wrong

  • The AI infrastructure bet fails to convert. If AWS revenue growth decelerates below 15% as competitors — Microsoft Azure and Google Cloud — match Amazon's custom-silicon cost advantage, the $128 billion-plus annual capital expenditure cycle will have produced returns below the cost of capital. The bear-case sum-of-parts value is approximately $177, a 23% nominal decline from the current price.
  • FTC antitrust structural remedy. An adverse ruling in the FTC trial requiring separation of AWS from the retail business would unbundle the capital-subsidy mechanism that allows AWS margins to fund logistics investment. This is the scenario most likely to cause permanent impairment of the investment thesis, not merely a temporary earnings reset.
  • Earnings multiple compression. Amazon's current valuation implies roughly 27 times trailing earnings. If management cash generation (which was just $1.2 billion for the twelve months ending March 2026, after deducting total capital expenditures) does not recover to levels that justify a high multiple, the share price could compress substantially even if the business continues to grow.
  • Tariff and China supply chain disruption. Amazon acknowledged in the quarterly filing that sellers domiciled in China account for significant portions of marketplace revenue and advertising income. A sustained tariff-driven decline in China-based seller activity or consumer reluctance to pay higher prices for imported goods would impair the retail and advertising profit pools.
  • Capital allocation failure on concentrated AI bets. The $15 billion OpenAI investment plus $35 billion forward commitment represents a large, concentrated position in a single AI supplier with no liquidity, no voting rights, and execution risk on the $100 billion AWS-OpenAI capacity expansion. If OpenAI falters or the relationship deteriorates, the carrying value of these instruments would be impaired.

Gaps in the Picture

The fair-value anchor in the valuation framework — a 6.0% earnings yield implying a share price of approximately $176 — was calibrated before the current AI capital expenditure cycle began suppressing reported cash generation. The sum-of-parts model in the detailed value analysis, which projects earnings on a post-normalisation basis, produces a base-case figure near $454. The gap between these two figures (61% by the earnings-yield divergence measure) is material and should be reviewed before the next re-validation: updating the anchor to reflect capex-normalisation assumptions would substantially change the valuation band positions.

The International segment's profitability in the quarter ending March 2026 included a $347 million favourable foreign exchange movement. Stripping that out, the underlying operating margin is thinner than the 3.6% headline figure. This is not a structural concern — the thesis requires only that the segment remains profitably operating — but it means the FX tailwind is worth monitoring across subsequent filings.

Owner earnings, as computed for the valuation framework, use a narrow definition of capital expenditure based on the total of property, plant and equipment purchases as reported under accounting standards. Amazon's management presents a different definition of cash flow that deducts a larger portion of capital expenditure — including incentives and gross property purchases — producing cash generation of just $1.2 billion for the twelve months ending March 2026, against $25.9 billion in the prior year. Both figures are derivable from the filing; neither is wrong. The narrow definition is used for the valuation bands; the management definition is the more conservative view of near-term cash yield.

The risk-free rate used in the score calculation (for the hurdle rate) could not be confirmed from the holding's own market data file or from a fallback to the US equity index market data. The hurdle rate has been computed using the available inflation-linked bond yield; the equity premium has been applied at the standard rate. The score of 18.94 is therefore robust to that gap, but the return-premium comparison against the risk-free rate in the Standing Orders or the Case for the Bet context is indicative rather than exact.

The Rules

The following rules govern what action the framework calls for under each condition. The price and thesis states are evaluated independently; a rule fires when either leg applies, and the more restrictive of the two takes precedence.

  • If the thesis is confirmed — that is, if the core exit conditions remain untripped and the AWS growth and margin drivers continue to confirm — the rule is to hold and continue compounding. No action is taken.
  • If the thesis is weakened — that is, if any driver deteriorates but no exit condition has fired — the rule is not to add to the position. A further re-validation should be run on the specific deteriorating driver, and a decision deferred until two consecutive quarterly filings have confirmed the direction of travel.
  • If the thesis is broken — that is, if any written exit condition fires — the rule is to exit the full position within 30 days. No averaging down, no waiting for a recovery. For AWS revenue and margin exits, two consecutive qualifying quarters are required before the exit fires; for an FTC structural remedy or unexpected auditor change, the exit is immediate.
  • If the price falls below the buy edge ($140) — the rule is to first confirm the thesis is intact and AWS growth is at or above 18%, then add to the position in tranches over one to two quarters.
  • If the price rises above the trim edge ($263) — the rule is to trim 25–33% of the position to harvest the premium. The core holding is retained and the remaining position is re-evaluated after the trim.
  • If the price rises above the exit edge ($421) — the rule is to exit the full position. At that reading, the market would be pricing in roughly eleven years of base-case compounding instantly; the implied forward return falls below the long-term equity premium, and reallocating to a cheaper alternative beats continuing to hold. No re-entry within 90 days.

Scenario Definitions

A scenario is a joint assumption about two independent variables: operating trajectory (how the holding's underlying cash flows grow relative to their long-run trend) and exit multiple (what the market pays for those cash flows at the horizon, relative to a fair-value anchor).

Scenario Operating trajectory Exit multiple
Bear Below trend Below fair value
Base At trend At fair value
Bull Above trend At or above fair value

Non-negotiable rule: In the bear case, both dimensions must be adverse simultaneously — a scenario where only the multiple moves is a re-rating scenario, not a bear.

Bear cases are comparable in structure across all holding types (company, ETF, bond), but not in magnitude. A company bear reflects idiosyncratic execution failure; an ETF bear reflects a broad market drawdown. Different mitigations, different diversification implications.
