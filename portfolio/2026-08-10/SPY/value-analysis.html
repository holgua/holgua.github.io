5. Owner Perspective (Buffett Frame)

"Would Berkshire deploy cash into this basket today?"

No. At $742.09 per share the basket earns approximately 3.75 cents per dollar invested — before accounting for the 4.60% available risk-free from 10-year Treasuries. The spread is −85 bps; equity owners are accepting less yield than bond owners while bearing meaningfully more risk. This is precisely the configuration Buffett cited when describing Berkshire's large cash position: when equities yield less than T-bills, cash wins on both a yield and risk-adjusted basis. The quality of the top-9 holdings is not in question — median look-through ROE of 37.28% confirms these are genuine capital compounders — but excellent businesses at high prices are not excellent investments. To clear the minimum +2% equity risk premium threshold written in the thesis, the basket would need to trade at roughly 18×–19× trailing earnings, implying a price approximately 29%–33% below today's level. Berkshire would not deploy capital here at current prices, and the thesis framework agrees.
