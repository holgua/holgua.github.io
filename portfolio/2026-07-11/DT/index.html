Recent Development

The headline from Dynatrace's most recent annual filing is simple: the numbers held. Net revenue retention (a measure of how much existing customers spend compared with the same group a year earlier) came in at 110% for the third consecutive fiscal year — matching the two years before it almost exactly and remaining well above the 107% level that would trigger a one-quarter watch. Annual recurring revenue grew 18% to $2.05 billion, reversing a mild deceleration from the prior year. Free cash flow reached $530 million, representing 26% of revenue and growing 23% year-on-year, comfortably above the 20% floor that sits as an exit condition.

Management put $479 million of its own cash back into the company's shares over the fiscal year, buying at an average price of about $42. That buyback was completed and replaced with a fresh $1 billion authorisation with $849 million remaining as of 31 March 2026 — a signal that the board believes the stock is undervalued relative to the business's cash-generation trajectory.

The one development worth flagging is that Palo Alto Networks confirmed its acquisition of Chronosphere, a monitoring specialist, in the 2026 filing. That is the preparatory move that the written thesis named as a precursor to exit condition six. The actual trigger — public disclosure of named Dynatrace customers switching to a bundled Palo Alto or Cisco platform — has not occurred. NRR holding at 110% is the sharpest counter-signal available. For how the share price reads against these developments, see below.

About Dynatrace

Dynatrace makes software that monitors and analyses the health of large corporate technology systems in real time. Its customers — mostly large enterprises running complex cloud infrastructure — plug Dynatrace into their environments and it automatically maps every component, measures performance, and uses AI-driven analysis to identify the root cause of problems before they affect end users. The company earns its money through annual subscriptions, with customers typically paying more over time as they expand their usage across a broader share of their technology estate.

The core of the business is a product called OneAgent, a lightweight piece of software that embeds itself across the entire enterprise technology stack — servers, containers, applications, cloud services — and instruments everything automatically. This design is unusual: competing products generally require engineers to set up each monitored component by hand. The cost of tearing OneAgent out and replacing it with a rival is measured in engineering-years, which is why existing customers rarely switch. Dynatrace holds 176 patents in force through November 2044 and estimates a three-to-five-year engineering barrier to replication.

Revenue crossed $2 billion in the most recent fiscal year, with 85% of that coming from recurring software subscriptions. The company is shifting its customers from older module-by-module licences onto a single platform subscription called DPS, which lets customers draw down any combination of Dynatrace's capabilities from a unified credit pool. That transition, now covering approximately 70% of annual recurring revenue, is designed to make cross-selling organic rather than transactional.

The Case for the Bet

The central idea is that Dynatrace's instrumentation layer is structurally embedded in enterprise technology stacks, producing cash flows that grow as customers consume more, with no practical path for a competitor to displace it without multi-year disruption. The conditions that must keep holding for that to remain true are the following:

  1. Annual recurring revenue growing at or above 13% on a constant-currency basis for any two consecutive quarters. This is the primary signal that the platform is still winning new spending and renewing existing commitments. Below 13% for two consecutive quarters is a written exit condition.

Latest reading: 18% reported growth (FY2026 annual filing, 2026-05-20). The annual filing does not separately disclose the constant-currency figure, but an 18% reported growth rate sits well above the 13% floor, and prior filings have shown minimal currency effect.

  1. Net revenue retention at or above 107% on a sustained basis. Net revenue retention measures what the same cohort of customers spends in the current year versus a year earlier, expressed as a percentage. A reading above 100% means existing customers are expanding their spend. The watch threshold is 107%; two consecutive quarters below that level would trigger an exit.

Latest reading: 110% constant-currency for FY2026, unchanged from FY2025 and marginally above FY2024 (111%). Three consecutive years at or above 110% is the strongest available evidence that the switching-cost moat is intact.

  1. Free cash flow margin above 20% for any full fiscal year. Free cash flow (what the company actually generates in cash after covering its day-to-day costs and capital spending) must stay above 20% of revenue. Falling below that level for a full fiscal year is a written exit condition.

Latest reading: 26.2% margin on $2.02 billion of revenue in FY2026, generating $530 million of free cash flow. Up from 25.3% the prior year. The exit floor has not been approached.

  1. No open-market equity sales by the chief executive or chief financial officer exceeding $5 million within any 90-day window. Executives selling their own company's stock in the open market is treated as a credible signal that they see less value ahead than the current price implies. Sales at scale are a written exit condition.

Latest reading: No open-market sales disclosed as of the 2026-05-20 filing. The company repurchased $479 million of its own shares in FY2026 and authorised a further $1 billion of buybacks, which is directionally the opposite signal.

  1. Dynatrace Platform Subscription covering at least 72% of annual recurring revenue for three consecutive quarters. The shift from older module licences onto the unified platform subscription is the mechanism that enables organic cross-selling and reduces reliance on new procurement cycles. Stalling below 72% for three consecutive quarters — especially if management stops disclosing the metric — is a written exit condition.

Latest reading: No numeric penetration figure disclosed in the annual 10-K filing (2026-05-20). The prior quarterly disclosure referenced approximately 70% of annual recurring revenue. The exit condition cannot be evaluated until the next quarterly earnings disclosure. Watch the next quarterly report.

  1. No confirmed public announcement of named Dynatrace enterprise customers switching to the Cisco/Splunk or Palo Alto/Chronosphere platform. This is the bundled-suite displacement test. The preparatory step — an acquisition by either competitor — does not itself trigger the condition.

Latest reading: The annual filing confirms Palo Alto Networks completed its acquisition of Chronosphere in 2026. No named Dynatrace customer displacements have been publicly disclosed. NRR at 110% is the primary counter-signal. The condition is a watch, not a trigger.

  1. Net cash remaining above $600 million without a disclosed acquisition rationale. The company's cash cushion is a backstop for operational flexibility and a signal of financial conservatism.

Latest reading: Net cash $823 million as of 31 March 2026, with long-term debt of only $274 million. Well above the $600 million floor.

The Share Price

The framework uses the ratio of cash flow generated to the company's market value (a measure of what percentage of the company's price flows back as actual cash each year — the earnings yield on a cash basis) as the central valuation lens. The current yield is 4.0%, which sits between the 3.3% trim level and the 5.9% buy level, placing the share price in the middle of the hold band.

The implied fair-value yield is 4.4%, which corresponds to a price of roughly $41. At today's price of $45, the stock trades at a 9% premium to that fair-value anchor — modestly above fair value. The gap is not large enough to warrant trimming but is equally not the margin of safety that would make adding attractive at scale.

In plain terms: the share price is in roughly the right zone for continued ownership but not at a discount.

What's Wobbling

The main wobble is the competitive-risk driver. Palo Alto Networks' confirmed acquisition of Chronosphere is the preparatory signal for exit condition six — the one that requires documented displacement of named Dynatrace customers by a bundled platform. That displacement has not been confirmed; NRR at 110% is the primary evidence it has not yet occurred at scale. However, the acquisition means the infrastructure for a potential competitive threat is now in place, and quarterly NRR readings deserve closer attention than they did before this filing.

A smaller wobble sits in subscription gross margin: 85% in FY2026, down one percentage point from 86% in FY2025. The compression reflects higher SaaS hosting costs as customers use more of the platform's features. Overall gross margin actually expanded to 82% from 81%, so the total picture is positive. The underlying cost pressure is consistent with what other cloud-scale software businesses experience as they grow, and it is not close to triggering any exit condition. Worth watching if it continues in successive years.

The annual filing also introduces two new risk disclosures that were not present in prior filings: generic large language models being used as general-purpose monitoring tools ("vibe coding"), and the concept of AI-generated code reducing the complexity of the environments that Dynatrace is paid to monitor. Neither has measurable impact on revenue retention as yet, but both represent structural questions about the future demand for specialised observability software.

What Could Still Go Wrong

  • Net revenue retention falls. The bet's core assumption is that customers expand their usage. If NRR declines toward 107% over two consecutive quarters, the exit rule fires regardless of where the share price sits. A single-quarter dip to the 107–110% range would trigger a one-quarter watch.

  • Datadog accelerates. Dynatrace's primary competitor has grown its customer base and annual recurring revenue faster over the past two years. If Datadog begins winning enterprise accounts at the expense of existing Dynatrace customers — visible via NRR compression or through named account disclosures — the competitive moat assumption unravels.

  • Bundled-suite displacement materialises. The Palo Alto/Chronosphere combination and the Cisco/Splunk platform are specifically designed to win enterprise security-plus-observability deals as bundled packages. A sustained period of named customer switching announcements would trigger exit condition six directly.

  • Free cash flow margin compresses. SaaS hosting costs rose in FY2026. If the trend continues and margin falls toward 20% for a full fiscal year, exit condition three fires. The current 26% reading provides substantial headroom, but the direction is worth monitoring.

  • Revenue growth decelerates below the floor. The 13% constant-currency floor is the written threshold. In the bear scenario, observability commoditises and growth falls to low single digits by FY2028, producing an annualised return over three years of about −8%.

The Rules

  • If all conditions are holding: maintain the full position. Monitor net revenue retention, annual recurring revenue growth, free cash flow margin, and platform subscription penetration each quarter. Consider adding modestly if the share price dips below $38 while net revenue retention stays at or above 107%.

  • If the thesis is weakening: reduce to a half position within 30 days of the trigger. Weakening signals are net revenue retention declining toward 107%, annual recurring revenue growth decelerating toward 14%, or platform subscription penetration stalling below 72%. Monitor for two more quarters before deciding to fully exit or rebuild.

  • If the thesis is broken: exit fully within one quarter of a confirmed break. Broken conditions include net revenue retention below 107% for two consecutive quarters, annual recurring revenue growth below 13% for two consecutive quarters, free cash flow margin below 20% for a full fiscal year, executive open-market sales above $5 million, or documented enterprise customer displacement by Cisco/Splunk or Palo Alto/Chronosphere. Do not add to the position on a broken thesis.

  • If the price falls below the buy level: accumulate toward a full position. A price at or below $31 implies a free cash flow yield above 5.9%, which is a materially better entry than the current level relative to the base-case fair value. Management's own buyback at $42 average in FY2026 sets a practical reference point.

  • If the price rises above the trim level: trim toward a half-to-two-thirds position as the share price closes at or above $55. At that level the stock approaches the base-case fair value of $58 (at a 10% discount rate) and further upside requires bull-case execution. Do not exit fully unless the thesis is simultaneously breaking.
