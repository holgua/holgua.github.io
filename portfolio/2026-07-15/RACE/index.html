Recent Development

Ferrari's annual filing for the year ended 2025-12-31 confirmed that every numerically measurable condition remained intact. Revenue reached EUR 7.1 billion, up 7% from FY2024; operating income reached EUR 2.1 billion, up 12%; net profit reached EUR 1.6 billion. About Ferrari

Ferrari manufactures ultra-luxury sports cars and gran turismo vehicles — fewer than 14,000 a year, by deliberate design. The production cap is not a capacity constraint; it is the strategy. Customers wait two to four years for delivery on a standard model, and allocation to limited-edition variants is governed by a formal record of prior ownership. Access to the rarest cars is gated by the depth of a customer's documented purchase history — a loyalty structure with no analogue in any other industry. Starting prices for a new Ferrari exceed EUR 250,000; pre-owned cars regularly sell above their original sticker price on the secondary market.

Alongside manufacturing, Ferrari earns revenue from brand licensing — merchandise, branded experiences, and a Ferrari World theme park in Abu Dhabi — and from the Scuderia Ferrari Formula 1 team, which has competed in every World Championship season since 1950. The racing programme generates revenue while simultaneously serving as a global marketing engine that cannot be replicated by any company that has not been doing it for seven decades. Ferrari's first fully electric car, known as the Elettrica, is expected to open its order book in FY2026 with a target price above EUR 500,000.

The Case for the Bet

Ferrari is held on the premise that a permanent gap between supply and demand — engineered deliberately through production caps — produces a pricing machine that compounds earnings steadily without requiring large capital reinvestment. Six conditions define what must remain true for the premise to hold:

  1. Annual car production stays below 16,000 vehicles. The entire investment logic depends on demand outstripping supply. At or above 16,000 units delivered in any single year, the production discipline is visibly relaxing and the waiting-list premium that underpins pricing power begins to erode.

Latest reading: Production volume for the year ended 2025-12-31 is not accessible from the structured financial tags; the figure appears in the narrative sections of the annual report, which could not be extracted this cycle. Based on all publicly available evidence as of 2026-07-15, Ferrari has consistently delivered approximately 13,500–14,000 vehicles per year, and no management communication has framed production expansion as a strategic priority. This condition is not triggered.

  1. The profit margin (the share of every euro of revenue that becomes operating profit, including the add-back of depreciation charges) stays above 35% for each full fiscal year. A sustained drop below 35% would signal either that the scarcity premium has been compromised or that the development costs of the Elettrica have permanently compressed the earnings structure.

Latest reading: The profit margin including the depreciation add-back reached 38.8% for the year ended 2025-12-31 — the highest in the five-year filing record, and well above the 35% threshold. This condition is not triggered.

  1. Cash flow from the business stays above net profit for each full fiscal year. Cash generation exceeding reported profit is the standard test of earnings quality — it confirms that the accounting numbers reflect genuine cash. A ratio below 1.0× for any single year would trigger an immediate accounting review, since it would be inconsistent with every year in the five-year filing record.

Latest reading: Net profit for the year ended 2025-12-31 was EUR 1.6 billion; operating cash flow was EUR 2.3 billion, giving a ratio of 1.47×. This condition is not triggered and has not been triggered in any of the five filing years on record.

  1. The average selling price per car does not decline for two consecutive years on standard-lineup models. A two-year decline in average realised prices on non-limited-series cars would signal that demand has softened enough to affect the core range — not merely the timing noise of limited-edition delivery cycles.

Latest reading: The number of cars delivered is disclosed in the narrative sections of the annual report, not in the structured tags, so the average selling price cannot be computed automatically for the year ended 2025-12-31. Revenue grew 7% year-on-year; if volumes were approximately flat — which is consistent with Ferrari's stated production discipline — that implies average price expansion. This condition is not triggered on the available evidence.

  1. US import tariffs do not return to 25% while simultaneously forcing a cut in Ferrari's profit margin guidance. Ferrari currently faces a 15% US import tariff under the EU-US trade arrangement in effect from mid-2025. A return to 25%, combined with management acknowledging it cannot pass on the cost to buyers without cutting the margin, would contradict the pricing-power thesis demonstrated during the brief period of 25% tariffs in 2025.

Latest reading: The current tariff rate is 15%, based on public announcements as of 2026-07-15. The narrative commentary in the FY2025 filing was not electronically accessible, but the structured financial data shows operating margin expansion year-on-year — consistent with no tariff-driven cost absorption. This condition is not triggered.

  1. Ferrari's first electric car (the Elettrica) oversubscribes its order book within 12 months of launch. The Elettrica represents the first real test of whether the Ferrari brand premium transfers from combustion to electric propulsion. Slow uptake, price concessions, or delivery deferrals would signal that the new pricing tier cannot hold, making the electric programme a cost drag rather than an extension of the franchise.

Latest reading: The Elettrica order book had not opened as of the filing date 2025-12-31; launch is expected in the FY2026–FY2027 period. This condition becomes testable when the order book opens.

The Share Price

The owner earnings yield — annual net profit divided by the total market value of the company — stood at 2.75% as of 2026-07-15, at a share price of $376. The model's fair-value anchor is also 2.75%, which translates to a fair-value price of approximately $377. The current price is at fair value: there is no margin of safety, but there is also no overpricing signal that would call for trimming.

The buy signal fires at $244, where the earnings yield would reach 4.25% — the level at which a fresh or extended position becomes justified on return grounds. The trim signal fires at $829, where the yield would fall to 1.25% — a level at which the market is pricing decades of compounding with no room for error. The current price sits between those two levels, closer to fair value than to either edge.

Standing orders (actionable).

- A standing trim order at $829. The rules call for reducing the position by 25–33% when the share closes at or above this level, harvesting the premium while keeping the core holding.

No exit-tier price has been pre-committed under the price-band rules. The trim order handles the over-rich scenario; a full exit on price grounds alone has not been pre-set.

What's Wobbling

The principal limitation this cycle is the data gap. Ferrari files its annual accounts in the European electronic regulatory format, and the text-extraction software that converts these filings into searchable prose could not extract the narrative sections of any of the five annual reports in the filing history, including FY2025. Four of the six monitored conditions — production volume discipline, Elettrica order book status, US tariff pricing-power commentary, and management/governance alignment — are assessed from the written sections of the report rather than the structured financial tables. All four are silent this cycle, meaning neither confirming nor challenging the thesis. The three conditions anchored in financial tables (operating margin, revenue, and net profit) all confirm.

This is an infrastructure limitation, not a business signal. The workaround is manual: retrieve the annual report PDF from Ferrari's investor relations page and read the production-volume disclosures and management outlook directly. Until that is done, acting on the Standing Orders carries the additional risk that a narrative-based exit condition has fired and has not yet been detected.

The second limitation worth noting is the return outlook. Even in the base case — where the business executes as expected over three years — the share price delivers approximately −13.4% a year from the current entry point. This is a function of the current price already reflecting a decade of anticipated compounding, not a prediction of business deterioration. The business is working; the price already reflects that.

What Could Still Go Wrong

  • A prolonged decline in ultra-luxury demand. A sustained fall in the wealth of very-high-net-worth individuals — driven by a downturn in major financial markets or a significant China macro shock — could place Ferrari in a dilemma: maintain the production cap and let the order backlog thin out, or relax the cap to sustain revenues. Either path erodes the scarcity premium over time. The three-year bear-case price return from the current level is approximately −35.5% a year.
  • The Elettrica disappoints at launch. If Ferrari's first electric car does not oversubscribe its order book promptly, or if Ferrari must concede on price or delivery timelines to generate demand, the electric programme shifts from a margin extension to a cost drag — and the case that the Ferrari franchise transfers cleanly to a new technology weakens materially.
  • US tariff re-escalation. If the United States re-imposes 25% import tariffs on European cars and Ferrari's management guidance signals that the company cannot pass on the full cost to buyers, the most recent real-world evidence of Ferrari's pricing power is contradicted.
  • Valuation de-rating from luxury to automotive. Ferrari's current market value implies something close to a luxury-goods classification — similar to the multiple commanded by leading luxury-goods houses. A re-rating toward automotive peers, whose profit margins are a fraction of Ferrari's 38.8%, would compress the valuation materially even with no change in the underlying business.
  • Electric-vehicle development costs exceed estimates. Building a battery-electric platform alongside the existing combustion range requires capital. If the engineering and procurement costs of the Elettrica programme run higher than modelled, the free-cash-flow margin — which reached 19.7% of revenue in FY2025 — could compress, reducing returns to shareholders and the derived fair-value price.

The Rules

  • If all the conditions above are intact, hold the position with no action.
  • If one or more conditions show evidence of deterioration but no exit condition has fired, do not add to the position. Monitor through at least one full fiscal year before reassessing position size.
  • If any single exit condition fires — particularly if annual production exceeds 16,000 vehicles, which destroys the core moat irreversibly — close the full position within 30 days. No averaging down. Document which condition triggered the exit.
  • If the share price falls to or below $244 (earnings yield above 4.25%) and all conditions above are intact, add in tranches over one to two quarters.
  • If the share price rises to or above $829 (earnings yield below 1.25%), trim 25–33% of the position.
  • If the share price reaches a level where the earnings yield falls below 1.0%, close the full position. No re-entry within 90 days.

Scenario Definitions

A scenario is a joint assumption about two independent variables: operating trajectory (how the holding's underlying cash flows grow relative to their long-run trend) and exit multiple (what the market pays for those cash flows at the horizon, relative to a fair-value anchor).

Scenario Operating trajectory Exit multiple
Bear Below trend Below fair value
Base At trend At fair value
Bull Above trend At or above fair value

Non-negotiable rule: In the bear case, both dimensions must be adverse simultaneously — a scenario where only the multiple moves is a re-rating scenario, not a bear.

Bear cases are comparable in structure across all holding types (company, ETF, bond), but not in magnitude. A company bear reflects idiosyncratic execution failure; an ETF bear reflects a broad market drawdown. Different mitigations, different diversification implications.
