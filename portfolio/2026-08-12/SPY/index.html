About SP500

The SPDR S&P 500 ETF Trust is a passive fund that tracks the S&P 500 Index — a rules-based list of roughly 500 large US companies selected by Standard & Poor's for market size, liquidity, and financial viability. The fund owns shares in each constituent in proportion to its market capitalisation, so the largest businesses command the largest weights. Technology companies currently account for roughly 39% of the basket, with Nvidia, Apple, Alphabet, Microsoft, and Amazon together making up the top five positions. The remaining 61% spans financial services, healthcare, consumer products, industrials, and other broad sectors of the US economy.

The fund is structured as a Unit Investment Trust, the original legal wrapper used by exchange-traded funds in the United States. At $795 billion in assets, it is one of the largest and most liquid equity funds in the world, trading an average of tens of billions of dollars each day. Annual cost is 0.09% of assets — among the cheapest available for any US large-cap fund.

The Case for the Bet

The bet is that the S&P 500 basket, held as core US equity exposure, will deliver a return above what risk-free government bonds pay — measured by the basket's earnings yield minus the ten-year Treasury yield. Six conditions govern when that bet remains intact and when it does not:

  1. Equity risk premium stays positive across two consecutive quarter-ends. The basket's earnings yield (total profits divided by market price, expressed as a percentage) must exceed the ten-year Treasury yield by more than zero. The exit gate requires two consecutive quarter-end readings below zero, not just a single snapshot, to filter out temporary dislocations.

Latest reading: Earnings yield 3.83%, ten-year Treasury 4.70%, equity risk premium −0.86% as of 2026-08-10. Negative for 15 consecutive pipeline snapshots since 2026-06-16. Q2 2026 quarter-end was negative; Q3 2026 quarter-end falls on 2026-09-30 — if negative then, the gate confirms and the exit condition fires.

  1. Annual expense ratio stays at or below 0.15%. State Street charges shareholders an annual fee; if that fee rises above 0.15% without a competitive response, the vehicle's cost advantage has eroded to the point where switching to a cheaper alternative (Vanguard's VOO or iShares' IVV) is warranted.

Latest reading: Annual expense ratio 0.09% as of 2026-08-10, sourced from yfinance. Six basis points below the ceiling; exit condition (2) not triggered.

  1. Assets under management stay above $100 billion. A fund this far below its current $795 billion would face liquidity strains and potential forced liquidation; the floor marks an extraordinary stress scenario, not normal market movement.

Latest reading: Assets under management totalling $795.31 billion as of 2026-08-10 — 7.95 times the floor. Exit condition (3) not triggered.

  1. Top single holding stays below 13% of the basket. The S&P 500 is a market-cap-weighted index; when one company becomes dominant enough to exceed 13% of the whole basket, the fund is no longer meaningfully diversified and the broad-market mandate has effectively been undermined.

Latest reading: Nvidia at 7.55% — 5.45 percentage points below the ceiling as of 2026-08-10. Exit condition (4) not triggered.

  1. S&P 500 index methodology remains broadly unchanged. Standard & Poor's controls the rules that determine which companies are included and how they are weighted. A material restructuring of those rules — for instance, shifting to an earnings-weighted or equal-weighted methodology — would change what the fund actually represents and would require re-evaluating the thesis. See the Rules for the pre-committed response.

Latest reading: No S&P Dow Jones Indices methodology restructuring or State Street trust-structure change on record as of 2026-08-12. Exit condition (5) not triggered.

  1. Median return on equity of the top nine constituents stays above 15%. The basket thesis rests partly on the quality of the underlying businesses; if the leading companies' collective earning power collapses, the fundamental case for holding equities — rather than bonds — weakens regardless of price.

Latest reading: Median return on equity of the deduped top nine constituents 34.0% as of 2026-08-10, sourced from the latest re-validation. Weighted-mean 72.0% is inflated by Nvidia and Apple's buyback-shrunken book equity; the median is the load-bearing figure and sits 19 percentage points above the 15% floor. Exit condition (6) not triggered.

What's Wobbling

The equity risk premium — the spread between what the basket earns for shareholders and what government bonds pay — is the single wobble, and it has been persistent. The fifteen consecutive negative readings since 2026-06-16 have deepened from −0.72% at the start of the sequence to −0.86% at the 2026-08-10 snapshot. This trend does not cross any of the six numbered conditions in the Case for the Bet until two quarter-ends confirm the pattern. Q2 2026 was the first; Q3 2026-09-30 will be the second or not.

Everything else is either stable or well inside its lines. The five vehicle-integrity conditions — cost, assets under management, top-holding concentration, index methodology, and underlying business quality — all read well inside their floors. The wobble is entirely a valuation story, not an operational one. The basket is excellent; it is simply priced for returns that, at today's entry, do not compensate for the additional risk of holding equities over bonds.

What Could Still Go Wrong

  • Earnings recession with simultaneous multiple compression. The bear case requires both below-trend earnings growth and a retreat in what the market will pay for those earnings. The basket's current price-to-earnings ratio of 26× is well above the long-run historical average of 16×; a return to average multiples alone would imply a price decline of roughly 40% before any earnings change.
  • Sustained negative equity risk premium until Q3 2026-09-30 confirms the exit gate. The two-consecutive-quarter condition has one leg standing; if the earnings yield stays below the ten-year Treasury at quarter-end, the pre-committed plan moves from trim to full exit.
  • Technology sector re-rating. Technology at 39% of the basket is the dominant weight driver. A sector-level contraction in valuation multiples — driven by margin compression, regulatory action, or a shift in the AI investment cycle — would cascade through the basket's earnings yield and price more sharply than a broad-market move.
  • Expense-ratio increase without competitive response. Unlikely given competitive pressure from Vanguard's VOO and iShares' IVV, but the condition is written for a reason. A fee increase to 0.16% or above that is not matched by competitors would trigger exit condition (2).
  • Single-name concentration breach. Nvidia at 7.55% has risen quickly; a further doubling of Nvidia's market weight — while the rest of the basket stagnates — could push it toward the 13% ceiling that marks regime-change concentration. That is a long distance from here, but cap-weight dynamics can move fast in a bubble.
  • (For this fund, exit fires when a vehicle-integrity condition breaches, when the equity risk premium remains negative across two consecutive quarter-ends, or when any of the six named conditions triggers — any of these paths leads to the same exit.)

The Rules

The pre-committed rules for this holding work as follows:

  • If all six conditions are confirmed and the basket earnings yield sits in the hold band (between the buy edge and the trim edge), then hold — the basket is reasonably priced and the vehicle is sound.
  • If any driver challenges the thesis — most notably if the equity risk premium is negative but the two-quarter gate has not yet elapsed — then do not add. Re-evaluate after the next quarter. If the equity risk premium stays negative or the median look-through return on equity keeps falling, swap to an alternative fund or move to cash.
  • If the thesis is broken — two consecutive quarter-end readings confirm a negative equity risk premium, or any vehicle-integrity condition breaches — then exit. Move proceeds to risk-free instruments or to a holding that clears the value test.
  • If the basket earnings yield rises to or above 6.25% (the buy edge), then add to the position. The basket is meaningfully cheap relative to the risk-free rate.
  • If the basket earnings yield falls to or below 4.00% (the trim edge), then trim 25–33%. The basket is meaningfully expensive relative to the risk-free rate; harvesting the premium is the pre-committed response.
  • If the basket earnings yield falls to or below 3.00% (the exit edge), then exit entirely. At that level the basket is fully priced against the risk-free rate; the pre-committed plan calls for redeploying proceeds to risk-free instruments or to a holding that clears the value test.

Scenario Definitions

A scenario is a joint assumption about two independent variables: operating trajectory (how the holding's underlying cash flows grow relative to their long-run trend) and exit multiple (what the market pays for those cash flows at the horizon, relative to a fair-value anchor).

Scenario Operating trajectory Exit multiple
Bear Below trend Below fair value
Base At trend At fair value
Bull Above trend At or above fair value

Non-negotiable rule: In the bear case, both dimensions must be adverse simultaneously — a scenario where only the multiple moves is a re-rating scenario, not a bear.

Bear cases are comparable in structure across all holding types (company, ETF, bond), but not in magnitude. A company bear reflects idiosyncratic execution failure; an ETF bear reflects a broad market drawdown. Different mitigations, different diversification implications.
