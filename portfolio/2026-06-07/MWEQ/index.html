World Equal Weight: The Bet Is Wobbling

The Bottom Line

The fund holds roughly fifteen hundred global developed-market companies, each given the same small slice of the basket. It is meant to be the calm alternative to letting a handful of giants — the Nvidias, Apples and Microsofts of the world — dominate everything. As of June 2026 the fund is still doing exactly what it was built to do, but two of the gauges that should be flashing green are pale amber instead.

The Verdict

The basket is fairly priced, the fund itself is healthy, and the equal-weight machinery is grinding away as designed — but the quality of the largest companies inside it has slipped, and the extra return for taking on share-market risk over a safe German bund has shrunk to almost nothing. The reading is "still working, but watching".

A simple way to think about the next move is to imagine five readers, each holding the same shares for a different reason.

  • Fresh, never owned it. Today is not the day to start. The basket sits between cheap and expensive, the quality wobble is real, and the extra return over a safe bond is too thin to compensate for the volatility. Better to set alerts at the fair-value mark of €5.49 and the cheap-edge mark of €4.39 and wait for one of them to be hit.
  • Big gain, bought below €5. Sit tight. The shares have already rewarded the early move; nothing in the latest figures says to harvest. Let the position run while watching the quality reading and the bund-versus-basket spread.
  • Roughly even, bought between €5 and €6. This is where today's price of €5.74 lives. The honest read is "no edge either way". The structural reason for holding — diversifying away from the mega-cap concentration of cap-weighted funds — is still intact, but it is doing quiet rather than loud work right now. Do not add. Wait for the next quarter's rebalance to refresh the picture.
  • Mild loss, bought between €6 and €7. Roughly four to eighteen per cent underwater. Uncomfortable, but the rules say a wobble is not a break. The fund itself has not failed any survival test. Hold the position and re-read the situation at the next rebalance.
  • Deep loss, bought above €7. Down eighteen per cent or more. The instinct to capitulate is strong; the written rules push back the other way. Do not add either — the case for averaging down requires a price at the cheap edge, which today's market has not delivered.

About the Fund

The fund follows an index built by MSCI that takes the entire developed-market share universe — roughly fifteen hundred companies across the United States, Japan, Britain, Europe, Canada, Australia and a handful of smaller markets — and gives every single one of them the same starting weight. That works out to about one fifteen-hundredth of the basket each, or roughly 0.07 per cent per name. Every three months the fund resets those weights back to equal, which means it quietly sells whatever has run up and buys whatever has fallen behind.

That is the whole idea. A cap-weighted global fund lets the winners get bigger and bigger inside the basket — at the moment of writing, a single American chipmaker is over five per cent of the cap-weighted version of the same universe. The equal-weight version refuses to play that game. It deliberately tilts away from the giants and toward the smaller and mid-sized companies, away from the United States and toward Japan and Europe, and away from the most expensive names and toward the cheaper ones. The cost of doing that is a slightly higher running charge (0.20 per cent a year, against roughly 0.12 for cap-weighted alternatives) and a small drag from the quarterly reshuffle.

The Case for the Bet

The single idea behind owning this fund is that diversification away from mega-cap concentration is worth paying a modestly higher running cost for. Three conditions have to keep being true for the fund itself to remain a sensible vehicle for that idea.

  1. Scale. The fund's assets under management must stay comfortably above €500 million. UCITS-style funds that drop below that level have a habit of being quietly wound up by their sponsors.
  2. Cost. The annual running charge must stay at or below 0.30 per cent. Equal-weight funds run more expensively than cap-weighted ones because of all the quarterly reshuffling; above 0.30 per cent the cost of the tilt starts eating the benefit of it.
  3. Method. MSCI must keep running the index the way it does today — equal weights, quarterly resets, the same global developed-market universe. If MSCI changes the rulebook (introducing factor tilts, slowing the rebalance, or narrowing the universe), the fund tracks something different from what was bought.

The Rules

These five lines were written down when the picture was calm. Each one is an instruction to follow when a particular thing becomes true.

  • If the underlying thesis still holds → hold the position; the basket is fairly priced, the vehicle is sound, and the extra earnings yield over the cap-weighted version remains intact.
  • If the thesis weakens → do not add. Re-read the figures after the next quarterly rebalance. If the equal-weight earnings-yield edge over the cap-weighted version shrinks below half a percentage point for two quarters running, the diversification benefit no longer pays for the higher running cost — consolidate into a cap-weighted global fund instead.
  • If the thesis breaks → exit. The fund has failed a survival test (assets, cost, drift, or method) or the basic value test has gone. Move the proceeds into a cap-weighted global fund or into a safe bond.
  • If the price drops below the cheap edge → add to the position. The structural earnings-yield premium of the equal-weight basket over the cap-weighted version should make this rare and decisive.
  • If the price climbs above the expensive edge → trim a quarter to a third. The basket has become meaningfully expensive against a safe bond; harvest the premium and redeploy at the next reshuffle.

The Conditions Today

Scale. The fund holds about €1.43 billion in assets. That is roughly two and three quarter times the €500 million survival floor. Comfortable headroom for a fund that is only about twenty-one months old.

Cost. The most recent snapshot did not surface the running charge directly. The most recently disclosed figure was 0.20 per cent, well clear of the 0.30 per cent ceiling. This is worth confirming at the next refresh — silence is not the same as a green light.

Method. MSCI has made no announcement that would change how the index is built. The fund's sponsor — Invesco — has likewise made no move that suggests it is rationalising the product. Both readings rely on watching the press rather than reading data feeds.

The quality wobble. When the ten largest names in the basket are looked at together, the typical return on shareholders' equity comes out at about eight per cent. The written rule asks for at least twelve per cent before that gauge is considered comfortable. Eight is below twelve. The top of the basket is currently dominated by semiconductor and networking-hardware companies (Marvell, Intel, AMD, Kioxia, STMicro, Ciena, Nokia and Bloom Energy among them) caught in the middle of a chip-cycle trough. Some of these businesses earn very well at the top of the cycle; right now most are earning poorly or losing money. This is the price equal-weighting pays for diversification — when the rebalance pulls money out of the high-quality compounders and spreads it across the wider cross-section, the visible top of the basket starts to look more cyclical and less impressive.

The reward-over-safe-money gauge. The basket as a whole is earning about 4.78 per cent on the price being paid for it. A safe German government bund pays about 4.54 per cent right now. The difference — twenty-five hundredths of one percentage point — is what is being earned for accepting share-market volatility, currency wobble, and a small drag from the quarterly reshuffle. The written rule asks for at least two full percentage points. The current quarter of one percentage point is well short. Not yet a breach (that requires a negative number for two quarters in a row), but the thin end of the wedge.

The Standing Orders

Two alerts and two orders, set on the share price.

  • Alert at the cheap edge (€4.39). The structural earnings-yield premium over the cap-weighted version has opened up enough to deserve attention.

  • Order: trim above the expensive edge. A move above €6.87 triggers a 25 to 33 per cent reduction.

A note on the sell side: for an index basket like this one, the case for selling is governed less by the share price and more by whether the fund itself is still doing its job. A breach of any of the survival tests — assets below floor, cost above ceiling, top-name drift above the equal-weight cap, or a methodology shift — is what triggers an exit. Price triggers a trim; vehicle integrity triggers an exit. The two are distinct.

What's Wobbling

The quality of the top of the basket is the most honest worry. A median return on equity of eight per cent across the ten largest names is below the twelve per cent threshold written into the rules and a long way from the fifteen per cent that signals a basket of high-quality compounders. The reading is not catastrophic — it is what happens when a chip cycle bottoms while a wider equal-weight basket is being scored at the top — but it does mean that the visible top of the fund is currently a parade of cyclicals rather than a parade of compounders. Whether that lifts on the next quarter's reshuffle, or settles in as a structural feature, is what the next reading needs to clarify.

The extra-return-over-bunds gauge is the secondary worry. A quarter of one percentage point is positive but thin; it does not yet trigger any rule, but it is the warning shadow of a future trigger.

What Could Break the Fund

  • A sustained drop in assets below €500 million, putting the fund at risk of being quietly wound up by Invesco.
  • A rise in the annual running charge above 0.30 per cent, at which point the cost of the equal-weight tilt eats the benefit of it.
  • A drift of any single holding above 0.20 per cent of the basket — three times the equal-weight anchor — which would mean the quarterly rebalance machinery is failing or has been changed.
  • A change of method by MSCI: a shift to factor tilts, a slowdown of the rebalance frequency, or a narrowing of the universe.
  • A collapse of the equal-weight earnings-yield premium over the cap-weighted version of the same universe to below half a percentage point for two quarters running, at which point the diversification stops paying for itself.
