About OMV

OMV AG is Austria's largest integrated oil, gas and chemicals company. The business has three distinct earning engines. The first is upstream: OMV explores for and produces oil and gas in Central and Eastern Europe, Norway, and the United Arab Emirates, with domestic Austrian licences underpinned by the Austrian government's energy-security priorities. The second is downstream: three European refineries (in Austria, Germany, and Romania) convert crude into fuels and petrochemical feedstocks, supported by the densest retail fuel network in Austria and Romania — a position built over decades that cannot be replicated cheaply. The third is chemicals: a 46.94% stake, accounted for as an equity investment, in Borouge Group International AG (BGI), formed in March 2026 by combining Borealis (the polyolefin producer previously majority-owned by OMV), Borouge (an Abu Dhabi–listed chemicals company), and NOVA Chemicals (a North American producer) into what became the world's fourth-largest polyolefin producer. Polyolefins — the plastics used in packaging, automotive parts, and agricultural films — are the chemical building block BGI supplies at scale.

The Austrian government, through its state investment holding company, holds 31.5% of OMV, and Abu Dhabi's energy company holds a further 24.9% through a stake acquired in 2020. Together, state actors control more than 56% of the voting rights. That ownership protects OMV from hostile takeover bids and provides social licence for domestic gas projects, but it also means that dividend policy and capital allocation decisions reflect energy-security priorities as much as shareholder return optimisation. The same governments that anchor OMV's licence position also advocated for the windfall levy that added a €552 million one-off tax charge in 2023.

The Case for the Bet

The bet is that OMV's operating cash engine — reinforced by its state-anchored licence position in CEE and the global polyolefin scale of the BGI stake — can generate durable cash returns above the cost of capital, support the current dividend yield, and re-rate toward intrinsic value as capital expenditure peaks and the chemicals pivot delivers. Six conditions must keep holding:

  1. Annual operating cash flow stays above €4.5 billion at prevailing oil prices. This is the structural floor. Operating cash flow below €3.5 billion at oil above 60 US dollars a barrel would signal that the cost structure has deteriorated beyond what commodity prices explain, confirming the bear scenario is materialising.

Latest reading: €5,215 million in FY2025, down 4.4% from FY2024 but above the threshold for the fifth consecutive year. Cash earnings quality is confirmed by an accruals ratio of −0.08.

  1. Capital expenditure stays below 90% of operating cash flow for any two consecutive years. At or above that level, free cash flow effectively disappears and the dividend can no longer be self-funded from operations — the company would need to borrow to pay shareholders.

Latest reading: Roughly 74% in FY2025 by the broader thesis estimate (53% using the narrower investing cash flow figure alone). Both readings are below the 90% exit trigger, though the expected peak in spending has not yet materialised. Two consecutive years above 90% would trigger exit condition one.

  1. The BGI equity stake holds above €4.0 billion in book value for two consecutive years. A sustained fall below that level would confirm the chemicals pivot is destroying rather than creating value.

Latest reading: €5,255 million at the end of FY2025 — 31% above the exit floor and above the €5.0 billion stability threshold. The March 2026 BGI formation had not yet appeared in these numbers; the FY2026 annual report will be the first to show the combined entity.

  1. The effective tax rate stays at or below 55% in any year after 2026. A sustained breach confirms the EU windfall levy has become a recurring structural charge rather than a one-off, impairing earning power beyond what the original thesis accounted for.

Latest reading: 54.7% in FY2025, declining from 58.4% at the 2023 solidarity-levy peak. The condition technically does not apply to FY2025, but the 0.3 percentage-point margin is narrow, and the political backdrop — five EU finance ministers' joint letter in April 2026 — keeps the risk elevated for 2026.

  1. The interest coverage ratio stays above 4.0 times for two consecutive years. Below that level, leverage stress is threatening the investment programme.

Latest reading: 8.0 times in FY2025, down from 10.8 times in FY2024 as operating profit compressed with lower commodity prices. Still well above the exit threshold.

  1. OMV's annual fuel sales volume in Austria and Romania does not fall by more than 10% over any two-year period. A decline of that magnitude, confirmed in the annual filing, would signal structural erosion of the CEE retail moat.

Latest reading: Not available for FY2025 — the annual filing's narrative section, where this figure is disclosed, could not be extracted from the structured data. The FY2024 reference point was 16.2 million tonnes across eight European countries. PKN Orlen's expansion across the region is the primary competitive threat to watch.

The share price — €62 at the latest reading — sits at or just below the buy edge of roughly €63 per share, which corresponds to an owner earnings yield (annual earnings relative to market capitalisation) of 7.5%. That yield threshold was set as the point at which the market is offering a 25% discount to the discounted cash flow estimate of intrinsic value. In other words, the current price implies the market is valuing OMV below what the business is worth on a conservative, no-growth-premium basis — which is the buy signal the Rules define.

The fair-value anchor in the current setup implies a price of roughly €84 per share. The share price of €62 is meaningfully below that, which is consistent with the buy-edge reading. That said, a separate scenario analysis using peer earnings multiples — a different method — points to a base-case value of €61, almost exactly where the share price stands today. The two fair-value estimates diverge substantially, and that divergence is the reason the valuation picture carries more uncertainty than the simple yield signal alone would suggest. What's Wobbling

Three wobbles sit in the current reading. Each warrants monitoring, though the thesis verdict is unchanged.

Tax rate proximity to the exit threshold. The effective tax rate of 54.7% in FY2025 is the closest it has been to the 55% exit condition since the 2023 solidarity-levy year, when it hit 58.4%. The partial normalisation since then is genuine — two years of partial retreat from the peak — but the structural plateau well above the pre-levy rate of roughly 42% in 2021 suggests European fiscal pressure on integrated oil and gas has not eased. The April 2026 joint letter from five EU finance ministers calling for a new solidarity levy keeps the risk alive for the 2026 reporting year. No escalation beyond a watchlist item is warranted until a specific enactment occurs, but it is the single highest-urgency item entering the next annual filing cycle.

Capital expenditure peak not yet confirmed. The investment-to-cash ratio of roughly 74% in FY2025 is broadly flat from the prior year, meaning the expected reduction in spending intensity has not yet materialised in the observable data. Two consecutive years above 90% would trigger the exit condition; the current reading is 16 percentage points below that line. The incoming chief executive — joining from bp in September 2026 and having stated capital discipline as a priority — represents the next meaningful catalyst for revised spending guidance.

Competitive blind spot on CEE fuel volumes. The annual fuel sales figure for Austria and Romania, which exit condition six monitors, could not be extracted from the structured filing data for FY2025. The ESEF annual filing's narrative sections, where this figure is typically disclosed, failed to parse from the structured data. This is not a signal that volumes have declined — it is a data gap. The FY2024 reference point of 16.2 million tonnes across eight European countries is the last available reading. PKN Orlen's expansion across the region is the named competitive threat to track.

What Could Still Go Wrong

  • Oil price collapse. If Brent crude falls to around 55 US dollars a barrel and stays there, operating cash flow likely turns negative, the dividend — currently absorbing roughly 86% of net income — becomes unsustainable, and the bear-case scenario fair value of roughly €14 per share (using the earnings-multiple method) represents an 81% decline from the current price. Commodity businesses cannot protect themselves against price cycles of that magnitude.
  • EU windfall levy recurrence. A formal enactment of a new solidarity levy for the 2026 financial year would likely push the effective tax rate above 55%, triggering exit condition three. The 2023 precedent added a €552 million one-off charge and drove the tax rate to 58.4%. A repeat would permanently restructure the earnings base at a lower level. Five EU finance ministers are already on record calling for this.
  • BGI chemicals trough deepens. The Borouge Group International stake — valued at €5.26 billion at the end of FY2025 — is exposed to the global polyolefin supply/demand cycle. A sustained trough with oversupply from Middle Eastern and North American producers could push the book value toward the €4.0 billion exit floor over two annual periods. The March 2026 BGI formation brings scale but also consolidates the exposure.
  • Capital expenditure trap. If spending does not peak and instead rises further — driven by state-directed domestic energy investment, E&P licence commitments, or BGI integration spending — the ratio of capital expenditure to operating cash flow could approach the 90% exit trigger. At that point, the dividend and the investment programme can no longer both be funded from operating cash, and one of them gives way.
  • CEE fuel retail erosion. PKN Orlen's post-merger expansion across Central and Eastern Europe is the primary competitive threat to OMV's retail moat in Austria and Romania. A structural 10% decline in fuel sales volume over two years — the exit condition — is not inconceivable over a five-year horizon given regulatory mandates on electric vehicles and Orlen's aggressive network expansion.

Gaps in the Picture

The owner earnings figure used throughout this evaluation — approximately 1.74 billion US dollars — is a manual override derived from the 2025 net income figure of €1,520 million. It bypasses the automated calculation because capital expenditure is not tagged in OMV's European-format structured filing data, making a direct free cash flow derivation impossible. The override should be updated when the underlying value analysis is refreshed.

The fair-value anchor in the system carries a recorded mismatch. The recorded fair-value threshold of 5.6% diverges from the current earnings yield of 7.58% by more than 30%, and the peer-multiple scenario analysis implies a base-case yield of roughly 8.75% — diverging from the recorded anchor by more than 50%. This means the buy edge of €63 is derived from a fair-value anchor that may be stale. The practical effect is that the buy signal is more conservative than a freshly calibrated anchor would produce — the verdict is to add, not to hold, and a recalibration would likely reinforce rather than reverse that reading. The two fair-value estimates cited in the three-year scenario return block (€84 from the yield method, €61 from the peer-multiple method) reflect this unresolved divergence.

The CEE fuel sales volume figure for FY2025 is unavailable in the structured data — the annual filing narrative, where this figure is disclosed, could not be extracted by the automated process. A manual review of the annual report document is required to update exit condition six's monitoring reading.

Governance disclosures — supervisory board composition, related-party transactions, and any ÖBAG-directed capital allocation announcements — are not available from structured data. These are disclosed in the narrative sections of the annual report, which also failed to extract. The ownership structure (Austrian government vehicle 31.5%, Abu Dhabi energy company 24.9%) is confirmed from prior sources and has not changed, but the absence of narrative data means early-warning signals on governance cannot be assessed from the structured filing alone.

The Rules

The holding's pre-committed action plan translates into five direct rules:

  • If the thesis is confirmed — operating cash flow above €4.5 billion, spending intensity declining toward 60% of cash flow, BGI book value stable or rising, tax rate below 55%, and interest coverage above 6 times — hold the current allocation and collect the dividend yield. Do not add at the current price; the probability-weighted three-year return from this level is negative.
  • If the thesis is weakening — spending intensity above 80% for one year, BGI book value declining toward €4.5 billion, tax rate sustained above 53%, oil below 60 US dollars a barrel for two consecutive quarters, or PKN Orlen capturing material fuel retail volume from OMV — do not add. Reduce the review cadence to annual and re-evaluate after the next annual filing.
  • If the thesis has broken — any one of the six exit conditions triggered — exit the full position. Move the proceeds to a European diversified-energy fund or to short-term bonds depending on portfolio concentration at the time.
  • If the share price falls below the buy band — an earnings yield above 7.5%, corresponding to a price at or below roughly €62 per share — add to the position in two tranches. Confirm that operating cash flow is not deteriorating before committing the second tranche. Maximum addition is 50% of the existing allocation per tranche.
  • If the share price rises above the trim band — an earnings yield below 4.5%, corresponding to a price above roughly €94 per share — trim 25 to 33 per cent of the position to harvest the premium.

Scenario Definitions

A scenario is a joint assumption about two independent variables: operating trajectory (how the holding's underlying cash flows grow relative to their long-run trend) and exit multiple (what the market pays for those cash flows at the horizon, relative to a fair-value anchor).

Scenario Operating trajectory Exit multiple
Bear Below trend Below fair value
Base At trend At fair value
Bull Above trend At or above fair value

Non-negotiable rule: In the bear case, both dimensions must be adverse simultaneously — a scenario where only the multiple moves is a re-rating scenario, not a bear.

Bear cases are comparable in structure across all holding types (company, ETF, bond), but not in magnitude. A company bear reflects idiosyncratic execution failure; an ETF bear reflects a broad market drawdown. Different mitigations, different diversification implications.
