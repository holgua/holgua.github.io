The Bottom Line

Data as of 2026-07-30 · Latest filing 2026-07-09.

Recent Development

The latest quarterly filing, covering the 24 weeks ending 2026-06-13, showed PepsiCo's persistent beverage volume problem holding without a positive turn. The PepsiCo Beverages North America segment — the part of the business that makes and sells carbonated soft drinks and non-carbonated beverages in the United States — saw unit volume fall 3% in the first half of 2026 and 4% in the second quarter alone, with both carbonated and non-carbonated categories declining. A 6 percentage-point commodity cost headwind in the second quarter compressed the segment's operating economics further. Nine or more consecutive quarterly reporting periods have now passed without a positive volume reading, and that persistence is the sole reason the thesis sits in a watching state rather than a confirming one.

The rest of the picture is more constructive. Consolidated gross profit margin — the share of every dollar of sales that remains after paying for ingredients, packaging, and manufacturing — came in at 54.2% for the quarter and 54.7% for the first half, both within the 53–55% corridor that the thesis defines as evidence the brand moat is absorbing cost pressure. Operating profit on a reported basis recovered strongly against a prior year distorted by a large write-down of the Rockstar Energy brand, and core operating profit (excluding one-off charges) rose 4% in the second quarter and 6% in the first half. Free cash flow recovered sharply: the first half generated $1,170M of free cash, compared with a small negative figure in the same period last year. Management also raised the annual dividend by 4% to $5.92 per share, extending a consecutive-annual-increase record that now stands at 54 years.

The valuation reading was unchanged. At $139.10, an earnings yield of 4.72% — the ratio of the company's normalised profit to its market value — sits essentially at the 4.70% level defined as fair value, confirming that no valuation threshold was approached in either direction. For more on what that means for price levels and the Standing Orders, see the Share Price.

About PepsiCo

PepsiCo makes its money selling snack foods and beverages — Lay's crisps, Doritos, Cheetos, and Quaker oats through its foods divisions, and Pepsi-Cola, Gatorade, Mountain Dew, and Lipton through its beverages arm. Revenue of $93.9B in FY2025 spread across six reporting segments, with roughly 56% of sales generated outside North America. The business manufactures and ships directly to retailers and outlets through a capital-intensive direct-outlet-delivery network, which means PepsiCo trucks deliver to shelves rather than going through a distributor middleman. That delivery infrastructure — which costs roughly $4.4B a year in capital spending to maintain — is the structural backbone that makes the brand portfolio worth more than the sum of its names: any new entrant must either build an equivalent system from scratch or cede the shelf placement and freshness advantages that sustained, route-based delivery provides.

Twenty-three brands each generate more than $1B a year in retail sales. No single competitor replicates the combination of roughly 60% share in United States salty snacks through the Frito-Lay business and a leading position in United States liquid beverages through PepsiCo Beverages North America simultaneously. Internationally, the Iberia, Beverages Franchise, Europe, Middle East and Africa, and Latin America Foods segments provide geographic diversification across more than 200 countries and territories. The business has sustained a gross profit margin between 53% and 55% through five years that included a commodity price spike in 2022 and significant tariff cost pressure in 2025 and 2026 — direct evidence that the brand premium is real and durable at the revenue-to-gross-profit line.

The Case for the Bet

PepsiCo is held as a durable consumer staples business whose brand portfolio and distribution network should sustain pricing power through commodity cycles. Five numbered conditions define whether that case holds:

  1. Gross margin stays above 52% for any two consecutive quarterly reporting periods. PepsiCo's competitive moat shows up most directly as the ability to raise prices when input costs rise without losing enough volume to compress the gross profit line. A gross margin falling and staying below 52% for two consecutive quarters — the ratio of gross profit to net revenue in the quarterly income statement — would signal that brand pricing power is no longer sufficient to absorb commodity cost pressure, meaning the moat has eroded structurally rather than facing a temporary headwind.

Latest reading: 54.7% for the first half of 2026 and 54.2% for the second quarter alone (as of 2026-06-13). Both readings are comfortably within the 53–55% corridor and well above the 52% floor. The IEEPA tariff claims submitted to US Customs following a Supreme Court ruling add a modest positive wrinkle: if refunds are received, the actual cost pressure is lower than the 6 percentage-point headwind reported.

  1. The annual dividend per share must exceed the prior year's figure in every annual announcement. PepsiCo has raised its dividend every year for 54 consecutive years without a single freeze or cut, through every economic cycle since 1972. Management views the dividend as a signal of its own confidence in the durability of cash generation. A freeze or cut — in any 10-K or dividend-announcement filing — ends that streak and signals that management itself no longer believes the earnings base is secure.

Latest reading: The annual dividend was raised 4% in February 2026 to $5.92 per share, effective with the payment made in June 2026. Per-share dividends paid in the first half of 2026 were $2.9025, up from $2.7775 in the same period of 2025. Exit condition (2) is definitively not triggered.

  1. PBNA segment organic volume turns and holds positive. PepsiCo Beverages North America is the segment most exposed to the structural headwinds facing the US beverage market — GLP-1 medication adoption, SNAP food-assistance restrictions, and changing consumer preferences. The thesis requires that volume eventually recovers; continued decline raises the question of whether the business can sustain revenue growth through price alone indefinitely.

Latest reading: Volume declined 3% in the first half of 2026 and 4% in the second quarter alone (as of 2026-06-13), with both carbonated and non-carbonated beverage categories negative. Nine or more consecutive quarterly reporting periods have produced a negative reading. This is the sole driver in a watching state — the compound exit condition (1) also requires operating margin below 11.5%, which is far from being approached.

  1. Operating margin (as reported under GAAP) stays above 11.5% for any two consecutive quarters. Exit condition (1) has two legs: the margin leg and the volume leg. The 11.5% margin floor is the level at which FY2025's cost pressure would have to be structural and permanent — not attributable to a one-off impairment charge or a single tariff episode — for the base case thesis to collapse into the bear case.

Latest reading: GAAP operating margin was 16.6% for both the second quarter and the first half of 2026 (as of 2026-06-13), well above the 11.5% floor. The prior-year comparison was distorted by the Rockstar Energy write-down of $1.86B; the clean comparison, on core operating profit, shows underlying improvement of 4–6% year on year.

  1. Net Debt relative to earnings before interest, tax, depreciation and amortisation must not exceed 3.0 times for two consecutive fiscal years without a credible deleveraging plan. PepsiCo borrowed substantially to fund the Siete Foods and poppi acquisitions in 2025, lifting the leverage ratio to 2.48 times at the end of FY2025 — the highest in the three-year monitoring window. At 3.0 times, the dividend programme would come under pressure from credit metrics, and financial flexibility would be meaningfully constrained.

Latest reading: Long-term debt was essentially flat at $42,612M as of 2026-06-13, against $42,321M at the end of FY2025. Commercial paper outstanding was $6.1B. The interim filing does not disclose a full earnings-before-interest-taxes-depreciation-and-amortisation figure, so the leverage ratio cannot be precisely recomputed, but the direction is flat to marginally improving as free cash flow recovered. Exit condition (3) applies to two consecutive fiscal years measured from the annual 10-K — it is not remotely in danger based on interim data.

The Share Price

At $139.10, PepsiCo trades at what the earnings-yield framework calls fair value. The metric is the ratio of the three-year average normalised profit ($8,964M) to the total market value of the shares — a yield of 4.72% against a fair-value definition of 4.70%. The precision here is intentional: essentially no gap exists between the current price and the level the Rules define as fair. The share sits in the middle of the hold band, above the buy edge at $101 and well below the trim edge at $205.

That positioning means the price is giving the Rules no signal in either direction. It is not cheap enough to warrant adding, not expensive enough to warrant trimming. It is priced to deliver a long-run total return — the yield plus the business's assumed base-case growth rate — of roughly 8% a year at today's level, which clears the equity hurdle but without a meaningful margin.

One note on context: the two fair-value anchors used in this analysis do not fully agree. The earnings-yield method, which anchors the buy and trim edges, puts fair value at roughly $140. A separate multi-method peer comparison analysis — which looks at what comparable businesses trade at and applies those multiples to PepsiCo's expected cash flows — places the base-case central estimate at $216. The gap is documented in Gaps in the Picture; it does not change the position label or the Standing Orders, both of which are anchored to the yield-method edge prices.

What's Wobbling

One driver is in a watching state, and it has been for an extended period. The PepsiCo Beverages North America segment has delivered negative unit volume for at least nine consecutive quarterly reporting periods. Both the carbonated soft drink and non-carbonated beverage categories declined in the second quarter of 2026, by 3% and 4% respectively. A 6 percentage-point commodity cost headwind in the second quarter added pressure on segment margins. There is no positive inflection visible in the data.

This wobble does not cross the exit threshold defined in the Case for the Bet. Exit condition (1) requires both the operating margin leg — GAAP margin below 11.5% for two consecutive quarters — and the volume leg to be satisfied simultaneously. The margin leg is nowhere near firing: GAAP operating margin was 16.6% in the second quarter. The volume leg alone does not trigger the exit. What it does is sustain the thesis in a watching state rather than a confirming one.

The parallel organic revenue picture is mixed rather than challenged. Consolidated organic revenue grew 2% in the second quarter and 2.5% in the first half, but this is below the 3.8% annual rate the base case requires. The underperformance traces to the PepsiCo Foods North America segment, where promotional activity produced negative net pricing in the second quarter. International segments — Iberia Beverages Franchise, Europe, Middle East and Africa, and Latin America Foods — all delivered positive organic performance, which provides some offset.

Nothing in the Q2 2026 filing crosses an exit line. But the persistence of the volume decline is the one thing watching this business requires continued patience on.

What Could Still Go Wrong

  • GLP-1 medication adoption could accelerate beyond current levels (18% of US adults as of 2026), reducing per-capita snack consumption structurally rather than cyclically. If PFNA volumes turn and stay negative while gross margin simultaneously falls below 53%, the brand moat is eroding faster than the base case allows.
  • Net debt at 2.48 times earnings before interest, taxes, depreciation, and amortisation — the highest in the three-year monitoring window — leaves less financial flexibility than the business has historically maintained. A recession or a significant earnings miss in FY2026 could push this ratio toward 2.7–3.0 times, where credit ratings and the dividend programme come under pressure.
  • The acquisitions of Siete Foods ($1.2B) and poppi ($1.95B) closed in 2025 without yet showing any contribution in EDGAR-filed financials. The contingent consideration for the poppi acquisition fell from $278M to $117M in the first half of 2026, suggesting that the $300M revenue and margin milestones tied to a mid-2027 target are now estimated as less probable — a minor precursor signal, not yet an exit condition.
  • The Mars-Kellanova combination, finalised in December 2025, created the first credible scaled competitor in salty snacks with Pringles, Cheez-It, and Pop-Tarts under one roof. Frito-Lay's 60% share in US salty snacks and its direct-outlet-delivery infrastructure preserve the lead, but the competitive landscape is more formidable than it was two years ago.
  • Two confirmed political headwinds — a 10% tariff on concentrate imported through the Irish subsidiary (costing roughly $189M a year) and ongoing SNAP food-assistance restrictions on sugary beverages in various US states — compress margins without threatening the structural moat. The tariff landscape shifted modestly in 2026 after a Supreme Court ruling that IEEPA-based tariffs were invalid; PepsiCo has submitted refund claims and expects to continue doing so. The exact benefit is uncertain.

Gaps in the Picture

The owner earnings figure used to compute the yield and set all edge prices is a manually maintained fixed number of $8,964M — the three-year average of normalised profits for FY2023–FY2025. This figure does not update automatically when the Value-Analysis is refreshed; it requires a human check at each refresh to confirm it still reflects the current best estimate. The EDGAR structured-data tag for capital expenditures does not report for PepsiCo, so the automated pipeline cannot compute free cash flow directly from filed data.

There is a structural divergence between the two fair-value estimates in use. The yield method, which anchors all edge prices, places fair value at approximately $140 per share — consistent with the current price. A separate peer-comparison analysis that looks at what similar businesses trade at by earnings, cash flow, and revenue multiples places the base-case central estimate at $216 per share. The two methods answer different questions: the yield method asks what price produces a given return on current earnings; the peer method asks what a rational buyer might pay for the whole business based on market comparables. Neither number is wrong, but they imply meaningfully different fair-value signals. Until the two anchors are reconciled into a single operative measure, the yield method governs the Standing Orders.

The Rules

The pre-committed action plan defines exactly what follows from each state of the evaluation. There are six possible states, each with a stated response:

  • If the thesis is confirmed — all drivers reading positively and no exit condition triggered — collect the dividend yield and re-evaluate at the next quarterly filing, expected around October 2026. The two near-term signals that would confirm the thesis: PBNA volume turning positive and operating margin recovering toward 13.0%.
  • If the thesis is weakened — as it currently is, with PBNA volume the sole challenged driver — do not add to the position. Wait for the next two quarterly filings before drawing a conclusion. The 90-day cadence is intentional given three simultaneous uncertainties: tariff trajectory, PBNA volume recovery, and the integration of the 2025 acquisitions.
  • If the thesis is broken — any of exit conditions (1), (2), (3), (4), or (5) triggered as described in the Case for the Bet — exit the full position within 30 days. No averaging down. Record the triggered condition and the exit price.
  • If the share price falls below the buy edge — confirm the thesis is intact and operating margin is recovering, then add to the position in tranches over one to two quarters, targeting a full position if the earnings yield exceeds 6.5%, approximately $101 per share at the normalised earnings figure.
  • If the share price rises above the trim edge — trim 25–33% of the position to harvest the premium. At 3.2% yield (approximately $205 per share), the market is pricing in long-run growth well beyond the base case.
  • If the share price rises above the exit edge — exit the full position. At approximately $469 per share, the market is pricing in roughly 33 years of base-case compounding immediately; the implied forward return falls below the long-run equity premium, and redeployment into a higher-conviction opportunity is the stated response. No re-entry within 90 days to defuse any anchor bias from the prior holding.

Scenario Definitions

A scenario is a joint assumption about two independent variables: operating trajectory (how the holding's underlying cash flows grow relative to their long-run trend) and exit multiple (what the market pays for those cash flows at the horizon, relative to a fair-value anchor).

Scenario Operating trajectory Exit multiple
Bear Below trend Below fair value
Base At trend At fair value
Bull Above trend At or above fair value

Non-negotiable rule: In the bear case, both dimensions must be adverse simultaneously — a scenario where only the multiple moves is a re-rating scenario, not a bear.

Bear cases are comparable in structure across all holding types (company, ETF, bond), but not in magnitude. A company bear reflects idiosyncratic execution failure; an ETF bear reflects a broad market drawdown. Different mitigations, different diversification implications.
