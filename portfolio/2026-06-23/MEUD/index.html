positive but thin — the basket's earnings yield barely clears the risk-free

wobble since the fund was first added to the portfolio.

About the Fund

Europe 600 is a share in the Amundi Core STOXX Europe 600 UCITS fund, a
Luxembourg-domiciled accumulating fund managed by Amundi Luxembourg S.A.
(a Crédit Agricole subsidiary). The fund holds approximately 600 European
developed-market companies in free-float proportion to their market
capitalisation, reviewed quarterly by STOXX Ltd (Deutsche Börse Group). All

The Case for the Bet

The bet is structural diversification: European developed-market equities offer
a different risk factor from the other funds in the portfolio, which are
approximately 70–100% US-weighted. European equities have historically traded at
lower valuations than US equities, producing a structurally higher basket
earnings yield (the share of the share price represented by the basket's total
earnings), and that yield provides the equity risk premium the bet depends on.
For the bet to keep working, six conditions must stay intact:

  1. Equity risk premium above zero for two consecutive quarters. The basket's
    earnings yield must exceed the yield on 10-year US Treasury bonds by enough to
    justify holding equities over risk-free assets. If the premium turns negative
    for two quarters in a row, the risk-premium case has failed. (Early-warning
    flag below the 2% confirmation threshold, currently 0.95%.)

  2. Annual cost below 0.15%. The fund's annual expense ratio is currently
    0.07%. A rise above 0.12% (early-warning flag) signals cost drift; above 0.15%
    signals a business model change and triggers exit condition (2).

  3. Fund size above €1 billion. The fund complex manages approximately
    €13–15 billion per Amundi's product page — around 13–15 times the floor.
    European funds below this size face elevated closure risk; a sustained decline
    to the floor would be a vehicle-survival signal rather than a market signal.
    (Early-warning flag below €2 billion.)

  4. Top holding below 8% of the basket. STOXX Europe 600 is diversified by
    design; top names have historically sat between 2% and 4%. A rise above 5%
    (early-warning flag) signals concentration drift; above 8% signals a structural
    concentration event or methodology change triggering exit condition (4).

  5. STOXX Europe 600 index methodology unchanged. STOXX Ltd must not
    materially restructure the index — no shift from free-float cap-weighting to
    factor weights, no universe change, no merger with another index family, no
    Amundi mandate transfer to a different index provider. This condition cannot be
    automatically monitored; it requires external awareness of STOXX Ltd
    announcements.

  6. Median return on equity of the top-10 constituents above 15%. The visible
    quality tier of the basket — the ten largest names — must collectively compound
    capital at a meaningful rate. A median below 12% would signal quality
    deterioration across the basket's most visible names.

The Rules

There are five pre-committed responses to different combinations of conditions
and share price:

  1. If all conditions are intact and the share price is in the hold band: hold.
    Maintain the allocation as a counterweight to the portfolio's US tilt.

  2. If any condition is weakened but not broken: do not add. Re-validate after
    the next quarter. If the equity risk premium stays below 0.5 percentage points
    for two quarters with no recovery signal, consider reducing the allocation
    toward URTH or risk-free assets.

  3. If any exit condition fires or a vehicle-integrity breach occurs: exit. Move
    the proceeds to a global cap-weight fund or risk-free assets.

  4. If the share price falls to or below €245 (basket earnings yield above 7%):
    add decisively. European equities at that yield are priced at historically
    undervalued levels — near the trough valuations reached during the financial
    crisis of 2008–2009.

  5. If the share price rises to or above €381 (basket earnings yield below
    4.5%): trim 25–33%. European equities at that yield are priced at stretched
    levels — valuations rarely sustained in markets with normal interest rates.

The Conditions Today

Condition 1 — equity risk premium. The basket's earnings yield is 5.46%
against a 10-year US Treasury yield of 4.509%, giving a risk premium of 0.95
percentage points. That is positive — exit condition (1) requires negative risk
premium for two consecutive quarters, which has not happened — but it falls below
the 2.0 percentage point threshold the rules treat as confirmation. Three
consecutive readings have been positive, ranging from 0.95% to 1.04%. Worth
noting: against the German government bond yield (approximately 2.6%), the
implied risk premium rises to about 2.9 percentage points, which would pass the
confirmation test. The machine uses the US Treasury yield as a conservative
default.

Condition 2 — annual cost. The fund's expense ratio is 0.07%, unchanged.
That is 5 basis points below the early-warning level of 0.12% and 8 basis
points below the exit ceiling of 0.15%. No cost drift.

Condition 5 — index methodology. No automated signal is available for this

Condition 6 — look-through quality. The median return on equity of the

The Share Price

The basket's earnings yield — the total earnings of all underlying companies
expressed as a percentage of the basket's share price — is the core valuation
metric for this fund. A higher yield means a cheaper basket; a lower yield means
a more expensive one. The fair-value anchor is 5.50%, corresponding to a share
price of approximately €312.

As of 2026-06-23, the basket earnings yield is 5.46% and the share price is
€314.00 — fractionally above the €312 fair-value price, meaning the basket is
trading at almost exactly fair value. At this reading the basket is not cheap,
but neither is it in territory that would trigger a trim. To reach the buy edge
(yield 7.0%), the share price would need to fall to €245 — a drop of roughly
22% from today. To reach the trim edge (yield 4.5%), the share price would need
to rise to €381 — a gain of about 21%.

The fund's share price also trades within 0.73% of its net asset value, well
within the 3.0% tolerance the rules set for a full-replication UCITS fund — no
premium or discount worth noting.

The Standing Orders

The rules translate the share-price bands into two standing orders and two price
alerts. Each is the operationalisation of a pre-committed rule — not a new
judgement made here.

Price alerts (informational).
- A first alert at €312, the fair-value price. A crossing means the basket
has moved from above to below fair value (or vice versa); no action follows,
but a fresh read of the six conditions is worthwhile before doing anything.
- A second alert at €245, the cheap-edge price. When the basket touches this
level, the basket's earnings yield will have reached 7% — the threshold at
which the rules call for adding. Before acting, confirm the conditions are
still intact.

Standing orders (actionable).
- A standing buy order at €245. The rules call for adding in tranches when the
share price closes at or below this level and all exit conditions remain
inactive. The basket at that price would be offering a yield historically
associated with trough European valuations. The order may be staged across one
or two quarters rather than filled in a single trade.
- A standing trim order at €381. The rules call for trimming 25–33% of the
position when the share price closes at or above this level, to harvest the
premium while retaining the core holding.

No exit-price order is set for this fund. Vehicle-integrity breaches — a fund
closure, a sponsor failure, a structural index change, or a sustained
assets-under-management decline below the floor — govern the sell side of this holding, not the
share price alone.

What's Wobbling

The equity risk premium is the single non-confirming condition. At 0.95
percentage points above the US Treasury yield, it sits below the 2.0 percentage
point level the rules treat as confirmation. The spread is positive — exit
condition (1) requires negative spread for two consecutive quarters, which has
not occurred — but it has been persistently thin across all three readings to
date.

The plausible explanation is the benchmark: the US 10-year Treasury yield (4.51%)
is materially higher than the German government bond equivalent (approximately
2.6%). Against the German bond, the implied premium is 2.9 percentage points,
which would confirm the thesis. The machine uses the US benchmark by default.

Any further compression in the basket's earnings yield — or any further rise in
the US Treasury rate — would narrow the gap to the 0.0% line where the
assessment would shift from mixed to challenged. The conditions today
show one driver in a persistently mixed state; that is the sole open item.

What Could Break the Fund

  • Deep European recession with multiple compression. The bear-case scenario
    modelled in the return analysis assumes the basket's price-to-earnings ratio
    contracts from the current 18.3 times to 13 times over five years — roughly
    the trough seen during the financial crisis of 2008–2009. Under that scenario
    the fund loses approximately 4.6% a year even if the underlying companies grow
    earnings at 2% a year. That is the cost of entering at fair value in a basket
    that is not cushioned by a deep yield discount.

  • Structural European earnings stagnation. If European large-cap companies
    lose their ability to grow earnings — demographic decline, energy-cost
    disadvantage, regulatory burden — the base case of 5% annual earnings growth
    disappears. Without growth, the base return collapses toward the bare earnings
    yield of 5.46% before accounting for any re-rating.

  • Fund closure or index change. A sustained decline in fund assets below
    €1 billion, or STOXX Ltd restructuring the Europe 600 index (e.g. shifting
    from free-float cap-weighting, merging with another family), would trigger the
    vehicle-integrity exit. Neither is signalled at present, but neither is
    automatically monitored.
