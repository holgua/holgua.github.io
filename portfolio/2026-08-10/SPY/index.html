About SP500

The SPDR S&P 500 exchange-traded fund tracks the S&P 500 — the index of the 500 largest publicly traded US companies by market value, maintained by S&P Dow Jones Indices. Owning a single share of this fund gives exposure to approximately 500 companies across every major sector of the US economy, from semiconductor designers and software platforms to banks, healthcare companies, and consumer brands. The basket is weighted by market capitalisation: the largest companies (currently NVIDIA, Apple, Alphabet, Microsoft, and Amazon) carry the biggest weights; smaller members of the index carry tiny slivers. That weighting tilts the fund toward whichever businesses the market values most highly at any given time.

The fund has been in continuous operation since January 1993, structured as a Unit Investment Trust — the oldest US exchange-traded fund wrapper. Unlike a conventional fund, the UIT structure means the fund cannot reinvest dividends between distribution dates; instead it holds a small cash float (approximately 0.25% of assets) until distributions are paid. The annual running cost is 0.09%, or nine hundredths of a percentage point per year — among the lowest available for any US large-cap equity fund. The fund pays no K-1 tax form; dividend distributions are expected to be mostly qualified (eligible for lower tax rates), though the exact percentage is not confirmed in the available data. Annual cost: 0.09% total expense ratio.

The Case for the Bet

The fund is held for as long as the basket clears a simple value test: the basket's owner-earnings yield (the share of every dollar invested that the underlying companies collectively earn for their shareholders) must exceed the risk-free 10-year US Treasury yield by at least two percentage points. Six numbered conditions govern whether that bet remains valid:

  1. The equity risk premium must stay positive. The basket earnings yield must exceed the 10-year US Treasury yield (currently 4.60%). If it falls below for two consecutive quarter-end readings, the risk-premium case has failed and the position is exited regardless of the absolute earnings yield level.

Latest reading: 2026-07-30. Basket earnings yield 3.75% versus Treasury 4.60% — the equity risk premium is −0.85 percentage points, negative for the fourteenth consecutive snapshot. The two-consecutive-quarter calendar gate is pending the end-of-September 2026 quarter-end reading; the exit condition has not yet fired, but the trajectory is clear.

  1. The annual expense ratio must stay below 0.15%. State Street (the fund's sponsor) must not raise the fee above this ceiling without a competitive response from other providers.

Latest reading: 2026-07-30. The expense ratio is 0.0945%, giving 5.55 hundredths of a percentage point of headroom before the ceiling is breached. Exit condition (2) is not triggered.

  1. Assets under management must remain above $100 billion. A fund this large would only fall below that floor in an extreme, extended redemption event — at which point liquidity would be severely impaired.

Latest reading: 2026-07-30. Assets under management stand at $781.2 billion, approximately 7.8 times the floor. Average daily trading volume is $39 billion. Exit condition (3) is not triggered.

  1. The single largest holding must not exceed 13% of the basket. Above that level the fund ceases to be meaningfully diversified and begins to behave like a concentrated single-name bet.

Latest reading: 2026-07-30. NVIDIA holds the top position at 7.50% of the basket — 5.5 percentage points below the ceiling. Exit condition (4) is not triggered.

  1. The S&P 500 index methodology must remain intact. S&P Dow Jones Indices must not materially restructure the definition of the index in a way that undermines its broad-market mandate.

Latest reading: 2026-07-30. No automated signal for methodology drift is available in this pipeline; monitoring is done externally. No change has been announced or reported.

  1. The median return on equity of the top ten holdings must stay above 15%. This measures whether the underlying basket has retained its quality as a collection of genuinely capital-efficient businesses.

What Could Still Go Wrong

The bear case for this fund is a broad market drawdown, not a company-specific failure. Five scenarios could force the hand of the exit rules:

  • Cost increase. State Street raises the annual expense ratio above 0.15% without a competitive fee response. At the current 0.0945% the headroom is ample, but a regulatory change or a business-model shift at the sponsor could compress it.
  • Liquidity event. Assets under management fall below $100 billion — a scenario that would require sustained large-scale redemptions of a scale not seen in this fund's history. At $781 billion the floor is 7.8 times away.
  • Index redefinition. S&P Dow Jones Indices materially restructures the composition rules of the S&P 500 in a way that makes it no longer a genuine broad-market proxy. No signal of this has appeared.
  • Concentration drift. A single holding grows past the 13% ceiling. NVIDIA is the candidate most likely to approach this level; at 7.50% it remains 5.5 percentage points below.
  • Bear case return. The five-year bear scenario — earnings contracting by 12.5% and the market repricing the basket at 15 times earnings rather than today's 26.7 times — implies an annualised return of −22.4% over five years. This scenario requires both dimensions to turn adverse simultaneously: earnings recession and a re-rating of the multiple. (For this fund, exit fires when the basket earnings yield falls to or below 3.0%, when a vehicle-integrity condition breaches, or when the named exit-condition trigger fires — any of three paths.)

The Rules

If the basket remains reasonably priced and the vehicle is sound, hold — the position is working as intended and no action is called for.

If any thesis driver weakens — particularly if the equity risk premium stays negative through consecutive quarter-end readings — do not add to the position. Re-validate after the next quarter. If the negative risk premium persists or if the median look-through return on equity keeps falling, the rules call for switching to a comparable fund or moving to cash in line with the position-sizing rule.

If a vehicle-integrity condition fires, or if the exit trigger is confirmed — meaning the equity risk premium has been negative for two consecutive quarter-end readings — exit the full position and move the proceeds to risk-free instruments or to a holding that meets the value test.

If the basket earnings yield rises above 6.25% (meaning the basket has become meaningfully cheap relative to risk-free rates), add to the position in tranches.

If the basket earnings yield falls below 4.0% (meaning the basket is expensively priced relative to risk-free rates), trim 25–33% of the position to harvest the premium — this rule is currently active.

If the basket earnings yield falls to or below 3.0% (meaning the basket is fully priced against risk-free rates, with the equity risk premium deeply negative), exit the full position and redeploy the proceeds to risk-free instruments or to a holding that meets the value test.

Scenario Definitions

A scenario is a joint assumption about two independent variables: operating trajectory (how the holding's underlying cash flows grow relative to their long-run trend) and exit multiple (what the market pays for those cash flows at the horizon, relative to a fair-value anchor).

Scenario Operating trajectory Exit multiple
Bear Below trend Below fair value
Base At trend At fair value
Bull Above trend At or above fair value

Non-negotiable rule: In the bear case, both dimensions must be adverse simultaneously — a scenario where only the multiple moves is a re-rating scenario, not a bear.

Bear cases are comparable in structure across all holding types (company, ETF, bond), but not in magnitude. A company bear reflects idiosyncratic execution failure; an ETF bear reflects a broad market drawdown. Different mitigations, different diversification implications.
