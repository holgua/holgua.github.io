The Bottom Line

Data as of 2026-07-07 · Latest filing 2025-12-31.

Recent Development

The most significant fact from the latest annual filing (for the year ending 2025-12-31) is what did not happen: operating cash flow held above €5 billion for the fifth consecutive year despite revenue having fallen 61% from the 2022 commodity-crisis peak. Cash from operations came in at €5,215 million in FY2025, down a modest 4.4% from the €5,456 million posted the year before — well above the €4.5 billion threshold that defines the bull-signal for this driver, and nowhere near the €3.5 billion level at which the bear-scenario alarm rings.

The filing also confirmed that none of the six written exit conditions has been crossed. The capital-spending burden (measured as the ratio of capital expenditure to operating cash flow) came in at 73.8% — elevated and rising from a low of 35.6% in FY2021, but still below the 90% level that would begin the two-year clock on exit condition (1). The effective tax rate was 54.7%, up three percentage points from the prior year and approaching the 55% threshold, but the exit condition for the tax rate only begins counting in years after FY2026. The chemicals investment book (the Borealis equity stake, now in transition to Borouge Group International) stood at €5,255 million — above the €4.0 billion exit floor by a 24% margin.

The result is a thesis that remains working but is generating monitoring signals on three fronts simultaneously, which is a different character than a clean reading. The wobbles are real; they simply haven't reached the written exit lines yet.

About OMV

OMV is Austria's largest oil, gas, and chemicals company. It operates across three distinct activities. The first is exploration and production — finding and extracting oil and gas, primarily in Central and Eastern Europe, Norway, and the United Arab Emirates. The second is refining and petrochemicals — processing crude oil and gas into fuels and chemical feedstocks at three European refineries. The third is a large equity stake, now approximately 47%, in Borouge Group International, the world's fourth-largest producer of polyolefins (the plastics used in packaging, pipes, and automotive parts), formed in March 2026 by combining the former Borealis business with Borouge and NOVA Chemicals of Canada.

The company's revenues fluctuate with global commodity prices — when oil rises, revenue and profits rise; when oil falls, they fall — and OMV is, in the language of markets, a price-taker. It cannot choose what it charges for crude or natural gas. What it can do is control costs and how it allocates capital. The Austrian government, through its investment holding company ÖBAG, owns 31.5% of OMV's shares and sits on the supervisory board; the Abu Dhabi national oil group XRG (formerly ADNOC) owns a further 24.9%. That 56.4% combined state-actor presence shapes the company's strategic priorities, its energy-security role in Austria and Central Europe, and, to some degree, its capital allocation decisions.

The Case for the Bet

The bet is that OMV's integrated model — where oil and gas revenues fund a rising chemicals investment — will generate enough cash to sustain a meaningful dividend yield while the chemicals pivot matures. Six conditions must keep holding for that bet to remain intact.

1. Operating cash flow (OCF) stays above €3.5 billion at Brent oil above $60 a barrel. This is the baseline test that the cash engine is functioning. A reading below €3.5 billion would signal that the integrated model is not buffering the cycle as expected.

Latest reading: €5,215 million in FY2025 — the fifth consecutive year above €5 billion. The cash engine is currently passing this test comfortably.

2. The capital-spending burden (capital expenditure divided by operating cash flow) stays below 90% for two consecutive years. When spending consumes 90 cents or more of every euro the business generates from operations, free cash flow effectively disappears and the dividend becomes unfunded by operations. The exit trigger requires two consecutive years above that level.

Latest reading: 73.8% in FY2025, up from roughly 35% in FY2021 and above the 80% early-warning level. One year above 80% is a monitoring signal, not an exit trigger; two consecutive years above 90% would be. The trajectory is the concern — CapEx has risen from €2.5 billion in FY2021 to €3.8 billion in FY2025, and the thesis assumes this peaks in FY2025. The FY2026 annual report will be the first test of that assumption.

3. The Borouge Group International equity investment holds above €4.0 billion for two consecutive years. This book value is the proxy for whether the chemicals pivot is creating or destroying value. A sustained fall below €4.0 billion would mean the market and the auditors jointly assess the chemicals assets as worth less than that threshold over two filing cycles.

Latest reading: €5,255 million in FY2025 — a 24% buffer above the exit floor. The book value has declined roughly €2 billion from its FY2022 peak, which warrants monitoring; the exit condition requires the value to fall below €4.0 billion for two consecutive years, which has not come close to occurring.

4. The effective tax rate (the share of pre-tax profits paid to governments) stays below 55% in any year after FY2026. The Austrian and EU windfall-tax arrangements that produced a 58.4% effective tax rate in FY2023 and 54.7% in FY2025 are the primary risk here. If the rate exceeds 55% in FY2027 or later, exit condition (3) begins counting; a second year triggers the exit.

Latest reading: 54.7% in FY2025, up from 51.7% in FY2024. The exit condition does not begin counting until FY2026 data is available; FY2025 is a pre-count year. The upward trend is concerning but not yet a trigger.

5. Interest coverage (operating profit divided by interest expense) stays above 4.0 times for two consecutive years. Below 4.0 times, the debt-service burden begins to crowd out the investment programme and threaten financial flexibility.

Latest reading: 8.02 times in FY2025, down from 10.77 times in FY2024. Coverage remains more than double the exit floor; the accruals ratio (a measure of earnings quality) confirms that the operating profit figure is backed by real cash, not accounting adjustments.

6. Fuel sales volume in Austria and Romania does not decline more than 10% over any two-year period. This tracks whether PKN Orlen, the Polish state energy group expanding aggressively into Central European fuel retail, is meaningfully eating into OMV's dominant position in the region.

Latest reading: Not available from the structured digital filing — retail volume data appears only in the narrative portion of the annual report in PDF form, which is not accessible from the machine-readable version. The last available figure is 16.2 million tonnes across eight European countries in FY2024. A manual review of the OMV FY2025 Annual Report PDF is required to update this driver.

The Share Price

At €57, the share price sits below the threshold at which the valuation rules call for adding to a position. That threshold — the buy edge — is €71, which corresponds to an owner earnings yield (operating profit after maintenance spending, divided by market value) of 7.5%. At €57, the current yield is approximately 9.4% — about 186 basis points above the buy edge, meaning the market is pricing in considerably more pessimism than the valuation rules require. The fair-value anchor — where the yield would match the DCF intrinsic estimate — is €95, implying the share currently trades at roughly a 40% discount to fair value. The trim edge, where the yield falls to a level that suggests the market is pricing in excessive optimism, sits at €119.

The disconnect between the current price and the fair-value anchor is unusual in its magnitude. Part of that gap reflects the market's legitimate concern about the bear scenario: if oil falls to $55 a barrel, chemicals margins stay compressed, and the capital-spending programme consumes all operating cash flow, the valuation picture deteriorates sharply. The market appears to be assigning considerable weight to that outcome.

What's Wobbling

Three of the seven thesis drivers are sending monitoring signals rather than clear readings.

Capital spending is rising, not falling. The ratio of capital expenditure to operating cash flow has climbed steadily from roughly 35% in FY2021 to 73.8% in FY2025. The thesis assumes this peaks in FY2025 as the major Borouge Group International transaction completes and the reinvestment programme stabilises. That assumption will be tested when the FY2026 annual report is published. If the ratio approaches or exceeds 80% for a second consecutive year, the action plan calls for no further additions and an annual (rather than semi-annual) review cadence.

The effective tax rate is approaching the exit ceiling. The rate rose from 51.7% in FY2024 to 54.7% in FY2025, closing the gap with the 55% level that would, in any year after FY2026, begin a two-year exit countdown. A proposed EU windfall tax that could repeat the FY2023 solidarity levy represents the most immediate risk here. The rate does not need to cross 55% to be a problem — sustained readings above 53% are the early-warning level in the monitoring plan.

The chemicals investment book is declining. The equity-method book value of the Borealis (now Borouge Group International) stake has fallen roughly €2 billion from its FY2022 peak to €5,255 million in FY2025. The exit floor is €4.0 billion, and there is currently a 24% buffer. The concern is directional — the trend is down, the transition to BGI introduces re-labelling complexity in the FY2026 filing, and the exit threshold may need recalibrating upward to reflect the larger combined entity that now includes NOVA Chemicals.

Two further drivers are effectively blank: retail fuel volume in Austria and Romania (cannot be extracted from the structured filing) and ÖBAG governance quality (also narrative-only, not in machine-readable form). The absence of adverse evidence on those two fronts is not confirmation that they are fine — it is simply a gap.

What Could Still Go Wrong

  • Oil falls hard and stays low. At $55 a barrel, the modelling suggests cash from operations would fall materially and free cash flow could turn negative, putting the dividend — which was consuming 86% of reported earnings in FY2025 — under acute pressure. A dividend cut would remove the primary current reason to hold at today's price.
  • The EU windfall tax recurs. The FY2023 solidarity levy cost €552 million and pushed the effective tax rate to 58.4%. If a 2026 EU windfall tax proposal is enacted, the FY2026 rate could cross 55% and start the exit clock.
  • Capital spending does not peak. If the Borouge Group International integration requires more investment than modelled, or if the three European refineries need transition-related spending, the CapEx-to-OCF ratio could approach or exceed 90% — the level at which, for two consecutive years, the exit trigger fires.
  • Borealis/BGI destroys value. The chemicals pivot is the primary long-run value-creation thesis. If polyolefin margins remain compressed for years and the book value of the BGI stake falls through the €4.0 billion floor, the chemicals bet has not delivered what the thesis required.
  • PKN Orlen erodes the CEE retail moat. The Polish state energy group is expanding aggressively into Central European fuel retail. A 10% cumulative decline in OMV's Austrian and Romanian fuel volumes over two years would signal structural moat erosion and trigger exit condition (6) — though this cannot currently be verified from the structured filing.

Gaps in the Picture

Two drivers — retail fuel volume in Austria and Romania, and governance quality of the ÖBAG-connected supervisory board — cannot be assessed from the machine-readable annual filing. The PDF narrative sections where this information appears are not accessible via structured data extraction; a manual review of the OMV FY2025 Annual Report PDF from the company's investor-relations pages is required.

The earnings figure used in the valuation ($1.74 billion — net income treated as owner earnings, since depreciation and maintenance capital spending are assumed to offset) is drawn from FY2025 results and uses depreciation as a proxy for maintenance capital spending. This is a simplifying assumption: for a business spending €3.8 billion per year in total capital expenditure, the maintenance versus growth split is material. The figure should be refreshed each time a new annual report is published.

The risk-free rate used in the hurdle-rate calculation is sourced from a snapshot of a related holding rather than from a dedicated OMV market-data field — the underlying data infrastructure does not yet carry a per-holding rate. The effective date of that snapshot is 2026-07-07.

The Rules

The action plan for this holding has five branches. Each maps to a specific state of the evaluation; the rules are intended to prevent decisions from being made on the fly when conditions shift.

  • If the evidence confirms the thesis is intact: hold the current position. Collect the dividend. Do not add at the current price of €57 — the probability-weighted three-year expected return is negative from this level under the scenario model, and the thesis requires a lower entry point for new capital. The dividend yield (approximately 5.5%) provides real income return in the meantime.
  • If the thesis begins to weaken: do not add. Reduce the review frequency to annual. Specific signals that would trigger this branch: the capital-spending ratio rising above 80% for one year; the BGI book value declining toward €4.5 billion; the effective tax rate sustained above 53%; Brent oil below $60 a barrel for two consecutive quarters; or PKN Orlen capturing a visible share of OMV's Central European retail volume.
  • If any written exit condition fires: exit the full position. The six exit conditions are enumerated in the conditions section above. On trigger, proceed to sell; move proceeds to a European diversified-energy fund or EUR cash depending on portfolio concentration at the time of exit.
  • If the share price falls to or below €71 (the buy edge): add to the position. Scale in proportionally over two tranches, confirming between tranches that operating cash flow is not deteriorating. Maximum addition: 50% of the existing allocation per tranche.
  • If the share price rises to or above €119 (the trim edge): trim 25–33% of the position. A price at or above €119 would imply the market is pricing in structural earnings recovery beyond the base case; the rules call for harvesting partial gains at that level.
