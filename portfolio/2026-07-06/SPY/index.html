About the Fund

SP500 is the SPDR S&P 500 ETF Trust, the first US-listed exchange-traded fund, launched in January 1993 and operated continuously since then by State Street Investment Management. It tracks the S&P 500 index — a cap-weighted basket of the five hundred largest publicly traded companies in the United States, maintained by S&P Dow Jones Indices. When the share prices of the largest companies rise, their weight in the basket rises too; the basket therefore tilts naturally toward whatever has done well recently.

The fund's annual charge is 0.0945% — roughly 9½ pence on every £100 of investment per year, or just under one dollar per year on every thousand invested. The fund operates as a Unit Investment Trust, the oldest US exchange-traded fund structure, which means it cannot reinvest dividends between distribution dates; a small cash sleeve (0.11% of assets) accumulates between payouts as a result. No K-1 tax form is issued. Tracking difference and tracking error figures are not available from the fund's data feed; they should be verified from the State Street SPDR factsheet directly.

  1. Equity risk premium turns and stays negative. The basket's earnings yield falls below the ten-year US Treasury yield for two consecutive quarterly snapshots. The premium must be absent, not just thin.

  1. Assets under management fall below $100 billion. A sustained drawdown of that magnitude would signal extraordinary liquidity stress.

Latest reading: Assets under management are $783.8 billion — 7.8 times the floor. Exit condition inactive.

  1. Top single-name weight exceeds 13%. Single-company concentration at that level signals a regime change, not normal market drift.

Latest reading: NVIDIA holds 7.89% of the basket, inside both the 10% early-warning band and the 13% hard ceiling. Exit condition inactive.

  1. S&P 500 index methodology is materially restructured. S&P Dow Jones Indices changes the broad-market mandate in a way that undermines what the basket represents.

Latest reading: No methodology change has been announced. Exit condition inactive.

  1. Median return on equity of the top ten holdings collapses below 15%. The basket has lost its quality anchor.

What's Wobbling

The equity risk premium is the one active wobble. The basket's earnings yield has been below the ten-year Treasury yield continuously since 2026-06-16. The spread has ranged from −0.594% to −0.758%, with the 2026-07-06 reading at −0.736%. This is the "Buffett cash" condition: riskless government bonds currently pay more than the underlying businesses earn per dollar of market price.

The written exit condition for this holding requires two consecutive quarterly readings of negative risk premium, not merely sustained weekly observations. The seven snapshots on record all fall within a single quarter. If the next quarterly re-validation, expected in the three months ending September 2026, also shows a negative premium, the exit condition fires and the thesis transitions from wobbling to broken.

The wobble does not overturn the trim call — it reinforces it. The valuation-band rule (earnings yield below the 4% trim threshold) is already active and takes priority over the drivers-only assessment. What the negative premium adds is urgency: the basket is not just expensive by historical earnings standards; it is priced below what a riskless bond delivers. Both signals point in the same direction.

What Could Still Go Wrong

  • The basket's price could rise further before any correction, driven by continued multiple expansion in the technology sector, which currently accounts for 39% of the basket. A buyer at $745 who trims late would harvest less of the premium.
  • A sharp fall in the ten-year Treasury yield could restore a positive earnings premium without any change in the share price, turning the wobble into a confirmation. The trigger for this would be a Federal Reserve pivot toward easing.
  • The bear case — a return of the basket's earnings multiple from today's 26.7 times earnings to the long-run average of 16 times — delivers an annualised loss of roughly 6.9% over five years, without any deterioration in the underlying businesses.
  • NVIDIA's weight at 7.89% is still inside the 10% early-warning band, but continued share price outperformance could push it toward that band within one or two quarters. At 10% concentration, an early-warning flag fires; at 13%, the exit condition triggers.
  • The quarterly earnings-premium snapshot due in the three months ending September 2026 is the single most important near-term data point. If negative, the thesis moves to broken. (For this fund, exit fires when the share price reaches the exit edge, when a vehicle-integrity condition breaches, or when the named exit-condition trigger fires — any of three paths.)
