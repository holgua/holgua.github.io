Amazon: The Bet Still Works

The Bottom Line

Data as of 2026-07-06 · Latest filing 2026-04-30.

The bet is working. The three engines at the heart of the investment case — Amazon Web Services, the company's cloud computing business, advertising, and the international retail business — all reported ahead of their respective watch-lines in the quarter ending March 2026, and the conditions are working. The share price sits in the upper half of the hold band, well above fair value. Two items merit watching: a capital-spending programme running at roughly $147 billion in the trailing twelve months, whose conversion to revenue still has to be proved across the FY2027 and FY2028 filing years, and an unresolved FTC antitrust case — though the enforcement posture has softened considerably since early 2025.

Recent Development

The three months ending in March 2026 were the best quarter Amazon Web Services has posted in two years. Revenue grew 28% compared with the same period a year earlier, and the contracted order backlog reached $364 billion — a figure that represents roughly five and a half years of committed future spend from enterprise customers. That backlog, combined with the announcement that OpenAI expanded its existing deal to a $100 billion, eight-year compute commitment on Amazon's cloud infrastructure, is the most direct piece of evidence that the capital-spending cycle is converting to demand rather than stranding assets.

Advertising held up equally well. The segment that runs sponsored listings, video ads, and marketplace placements reported $17.2 billion in the quarter, up nearly 24% year-on-year, and was cited as the primary driver of profit growth in both the North America and International retail businesses. International itself swung to a 3.6% operating margin — a continued step-up from 3.0% a year earlier — confirming that the profitability improvement outside the United States is not a one-quarter artefact.

The headline qualifier is the capital-spending trajectory. Purchases of physical infrastructure reached $43.2 billion in the single quarter ending March 2026 — more than double the $24.3 billion in the same quarter a year earlier. That rate, if sustained, points to an annualised investment level well above $150 billion. The thesis does not require that spending to stop; it requires spending to keep converting into AWS revenue at the rate seen so far. The current data supports that conversion. Whether it continues into 2027 and 2028 is the question the share price has already priced a strong answer to.

About Amazon

Amazon makes its money in three distinct pools that share infrastructure but operate on different economics. The first and largest by revenue is the North America retail and logistics business — the familiar online shop, the marketplace where third-party sellers list products, and the Prime subscription that bundles free shipping with video streaming and music. This operation generated $426 billion in full-year 2025 revenue at an operating profit margin of about 7 cents on every dollar of sales. It is large and growing, but it is primarily the cash-generative engine that subsidises the other two pools.

The second pool is Amazon Web Services, the company's cloud computing business. Businesses and governments rent computing capacity, storage, databases, and increasingly AI model infrastructure from Amazon's global network of data centres. This segment brought in $129 billion in 2025 revenue and earned a profit margin of 35 cents on every dollar — the most profitable large cloud business in the industry, contributing 57% of Amazon's total operating profit on only 18% of its revenue. It is the economic heart of the company.

The third pool is advertising. When someone searches Amazon for headphones or watches a show on Prime Video, Amazon sells the surrounding space — sponsored listings, display ads, video spots — to brands who want to reach a buyer at the moment of intent. That business generated $69 billion in 2025 and grew at 22% year-on-year. It runs on top of the retail platform at near-zero incremental cost, making it the highest-margin layer of all three.

The Case for the Bet

The central argument for owning Amazon is that the retail and logistics business funds two compounding profit pools whose unit economics are still well below maturity. As long as the conditions below keep holding, the compounding continues.

1. Amazon Web Services grows at 18% or faster year-on-year; the confirmed exit line is two consecutive quarters below 15%.

The 15% line was chosen because it would signal that the AI-workload wave failed to absorb the 2025–2026 capital spending — that the data centres being built are not filling up with paying customers. At 28% growth in the quarter ending March 2026, the margin to the exit line is substantial.

Latest reading: Amazon Web Services reported $37.6 billion in revenue for the three months ending in March 2026, 28% above the same period a year earlier. The $364 billion contracted backlog (weighted-average remaining life of 5.5 years) and the OpenAI $100 billion expansion deal provide forward demand visibility that is unusual in any industry.

2. Amazon Web Services operating profit margin (the share of each dollar of cloud revenue that becomes operating profit) stays above 28% for two consecutive quarters; sustained compression below that line triggers exit.

The 28% line is deliberately placed 970 basis points below the current reading to distinguish a cyclical investment dip from a structural break in cloud unit economics.

Latest reading: Amazon Web Services earned $14.2 billion of operating profit on $37.6 billion of revenue in the quarter ending March 2026 — a margin of 37.7%. That is approximately 170 basis points below the same quarter a year earlier, as the company spent heavily on infrastructure. The filing attributes the compression explicitly to capacity investment to support growth; the 28% exit line is not approached.

3. Advertising revenue growth stays above single digits year-on-year, measured across two consecutive years.

Single-digit growth would signal that the third compounding engine — monetising the purchase-intent data generated by the retail platform — has reached saturation.

Latest reading: Advertising services grew 23.9% year-on-year in the quarter ending March 2026, reaching $17.2 billion. Growth at that rate for two consecutive years would need to decelerate by roughly 15 percentage points to approach the exit condition.

4. The International retail segment continues trending toward profitability.

International turning durably profitable extends the runway for operating income to compound faster than revenue across the full consolidated business.

Latest reading: International posted $1.4 billion in operating profit in the quarter ending March 2026, against $1.0 billion in the same quarter a year earlier. The margin improved from 3.0% to 3.6%. The improvement was driven by the same advertising growth that lifts North America.

5. Capital spending in FY2027 and FY2028 does not remain above $120 billion per year absent commensurate Amazon Web Services revenue acceleration.

Exit condition four monitors whether the current investment wave builds assets that generate returns, or builds assets that sit idle. The test is not triggered by spending alone — it requires spending to remain elevated while Amazon Web Services growth decelerates. Neither leg has fired.

Latest reading: Infrastructure spending reached $43.2 billion in a single quarter (ending March 2026), or more than double year-earlier levels. On a trailing twelve-month basis, net capital investment stood at approximately $147 billion. Amazon Web Services growth of 28% and the $364 billion backlog mean the "without commensurate acceleration" leg of the exit condition is not triggered — though the trajectory demands monitoring across every quarterly filing through 2027.

6. No court-ordered structural remedy is imposed in the Federal Trade Commission case.

A forced separation of the marketplace from Prime logistics, or algorithmic restrictions on the cross-subsidy between them, would mechanically impair the flywheel that makes both the retail and advertising businesses work.

Latest reading: The FTC case appears in Amazon's quarterly filing as a standard litigation risk factor alongside general legal proceedings. No new trial timeline was disclosed, no structural remedy has been imposed, and exit condition three is not triggered. The enforcement posture at the agency has softened materially since early 2025, reducing (though not eliminating) the probability of a structural outcome.

7. No material weakness in internal financial controls is disclosed, and the auditor (Ernst and Young) is not replaced unexpectedly.

These are integrity signals. A material weakness would indicate the financial records cannot be trusted; an unexpected auditor change would raise the question of why.

Latest reading: No material weakness has been disclosed. Ernst and Young has audited Amazon since 1996, with no replacement. The Beneish manipulation score — a statistical test of whether the financial statements show signs of earnings management — sits at −2.663, well below the −1.78 threshold that would trigger concern.

8. A cash-yield measure — free cash flow divided by market capitalisation — does not fall below 3.0%.

A yield below 3.0% would signal the market has priced in optimistic capital spending normalisation that has not yet materialised in the cash flow statements.

Latest reading: The yield stands at 4.2% based on full-year 2025 cash flow of $113 billion and a market capitalisation of approximately $2.69 trillion. The yield is above the 3.0% exit line and above the 4.0% line below which trimming is called for — but it sits close enough to the trim line that any further share price appreciation matters.

The Share Price

At $250 today, Amazon sits in the upper half of the hold band. The valuation methodology measures how much cash the business generates relative to its market capitalisation — an approach called owner earnings yield. The result is 4.2%, meaning that for every $100 invested in Amazon at today's price, the company generates the equivalent of $4.20 in annual cash after spending what it needs to maintain its assets.

The rules were set with a fair-value reading of 6.0% — the level that implies a fair exchange for a wide-moat business. The 4.2% reading is below fair value, meaning the shares are priced above what the rules would call neutral. The trim line sits at 4.0%; the current reading is 0.2 percentage points above it, which is close but not at the line.

There is an important caveat in the picture. A parallel valuation model — built from scenario analysis of Amazon Web Services growth, operating margins, and capital spending normalisation — places a base-case fair value at roughly $530 per share, substantially above both the $176 implied by the 6% yield target and today's $250. The two methods start from different assumptions about how much of Amazon's current capital spending is genuinely maintenance (and should reduce earnings) versus investment in future growth (and should be capitalised). The divergence between them is material. Gaps in the Picture notes this explicitly.

Why the Market Sees It Differently

The market prices Amazon at $250 and our yield-based fair value sits at $176 — a gap of 42%. The market is saying the business will earn more over time than a 6% yield-at-fair-value model implies. Our view is more cautious.

Three hypotheses could explain the premium. The first, and most directly supported by the filing, is that the market is pricing in faster growth in Amazon Web Services demand than the base case assumes. The $364 billion backlog, the OpenAI $100 billion expansion, and the 28% year-on-year growth in the quarter ending March 2026 all provide real evidence for the optimistic growth story. The second hypothesis is that margins across the business will expand faster than the model allows — the data partially supports this too: consolidated operating profit grew 30% year-on-year in the quarter despite the capital spending ramp, and advertising margins are expanding. The third hypothesis is that the market is valuing Amazon's balance-sheet stakes in Anthropic and OpenAI — positions totalling roughly $48 billion — as genuine optionality that the yield model ignores because those investments do not yet generate free cash flow.

At least two of those three hypotheses have real supporting evidence in the most recent filing. The market premium is not obviously irrational. What would have to happen for the premium to compress: Amazon Web Services growth would need to decelerate meaningfully — below 20% for two consecutive quarters — or the capital spending cycle would need to produce impairment charges that signal the buildout exceeded demand. Neither has happened; the question is whether it will.

What's Wobbling

Two things sit in the amber zone. The first is the capital-spending trajectory. Infrastructure purchases running at $43 billion per quarter are double the year-earlier rate, and the consolidated balance sheet now carries $357 billion in net physical assets — a fivefold increase from five years ago. The spending is entirely consistent with the thesis as long as Amazon Web Services revenue keeps pace; the worry is that it keeps growing in 2027 and 2028 even if that pace does not materialise. Exit condition four monitors exactly that; it has not fired, but every quarterly filing through 2027 is a test.

The second is capital allocation. The company's share repurchase authorisation — $6.1 billion — has sat unused since March 2022. In 2022 and 2023, the share price traded between $80 and $103, which was a period of demonstrated cheapness by most valuation methods. The authorisation was not used at those prices. Instead, the company issued $53.8 billion of new long-term debt in early 2026 to fund infrastructure and a $15 billion investment in OpenAI's preferred shares, alongside a $35 billion commitment letter for further investment. These are strategic choices that prioritise scale over per-share compounding — consistent with what the thesis describes as "acceptable but not excellent" capital allocation. No exit condition has been triggered; the characterisation remains.

What Could Still Go Wrong

  • Amazon Web Services loses ground to Azure and Google Cloud in AI workloads. Microsoft's Azure and Google Cloud both grew faster than Amazon Web Services in the most recent comparable quarter. If the custom silicon advantage from Trainium and the Anthropic model relationship do not translate into workload share, the AI capex wave could produce impairments rather than returns. The bear-case scenario models this explicitly: AWS growth falls to mid-teens and margins compress toward 9% at the consolidated level.
  • A structural regulatory remedy separates the marketplace from Prime logistics. Forced API access or algorithmic restrictions on the cross-subsidy between Prime membership, FBA fulfilment, and advertising attribution would mechanically impair all three businesses simultaneously. This is the single scenario that could cause permanent rather than cyclical impairment to the moat.
  • Capital spending fails to convert to revenue in 2027–2028. Approximately $147 billion of trailing twelve-month infrastructure investment must show up as Amazon Web Services revenue. If demand falls short — because frontier AI models prove cheaper to run than expected, or because enterprise workloads migrate to on-premises alternatives — asset write-downs and margin compression would follow simultaneously.
  • The Bezos-Trump relationship reverses. The current administrative tailwinds — softened FTC posture, National Labour Relations Board relief, expanded federal cloud contracts — are tied to a personal relationship between Jeff Bezos and the current administration. The 2017–2020 period demonstrated that the same relationship can destroy market capitalisation as quickly as it creates it. The bipartisan-durable portion of Amazon's government contracts is roughly 60% of federal revenue; the remainder is relationship-dependent.
  • Valuation already prices the base case. The base-case scenario delivers approximately 3.2% annualised return over three years from today's price. That is below the hurdle rate by a meaningful margin. A new position at $250 is not compensated for the bear-case risk; an existing position at a lower cost basis is a different calculation.

Gaps in the Picture

Two fair-value anchors coexist and they diverge sharply. The yield-based methodology embedded in the rules produces a fair value of $176, derived from applying a 6% yield target to full-year 2025 cash flow. A separate scenario analysis of Amazon Web Services growth and capital-spending normalisation produces a base-case midpoint closer to $530. The two methods use different assumptions about how much of the current capital spending is maintenance versus growth investment — the narrower accounting definition of capital expenditure ($6.7 billion) and the broader management definition including finance leases ($128 billion) lead to very different conclusions. Until the rules are recalibrated to resolve this, the fair-value reference in the yield method should be read with caution.

The capital expenditure figure used in the cash-yield calculation is the narrow accounting definition. The full infrastructure investment — including finance leases, operating right-of-use assets, and all infrastructure spending — is approximately twenty times what the accounting cash-flow statement records as capital expenditure. This is not an error in the calculation; it is a deliberate methodology choice that excludes lease-financed assets. The practical effect is that the reported cash yield is higher than it would be under a management-definition capital spending approach.

The risk-free rate used in the score calculation was read from the S&P 500 index fund's market-data snapshot rather than from Amazon's own data. The holding-level field is not populated. The figure (from a 2026-07-07 snapshot) is used as the cost-of-capital anchor across the hurdle rate calculation.

The Rules

If the bet is confirmed — no exit condition triggered, drivers reading at or above their thresholds — hold without action and continue compounding the Amazon Web Services and advertising flywheel.

If the bet weakens — any single driver deteriorates meaningfully but no exit condition fires — do not add to the position. Re-run the thesis check against the specific deteriorating driver, typically Amazon Web Services growth or margin. Wait for two consecutive quarterly filings to confirm the direction before deciding. One quarter of data does not constitute a trend.

If the bet breaks — any exit condition fires — exit the full position within 30 days. No averaging down. Note which condition triggered for future learning. Particular care: Amazon Web Services revenue and margin exits should be confirmed across two consecutive quarters before action; an FTC structural remedy or an auditor change is an immediate exit with no waiting period.

If the share price falls below the buy edge ($140) — confirm the thesis is intact first (no exit condition triggered; Amazon Web Services growth at or above 18%). If confirmed, add to the position in tranches over one to two quarters.

If the share price rises above the trim line ($263) — trim 25 to 33% of the position to harvest the premium while keeping the core holding. Re-evaluate the remaining position size after trimming.

If the share price rises above the exit line ($421) — exit the full position. The market is pricing in roughly 11 years of base-case compounding instantly at that reading; redeploy proceeds at the next cheap-edge crossing or into a cheaper alternative. No re-entry within 90 days.
