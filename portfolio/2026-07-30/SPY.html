The Bottom Line

Data as of 2026-07-30 · Latest filing 2026-07-30.

The bet is wobbling. The S&P 500 basket has been paying investors less in earnings yield than a ten-year US Treasury bond for fourteen consecutive measurement snapshots — and that reversal is precisely the condition the written bet is designed to notice. The vehicle itself remains sound; the question mark sits entirely on the valuation side, where the basket's earnings yield has slipped 25 basis points below the trim-band edge and shows no sign of reversing. The wobble to watch is the negative equity risk premium: if it persists through a second consecutive quarter-end, exit condition (1) fires.

As of 2026-07-30, the basket's earnings yield stands at 3.75%, while the ten-year Treasury yield sits at 4.60% — meaning the broad US market is paying investors 85 basis points less than a government bond that carries no equity risk. The structural case for owning the basket is not in doubt — only the price at which it is currently offered. That distinction matters for the Standing Orders and for the cost-basis verdicts in the Rules.

About SP500

The SPDR S&P 500 ETF Trust — managed by State Street Investment Management and listed on the New York Stock Exchange as SPY — tracks the S&P 500 index, a collection of roughly 500 large US companies selected by S&P Dow Jones Indices on the basis of market capitalisation, profitability, and liquidity. Each company's weight in the basket is proportional to its market value, so the largest companies by stock-market size carry the largest weight. Technology businesses currently account for 39% of the basket, reflecting the dominant capitalisation of companies such as NVIDIA, Apple, Microsoft, and Alphabet; financial services (12%) and communication services (10%) are the next two largest slices. The fund has been in continuous operation since January 1993, making it the oldest exchange-traded fund in the United States.

The legal structure is a Unit Investment Trust — an older ETF wrapper that cannot reinvest dividends between distribution dates, leaving a small cash sleeve (currently 0.25%) sitting uninvested until the next distribution. The fund's annual running cost is 0.09%, one of the lowest available for any large-cap equity fund. No tracking difference or tracking error figures are currently available from the State Street factsheet; those would confirm how closely the fund mirrors the index after costs and dividend timing. The tax profile for a holder domiciled outside the United States carries its own complexities (notably withholding tax on dividends), but K-1 forms are not issued and there is no capital-gain distribution history to speak of — the in-kind redemption mechanism that UITs use typically generates no taxable events at the fund level.

The Case for the Bet

SPY is held as core US large-cap passive exposure on one condition: that the basket's earnings yield (what the companies in the index collectively earn relative to the price paid) clears a minimum threshold above the risk-free rate. When equities compensate investors adequately for the extra risk of owning shares rather than bonds, the position is justified. When they do not, the Rules call for action. Six specific conditions underpin that general principle:

  1. The basket earnings yield must stay above the ten-year Treasury yield — the equity risk premium must remain positive. Exit condition (1) is the load-bearing one: if the basket earns less in yield than a risk-free bond, for two consecutive quarter-end readings, the fundamental case for owning equity over bonds has failed. The threshold is not absolute but relative: any earnings yield below the current Treasury rate qualifies.

Latest reading: Earnings yield 3.75% versus Treasury at 4.60%, as of 2026-07-30. The equity risk premium is −0.85% — the fourteenth consecutive negative reading since 2026-06-16. The two-consecutive-quarter calendar gate requires confirmation at Q3 2026-09-30 quarter-end. Exit condition (1) is not yet triggered, but the trajectory is in the wrong direction.

  1. The annual expense ratio must stay at or below 0.15%. State Street has never raised the expense ratio above its current level, and competition from Vanguard and BlackRock iShares makes a unilateral increase commercially difficult — but the condition is written as an objective threshold, not a forecast of management behaviour.

Latest reading: Annual expense ratio 0.09% as of 2026-07-30, 6 basis points below the ceiling. Exit condition (2) is not triggered.

  1. Assets under management must remain above $100 billion. A collapse of assets to this level would signal an extraordinary market event — not ordinary volatility but a structural liquidity failure.

Latest reading: Assets under management at $781 billion as of 2026-07-30, approximately 7.8 times the floor. Average daily trading volume $39 billion. Exit condition (3) is not triggered.

  1. No single company may exceed 13% of the basket. At that weight, a single-name disaster would move the index materially; the basket would no longer represent broad market exposure.

Latest reading: NVIDIA, the largest holding, represents 7.50% as of 2026-07-30 — 5.5 percentage points below the ceiling. Exit condition (4) is not triggered.

  1. The S&P 500 index methodology must remain broadly intact. S&P Dow Jones Indices reserves the right to change how the index is constructed. A material restructuring that undermined the broad-market mandate — for instance, a move to a narrow sector focus or a change in the profitability screen — would change the nature of the exposure.

Latest reading: No automated signal for methodology change as of 2026-07-30. This condition is monitored manually; no pipeline data is available. Exit condition (5) status: silent.

  1. The median return on equity among the top-ten basket constituents must stay above 15%. This is the quality floor: the basket should reflect genuinely capital-efficient businesses, not a collection of mediocre companies inflated by momentum.

Latest reading: Median return on equity among the top nine constituents (after removing a duplicate share class) is 37.28% as of 2026-07-30, anchored by Alphabet, Broadcom, Microsoft, and Meta. The weighted-mean of 70% is distorted by Apple and NVIDIA's negative book equity from buybacks — the median is the honest figure. Exit condition (6) is not triggered.

What's Wobbling

One wobble is active and material: the equity risk premium is negative.

The S&P 500 basket currently yields 3.75% in earnings while the ten-year US Treasury yields 4.60%. That −0.85 percentage-point gap means equity owners are accepting less income than bond owners while bearing considerably more risk. In the framework behind this position, that inversion is not a signal to watch passively — it is the primary exit trigger. The gate requires two consecutive quarter-end readings to confirm the pattern is not a transient spike; the first such quarter-end confirmation falls on 2026-09-30 and has not yet elapsed. In the meantime, the trim rule fires on the valuation position: the basket earnings yield is already below the 4.00% trim-band edge, and the framework calls for reducing the position by 25–33% at that level.

The five remaining conditions are all clear. Vehicle guardrails (cost, size, single-name concentration) have comfortable headroom. The basket's underlying quality — as measured by the median return on equity of its largest holdings — is strong at 37.28%, well above the 15% floor that exit condition (6) monitors. Index-methodology stability cannot be monitored automatically, but there is no external signal of concern.

In plain terms: the basket is a well-constructed vehicle holding excellent businesses, and it is priced expensively for what it offers relative to the currently available risk-free rate.

What Could Still Go Wrong

  • Cost rises unilaterally. State Street could raise the expense ratio above 0.15% — unlikely given competitive pressure from lower-cost alternatives, but the trigger is binary: any crossing fires exit condition (2) regardless of size.

  • Assets collapse in an extraordinary redemption event. A structural liquidity failure — not ordinary market volatility — could push assets under management below $100 billion. At $781 billion today, the headroom is very large, but a severe market dislocation combined with a structural loss of investor confidence is the tail scenario.

  • Single-name concentration spikes. If one company's market capitalisation dominates relative to the rest — as NVIDIA's weight has risen from historical norms — and crosses 13% of the index, exit condition (4) fires. NVIDIA at 7.50% has 5.5 percentage points of headroom, but the cap-weight mechanism amplifies momentum.

  • Index methodology changes materially. S&P Dow Jones Indices could restructure the S&P 500 in a way that narrows the mandate (sector caps, factor overlays, a shift to equal-weight) and changes the nature of the exposure in ways that are not visible in the live data feed.

  • The earnings-yield gap widens further and the calendar gate fires. If the basket earnings yield stays below the ten-year Treasury yield through 2026-09-30, exit condition (1) triggers and the position must be closed in full. The current trajectory — fourteen consecutive negative readings with no sign of reversal — makes this the central risk. (For this fund, exit fires when the share price reaches the exit edge, when a vehicle-integrity condition breaches, or when the named exit-condition trigger fires — any of three paths.)

The Rules

The position is governed by six pre-committed rules, stated as conditions:

  • If the thesis is confirmed — basket priced reasonably, vehicle sound, equity risk premium positive — hold. No action required.

  • If the thesis is weakened — specifically if the equity risk premium turns negative or the median look-through return on equity begins falling — do not add. Re-validate after the next quarter. If the negative equity risk premium persists to the next quarter-end or the quality metric keeps falling, wind down the position and swap to an alternative or move to cash per the position-sizing rule.

  • If the thesis is broken — meaning a vehicle-integrity condition has been breached or exit condition (1) (two consecutive quarter-end negative equity risk premium readings) has fired — exit the full position. Move proceeds to risk-free instruments or to a holding that passes the value test.

  • If the price falls below the buy-band edge (basket earnings yield at or above 6.25%) — add to the position. A basket at that yield level versus the risk-free rate is historically rare and represents a decisive value opportunity.

  • If the price rises above the trim-band edge (basket earnings yield at or below 4.00%) — trim 25–33%. The basket is meaningfully expensive versus the risk-free rate; harvest the premium by reducing the position.

  • If the price rises above the exit-band edge (basket earnings yield at or below 3.00%) — exit the full position. At that yield level the basket is fully priced versus the risk-free rate; the risk-reward for continued ownership is poor, and proceeds should be redeployed to risk-free instruments or to a holding that meets the value test.

Scenario Definitions

A scenario is a joint assumption about two independent variables: operating trajectory (how the holding's underlying cash flows grow relative to their long-run trend) and exit multiple (what the market pays for those cash flows at the horizon, relative to a fair-value anchor).

Scenario Operating trajectory Exit multiple
Bear Below trend Below fair value
Base At trend At fair value
Bull Above trend At or above fair value

Non-negotiable rule: In the bear case, both dimensions must be adverse simultaneously — a scenario where only the multiple moves is a re-rating scenario, not a bear.

Bear cases are comparable in structure across all holding types (company, ETF, bond), but not in magnitude. A company bear reflects idiosyncratic execution failure; an ETF bear reflects a broad market drawdown. Different mitigations, different diversification implications.
