5. Owner Perspective (Buffett Frame)

"Would Berkshire deploy cash into this basket today?"

Owning MEUD is economically equivalent to buying a diversified collection of European large-cap businesses at roughly 18.70× trailing earnings, with those businesses collectively generating a median ROE of 18.79% on book. The earnings yield of 5.35% exceeds the 10-year Treasury (4.62%) by only 72 basis points — adequate compensation for equity risk in isolation, but well short of the 200-bp threshold Buffett historically cites as the floor for favouring equities over bonds decisively. By Buffett/Munger standards, this basket earns a hold rather than a buy: the underlying businesses are high-quality compounders (median ROE 18.79% comfortably above cost of equity), the price is fair-to-marginally-stretched, and the margin of safety is thin. Berkshire would likely not deploy material incremental cash here given the thin ERP, but would hold an existing position for its diversification value and quality characteristics.
