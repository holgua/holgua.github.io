About the Bond

The Federal Republic of Germany issued this bond in February 2021 with a maturity date of 2033-04-15. The stated coupon is 0.10% per year, but that figure understates the actual cash flow: both the coupon payment and the principal repayment at maturity are scaled by an inflation index, so the real purchasing power of the position grows alongside consumer prices rather than eroding.

The index used is the German Harmonised Index of Consumer Prices excluding tobacco, maintained by Eurostat. At the most recent settlement date, the index ratio stood at 1.26343, meaning €100 of original face value has grown to approximately €126 in nominal terms. A buyer today pays the dirty price — the quoted clean price of €95.00 multiplied by 1.26343, then adjusted for accrued interest — which amounts to approximately €120.06 per €100 of original face value at settlement. Germany holds a AAA credit rating from all three major agencies (Fitch, Moody's, and S&P), meaning the counterparty risk of owning this bond is as close to zero as any EUR-denominated instrument offers.

The Case for the Bet

The holding is sized as a portfolio inflation-hedge, not as a rates trade. The bond's principal and coupon grow with German consumer prices, which means that even if inflation runs persistently above expectations, the position does not lose real purchasing power. The bet is structural: the inflation-hedge function either works or it does not, and the real yield vs. EUR cash is the primary exit signal.

Three numbered conditions govern when the hedge no longer serves its purpose:

  1. Real yield falls below −1.00% for two consecutive quarters while EUR cash or nominal German government bonds offer a superior after-tax real return. At that point, the guaranteed real loss embedded in the bond price outweighs the inflation-hedge benefit.

Latest reading: Real yield stands at 0.86% as of 2026-07-07, comfortably above the −1.00% exit threshold. The compound trigger is not active: the bond has spent zero of the required 90 days below the exit level, and EUR cash currently earns a real yield of approximately 0.30% — 56 basis points behind the linker.

  1. Germany's sovereign credit is downgraded below AA− by any two of the three major agencies. Such a downgrade would signal a structural shift in issuer creditworthiness that alters the risk profile of the holding.

Latest reading: As of the 2026-06-15 ratings check, all three agencies rate Germany at their highest tier: Fitch AAA, Moody's Aaa, and S&P AAA. Exit condition (2) is not triggered and is not close to triggering.

  1. The Eurostat Harmonised Index of Consumer Prices excluding tobacco is materially restructured in a way that severs the link between the index and realised consumer-price inflation. An example would be a substitution of the reference index by Eurostat decision.

Latest reading: No pending index methodology restructuring was found in the 2026-06-15 sidecar check. Exit condition (3) is not triggered.

What Could Still Go Wrong

  • Real yield falls and the cash comparison flips. If the European Central Bank cuts rates aggressively in response to a deflationary shock, EUR cash real yields could fall while bond prices rise, compressing the linker's real yield below −0.50% and eventually below −1.00%. A scenario where EUR cash simultaneously becomes more attractive than the linker — the compound trigger for exit condition (1) — would set the trim-and-exit sequence in motion.

  • German sovereign downgrade. A loss of the AAA rating by two agencies — most likely triggered by a sustained fiscal deterioration or a structural shock to the German economy — would fire exit condition (2) regardless of the real yield. This is regarded as a low-probability event but is the most structurally distinct of the three exit triggers.

  • Harmonised consumer-price index methodology change. A Eurostat decision to alter the index basket composition materially, substitute a different reference index, or restructure the tobacco exclusion in ways that change the economic substance of the linkage would fire exit condition (3). Methodology changes are infrequent and subject to advance public consultation, making this the lowest-probability exit trigger of the three.

The Rules

  • If the thesis is confirmed — all exit conditions are unsatisfied and the real yield is within the hold band — hold the position. Real yield is within band, German credit is intact, and the index linkage provides inflation protection as intended.
  • If the thesis is weakened — real yield moves below 0% but has not yet triggered exit condition (1) — do not add. Re-assess by comparing the real after-tax return against EUR cash and nominal German government bonds before any action.
  • If the thesis is broken — one or more numbered exit conditions fires (real yield persistently below −1%, a German downgrade, or an index methodology change) — exit the position and move proceeds to nominal short-duration German government bonds or EUR cash.
  • If the price is at or below the buy edge (real yield at or above 1.50%) — add to the position. A 1.50% real yield on a seven-year AAA EUR sovereign linker is highly attractive; scale in proportionally to the inflation-hedge allocation target.
  • If the price is at or above the trim edge (real yield at or below −0.50%) — trim 25% on the first crossing. If the real yield remains below −0.50% at the next quarterly review (at least 90 calendar days later), trim an additional 25%. Park proceeds in EUR cash or short conventional German government bonds. The 25 + 25 structure is bounded — further crossings do not trigger additional trims until the compound trigger for exit condition (1) fires.
  • If the compound exit trigger has fired (real yield below −1.00% for at least 90 days and EUR cash simultaneously offers a superior real return) — exit the full position and switch proceeds to EUR cash or short-duration nominal German government bonds; the inflation-hedge function is no longer additive compared to the alternative.
