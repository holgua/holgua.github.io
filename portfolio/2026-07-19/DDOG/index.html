About Datadog

Datadog makes software that tells engineers what is happening inside their cloud infrastructure. Every server, container, and application function runs a small Datadog monitoring agent that streams performance data back to a centralised platform, which stitches signals from hundreds of services into a single view. Customers pay for the volume of data they send in — so as their cloud footprints grow, Datadog's revenue from them grows automatically.

In the twelve months to the end of 2025, Datadog generated $3.4 billion in revenue, growing at 28% from the prior year, with a cash-flow margin of 29%. The platform covers observability (monitoring performance), security (detecting threats), and AI workload monitoring (tracking the behaviour and cost of large language models). Eleven per cent of customers now use ten or more of Datadog's products, up from 6% a year ago — each additional product embeds deeper into a customer's workflow and makes switching harder.

The Case for the Bet

The central idea is that cloud infrastructure is permanently complex, cross-cloud correlation is structurally difficult for any single cloud provider to offer, and Datadog's agent-based model means displacement requires months of engineering effort rather than a software swap. The conditions that must keep holding for that idea to remain sound are:

  1. Net revenue retention above 115%, with no two-quarter consecutive reading below 112%. The retention rate measures how much additional revenue existing customers generate each year. It is the compounding mechanism at the heart of the business model — a sustained reading below 112% would signal customers are actively shrinking their Datadog footprint. Two consecutive quarters below 112% trigger the exit condition.

Latest reading: Low-120%s as of the three months ending 2026-03-31 (up from high-110%s a year earlier). Well above the 115% watch threshold and the 112% two-quarter exit level; exit condition not triggered.

  1. Gross margin above 77% for at least two consecutive quarters. Gross margin is the share of revenue remaining after the direct cost of running the platform (third-party cloud infrastructure). If those costs grow faster than revenue, the margin falls; a sustained drop below 77% would signal deteriorating unit economics.

Latest reading: 79.1% in the three months ending 2026-03-31, flat year on year. Above the 78% watch threshold; exit condition not triggered.

  1. Stock-based compensation below 25% of revenue, with a credible glide-path toward 20%. Datadog pays a large part of employee costs in shares rather than cash. In 2025, this reduced the true economic profit from the $1.0 billion headline cash figure to roughly $250 million. If compensation in shares rises above 25% of revenue with no plan to reduce it, that gap becomes structural.

Latest reading: 19.6% (expensed) in the three months ending 2026-03-31, improved from 21.6% a year earlier. Below the 25% exit level; the direction is correct.

  1. Revenue growth above 20% for at least two consecutive quarters. Growth below 20% for two successive quarters would signal deceleration beyond what scale alone explains — consistent with competitive displacement or a tightening of enterprise software budgets.

Latest reading: 32% year on year in the three months ending 2026-03-31, re-accelerating above the base-case range of 18–20%. Exit condition not triggered.

  1. No single acquisition above $2 billion at premium multiples. Datadog's acquisition history is disciplined: no single deal has exceeded $220 million. A large purchase at a premium price would introduce integration risk inconsistent with the current earnings base.

Latest reading: Total acquisitions in 2025 were $118 million. No large acquisition announced; exit condition not triggered.

  1. No material regulatory enforcement action on cross-border data telemetry or AI monitoring. Streaming infrastructure telemetry across borders exposes Datadog to data-privacy regulation in Europe and data-sovereignty rules in other markets. The exit fires if a material fine or market-exit requirement affects more than 5% of revenue.

Latest reading: Extensive disclosure in the latest filing on relevant regulations — but no enforcement actions, fines, or market-exit events as of 2026-05-07. Exit condition not triggered.

The Share Price

The share price of $261 sits above the trim line — the level at which the Rules call for reducing the position. More precisely, the trim line sits at $47, which is the price at which the yield on Datadog's economic profit (cash flow minus the cost of equity compensation) would reach 1.5%, the minimum level set in the thesis for holding. At $261, that yield is only 0.27% — roughly one-fifth of the trim threshold, and less than one-tenth of the buy threshold.

Put plainly, the market is paying approximately $261 for a business that generates roughly $250 million in economic profit per year. That implies a waiting time of something like ten years before the economic profit catches up to the price — and only if growth continues at today's pace with no stumbles. The stock is well above fair value on a yield basis.

That much is settled. The complication is that a separate bottom-up valuation of Datadog's future cash flows, accounting for growth at 20% a year through 2028, arrives at a central estimate of around $106 per share — still below the current price of $261, but considerably higher than the $28 yield-based floor. Both methods confirm the same direction: overvalued. The precise magnitude depends on which framework is applied. See Gaps in the Picture for a note on the two-anchor situation.

Cash-flow margin. Margin fell from 32% in the three months ending March 2025 to 29% in the same period of 2026, driven by higher cloud hosting costs and headcount additions. Still above the 25% watch threshold — no line from the Case for the Bet is crossed — but the direction is worth tracking in the next two quarterly filings.

AI-native cohort optimisation. The cohort that includes Datadog's largest customer itself grew at a high-single-digit percentage year on year in the quarter ending March 2026. Management flagged in the latest filing that these customers may optimise or fail to renew. The overall retention rate improved to the low-120%s, so no impairment is visible today — but if AI-native customers as a group begin reducing spend, the aggregate retention figure would deteriorate. The watch level is retention below 115% for two consecutive quarters.

Source code security incident. In April 2025, an unauthorised third party accessed Datadog's source code repositories. Datadog terminated the access; the filing warns that residual vulnerability from the exposed code may remain. No exit condition is triggered, but it is a governance note worth retaining.

What Could Still Go Wrong

  • Hyperscaler native tools reach good enough. Amazon Web Services' and Microsoft Azure's monitoring tools are already adequate for single-cloud deployments. If multi-cloud architectures consolidate or a single provider builds sufficient cross-cloud capability, Datadog's structural advantage narrows. Confirming signal: retention below 115% for two consecutive quarters.
  • AI-native customers optimise rather than expand. The largest AI customers have shown they can and will reduce telemetry spend aggressively. If AI model training concentrates in fewer providers who build monitoring in-house, Datadog could lose its fastest-growing segment precisely when the market is paying for AI re-acceleration.
  • Stock compensation stays elevated. If the company matures while compensation in shares remains above 20% of revenue, the economic profit figure stays far below the headline cash number — and the fair value anchors in this analysis stay far below the price.
  • A large acquisition disrupts the model. Datadog's track record is disciplined: no single deal above $220 million. One purchase above $2 billion would introduce integration risk into a business that works precisely because it is tightly focused.
  • Regulatory fragmentation. Data-sovereignty requirements in Europe and Asia could force Datadog to hold and process telemetry locally in each jurisdiction. That would add cost and reduce the cross-cloud correlation advantage that is the product's core selling point.

Gaps in the Picture

Two fair-value anchors coexist. The yield-based method — dividing economic profit by the required yield — produces a fair value of approximately $28. A bottom-up discounted cash-flow model using projected growth at 20% per year through 2028 produces a central estimate of approximately $106. Both are well below $261, so both agree on direction. But the appropriate action-plan thresholds are anchored to the yield-based method, and that divergence is unresolved.

The economic-profit figure deducts the full value of stock-based compensation from cash flow. This is deliberately conservative and will produce a lower fair value than methodologies that use pre-compensation cash flow.

The net revenue retention rate is disclosed as a range ("low-120%s," "high-110%s") rather than a precise number, which makes it impossible to track the exact trajectory against the specific 115% watch threshold.

The Rules

The rules for each condition and price state:

  • Thesis confirmed, valuation above trim line: no position today. Monitor retention, revenue growth, and AI observability traction quarterly. Revisit entry only if the economic-profit yield rises to at least 1.5% (approximately $200 per share) with the thesis intact. At today's 0.27% yield, the position stays in watch territory.

  • Thesis weakens: do not initiate. If retention declines toward 115% or revenue growth falls below 20%, reassess within one earnings cycle. A weakening thesis at an elevated price has strictly negative risk and reward.

  • Thesis breaks: do not enter. If a position is held, close it within 30 days. Triggered by retention at or below 112% for two consecutive quarters, gross margin below 77% for two consecutive quarters, or stock compensation above 25% with no credible normalisation plan. No averaging down.

  • Valuation corrects to buy edge (economic-profit yield at or above 4.0%, approximately $18): confirm all six conditions are working first — retention at or above 115% and cash-flow margin at or above 25% for two consecutive quarters. If confirmed, initiate in two tranches over one quarter.

  • Valuation above trim edge (yield below 1.5%, approximately $47): do not initiate. If a position is held, trim 25–33%. Do not add. The share price of $261 is already well above this level.

  • Valuation well above exit edge: close the full position or remain out. Redeploy to a holding with positive risk and reward. No re-entry within 90 days.

Scenario Definitions

A scenario is a joint assumption about two independent variables: operating trajectory (how the holding's underlying cash flows grow relative to their long-run trend) and exit multiple (what the market pays for those cash flows at the horizon, relative to a fair-value anchor).

Scenario Operating trajectory Exit multiple
Bear Below trend Below fair value
Base At trend At fair value
Bull Above trend At or above fair value

Non-negotiable rule: In the bear case, both dimensions must be adverse simultaneously — a scenario where only the multiple moves is a re-rating scenario, not a bear.

Bear cases are comparable in structure across all holding types (company, ETF, bond), but not in magnitude. A company bear reflects idiosyncratic execution failure; an ETF bear reflects a broad market drawdown. Different mitigations, different diversification implications.
