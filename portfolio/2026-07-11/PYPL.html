Recent Development

The filing for the three months ending in March 2026 confirmed that the business's two most load-bearing pillars — capital return and credit quality — are working as described in the thesis. PayPal repurchased 34 million shares for $1.5 billion at an average price of $44.60, reducing the share count from 920 million to 892 million in a single quarter. The remaining buyback authorisation stood at $12.4 billion. Separately, the consumer net charge-off rate — the share of outstanding consumer loans that are written off as uncollectible — fell to 3.0% annualised from 4.5% a year earlier, well below the 10% exit-condition threshold. These two developments together are the core of the argument for holding.

The shadow on the quarter was operating margin. In the three months ending March 2026, the profit margin on operations (operating income divided by revenue) fell to 18% from 20% in the same period a year earlier. The cause is well-understood: Braintree, which processes payments for other businesses rather than directly serving consumers, has been growing faster than PayPal's own branded checkout, and Braintree carries a higher transaction-expense rate, dragging the blended margin down. The full-year 2025 operating income of $6.1 billion still cleared the $5.5 billion exit-condition floor by a comfortable margin, so the margin slide is a watching point rather than a trigger.

On the regulatory side, the UK's Financial Conduct Authority opened an investigation in March 2026 into certain provisions in PayPal's contractual agreements with Visa and Mastercard — a commercial competition matter, not an enforcement action restricting core product features, and therefore not a trigger for exit condition two. The Darcy and Norfolk securities class actions remain at the pleadings stage with no admission or judicial finding. Details of these legal matters are in What's Wobbling. The case for the overall verdict is in The Bottom Line.

About PayPal

PayPal operates a two-sided digital payments network: it sits between consumers and merchants and earns a fee every time money moves across it. When a consumer checks out online using a PayPal wallet or through the Venmo app, PayPal takes a slice. When a merchant processes payments through Braintree — PayPal's underlying payment infrastructure — it earns a transaction fee. When customers hold balances or borrow via its buy-now-pay-later service, it earns interest and risk-based revenue. The model is structurally simple: a digital toll-booth on e-commerce that has operated in essentially the same way for fifteen years. In the full year 2025 the company processed $1.79 trillion in total payment volume across 439 million active accounts in approximately 200 countries.

The strategic tension is well-documented in the official filings. Branded checkout — the consumer-facing PayPal button at the checkout page — has been losing share to payment methods baked into mobile operating systems (Apple Pay, Google Pay), while Braintree, the higher-volume lower-margin infrastructure product, has grown faster. The net result is that PayPal's total volume keeps growing, but the mix is shifting toward lower-margin processing. In March 2026, CEO Enrique Lores joined from HP, making this the third chief-executive change in five years. A strategic reorganisation announced in April 2026 targets at least $1.5 billion in annualised cost savings over two to three years.

The Case for the Bet

The bet is that PayPal's combination of capital discipline and structural pricing discount makes the current share price attractive even if the top line grows only modestly. The compounding engine is not revenue growth — it is the company spending $5–7 billion a year buying back its own shares at prices that imply little or no future growth.

  1. Branded checkout must not lose share at an accelerating rate. The thesis does not require PayPal to win the checkout wars. It requires that the loss is gradual rather than abrupt — specifically, that branded checkout share does not fall by more than 2 percentage points in a single fiscal year. A sudden step-down would signal structural displacement rather than cyclical softness.

Latest reading: Branded checkout total payment volume is not separately disclosed in the filing for the three months ending March 2026. Revenue from PayPal-branded products grew approximately $140 million year-on-year in the quarter, alongside $410 million from Braintree and $70 million from Venmo — indicating that branded checkout is growing in absolute dollar terms, if more slowly. Two securities class actions (the Darcy and Norfolk cases) allege that earlier management concealed impediments to branded checkout growth, but both remain at the pleadings stage with no judicial finding. Evidence is mixed: growth is positive but disclosure is incomplete.

  1. No consent order restricting core product features. Exit condition two fires if a regulatory authority — specifically the Consumer Financial Protection Bureau or the Department of Justice — issues a formal order materially restricting what PayPal can offer its customers. An informal inquiry or investigation does not trigger the condition.

Latest reading: As of March 2026, the Consumer Financial Protection Bureau and the Federal Trade Commission each have active civil investigative demands outstanding — essentially formal information requests at the pre-enforcement stage. The UK Financial Conduct Authority opened a new investigation in March 2026 into contractual terms between PayPal and Visa and Mastercard, a competition-law matter unrelated to core product restrictions. The earlier PPH securities class action was dismissed with prejudice in March 2026. No consent order and no product restriction is in place. Evidence confirms this condition is not triggered.

  1. Consumer credit charge-off rate must stay below 10%. PayPal holds consumer loan receivables from its buy-now-pay-later business. The charge-off rate — the share of outstanding balances written off annually — has a hard exit trigger at 10%. Above that level without a disclosed recovery path, the thesis is broken.

Latest reading: The annualised consumer net charge-off rate improved materially to 3.0% in the three months ending March 2026, down from 4.5% a year earlier. The share of consumer loans rated current (up to date on payments) held steady at 96.3%. The transaction loss rate on PayPal's broader payments business remained stable at 0.06% of total payment volume. Evidence confirms this condition is tracking well below the exit floor.

  1. Buyback programme must continue. The central mechanism compounding per-share value is management buying back shares at prices below intrinsic value. The condition breaks if management abandons the programme or takes on incremental debt above 2.0 times operating profit before depreciation and amortisation — a measure that approximates the cash a business generates before accounting write-downs are applied.

Latest reading: In the three months ending March 2026, PayPal repurchased 34 million shares for $1.5 billion at an average price of $44.60, the fourth consecutive quarter at or above that pace. The remaining authorisation under the February 2025 programme stood at $12.4 billion. The share count fell from 920 million to 892 million in the quarter alone. The April 2026 strategic reorganisation targets $1.5 billion in annualised savings, which, if realised, would increase cash available for buybacks. Evidence confirms this condition.

  1. Operating income must stay above $5.5 billion per full year. The final income-protection condition bars management from allowing annual operating profit to collapse below $5.5 billion, which would signal that the margin-recovery thesis has broken down. A secondary trigger fires if management explicitly guides non-GAAP (adjusted) operating income below $5.0 billion for the following year.

Latest reading: Full-year 2025 GAAP operating income was $6.065 billion, 10% above the $5.5 billion exit floor. The quarterly run rate in the three months ending March 2026 was $1.488 billion — an annualised pace of approximately $5.95 billion, again above the floor. The quarterly margin slipped to 18% from 20% a year earlier due to Braintree mix, but no guide below $5.0 billion has been issued. Evidence confirms this condition.

The Share Price

The share price at the time of the most recent quarterly filing was $45.65. The valuation framework used here measures the yield earned on owner earnings (net income plus depreciation, minus capital expenditure) as a percentage of the market's total price for the business. At the current price, that yield is 11.1% — above the fair-value anchor of 10.5% and within the hold band set by the buy threshold of 12.5% and the trim threshold of 8.5%.

Put plainly: the share is neither cheap enough to add nor expensive enough to trim. It sits in the lower half of the zone between those two lines, closer to the buy edge than to the trim edge. The fair-value implied price — the price at which the yield would equal the fair-value anchor of 10.5% — works out to approximately $48. At $45.65, the share is about 6% below that anchor.

The market's total implied price-to-earnings multiple at this share price is roughly 8.4 times trailing earnings — historically in the lowest decile of PayPal's own trading history. The zero-growth value (what the business is worth if earnings never increase) implies a share price of approximately $57. That means the current market price embeds a material degree of earnings decline that the most recent reported figures do not support.

What's Wobbling

Three wobbles are present in the latest quarterly filing, none of which crosses a written exit condition.

Braintree margin drag. The operating margin in the three months ending March 2026 fell to 18% from 20% a year earlier, driven entirely by Braintree's faster growth relative to branded checkout. Braintree carries a higher transaction-expense rate because it processes on behalf of large merchants who negotiate thin margins. Management is guiding toward margin stabilisation as the Braintree-to-checkout mix normalises; the full-year 2025 operating income of $6.1 billion already clears the exit-condition floor by $565 million. This is a headwind to watch, not a trigger.

Branded checkout disclosure gap. PayPal no longer separately reports branded checkout total payment volume as a quarterly metric. The Darcy and Norfolk class actions, both at the pleadings stage as of March 2026, allege that earlier management concealed impediments to the branded checkout growth strategy. If those allegations produce a judicial finding or an admission, the assessment of whether exit condition one has been breached would need to be revisited. As of now, they do not.

Fair-value model drift. The fair-value calculation used in this holding's framework — based on measuring the annual cash return as a percentage of the total market price — implies a fair-value price of approximately $48, while a separate scenario-based valuation model implies a base-case midpoint of approximately $141. That is a large divergence. Both frameworks use different methodologies and different discount-rate assumptions, and the two are not reconciled. The standing-orders prices and the valuation band readings above all come from the cash-return framework; the scenario model's figures are not used.

What Could Still Go Wrong

  • Branded checkout share loss accelerates beyond the 2 percentage-point annual threshold. If the Darcy and Norfolk allegations prove accurate and disclosure of a systematic decline follows, exit condition one fires.
  • A Consumer Financial Protection Bureau or Department of Justice investigation converts into a formal consent order restricting core product features. Two active civil investigative demands are at the pre-enforcement stage; escalation is a tail risk rather than a base case.
  • CEO Enrique Lores, arriving in March 2026 from a hardware company with no direct fintech experience, makes decisions that destroy capital — large acquisitions at inflated prices, debt-funded diversification, or reversal of the buyback programme. Three chief executives in five years is a governance yellow flag.
  • The Braintree margin drag persists longer than expected and the full-year operating income approaches the $5.5 billion exit-condition floor. At the current quarterly run rate of approximately $1.5 billion per quarter, there is limited headroom for a further margin compression.
  • AI-agent commerce routes consumer purchasing natively through Apple Pay or Google Wallet, bypassing the PayPal checkout layer entirely even on merchants that currently accept PayPal. This is a structural rather than near-term risk but represents the clearest single scenario under which the moat narrows materially.

The Rules

  • If the thesis is confirmed and no condition is deteriorating, hold with no action. The buyback machine is compounding per-share value and no exit condition is active.
  • If a thesis driver shows sustained weakening, do not add to the position. Run a fresh re-validation on the specific deteriorating driver. Two consecutive quarters of confirming data are required before the reading is treated as structural — one quarter of weakness is not enough to change the posture.
  • If a written exit condition fires, close the full position within 30 days. No further purchases. Document which condition fired for reference.
  • If the share price falls to or below the buy edge ($41) and the thesis is intact, add to the position in tranches across one or two quarters.
  • If the share price rises to or above the trim edge ($60), trim 25 to 33 per cent of the position to harvest the premium. The core position remains.
  • If the share price rises to or above the exit edge ($78), close the full position. The market is at that point pricing in roughly 11 years of base-case compounding instantaneously. No re-entry within 90 days.
