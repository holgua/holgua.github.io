Amazon (AMZN) — Growth Analysis

Analysis date: 2026-08-10 Sources: AMZN-Financial-Brief.md (2026-07-30), AMZN-Market-Data.json (2026-08-10), AMZN-Political-Exposure.md (2026-07-29) Current price anchor: $277.77 (August 2026 close, Market-Data.json history_monthly)

SECTION 1 — GROWTH INVESTOR'S VERDICT

1.1 One-Paragraph Thesis

Amazon is a rare three-engine compounding machine — AWS cloud infrastructure, digital advertising, and high-frequency e-commerce — whose most valuable engine (AWS) is simultaneously accelerating revenue growth (28% YoY in Q1 2026) and rapidly expanding margins ($45.6B segment operating income in FY2025, ~35% segment margin), driven by a once-in-a-decade secular shift to AI cloud workloads. The single most compelling reason to own Amazon as a growth investor today is the structural ROIC expansion already underway: AWS operating income tripled from FY2022 to FY2025 while the advertising segment compounds at high teens, creating a profit mix shift that is materially underrepresented in trailing earnings multiples still anchored to a period of near-zero profitability. The single biggest risk is capital destruction: $128.3B of management-defined capex in FY2025 — nearly doubling in a single year — has compressed management FCF to $11.2B, and if the AI infrastructure investment cycle fails to yield adequate returns within 3–4 years, the thesis becomes a costly stranded-asset story rather than a compounding one.

1.3 Investment Stance

Buy — Conviction: Medium-High

The thesis holds so long as AWS revenue growth remains above 20% YoY for at least two consecutive quarters and management FCF trends toward recovery as the capex cycle matures — if both conditions deteriorate simultaneously, the reinvestment case collapses and the valuation premium is not warranted.

SECTION 2 — QUALITY OF GROWTH

2.1 Revenue Growth Decomposition

Revenue grew from $469.8B (FY2021) to $716.9B (FY2025), a 4-year CAGR of approximately 11.2%. Annual growth rates: 9.4% (FY2022), 11.8% (FY2023), 11.0% (FY2024), 12.4% (FY2025) — a reacceleration notable given the absolute base size.

AWS (cloud computing): The highest-quality revenue stream — enterprise contracts, high switching costs, programmatic consumption model. AWS grew 20% in FY2025 and 28% in Q1 2026, accelerating materially as AI workloads (training clusters, inference, Bedrock managed AI services) compound on top of the existing cloud migration cycle. AWS segment operating income reached $45.6B in FY2025 at a ~35% margin, generating more operating profit than Amazon's entire company earned three years earlier. This growth is structural, not cyclical.

Advertising: Digital advertising embedded in Amazon's shopping surfaces is the second-highest-margin business, growing high-teens YoY. Advertising benefits from the 3P marketplace network effect — more buyers attract more sellers who bid for sponsored placement. Revenue quality is high: no inventory risk, near-100% incremental margins, entrenched in the seller cost structure.

Stores (Retail + 3P + Subscription): The largest revenue segment by absolute dollars grows at high single digits, driven by Prime subscriber expansion, same/next day delivery improvements (13B+ deliveries FY2025), and International expansion. Retail revenue quality is lower than AWS or advertising — thin margins, logistics capex-intensive — but it funds the flywheel and provides the demand signal that makes AWS and advertising defensible.

Revenue mix is improving structurally: AWS and advertising (highest-margin) are growing faster than 1P retail (lowest-margin), creating a durable margin tailwind embedded in the top-line growth story.

2.2 Earnings Quality & FCF Conversion

XBRL FCF conversion: Strong and improving. Operating cash flow grew from $46.8B (FY2022) to $139.5B (FY2025). XBRL FCF (operating cash flow minus XBRL-defined capex) grew from $43.3B to $132.8B — FCF margin 18.5% in FY2025 vs. 8.4% in FY2022.

Management FCF: Management-defined FCF (operating cash flow minus all PP&E additions) fell to $11.2B in FY2025 from $38.2B in FY2024, a deliberate consequence of $128.3B management-defined capex. This is the correct valuation FCF measure. The $121.6B gap between XBRL FCF and management FCF represents the scale of the current infrastructure investment program.

Accruals ratio: Consistently negative — −0.103 (FY2023), −0.091 (FY2024), −0.076 (FY2025). Negative accruals ratio means cash earnings exceed accrual earnings: a high-quality signal. Trend toward zero reflects operating income acceleration closing the gap.

Beneish M-Score: −2.663, well below the −1.78 manipulation threshold. No earnings quality concerns.

One-time items: FY2022 net income of −$2.7B was distorted by a $12.7B Rivian equity investment mark-to-market loss — a non-operating item that created an artificially depressed base for the subsequent EPS recovery narrative.

Working capital: Amazon operates with negative net working capital in its retail segment (customers pay before Amazon pays suppliers), providing structural cash generation that is a persistent quality feature of the marketplace model.

2.3 Margin Trajectory

EBITDA margin: 10.5% (FY2022) → 14.9% (FY2023) → 19.0% (FY2024) → 20.3% (FY2025). A 980bp expansion over three years driven by AWS mix shift, advertising growth, and fulfilment operational efficiency.

Operating margin: 2.4% (FY2022) → 6.4% (FY2023) → 10.8% (FY2024) → 11.2% (FY2025). Recovery from near-zero profitability to double-digit operating margins in three years is exceptional at this revenue scale. Q1 2026 operating income of $23.9B implies annualised operating margin above 13%, suggesting continued expansion.

Incremental margin: The progression from $12.2B operating income on $514B revenue (FY2022) to $80.0B on $717B revenue (FY2025) implies approximately 33% incremental operating margins on the $203B of incremental revenue — demonstrating operating leverage as AWS and advertising grow faster than the cost base.

SECTION 3 — REINVESTMENT ENGINE

3.1 ROIC vs. Cost of Capital

ROIC trend: 8.2% (FY2023) → 13.3% (FY2024) → 10.7% (FY2025). The FY2024-to-FY2025 decline is explained by the invested-capital surge (PP&E net: $72.7B FY2023 → $252.7B FY2024 → $357.0B FY2025) from the capex acceleration — NOPAT grew but invested capital grew faster. This is a timing mismatch, not a structural deterioration.

Estimated WACC: Financial Brief cites ~9.0% (risk-free rate 4.3%, ERP 4.5–5.5%, beta 1.1–1.3). Market-Data.json beta of 1.454 implies cost of equity closer to 10–11%. Amazon's net cash position (net debt −$21.3B) means WACC effectively equals cost of equity. Range: 9–11%.

ROIC spread: Current ROIC of 10.7% sits modestly above the low end of estimated WACC (9%), providing a thin positive spread. The thesis is a bet on ROIC recovery to 15%+ as invested capital stabilises and AWS profits compound on a fixed base.

3.2 Reinvestment Rate & Efficiency

Reinvestment rate: $139.5B operating cash flow funded $128.3B management capex in FY2025 — an implied reinvestment rate of ~92%. Amazon is directing virtually all internally generated cash into infrastructure.

Reinvestment return horizon: At current investment levels, near-term incremental returns are low. AWS precedent from the 2015–2020 period — where capex in that era ultimately yielded 30%+ operating margins by FY2023 — supports the thesis that AI infrastructure investment will follow a similar trajectory with a 3–4 year lag.

Consistency check: Using g = ROIC × reinvestment rate: 10.7% × 92% ≈ 9.8%, which broadly tracks the ~11% observed revenue growth. This internal consistency check supports the reliability of the reported figures.

3.3 Capital Allocation Track Record

CapEx: $128.3B FY2025, nearly double FY2024's $65B. Directed at AWS AI infrastructure (data centres, Trainium/Graviton chip capacity, power) and logistics (same/next day expansion). Management has stated AI demand is currently supply-constrained — framing the investment as demand-pull rather than speculative.

Strategic investments: Anthropic ($13B for AI model access and differentiation); Zoox (autonomous vehicles); Project Kuiper (satellite internet). These represent strategic optionality, not yield optimisation.

Buybacks and dividends: No dividends. $6.1B buyback authority unused since 2022. Share count essentially flat: 10,296M (FY2021) → 10,827M (FY2025), +5.1% over 4 years from SBC — well-controlled dilution.

SECTION 4 — GROWTH RUNWAY ASSESSMENT

4.1 Total Addressable Market (TAM) & Penetration

AWS / Cloud: Global cloud infrastructure spending is heading toward $700B–$900B annually by the late 2020s at 20%+ growth. AWS implied revenue of ~$108–115B (at ~35% margin on $45.6B segment OI) represents 12–16% of the global cloud market — a large incumbent but still a growth share. AI workload migration is expanding the TAM faster than incumbents can build capacity.

Digital advertising: Global digital ad spend exceeds $700B annually. Amazon advertising (~$55–65B estimated FY2025) represents 8–9% penetration with structural advantages — it reaches buyers at the moment of purchase intent, commanding premium CPMs. Prime Video ad inventory (launched 2024) and international expansion provide multi-year runway.

E-commerce: Global retail e-commerce penetration is below 25% in most markets. Amazon holds ~37–40% US e-commerce share. International geographic expansion (India, EU, LATAM, Middle East) provides long-duration runway.

Government cloud (AWS GovCloud): The $50B federal AI infrastructure commitment (announced November 2025) and classified contracts (NSA ~$10B, CIA C2E multi-billion, JWCC $9B four-vendor pool) are creating a dedicated government-sector growth channel. Tracked USASpending awards grew at ~120% CAGR FY2022–FY2025 from a low base. This segment is not currently material at consolidated level but is becoming material within AWS.

4.2 Growth Drivers & Duration

  1. AWS AI infrastructure monetisation (3–7 year duration): Trainium adoption at triple-digit growth, Bedrock managed AI services, and inference workloads represent durable, high-switching-cost revenue streams. If AWS sustains 20–28% growth for 3+ more years, the revenue compounding is transformative.

  2. Advertising mix shift within the Amazon ecosystem (3–5 year duration): Prime Video ads maturing, sponsored products expanding internationally, and Amazon's purchase-intent data advantage positions advertising to become a $100B+ segment — each incremental advertising dollar carries ~80%+ operating margins.

  3. International e-commerce and Prime expansion (5–10 year duration): Deep US penetration but early-stage in India, Middle East, and parts of Europe. Prime subscriber growth in these markets drives recurring subscription revenue and fulfillment volume leverage.

  4. Fulfilment automation and operational leverage (2–4 year duration): Robotics programs (Sequoia, Proteus) are reducing per-unit handling costs. As the logistics network densifies for same/next day delivery, fixed costs spread over more units, improving store economics.

  5. Government cloud (AWS GovCloud) expansion (5–8 year duration): The $50B federal AI pledge and long-duration classified contracts provide a stable, high-switching-cost government revenue base growing rapidly from a small base.

4.3 Unit Economics & Scalability

AWS: Declining marginal cost of delivery as data centre capacity scales. Custom silicon (Trainium, Graviton) progressively reduces electricity and depreciation cost per unit of compute, defending margins against Azure and GCP pricing pressure. The 500+ services portfolio means customers consume multiple services simultaneously, increasing average revenue per customer and reducing churn.

Advertising: Each additional impression has near-zero marginal cost on infrastructure already deployed for e-commerce. Operating leverage is extremely high — estimated advertising EBIT margins above 80% based on the business model structure (no inventory, no marginal delivery cost).

Retail: Negative working capital cycle generates structural float. Robotics automation reduces per-package handling cost. The last-mile delivery network (Amazon Air, delivery stations) is evolving into a third-party logistics business (Amazon Shipping), extending unit economics beyond Amazon's own packages.

Scalability constraint: Amazon's architecture is inherently scalable. The primary near-term constraint is physical — power availability and data centre construction timelines — which is why the capex is so large. This constraint is also a competitive moat: rivals face identical physical limitations and Amazon has a multi-year head start in site acquisition, power contracts, and chip supply.

5.2 Growth-Adjusted Valuation (PEG Analysis)

At a trailing P/E of 22.3x and a 4-year historical EPS CAGR of ~22% (FY2021–FY2025), the PEG ratio is approximately 1.0x — historically considered fair value for a high-quality compounder. However, this PEG is misleading in two directions simultaneously.

Downward distortion (makes current P/E look expensive): Trailing EPS of $12.43 understates normalised earnings power because FY2025 management FCF of $11.2B reflects peak capex spend — the earnings base does not yet include returns on $128B+ of capital deployed in FY2025. As the capex cycle matures, EPS should accelerate materially beyond the current run-rate. Q1 2026 EPS of $2.78 alone implies a $11+ annualised run-rate, and the capex drag will progressively ease from FY2027.

Upward distortion (makes current P/E look cheap): The 22% EPS CAGR of FY2021–2025 benefited from a low base (FY2021 EPS $3.24 included the Rivian gain distortion; FY2022 was −$0.27 on Rivian losses). The forward EPS growth rate of 20–25% that the market is pricing into the 26.9x forward P/E requires sustained AWS acceleration and margin expansion — not guaranteed.

Bottom line: At ~$277, Amazon is priced for high-quality execution (PEG 1.0x on trailing, reasonable on forward) but not for failure. The margin of safety is moderate: meaningful upside if AI infrastructure returns materialise on schedule, but limited downside cushion if both the capex cycle disappoints and revenue decelerates.

5.4 Margin of Safety Assessment

At $277.77, the margin of safety is limited but present for a growth investor with a 3–5 year horizon. The stock does not screen as deeply undervalued by any trailing metric — P/E 22.3x, EV/EBITDA 18.3x, EV/Sales 4.0x are all in the upper quartile of the S&P 500. However, the margin of safety arguments for a growth investor are:

  1. Trailing metrics understate normalised earnings power — management FCF should recover materially from $11.2B as capex stabilises from FY2027; when normalised FCF reaches $50–80B on $1T+ revenue, current EV/normalised-FCF looks reasonable.
  2. AWS mix shift creates structural multiple re-rating potential — as AWS becomes a larger share of revenue and operating income, Amazon's blended multiple should converge toward cloud-sector multiples rather than retail multiples.
  3. The PEG of ~1.0x on trailing earnings is not demanding for a company with a demonstrably widening competitive moat, net cash balance sheet, and addressable markets growing at 20%+.

The primary downside risk is a bear scenario (25% probability per Financial Brief) where FCF collapses and the multiple compresses — a −70%+ loss outcome that a risk-aware growth investor must actively monitor for.

SECTION 5B — POLITICAL & REGULATORY ENVIRONMENT (GROWTH INVESTOR'S LENS)

5B.1 Political Tailwinds and Headwinds for the Growth Thesis

Tailwinds (current Trump administration):

The most directly quantifiable political tailwind is the One Big Beautiful Bill Act (signed July 4, 2025), which includes accelerated depreciation provisions that reduced Amazon's quarterly cash tax by approximately 45% — translating to an estimated $1.4B+ annual cash tax saving. For a company deploying $128B in capex annually, accelerated depreciation is a material structural benefit that improves management FCF by $1.4B+ annually without any operational change.

The GSA OneGov agreement (August 2025) provides up to $1B in government-wide cloud discounts positioned within the Trump AI Action Plan — a direct AWS revenue-access mechanism. Administration alignment with Amazon's AI-forward positioning means federal agencies are being directed toward cloud adoption, and AWS is the most government-credentialed provider (FedRAMP High, AWS GovCloud, classified regions).

The Bezos-Trump rapprochement — documented by Bezos's $1M inauguration donation, withheld Washington Post Harris endorsement, and private White House meeting July 2025 — has materially improved the regulatory climate. Under Biden, Amazon faced an active FTC antitrust trial and aggressive regulatory scrutiny. Under the current administration, the FTC trial (inherited from the Biden FTC) proceeds toward a late-2026 date but the current FTC Chair is less aggressive, reducing the probability of a structural remedy.

Headwinds:

The FTC antitrust trial (late 2026) remains an active downside risk. A structural remedy imposing marketplace access rules or algorithmic pricing constraints could reduce the take-rate advantage and seller lock-in that powers advertising revenue and 3P marketplace economics.

China tariff exposure on the third-party marketplace is a real but manageable headwind. Approximately 70% of Amazon's 3P goods originate from China; peak tariffs of 145% (April 2025, partially reduced through U.S.-China normalisation) raised seller costs and reduced conversion rates. CEO Jassy confirmed in January 2026 that tariffs have "started to creep" into Amazon prices. This creates consumer demand headwinds in the retail segment — a growth drag, not a catastrophic risk.

The Jassy-Anthropic intervention (June 2026, in which Jassy called Treasury Secretary Bessent to flag Anthropic AI vulnerabilities, triggering the first US government shutdown of a frontier AI model) creates a novel investment-relationship risk: Amazon subordinated a $13B strategic investment to administration relationship management, introducing uncertainty about whether Anthropic remains a reliable AI partner.

Net political impact on the growth thesis: Mildly positive. Administration tailwinds (tax savings, government contracting, reduced antitrust aggression) exceed current headwinds (FTC trial, tariff drag) in dollar magnitude and probability-weighted impact. Political risk rating: Medium (per Political-Exposure analysis).

5B.2 Government Contract Pipeline as a Growth Driver

Government cloud is transforming from an immaterial revenue footnote to a meaningful AWS growth driver. The trajectory:

  • Tracked USASpending awards: $35.2M (FY2022) → $372.8M (FY2025) — a 4-year CAGR of ~120% from a low base.
  • Classified (untracked) contracts: NSA Wild and Stormy (~$10B, awarded 2021), CIA C2E (multi-billion, 5-vendor pool including Amazon), JWCC ($9B 4-vendor Pentagon pool, Dec 2022) — not in public spending data but collectively suggesting $4–10B+ of AWS government revenue annually.
  • $50B federal AI infrastructure commitment (November 2025): Amazon committed to build 1.3GW of new compute capacity exclusively for federal agencies in classified and GovCloud regions beginning 2026. This is both a capex commitment (already captured in the $128B FY2025 figure) and a future revenue driver as agencies migrate to the new infrastructure.

At the midpoint analyst estimate of $7B annually in AWS government revenue, government represents ~15–20% of AWS segment revenue and is the fastest-growing AWS sub-segment. Government contracts carry high switching costs, long durations (3–10 years for classified infrastructure), and low churn — higher-quality revenue than the commercial cloud average.

For a growth investor, the government pipeline is a durable, underappreciated growth driver that provides downside protection: even if commercial cloud growth decelerates, the $50B federal pipeline commitment creates a minimum revenue floor for 5–8 years.

5B.3 Lobbying as a Growth Investment

Amazon's lobbying spend of $19.1M (2024) and $18.9M (2025 per available data) — approximately 0.003% of revenue — is near-negligible relative to the scale of policy outcomes it influences. The ROI on lobbying is extraordinary:

  • The One Big Beautiful Bill's accelerated depreciation alone generates $1.4B+ annually in cash tax savings — a return of approximately 73x the annual lobbying budget in a single policy win.
  • The GSA OneGov agreement opens $1B in government cloud spending to Amazon at a facilitated cost of effectively zero relative to lobbying spend.
  • Maintained access to Jassy-Bessent communication channels provides a direct escalation path for policy concerns (as demonstrated by the Anthropic intervention).

Amazon's in-house lobbying operation (32 registered in-house lobbyists) augmented by specialist external firms (Akin Gump for antitrust, Holly Strategies for defense, Federal Street Strategies for privacy) gives it policy coverage across all material regulatory risk areas. For growth investors, lobbying is not an ethical question but a strategic capital allocation decision: Amazon is spending $19M/year to protect and expand markets worth hundreds of billions. This is high-return capital deployment.

SECTION 7 — KEY TALKING POINTS

  1. The AWS operating income trajectory is the single most important number in Amazon's story. AWS segment OI went from $22.8B (FY2022) to $45.6B (FY2025) — it doubled in 3 years while maintaining ~35% margins, and Q1 2026 AWS growth of 28% implies further acceleration. For a cloud business of this size to accelerate, not decelerate, is structurally unusual and reflects AI demand supply-constrained by infrastructure availability rather than hitting market limits.

  2. $128.3B capex is the right size bet for the opportunity. AWS management has explicitly stated that AI demand currently exceeds available cloud supply. The capex cycle is therefore demand-pull: Amazon is building what customers are already trying to buy. Precedent from the 2015–2020 AWS investment cycle — where heavy data centre capex ultimately yielded 30%+ segment margins — supports the framework that infrastructure investment at scale creates durable margin expansion.

  3. The advertising segment is a hidden compounder. Growing high-teens on an estimated $55–65B revenue base at ~80%+ estimated incremental margins, advertising has become Amazon's second value engine and is rarely discussed with the same depth as AWS. Prime Video ad inventory launch in 2024 adds a structurally new advertising surface with premium CPMs.

  4. Operating margin recovery from 2.4% (FY2022) to 11.2% (FY2025) is one of the most dramatic in large-cap history. This recovery was not achieved through financial engineering — it was driven by AWS mix shift and advertising growth. Q1 2026 implies annualised operating margins above 13%, suggesting the expansion is not finished.

  5. The political setup is genuinely additive to the thesis. The One Big Beautiful Bill's accelerated depreciation generates $1.4B+ annually in cash tax savings with zero operational change. The $50B federal AI infrastructure pledge creates a dedicated, sticky government revenue channel for 5–8 years. These are not soft benefits — they are quantifiable, durable additions to the FCF trajectory.

  6. Management FCF recovery is the trigger to watch. Management FCF of $11.2B in FY2025 (vs. $38.2B in FY2024) is the most cited bear point. But this is the floor: as capex growth moderates from FY2027 onward and operating income continues expanding, management FCF should recover to $50–80B+ range. The stock's re-rating will likely be anchored to when this recovery becomes visible in reported results.

  7. Net cash position and clean balance sheet remove the traditional growth-stock blowup risk. Amazon has net cash of $21.3B and interest coverage of 25.1x. Even in a scenario of sustained capex and operating stress, Amazon cannot be forced into a liquidity crisis. This eliminates a common growth-stock failure mode and allows management to invest through cycles without financial constraint.

  8. PEG of ~1.0x on trailing earnings is not demanding for this franchise. At $277.77 and trailing P/E 22.3x, Amazon's PEG on a 22% historical EPS CAGR is approximately 1.0x — in line with or below long-run PEG for high-quality compounders with wide moats. The stock is not cheap by any absolute measure, but relative to demonstrated growth and the forward trajectory, the valuation does not require heroic assumptions to justify.

SECTION 8 — BEAR / BASE / BULL CASES (GROWTH INVESTOR'S LENS)

8.1 Bear Case

Core assumptions: AWS margin pressure from competitive intensity (Azure/GCP gaining share, potential commoditisation of AI infrastructure) AND the retail investment cycle extends without operational leverage materialising. CapEx cycle ($128B FY2025) fails to yield adequate returns by FY2028; management FCF remains near zero or turns negative for 2+ years. Consumer spending softens, compressing retail revenue and marketplace take-rates. FTC antitrust trial results in structural marketplace remedy, reducing take-rate by 100–200bp.

Financial outcome (per Financial Brief §12.2): Revenue CAGR FY2025–FY2028 of 8% → FY2028E revenue ~$902B. EBIT margin 8.5%; management FCF margin ~10% → FY2028E management FCF ~$90B (vs. near-zero now, reflecting modest capex normalisation even in bear case). EPS FY2028E ~$5.50.

Valuation: In a bear case where FCF recovery disappoints and AWS decelerates, market assigns 12–15x P/E on depressed EPS → implied price $66–83. This represents a −70% to −76% loss from current price of $277.77. Both operating failure AND multiple compression occur simultaneously (consistent with Pipeline-Conventions §13 bear definition).

Probability: 25% (per Financial Brief). This is a tail risk but not an extreme one — it requires adverse outcomes across multiple segments simultaneously.

8.2 Base Case

Core assumptions: AWS maintains 17–20% growth with expanding margins (approaching 38–40% segment margin by FY2028); advertising continues high-teens growth and becomes a $90–100B revenue segment; retail generates modest operational leverage from automation. CapEx returns materialise on a 3–4 year lag. FTC trial results in behavioural remedies (price disclosure rules, data access) rather than structural break-up.

Financial outcome (per Financial Brief §12.2): Revenue CAGR 13% → FY2028E revenue ~$1,031B. EBIT margin 13.5%; management FCF margin ~16% → FY2028E management FCF ~$165B, a substantial recovery. EPS FY2028E ~$12.50 (approximately flat to trailing EPS because share count grows modestly and near-term EPS growth is absorbed by capex-induced depreciation).

Valuation: At base EPS $12.50 and 22–25x P/E (appropriate for a company growing 10–13% with strong FCF conversion) → implied price $275–313. Total return approximately −1% to +13% over 3 years from $277.77. Annualised: −0.3% to +4.1% — modest, below cost of equity.

What makes this a "Buy" despite modest base-case return: The base case is not the investment thesis. The investment thesis is the bull case at 25% probability providing 25–31% annualised returns, combined with the asymmetric payoff structure where 50% base + 25% bull significantly outweighs 25% bear.

8.3 Bull Case

Core assumptions: AWS accelerates to 25–30% driven by AI-driven demand surge — Trainium/Graviton custom silicon captures AI training market share from commodity GPU providers; Bedrock positions Amazon as the enterprise AI platform of record. Advertising becomes the third leg equal to AWS ($100B+ revenue, ~80% EBIT margins). Leo (Project Kuiper) satellite revenue begins materialising in consumer and enterprise markets from FY2027. FTC trial dismissed or results in immaterial behavioural remedy.

Financial outcome (per Financial Brief §12.2): Revenue CAGR 18% → FY2028E revenue ~$1,177B. EBIT margin 16.5%; management FCF margin ~20% → FY2028E management FCF ~$235B. EPS FY2028E ~$18.00 as operating leverage amplifies revenue growth into EPS.

Valuation: At bull EPS $18.00 and 30–35x P/E (justified by AWS-comparable growth rate with widening margins) → implied price $540–630. Total return +95% to +127% from $277.77. Annualised: +25% to +31% over 3 years — substantially above cost of equity.

What drives bull case re-rating to 30–35x: If AWS sustains 25%+ growth through FY2028 and advertising crosses $100B, Amazon's blended multiple should converge toward cloud-sector peers (Microsoft, Google) trading at 28–35x, rather than a retail/conglomerate discount.
