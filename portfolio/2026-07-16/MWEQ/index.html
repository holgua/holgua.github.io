About World Equal Weight

The Invesco MSCI World Equal Weight UCITS fund tracks the MSCI World Equal Weighted Index, a version of the standard global developed-market index in which every constituent receives roughly the same share of the basket — around 0.07% each — at the time of each quarterly rebalance. The fund holds approximately 1,500 companies spanning North America, Europe, Japan, and the rest of the developed world, rebalancing four times a year to restore equal weights as prices drift. The fund is domiciled in Ireland, structured as an accumulating share class (returns are reinvested rather than paid out), and uses optimised sampling replication rather than holding every index constituent individually.

The purpose of equal weighting is diversification by design. A standard global index weights companies by market capitalisation, which means the largest companies — a handful of US technology giants — can account for 20–30% of the total basket. Equal weighting reverses that: a Japanese industrial conglomerate with a mid-sized market capitalisation gets the same slice as a US mega-cap. That tilts the basket toward smaller, internationally diversified, and typically lower-priced companies relative to their earnings — characteristics that should, in principle, produce a higher earnings yield than the cap-weighted alternative, even if the underlying businesses are on average somewhat less profitable.

Annual cost: 0.20% ongoing charge (from the Invesco factsheet; yfinance does not return this figure for the Xetra listing).

The Case for the Bet

The bet is structural: by distributing weight evenly across the MSCI World universe rather than concentrating it in the largest companies, the basket trades at a structurally higher earnings yield than a cap-weighted global fund, providing a better long-run entry point for broadly diversified equity exposure. The bet holds as long as the following six conditions continue to apply:

  1. Equity risk premium remains positive. The basket must earn more than the 10-year government bond yield. The exit trigger fires if this premium is negative for two consecutive quarters — not merely thin.

Latest reading: 2026-07-12. Basket earnings yield 4.87% minus 10-year US Treasury 4.57% gives a premium of 0.30 percentage points. Positive, but only just: this is the thinnest reading in the nine-snapshot history. The two-quarter persistence leg of the exit condition has not been met.

  1. Annual expense ratio remains at or below 0.30%. Equal-weight funds carry higher running costs than cap-weighted peers because the quarterly rebalance involves trading. The thesis allows for a slightly higher charge than a cap-weighted equivalent, but 0.30% is the ceiling above which the cost drag erodes the earnings-yield advantage.

Latest reading: 2026-07-12. The annual charge from the Invesco factsheet is 0.20%, 10 basis points below the 0.30% ceiling. The automated data feed does not return this figure; monitoring is manual.

  1. Assets under management stay above USD 500 million. The fund is approximately two years old. UCITS funds below roughly €300–500 million face elevated risk of closure — Invesco has shut sub-scale products before. The floor is a vehicle-survival signal, not a valuation signal.

Latest reading: 2026-07-12. Assets under management approximately USD 1.64 billion, 3.28 times the USD 500 million floor. No closure-risk flag.

  1. No single holding exceeds 1.0% of the basket. Equal weighting is the product: by design, every constituent starts at roughly 0.07% at each rebalance. If any name drifts above 1.0% — roughly 14 times its target weight — something has gone wrong, whether a methodology change, a rebalance failure, or an extreme price move that the sampling strategy has not corrected.

Latest reading: 2026-07-12. The largest holding is Astera Labs at 0.15%, well inside the 1.0% ceiling. Normal intra-quarter drift.

What Could Still Go Wrong

  • Rate rise erases the premium. If the 10-year US government bond yield rises by another 30 basis points without a corresponding rise in basket earnings, the equity risk premium goes negative. Two consecutive quarters of a negative premium trigger exit condition (1).
  • Fund closure below the assets-under-management floor. The fund is approximately two years old. A sustained drawdown in assets to below USD 500 million — whether through market decline or investor redemptions — triggers the vehicle-survival exit condition (3). Invesco has closed sub-scale UCITS products before.
  • Expense ratio creep. If Invesco raises the annual charge above 0.30%, the cost advantage of holding equal-weight rather than a cheaper cap-weighted alternative disappears, triggering exit condition (2). The current charge of 0.20% has 50% headroom, but the guardrail is monitored manually.
  • Concentration drift. The equal-weight mandate is enforced quarterly. Between rebalances, any name that surges well beyond its target weight distorts the basket's equal-weight character. If a holding exceeds 1.0% — approximately 14 times its target weight — exit condition (4) fires.
  • Index methodology change. MSCI controls the index rules. A shift to factor-tilted weights or a change of universe would transform the product being held. This is monitored externally and has no automated signal. (For this fund, exit fires when the share price reaches the exit edge, when a vehicle-integrity condition breaches, or when the named exit-condition trigger fires — any of three paths.)

The Rules

The evaluation produces a verdict for each combination of conditions and share price; the rules below state what each verdict requires.

  • If the thesis is confirmed — all conditions intact, no vehicle breach, basket price within the hold band — hold. The equal-weight basket is reasonably priced, the vehicle is sound, and the earnings-yield premium over a cap-weighted global fund remains intact.
  • If the thesis weakens — any condition deteriorating but not breached — do not add. Re-evaluate after the next quarter. If the equal-weight earnings-yield premium over a cap-weighted global alternative compresses below 0.5 percentage points for two consecutive quarters, the diversification benefit no longer justifies the higher annual charge; consolidate into a cap-weighted alternative.
  • If the thesis breaks — a vehicle-integrity condition breaches, or the value test fails — exit. Move the proceeds to a cap-weighted global fund or to a risk-free instrument.
  • If the share price falls to or below the buy edge of €4.6 — add to the position. At that price the basket earnings yield makes the case decisively; the structural premium over cap-weight global exposure should make this level rare and the buy response decisive.
  • If the share price rises to or above the trim edge of €7.2 — trim 25–33%. The basket is meaningfully expensive relative to the risk-free rate; harvest the premium and redeploy on the next rebalance.
  • If the share price rises to or above the exit edge of €9.6 — exit. At that level the basket earnings yield equals or falls below 3.0%, fully priced versus the risk-free rate. Redeploy the proceeds to a risk-free instrument or to a holding that meets the value test.

Scenario Definitions

A scenario is a joint assumption about two independent variables: operating trajectory (how the holding's underlying cash flows grow relative to their long-run trend) and exit multiple (what the market pays for those cash flows at the horizon, relative to a fair-value anchor).

Scenario Operating trajectory Exit multiple
Bear Below trend Below fair value
Base At trend At fair value
Bull Above trend At or above fair value

Non-negotiable rule: In the bear case, both dimensions must be adverse simultaneously — a scenario where only the multiple moves is a re-rating scenario, not a bear.

Bear cases are comparable in structure across all holding types (company, ETF, bond), but not in magnitude. A company bear reflects idiosyncratic execution failure; an ETF bear reflects a broad market drawdown. Different mitigations, different diversification implications.
