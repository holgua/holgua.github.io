Amazon: The Bet Still Works

The Bottom Line

Data as of 2026-07-06 · Latest filing 2026-04-30.

The bet is working. Amazon's cloud division — its outsized profit engine — grew sales by 28% in the three months ending in March 2026, well above the 18% line the thesis requires, and its operating margin held at a healthy 37.7%. The share price sits in the upper half of the hold band, well above fair value. The one condition to watch is the extraordinary pace of infrastructure spending — roughly $147 billion over the past twelve months — which must eventually convert to proportionally higher cloud revenue if the investment thesis is to hold all the way through 2027 and 2028.

Recent Development

Amazon's cloud division turned in its strongest quarterly growth in at least six quarters in the three months ending in March 2026, with sales up 28% compared to the same period a year earlier. The operating margin for that division came in at 37.7% — down slightly from 39.4% a year before, as the company accelerated infrastructure spending to meet demand — but well clear of the 28% floor that would trigger a re-examination of the thesis. The advertising business, Amazon's third compounding engine, grew 24% over the same period, continuing to prove out the thesis that monetising the transaction-intent data flowing through the platform is still far from its ceiling.

The shadow hanging over the latest filing is the scale of the infrastructure investment. Over the twelve months ending in March 2026, Amazon spent roughly $147 billion on cloud data centres and related infrastructure, nearly double the $88 billion of a year earlier. Amazon issued $37 billion in new dollar debt and over €14 billion in euro debt in a single month to fund part of that programme. The company has committed $364 billion of cloud services to future customers under long-term contracts, giving visibility over the revenue that must eventually materialise to justify the outlay; so far the evidence runs in Amazon's favour. Whether the conversion continues through 2027 and 2028 is the question the thesis is watching, and it connects directly to the share price.

The antitrust picture has not materially changed. The United States Federal Trade Commission's monopoly case against Amazon continues to be referenced in the company's risk-factor disclosures without any new trial timeline appearing in this filing. No structural remedy has been imposed.

About Amazon

Amazon is three businesses under one roof, each at a different stage of maturity. The oldest is the North America retail and logistics platform — online shop, warehouses, fulfilment, delivery — which generated $426 billion in sales in the full year 2025 at a 6.9% operating profit margin (the share of every sales dollar that becomes operating profit). It is the business most people can picture: buy something, it arrives quickly, returns are easy. The scale of the logistics infrastructure — 685 million square feet of fulfilment and data-centre space, $103 billion in annual shipping costs — is what makes the economics work; no single competitor has been able to replicate it.

The second business is Amazon Web Services, the cloud-computing infrastructure arm. It rents computing power, storage, and software services to companies, governments, and developers who prefer not to run their own data centres. In 2025 it produced $129 billion in sales and $46 billion in operating profit — 57% of Amazon's entire consolidated operating profit on only 18% of its revenue. It is the engine the overall valuation is really pricing.

The third business is advertising: Amazon sells space on its website and app to companies that want to reach shoppers with established purchase intent. In 2025 the advertising business generated $69 billion in revenue and is growing faster than either of the other two. Because the costs of running the advertising platform are largely shared with the retail operation, the incremental profit margin on each additional advertising dollar is very high.

The Case for the Bet

The central argument is that Amazon runs the most defensively positioned flywheel in large-cap technology — a retail business that generates cash and customer data, which funds a cloud business and an advertising business whose unit economics are not yet fully mature. The bet holds as long as the following conditions are met.

  1. The cloud division keeps growing above 18% a year. Below 15% for two consecutive quarters signals that the massive infrastructure investment has not yet been absorbed by paying customers.

Latest reading: Sales grew 28.4% in the three months ending in March 2026 — $37.6 billion versus $29.3 billion a year earlier. The $364 billion in contracted but unrecognised cloud revenue provides multi-year visibility. Both readings confirm the condition comfortably.

  1. The cloud division's operating margin stays above 28%. A drop below that level for two consecutive quarters without a clearly identified one-off charge would signal that the economics of the investment have broken down.

Latest reading: The margin was 37.7% in the three months ending in March 2026 — down from 39.4% the prior year but 970 percentage points (ppts) above the exit threshold. The compression is attributed to accelerated infrastructure investment rather than pricing pressure or structural deterioration.

  1. The advertising business keeps growing. A slowdown to single-digit growth for two consecutive full years would signal that the third compounding engine has saturated.

Latest reading: Advertising services grew 24% in the three months ending in March 2026 and are cited as a primary contributor to operating profit improvement in both the North America and International segments.

  1. The International segment continues to improve. The segment only turned profitable in 2024; its continued expansion adds a third source of operating profit growth.

Latest reading: The International segment produced $1.4 billion in operating profit in the three months ending in March 2026, up from $1.0 billion the prior year. The operating margin widened from 3.0% to 3.6%. Currency movements added a $347 million tailwind.

  1. No court-ordered structural break-up. A remedy in the Federal Trade Commission monopoly case requiring business separation would be an immediate trigger to exit the position.

Latest reading: The case is referenced in the risk factors as ongoing litigation; no structural remedy has been imposed and no new trial timeline appears in the latest filing.

  1. Capital spending normalises by 2027–28. The thesis allows for today's elevated infrastructure investment as long as the cloud division keeps growing above 18%. Exit fires if capital expenditure remains above $120 billion in both 2027 and 2028 without matching acceleration in cloud revenues.

Latest reading: Capital expenditure reached approximately $147 billion over the twelve months ending in March 2026. The exit condition targets 2027 and 2028; with the cloud division growing at 28% and $364 billion of contracted backlog, the "without commensurate acceleration" clause in the condition is not currently met.

The Share Price

The valuation method used here is the owner earnings yield — a measure of what the business generates in cash after maintaining and expanding its operations, expressed as a percentage of the total market value of the company. A higher yield means the buyer of the whole business is getting more cash for each pound paid; a lower yield means the market is pricing in substantial future growth.

At today's share price of $250, the owner earnings yield is approximately 4.2%. The rule governing the hold-band sets a fair-value yield of 6.0% — equivalent to a share price of roughly $176 — with a buy signal activated above 7.5% (equivalent to approximately $140) and a trim signal activated below 4.0% (equivalent to approximately $263).

At 4.2%, the share price is in the upper half of the hold band — inside the rules' boundaries but close to the trim edge, and sitting well above the $176 fair-value anchor. Put plainly: the share is not expensive enough to trim by the rules, but it is not cheap by any conventional measure. The gap between today's $250 and the $176 fair-value anchor is 42%, a meaningful premium.

The Standing Orders

The rules translate the share-price bands into three standing orders and two price alerts. Each is the operationalisation of a pre-committed rule — not a new judgement made by this evaluation.

Price alerts (informational).
- A first alert at $176, the fair-value anchor. A crossing means the share price has returned to a neutral reading; no action follows automatically, but a fresh read of the conditions is warranted.
- A second alert at $140, the cheap edge. The crossing arms the buy order below; before adding, the latest re-validation should confirm the conditions are still working and the cloud division's growth remains at or above 18%.

Standing orders (actionable).
- A standing buy order at $140. The rules call for adding to the position in tranches once the share price closes at or below this line and the cloud-division growth condition is intact. The order may be staged across one or two quarters rather than filled in a single transaction.
- A standing trim order at $263. The rules call for trimming 25–33% of the position when the share price closes at or above this line, to harvest the premium while keeping the core holding. The remaining position is re-evaluated after the trim.
- A standing exit order at $421. The rules call for closing the full position when the share price closes at or above this line. At that price the market is pricing in roughly 11 years of base-case compounding instantly; the implied forward return falls below what long-term equity ownership warrants. Proceeds are to be redeployed at the next cheap-edge crossing or into a cheaper alternative, with no re-entry within 90 days to avoid anchoring to the original cost.

The three order levels do not contradict the cost-basis verdicts in the re-validation: those verdicts describe the current reading; the standing orders describe what the rules will execute if the share price moves to each line.

What's Wobbling

Infrastructure spending. The scale of capital investment is the dominant wobble. Roughly $147 billion was spent on cloud infrastructure over the twelve months ending in March 2026, against $88 billion the prior year. The financing included over $50 billion in new bond issuance in a single month. This level of spending does not yet trip any written exit condition — the exit condition requires high spending in 2027 and 2028 without matching cloud revenue growth, and cloud revenue is currently growing at 28%. But the trajectory means that any deceleration in cloud revenue growth in the next four to six quarters would simultaneously push the exit condition closer to firing and raise legitimate questions about the return on invested capital. This is the highest-priority item to monitor.

Tariff exposure. The latest official filing names China-based sellers as significant contributors to third-party marketplace and advertising revenues and flags trade-policy changes as a source of uncertainty. This is a real exposure: the removal of duty-free treatment for small packages from overseas sellers, or material tariff increases on the goods those sellers carry, would weigh on both marketplace volumes and advertising spending. Despite this, North America operating profit grew 41% in the three months ending in March 2026, suggesting the business has managed the exposure so far. No written exit condition maps directly to tariff drag unless advertising growth falls to single digits for two consecutive full years.

No share buybacks. Amazon has not repurchased a single share since the current authorisation was established in March 2022, despite the share price spending much of 2022 and 2023 at levels that, in retrospect, offered substantial value. The $6.1 billion remaining under that authorisation sits untouched while the company issues tens of billions in new debt to fund data centres. The thesis acknowledges that Amazon's capital allocation is "acceptable but not excellent" and does not treat the absence of buybacks as an exit condition. It is, however, a signal worth noting.

What Could Still Go Wrong

  • The cloud division's 28% growth rate depends heavily on enterprise demand for artificial-intelligence computing. If AI workload adoption by large customers slows materially, or if competing cloud providers narrow the performance gap, the $147 billion annual infrastructure investment could prove to have overshot demand — resulting in excess capacity, impairment charges, and a collapse in free cash flow that would trip exit condition 1 or 4.
  • The Federal Trade Commission monopoly case, due for trial in late 2026, carries the risk of a structural remedy requiring business separation. An order to divest the cloud division, the advertising platform, or the marketplace would be an immediate exit trigger under the thesis — and is a binary event with no early warning.
  • Amazon's reported profit for 2025 includes approximately $15 billion of non-cash Anthropic-related other income — comprising a roughly $7.7 billion upward mark-to-market adjustment on its equity stake in Anthropic and a roughly $5.6 billion reclassification of converted convertible notes — an artificial-intelligence laboratory. That mark-to-market gain is non-cash and subject to reversal; if Anthropic's funding environment deteriorates, the reversal would materially reduce reported earnings without affecting operations — a potential source of headline volatility that could damage sentiment without changing the underlying business trajectory.
  • The advertising business, growing at 24%, is increasingly dependent on China-based sellers for both inventory and advertising spend. A material disruption to cross-border commerce — from tariff escalation, de-minimis rule changes, or geopolitical pressure — could hit this growth engine at the same time as the retail marketplace, creating correlated rather than independent headwinds.
  • Amazon's share of new artificial-intelligence workloads is growing more slowly than those of its two largest cloud competitors. If that trend continues and accelerates, Amazon's cloud division could sustain headline revenue growth while losing ground in the fastest-growing segment of the market, eventually capping the bull case and compressing the fair-value ceiling.

Gaps in the Picture

Two fair-value anchors. The owner earnings yield method used in the standing orders produces a fair-value estimate of $176 per share. A separate scenario analysis using market multiples produces a base-case central estimate of $532. The two numbers reflect fundamentally different methodologies: the yield method asks "what yield does the current cash generation imply, and what price corresponds to a normal yield?"; the multiples method asks "at what price would the market typically value a business with Amazon's earnings, cash flow, and peer comparisons?" Both are legitimate; neither is authoritative. The gap is unusually wide — the multiples estimate is three times the yield estimate — partly because Amazon's owner earnings are currently depressed by the scale of the infrastructure investment relative to reported operating cash flow. A future schema revision will resolve which anchor governs the standing orders.

Maintenance capital spending. The owner earnings calculation does not separate maintenance from growth spending. Amazon spends a very large proportion of its capital budget on expanding cloud capacity — spending that is growth-oriented, not maintenance. The yield calculation uses the full capital expenditure figure, which produces a conservative (lower) owner earnings estimate. If only maintenance capital were deducted, the owner earnings yield would be substantially higher and the standing-order prices would be proportionally higher as well. Readers should treat the $176 fair-value anchor as a conservative floor rather than a precision estimate.

Equity risk-free rate. The hurdle rate uses the risk-free rate sourced from a United States market proxy rather than from Amazon's own market-data snapshot, because that field is not populated for individual company holdings. The number is treated as approximate.

The Rules

The rules that govern action are set in advance, when there is no live market signal creating pressure to decide quickly. They are stated here as conditions, not recommendations.

  • If all thesis conditions are working and the share price is within the hold band: hold the position. Continue compounding the cloud and advertising flywheel.
  • If one or more conditions are deteriorating but no exit line has been crossed: do not add to the position. Watch the specific deteriorating driver across the next two quarterly filings before making a change — one quarter of data is not sufficient; two consecutive quarters are required to confirm a trend.
  • If any written exit condition has been triggered: exit the full position within 30 days. No averaging down. The cloud revenue and margin exit conditions specifically require confirmation across two consecutive quarters before action is taken; an antitrust structural remedy or an unexpected auditor replacement is an immediate trigger with no waiting period.
  • If the share price falls to or below $140 and the thesis conditions are working: add to the position in tranches over one to two quarters. Confirm the cloud division is growing at or above 18% before acting.
  • If the share price rises to or above $263: trim 25–33% of the position to harvest the premium. Re-evaluate the remaining position size after the trim.
  • If the share price rises to or above $421: exit the full position. At that price, roughly 11 years of base-case compounding is priced in immediately; redeploy proceeds at the next cheap-edge crossing or into a cheaper alternative. No re-entry within 90 days.
