About Dynatrace

Dynatrace sells software that watches other software. Its AI-powered observability platform — a continuous monitoring system — tells enterprise technology teams why their applications are running slowly, failing, or consuming unexpected cloud resources. The typical customer runs dozens of applications across several cloud environments; a single performance problem can cascade into revenue loss within minutes.

The business model is subscription: enterprises pay an annual fee, and as they deploy more modules — performance monitoring, log management, security scanning, AI workload governance — their spending grows without any new sales cycle. This drives the 110% net retention rate: the average existing customer spends more each year not because prices rise but because scope expands. Revenue of $2 billion in the fiscal year ended March 2026 was almost entirely from these recurring subscriptions.

The company is headquartered in Waltham, Massachusetts, with its engineering centre in Linz, Austria. It listed on the New York Stock Exchange in 2019; its private equity sponsor fully exited in November 2024.

The Case for the Bet

The core of the bet: Dynatrace has built an enterprise monitoring platform that is difficult to replace, operates in a market that grows as cloud complexity grows, and generates a substantial and growing stream of cash — yet the share price implies only modest growth ahead, well below what the business has already been delivering. The conditions that must keep holding are:

  1. Net retention stays at or above 107%. The watch trigger fires at one quarter below this line; exit fires if the rate stays below 107% for two consecutive quarters.
  2. Annual recurring revenue growth, in constant currency, stays above 13% per year for each rolling pair of quarters.
  3. The cash margin stays above 20% for any full fiscal year (currently 26.2%).
  4. No senior executive makes open-market share sales worth more than $5 million within any 90-day window.
  5. The Dynatrace Platform Subscription adoption does not stall below 72% of annual recurring revenue for three consecutive quarters, especially if management stops disclosing the metric.
  6. Neither Cisco/Splunk nor Palo Alto/Chronosphere announces named Dynatrace enterprise customer wins, confirming bundled-suite displacement is occurring.
  7. Net cash stays above $600 million without a disclosed acquisition rationale (currently $1.1 billion).

The Rules

  • If the thesis remains confirmed: maintain the full position, monitor quarterly metrics, consider adding modestly on dips below $38 while net retention holds at or above 107%.
  • If the thesis shows signs of weakening (net retention toward 107%, or revenue growth toward 14%): reduce to a half position within 30 days, then watch for two quarters before deciding to exit or rebuild.
  • If the thesis is confirmed broken: exit fully within one quarter. No averaging down on a broken thesis.
  • If the share price falls below the buy edge ($31): accumulate toward a full position. At that price the cash yield rises above 5.9%, a sufficient margin of safety.
  • If the share price rises above the trim edge ($55): trim toward a half-to-two-thirds position. At that level the shares approach the base-case fair value and the remaining upside requires the bull case to materialise.

The Conditions Today

Net retention — clear. The dollar-based net retention rate for the fiscal year ended March 2026 was 110% on a constant-currency basis, unchanged from the prior year and the third consecutive year at this level. The two-stage exit threshold is not remotely triggered.

Revenue growth — clear. Annual recurring revenue reached $2.054 billion at March 2026, 18% higher than a year earlier — well above the 13% exit threshold on any plausible currency adjustment.

Cash generation — clear. The company generated $529 million in free cash in fiscal 2026, a 26.2% cash margin and 23% higher than the prior year. The 20% exit floor is not in prospect.

Platform subscription adoption — silent. The annual report does not disclose adoption as a percentage of annual recurring revenue. The most recent figure is 70%, from quarterly earnings commentary in late 2025. Watch for disclosure at the next quarterly release.

Insider conviction — clear. No senior executive made open-market equity sales in the twelve months covered by the filing. The company repurchased $479 million of its own shares and launched a new $1 billion buyback authorisation with $849 million remaining as of March 2026.

AI commoditisation risk — watch. Palo Alto Networks' acquisition of Chronosphere in 2026 is confirmed in the annual filing. Exit condition 6 requires named customer displacement, not merely an acquisition — but the combination is now in the field, and the next quarterly release is the first test.

Net cash — clear. As of March 2026, the company held $1.097 billion in cash and short-term securities with zero debt drawn on its credit facility. Exit condition 7 — a fall below $600 million without a disclosed acquisition rationale — is not remotely triggered.

Gross margin — mixed but not concerning. Subscription gross margin compressed by one percentage point to 85%, driven by higher cloud hosting costs from increased customer usage. Overall gross margin expanded to 82%. No exit condition references gross margin, and the 26.2% cash margin provides a large buffer above the 20% floor.

What's Wobbling

The Palo Alto/Chronosphere acquisition. Exit condition 6 requires named customer displacement, not just a competitor acquisition. Chronosphere had a modest footprint and the integration will take time — but the combination is in the field, and the next quarterly earnings release is the first real test.

Subscription gross margin compression. Cloud hosting costs grew faster than revenue in fiscal 2026, compressing the subscription gross margin by one percentage point. Sustained further compression would eventually pressure the cash margin — but at 26.2%, there is meaningful room before the 20% exit floor.

What Could Still Go Wrong

  • Datadog closes the platform gap. Datadog's resources are more than six times larger. If it achieves comparable auto-instrumentation, the basis for Dynatrace's pricing premium begins to erode.
  • Open-source observability becomes good enough. The OpenTelemetry standard is now widely adopted; startups are building similar capabilities at lower price points. Budget-constrained enterprise teams may accept a lower-capability tool.
  • Hyperscaler bundling accelerates. Amazon's CloudWatch and Microsoft's Azure Monitor are included at no marginal cost with cloud contracts, making the zero-marginal-cost alternative increasingly attractive for less complex monitoring needs.
  • AI commoditises the monitoring function itself. The company acknowledges in its annual filing that general-purpose large language models and AI-generated code could substitute for specialised monitoring tools, narrowing the addressable market to governance use cases.
