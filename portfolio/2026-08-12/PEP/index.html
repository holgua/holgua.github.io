The Bottom Line

Data as of 2026-08-12 · Latest filing 2026-07-09.

The bet is wobbling. PepsiCo's North American beverage business, PepsiCo Beverages North America (PBNA), has now posted negative unit volume in at least nine consecutive quarterly reporting periods, and the second quarter of 2026 brought no reversal: volume fell 4% versus the prior year. At $139, the current price sits in the middle of the hold band, at fair value by the yield measure. No exit condition has fired — the gross margin and dividend streak remain intact — but the core volume recovery that the base case depends on has not yet arrived.

Recent Development

The latest quarterly report, covering the 24 weeks ending 2026-06-13, confirmed what has become a persistent pattern: the snack business is holding up, the beverage business is not. Consolidated organic revenue grew 2.5% in the first half of 2026 — below the 3.8% base-case target — with international segments (International Beverage Franchise +8%, EMEA +6%, Latin America Foods +4%) carrying the load while the North American beverage segment, PBNA, continued to drag. PBNA unit volume fell 3% across the half-year and 4% in the second quarter, with both carbonated and non-carbonated categories declining. The commodity cost headwind ran at 6 percentage points in the quarter, compressing segment margins even as the rest of the business recovered.

The counterweight is meaningful. Gross margin across the consolidated business held at 54.7% in the first half and 54.2% in the second quarter — both within the thesis band of 53–55%, confirming that brand pricing power is still absorbing tariff and commodity pressure at the gross level. Operating cash flow recovered sharply to $2,365 million in the first half, compared to $996 million in the same period of 2025. The dividend was raised 4% to $5.92 annualised, extending the 54-year consecutive growth streak. In other words: the moat is intact where it can be measured directly; the volume recovery is the one thing that has not yet happened.

The practical implication for the Share Price is that valuation sits essentially at the fair-value yield of 4.7%, with no margin of safety on the buy side and no excess to trigger a trim.

About PepsiCo

PepsiCo makes its money in two distinct businesses that happen to share a corporate roof. The first is snack foods — Lay's potato chips, Doritos tortilla chips, Cheetos, Quaker oatmeal, and a further roster of branded convenience foods sold across more than 200 countries. The second is beverages — the Pepsi-Cola carbonated drink family, Gatorade sports drinks, and Mountain Dew, distributed primarily through a licensed bottling network and, in North America, through PepsiCo's own direct-delivery infrastructure. This proprietary delivery network — a fleet of trucks and sales routes that replenish retailer shelves directly, bypassing warehouse distribution — is the structural nerve of the North American business: it gives PepsiCo proprietary shelf placement, promotional control, and merchandising presence that smaller rivals cannot match without replicating decades of capital investment.

The company operates six reportable segments, roughly divided between North American snacks (PepsiCo Foods North America, or PFNA), North American beverages (PBNA), and four international divisions. Roughly 44% of revenue comes from outside the United States. Revenue in the fiscal year ending December 2025 was $93.9 billion. The 3-year average annual profit available to the business after maintenance costs — the figure used to judge whether the current price is fair — is approximately $8.96 billion.

The Case for the Bet

PepsiCo is held because it combines a wide economic moat with durable cash generation across economic cycles, and the combination gives a long-run owner confidence that the earnings will persist long enough to make the wait worthwhile. The bet requires five specific conditions to keep holding:

  1. Gross margin staying above 52% for any two consecutive fiscal quarters. At the gross level — revenue minus the cost of goods sold — brand pricing power either absorbs commodity and tariff cost pressure or it does not. A gross margin corridor of 53–55% has held for five years through commodity shocks; a sustained fall below 52% for two consecutive quarters would signal that the pricing premium is eroding structurally, not cyclically.

Latest reading: 54.7% (first half of 2026) and 54.2% (second quarter of 2026). Both readings are within the thesis band and well above the 52% floor. Brand pricing power is absorbing the current commodity headwind at the gross level.

  1. Operating margin (as reported under US accounting rules) staying above 11.5% for any two consecutive fiscal quarters, in conjunction with PBNA volume remaining negative. This is the compound exit trigger — it requires both legs to be adverse simultaneously. A single leg alone (volume declining but margin sound, or margin compressed but volume recovering) does not trigger an exit.

Latest reading: 16.6% for both the second quarter and first half of 2026 — well above the 11.5% floor. The margin leg of exit condition (1) is not even approaching the threshold. The volume leg is adverse (PBNA volume −4% in the quarter), but the compound condition requires both to be satisfied, and only one is.

  1. The 54-year consecutive annual dividend growth streak remaining intact. Every year since 1972, PepsiCo has paid a higher annual dividend than the year before. Management's willingness to continue that record is, in effect, their own ongoing confidence test on the durability of cash flow. A freeze or cut would signal management's own loss of faith in the earnings trajectory.

Latest reading: The dividend was raised 4% to $5.92 annualised in the first half of 2026 — the streak is intact. At the current share price, that represents a forward yield of approximately 4.3%.

  1. Net debt (total borrowings minus cash) staying below 3.0 times annual operating profit before depreciation and amortisation for two consecutive fiscal years. The leverage threshold is annual, not quarterly, because interim balance sheets do not disclose the full earnings denominator. At 2.48 times at the end of 2025 — the highest ratio in three years — the business is approaching a level where the dividend programme and future investment compete for the same cash.

Latest reading: Long-term debt was essentially flat at $42.6 billion as of 2026-06-13, with $6.1 billion in short-term commercial paper outstanding. The first-half free cash flow of $1,170 million (positive, versus negative $342 million in the prior year) is directionally helpful. The annual 3.0 times threshold requires two consecutive fiscal years of breach; on current trajectory, leverage is holding below that line.

  1. No goodwill or intangible impairment charge related to the poppi, Siete, or SodaStream acquisitions exceeding $300 million in any single fiscal year through 2028. The $1.86 billion write-down of the Rockstar Energy brand in 2025 established that PepsiCo's acquisition history contains failures. The three more recent acquisitions closed in 2025 at a combined cost of over $3 billion. If any of them require a similar write-down, it confirms a pattern rather than a single episode.

Latest reading: No impairment charges on the three acquisitions were recorded in the first half of 2026. The contingent consideration for poppi fell in fair value by $161 million, which is a signal to watch — it suggests the revenue and margin milestones tied to a $300 million earnout by the third quarter of 2027 are now considered less probable. That is not yet an impairment; the exit condition requires an actual charge above $300 million.

The Share Price

The owner earnings yield — the ratio of the business's normalised annual profit available to shareholders against the total market value of the company — currently runs at 4.72%. The fair-value anchor is 4.70%. Put plainly, the share price and the fair-value estimate are almost indistinguishable: at $139, the market is pricing PepsiCo within a rounding error of where the analysis says it should be priced.

That means there is no margin of safety from the buy edge ($101, equivalent to a 6.5% yield) and no excess toward the trim edge ($205, equivalent to a 3.2% yield). The position sits in the middle of the hold band. That is not a verdict of either comfort or alarm — it is simply the observation that the current price embeds the base case fairly, leaving no cushion for negative surprises and no windfall from positive ones.

A separate analysis using peer and historical earnings multiples arrives at a base-case fair value closer to $216 per share, well above the current price. Gaps in the Picture explains why the two estimates diverge and which deserves more weight at the moment.

What's Wobbling

The single driver flagged as adversely challenging the thesis is PBNA volume. The North American beverage segment has recorded negative unit volume in at least nine consecutive quarterly reporting periods. In the second quarter of 2026, volume fell 4% year-on-year — 4% in non-carbonated beverages and 3% in carbonated soft drinks. A 6 percentage-point commodity cost headwind in the quarter compressed segment margins further. There is no positive inflection visible in the data.

The important distinction — and the reason no exit condition has fired — is that exit condition (1) in The Case for the Bet requires a compound trigger: PBNA volume negative for three or more consecutive quarters AND the GAAP operating margin below 11.5% for two consecutive quarters simultaneously. The operating margin leg is not in danger: the consolidated operating margin ran at 16.6% in both the second quarter and the first half of 2026. A single-leg wobble keeps the position in the hold band, but it does not confirm recovery either.

Organic revenue growth across the consolidated business — 2.5% in the first half — is running below the 3.8% base-case target for the thesis. International segments are performing well; the shortfall is North America. The poppi brand integration adds reported revenue to PBNA but does not move the organic volume figure, which strips out acquisitions. That gap between reported and organic revenue growth will narrow only when the acquired brands start contributing to genuinely new volume rather than just replacing organic sales.

What Could Still Go Wrong

  • GLP-1 weight-loss medications accelerate the structural decline in North American snack consumption beyond the 3–5% per annum assumed in the bear case, compressing PFNA volumes to the point where pricing power cannot offset the volume shortfall at the gross level.
  • Tariff and commodity cost pressure proves stickier than the base case assumes — the 50% aluminium tariff remains in place and the Supreme Court ruling on IEEPA tariffs (June 2026) is reversed or narrowed on appeal — causing gross margins to compress below 53% even as volumes remain soft.
  • Leverage continues to rise toward 2.7 times operating profit before depreciation and amortisation as lower earnings meet the fixed dividend commitment, eventually constraining the balance sheet to the point where credit-rating pressure forces a choice between the dividend streak and investment.
  • One or more of the 2025 acquisitions (poppi, Siete) requires a goodwill impairment charge above $300 million before 2028, confirming that the pattern of capital misallocation begun with Rockstar has repeated — and directly triggering exit condition (5).
  • The Mars-Kellanova snack entity (formed December 2025 with $36 billion in combined revenue and brands including Pringles, Cheez-It, and Pop-Tarts) deploys its distribution scale against Frito-Lay's position in salty snacks faster than the thesis assumes, eroding the ~60% US market share more rapidly than organic trends alone would predict.

Gaps in the Picture

The owner earnings figure used as the valuation anchor — $8.96 billion — is a manually maintained override. The underlying automated data pipeline cannot extract capital expenditure from PepsiCo's regulatory filings because the relevant accounting tag is absent from the company's digital submission format, making it impossible to compute owner earnings automatically. The override is sourced from the value analysis, but it must be refreshed manually when a new value analysis is run; there is a risk of stale data if the two files fall out of sync.

The fair-value estimates produced by two parallel methods diverge significantly. The yield-based method, using the $8.96 billion owner earnings anchor, produces a fair-value price of approximately $140 per share — essentially the current price. The peer-and-history multiple method produces a base-case estimate of $216 per share, implying a 56% upside. The two figures coexist because they measure different things: the yield method asks what the business is worth if earnings stay flat; the multiple method asks what peers and PepsiCo's own history suggest the market will pay. A future update will align the two into a single framework; until then, both figures are reported and neither is concealed.

The historical multiple bands used in the second method — the peer comparison and self-multiple history — have limited data points for several metrics (the enterprise-value-to-revenue, enterprise-value-to-operating-profit, price-to-book, and price-to-free-cash-flow bands all lack sufficient history), so the base-case estimate of $216 rests primarily on the bands that do have data.   • If the thesis is weakened (one or more drivers challenging the thesis but no exit condition crossed — the current state): do not add to the position.   • If the thesis is broken (one or more exit conditions triggered): exit the full position within 30 days. The triggered exit condition and the exit price should be recorded for future reference. No averaging down.
  • If the share price falls below the buy edge ($101, equivalent to a 6.5% owner earnings yield): confirm no exit condition is triggered and that the operating margin is recovering. If confirmed, add to the position in tranches over one to two quarters, targeting a full position at the buy edge.
  • If the share price rises above the trim edge ($205, equivalent to a 3.2% owner earnings yield): trim 25–33% of the position. The market is pricing in long-run growth well beyond the base case at that level.
  • If the share price rises above the exit edge ($469, equivalent to the maximum exit threshold): exit the full position. The market is pricing in roughly 33 years of base-case compounding immediately. Redeploy proceeds at the next cheap-edge crossing or into a higher-conviction holding. No re-entry within 90 days.

Scenario Definitions

A scenario is a joint assumption about two independent variables: operating trajectory (how the holding's underlying cash flows grow relative to their long-run trend) and exit multiple (what the market pays for those cash flows at the horizon, relative to a fair-value anchor).

Scenario Operating trajectory Exit multiple
Bear Below trend Below fair value
Base At trend At fair value
Bull Above trend At or above fair value

Non-negotiable rule: In the bear case, both dimensions must be adverse simultaneously — a scenario where only the multiple moves is a re-rating scenario, not a bear.

Bear cases are comparable in structure across all holding types (company, ETF, bond), but not in magnitude. A company bear reflects idiosyncratic execution failure; an ETF bear reflects a broad market drawdown. Different mitigations, different diversification implications.
