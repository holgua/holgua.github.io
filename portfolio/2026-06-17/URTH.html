All World: The Bet Is Broken

The Bottom Line

The structural case for holding this fund — that a basket of the world's largest listed companies will earn more for its holders than simply holding government bonds — is no longer supported by the numbers. The fund's price and its underlying asset value have diverged by more than the hard limit allows, triggering an automatic exit signal. That breach may well turn out to be a data artefact rather than a genuine problem, and the fund's underlying businesses remain solid; but until the figure is verified, the rules treat it as real. On top of that, the basket's earnings yield has slipped below the 10-year government bond rate — meaning investors are currently accepting equity risk without any yield premium over risk-free paper.

The share price at $204 sits in fair-value territory — not cheap, not expensive — so the cost of exiting, if the breach is later confirmed to be noise, is modest. The one condition to watch is whether the NAV figure reflects stale data or a genuine disconnect.

About the Fund

All World is the iShares MSCI World ETF (ticker: URTH), a passively managed basket tracking the MSCI World Index — a collection of large and mid-sized companies listed in 23 developed countries. Roughly 70% of the fund sits in US-listed companies; the remaining 30% is spread across Japan, the United Kingdom, France, Switzerland, Germany, Canada, and the other major developed markets. The basket holds around 1,300 positions, weighted by market capitalisation (the larger the company's total stock-market value, the bigger its share of the fund). The fund is structured as a Delaware statutory trust — the standard US exchange-traded fund wrapper — and does not issue a Schedule K-1 tax form.

The annual fee is 0.24% of assets, well below the category average of around 0.72% for comparable passive global blend funds, though modestly above its UCITS-domiciled sibling iShares Core MSCI World (IWDA), which charges 0.20%. Securities lending revenue is not disclosed, so the net annual cost after any offsetting income is unknown. Tracking difference and tracking error against the MSCI World Index are also not available for this snapshot; both would normally appear in the fund's annual factsheet.

Capital gain distribution history is unconfirmed; US ETFs structured as ETF Trusts frequently avoid distributing capital gains because of in-kind redemption mechanics, but this has not been verified from iShares' published tax disclosures.

The Case for the Bet

The fund is held on the premise that the world's largest listed businesses, taken together, will compound value faster than a government bond over a multi-year holding period. More precisely: that the basket's earnings yield — the fraction of the fund's price that flows through as underlying company profits — will exceed the risk-free 10-year government bond rate by at least two percentage points. The gap between that earnings yield and the bond rate is the reward for accepting the volatility and uncertainty that come with owning equities rather than paper backed by a government.

For the bet to keep working, six conditions must all hold:

  1. Equity risk premium stays positive and meaningful. The basket's earnings yield must exceed the 10-year US Treasury yield by at least 2% on a sustained basis. If the yield falls below zero for two consecutive quarterly readings, exit condition (1) fires.

  2. Look-through quality stays high. The median return-on-equity of the fund's nine or ten largest holdings (the fraction of each dollar of book value the business earns back as profit) must remain above 15%. Below that threshold the underlying businesses are no longer compounding capital effectively.

  3. Fees stay contained. The annual expense ratio must remain at or below 0.35%. At 0.30% an early-warning flag is set; above 0.35%, exit condition (2) fires. Current fee is 0.24%.

  4. Top-1 concentration stays moderate. No single holding should exceed 10% of the fund's total weight. Above 8% an early warning is set; above 10% exit condition (4) fires. Currently the largest single position is NVIDIA at 5.42%.

  5. Fund size stays liquid. Assets under management must remain above $2.0 billion. Below $4.0 billion an early warning is set; below $2.0 billion exit condition (3) fires as bid-ask spreads typically widen materially for a fund of this type. Current assets are $8.1 billion.

  6. Index methodology stays stable. MSCI Inc. must not materially restructure the MSCI World Index in a way that undermines its developed-market mandate — for example by reclassifying major countries, dropping large developed-market constituents, or shifting from float-adjusted market-cap weighting to some other scheme. Exit condition (5) covers this; no automated signal exists and the condition is monitored manually.

The Rules

The fund's pre-committed action plan covers five outcomes:

  • If all conditions hold and the basket is fairly priced: hold — the vehicle is sound and the valuation is reasonable.
  • If a driver is challenged but not broken (for example, the equity risk premium turns negative for a single quarter): do not add to the position. Re-validate after the next quarter. If the premium stays negative or look-through quality keeps falling, swap to the cheaper UCITS structure (iShares Core MSCI World at 0.20%) or move to cash.
  • If the thesis breaks (any vehicle-integrity breach, or an exit condition is triggered): exit the full position and move proceeds to a risk-free instrument or a holding that meets the value test.
  • If the basket's earnings yield rises to 6.25% or above, signalling the basket is cheap: add to the position — the basket is meaningfully cheap relative to risk-free.
  • If the basket's earnings yield falls to 4.0% or below, signalling the basket is expensive: trim 25–33% — harvest the premium while keeping the core holding.

The Conditions Today

Equity risk premium is negative and has been for the first recorded reading. The basket's earnings yield stands at 4.13% while the 10-year US Treasury yield is 4.43%, giving an equity risk premium of −0.30%. Earning −0.30% of yield premium means holders are currently accepting equity risk in exchange for less yield than a government bond offers. Exit condition (1) requires this to persist for two consecutive quarterly readings; only one reading is on file, so the persistence leg has not yet been met. Condition 1 is challenged but not yet triggered.

Look-through quality is sound. The median return-on-equity of the fund's nine deduped top holdings is 37%, well above the 15% floor. The two largest positions — NVIDIA and Apple — carry figures above 100% due to aggressive share buybacks that have shrunk book value below one year's earnings; the median strips those out. The central tendency across Alphabet, Microsoft, Amazon, Broadcom, and Meta is 24–39% returns on equity. The quality pillar is intact.

Expense ratio is at 0.24%, well inside the 0.30% early-warning band and the 0.35% hard cap. No fee pressure detected.

Top-1 concentration is at 5.42% (NVIDIA), well inside the 8.0% early-warning and 10.0% hard cap. No concentration concern.

Fund size is $8.1 billion, roughly four times the early-warning floor of $4.0 billion and more than four times the hard floor of $2.0 billion. No liquidity risk.

Index methodology — no automated signal exists. iShares (BlackRock) operates the fund as a standard exchange-traded fund. No restructuring of the MSCI World Index mandate has been flagged.

NAV deviation — the breach that triggered the exit signal. The fund's price on 2026-06-16 was $203.65 against a published net asset value (the per-share value of the underlying stocks the fund holds) of $203.81, a deviation of 0.0783%. The hard limit is 0.03%. The 5-basis-point overshoot is small and has a plausible explanation: the data feed uses the prior-close net asset value against an intraday price, and for a fund whose non-US holdings close several hours before the New York session ends, small timing gaps are normal. The framework treats the breach as real until confirmed otherwise. Verifying directly via iShares' end-of-day published net asset value would resolve the question; if the breach clears, the recommendation would fall to hold (thesis weakened, given the negative equity risk premium).

What's Wobbling

The immediate issue is the NAV deviation. At 0.0783% against a 0.03% maximum, the gap between the fund's market price and the value of its underlying holdings has crossed the hard limit. The framework responds to any breach regardless of size — a 5-basis-point overshoot carries the same formal weight as a 500-basis-point one. The most likely explanation is a data-timing artefact: the feed compares an intraday price with a prior-close net asset value for a fund whose non-US stocks settle hours before New York closes. A direct check of iShares' published end-of-day net asset value would confirm or clear the signal; if cleared, the call shifts to hold (thesis weakened).

The deeper wobble is the equity risk premium. The basket currently yields 4.13% on an earnings basis while 10-year US Treasuries yield 4.43%, a gap of −0.30%. Holding equities for less expected yield than government paper is the opposite of what the bet requires. This is the first recorded quarter at a negative equity risk premium; exit condition (1) requires two consecutive negative readings, so the persistence clock has started. If the premium is still negative at the next quarterly re-validation, exit condition (1) will fire regardless of whether the NAV breach is resolved.

The technology concentration is worth noting, though it does not yet cross any threshold. Technology accounts for 31.25% of the fund's sector weight — just above the 30% single-sector mark. A sustained de-rating of large-cap technology stocks would affect this fund disproportionately and is the dominant sector-level risk in the basket.

What Could Break the Fund

  • Persistent negative equity risk premium. If the basket's earnings yield stays below the 10-year bond rate for a second consecutive quarter, exit condition (1) fires regardless of the NAV outcome. At the current trajectory this is the most likely near-term threat.

  • Technology de-rating. The top-10 holdings are dominated by US large-cap technology companies. A sustained compression of the multiples the market awards to these names — driven by regulatory action, slowing revenue growth, or a broad risk-off rotation — would drag the basket earnings yield up (which sounds good but means the price has fallen) while also reducing the look-through quality if earnings deteriorate.

  • Fee creep. URTH already sits at the high end of iShares' global lineup at 0.24%, versus the UCITS sibling at 0.20%. A further fee increase above the early-warning level of 0.30% would warrant a switch to the cheaper structure.

  • Fund-size drawdown. A sustained outflow that takes assets below $4.0 billion sets the early-warning flag; below $2.0 billion the exit condition fires. A $6-billion decline from the current $8.1 billion is a remote but not impossible scenario in a prolonged risk-off environment.

  • MSCI World methodology change. Country reclassifications, dropping major developed-market constituents, or a shift away from float-adjusted market-cap weighting would undermine the mandate. No signal of this exists today; it is monitored manually.
