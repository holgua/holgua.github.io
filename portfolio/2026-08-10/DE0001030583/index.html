About the Bond

The Federal Republic of Germany (ISIN DE0001030583, issued February 2021, maturing 2033-04-15) is a sovereign inflation-linked bond — an instrument in which both the principal repaid at maturity and the annual coupon are adjusted upward with realised consumer-price inflation, as measured by the German Harmonised Index of Consumer Prices excluding tobacco. That index is a European price series compiled by Eurostat covering a broad basket of goods and services; it is the same measure the European Central Bank uses when assessing whether inflation is close to its 2% target. Unlike a conventional bond, which pays a fixed nominal amount regardless of what prices do in the intervening years, this instrument guarantees that every euro returned at maturity has the same purchasing power as the euros invested at issuance — and then pays a small coupon of 0.10% per year on top of that inflation-adjusted principal.

Credit risk is effectively absent. The Federal Republic of Germany carries the highest available ratings from Fitch (AAA), Moody's (Aaa), and S&P (AAA) and has never defaulted on a euro-denominated obligation. The bond's index ratio currently stands at 1.2638, reflecting the cumulative rise in the reference price index since the bond's base period. A buyer pays the dirty price — the clean price multiplied by the index ratio, plus accrued interest — at settlement, not the €94.76 clean price alone. The €94.76 is the quoted price in real (purchasing-power) terms; the actual euros that change hands are approximately €94.76 × 1.2638, or roughly €119.70 per €100 original face value.

The Case for the Bet

The holding is sized as a portfolio inflation-hedge allocation, not as a rates trade. The contractual structure means the bond does what it promises regardless of whether inflation runs hot or cold: the real purchasing power of the position is preserved. The single most important signal governing whether to hold, add, trim, or exit is the real yield at the current market price — a number that moves with the bond's price and states exactly what annual return above inflation is on offer if the bond is held to the 2033-04-15 maturity.

Three specific conditions must continue to hold for the bet to keep working:

  1. Real yield at current market price does not fall below −1.0% for two consecutive quarters while EUR cash simultaneously offers a superior after-tax real return. The exit fires only when both legs are true at once — the yield is deeply negative and cash is a better deal. The threshold exists because a negative real yield means the bond's price embeds a guaranteed real loss, and the bet is only defensible as long as the alternative does not offer more.

What Could Still Go Wrong

  • Real yield turns persistently negative. If the ECB cuts rates aggressively while the bond's price is bid up by demand for safe inflation protection, the real yield could slide below −1.00% for an extended period. If EUR cash simultaneously offers a better real return, the compound exit trigger fires and the position should be closed.
  • Germany is downgraded. A severe fiscal shock — an extended conflict, a disorderly energy transition, or sustained banking-sector stress — could prompt two major rating agencies to cut Germany below AA−. The event would be unprecedented in the post-war period but is not impossible.
  • HICP methodology is restructured. Eurostat periodically updates the basket and methodology underlying the index. A structural redefinition — such as replacing the HICP ex-tobacco index with a narrower measure or capping the inflation adjustment — would impair the bond's hedging function even without a formal default.
  • ECB deposit rate rises sharply. If the ECB tightens policy materially beyond current market expectations, EUR cash real yields could converge on or exceed the linker's real yield, eliminating the 70-basis-point spread advantage that currently favours the linker. This would not trigger an exit condition directly but would narrow the margin of safety and reduce the case for holding versus EUR cash.

The Rules

The pre-committed rules cover six states. Each is stated as a condition on the current reading; the action follows from the rule rather than from judgement made at the moment.

  • If the thesis is confirmed (real yield inside the hold band, German credit intact, HICP linkage undisturbed): hold the position. The inflation-protection function is operating as intended.
  • If the thesis weakens (real yield moves below 0% but the compound exit trigger has not yet fired): do not add. Re-assess the after-tax real return on the linker against EUR cash and conventional Bunds before taking any action; the bar for adding at a negative real yield is high.
  • If the thesis breaks (one or more of the three numbered exit conditions fires): exit the full position. Move the proceeds to short-duration nominal Bunds or EUR cash.
  • If the price falls to the buy edge (real yield rises above 1.50%): add to the position in proportion to the inflation-hedge allocation target. A seven-year AAA EUR sovereign linker at 1.50% real yield is highly attractive relative to EUR alternatives.
  • If the price rises to the trim edge (real yield falls below −0.50%): trim 25% on the first crossing. If the real yield remains below −0.50% at the next quarterly review (at least 90 days later), trim a further 25%. The ladder stops there and does not escalate further until the exit trigger fires.
  • If the compound exit trigger fires (real yield has been below −1.0% for at least 90 days and EUR cash offers a superior real return): exit the full position. Switch proceeds to EUR cash or short-duration nominal Bunds; the inflation-hedge function is no longer additive relative to the alternative.

Scenario Definitions

A scenario is a joint assumption about two independent variables: operating trajectory (how the holding's underlying cash flows grow relative to their long-run trend) and exit multiple (what the market pays for those cash flows at the horizon, relative to a fair-value anchor).

Scenario Operating trajectory Exit multiple
Bear Below trend Below fair value
Base At trend At fair value
Bull Above trend At or above fair value

Non-negotiable rule: In the bear case, both dimensions must be adverse simultaneously — a scenario where only the multiple moves is a re-rating scenario, not a bear.

Bear cases are comparable in structure across all holding types (company, ETF, bond), but not in magnitude. A company bear reflects idiosyncratic execution failure; an ETF bear reflects a broad market drawdown. Different mitigations, different diversification implications.
