About the Bond

This holding is a German federal government bond whose payments are adjusted for inflation. Its formal name is Bundesrepublik Deutschland Inflationsindexierte Anleihe 2021/2033, issued in February 2021 and maturing on 2033-04-15. The Federal Republic of Germany is the borrower — rated AAA by Fitch, Aaa by Moody's, and AAA by S&P, making it one of the safest sovereign issuers in the world.

The bond pays a fixed real coupon of 0.10% per year on an inflation-adjusted principal. The inflation adjustment follows the German HICP ex-tobacco index (the Harmonised Index of Consumer Prices for Germany, excluding tobacco, produced by Eurostat). As consumer prices rise, both the principal and the coupon payment rise with them, so the bond's purchasing power is preserved in nominal terms. The index ratio, which converts original-face-value quantities to today's purchasing power, currently stands at 1.25978 — meaning €100 of original face value now corresponds to €125.978 of nominal principal.

Two prices are quoted for this bond: the clean price and the dirty price. The clean price (€95.07 as of 2026-06-23) is quoted in real terms — purchasing-power units anchored to the issuance date — and is the number that appears on most screens. A buyer pays the dirty price of €119.874 per €100 original face value at settlement, not €95.07. The €95.07 is the quoted clean price in real terms (same purchasing-power units as the issuance date). To get the actual euros that change hands, multiply by the index ratio (1.25978), then add accrued interest.

Because the coupon and principal adjust with inflation, the meaningful return metric is the real yield: the annualised return above and beyond whatever inflation turns out to be. A real yield of 0.85% means that, held to maturity, the bond is expected to earn 0.85 percentage points per year above the rate of inflation — guaranteed by a AAA sovereign.

The Case for the Bet

The bond is held as a structural inflation hedge for the portfolio — sized to an allocation target of 7.5% — rather than as a bet on near-term interest rate moves. The bet rests on one idea: a AAA-rated EUR sovereign linker offers a reliable, low-risk real return that other liquid EUR instruments do not, and the position should be held as long as that edge persists.

Four conditions must keep holding for the bet to work:

  1. Real yield remains positive and within the holding band. The real yield at the current market price should sit above −1.0% and, ideally, within the band between the trim threshold (−0.50%) and the buy threshold (1.50%). A persistent breach of −1.0% combined with a superior alternative triggers exit condition (1): real yield below −1.0% for at least two consecutive quarters and EUR cash simultaneously offering a better real return after tax.
  2. Break-even inflation stays within a plausible range. The break-even rate — derived from the spread between the linker's real yield and the yield on a nominal German government bond of similar maturity — should sit between 1.5% and 3.0%, consistent with the ECB's price-stability mandate. A sustained move outside this range would indicate either extreme deflation risk (below 1.5%) or runaway inflation expectations (above 3.0%), both of which would call for re-assessing the position.
  3. Germany's sovereign credit rating stays above AA−. The Federal Republic of Germany currently holds the highest available rating from all three major agencies. Exit condition (2) fires if any two of the three agencies downgrade Germany below AA−, signalling a structural shift in issuer creditworthiness.
  4. The HICP ex-tobacco index methodology remains intact. The inflation protection delivered by this bond depends on Eurostat's HICP ex-tobacco index continuing to track realised consumer-price inflation in the way it does today. Exit condition (3) fires if Eurostat materially restructures the index in a way that severs that link.

The Rules

The pre-committed action plan translates each condition into a specific instruction:

  • If all conditions hold and real yield is within the holding band: continue holding. The inflation-hedge function is operating as intended.
  • If the thesis weakens (real yield falls below 0% but has not yet triggered exit condition 1): do not add. Re-assess by comparing the real after-tax return against EUR cash and nominal German government bonds before taking any action.
  • If any numbered exit condition fires: close the full position. Move proceeds to nominal short-duration EUR bonds or EUR cash.
  • If the real yield rises above 1.50% (bond price falls below €91): add to the position. A real yield above 1.50% on a seven-year AAA EUR sovereign linker is unusually attractive; scale in proportionally to the inflation-hedge allocation target.
  • If the real yield falls below −0.50% (bond price rises above €104): trim 25% on the first crossing. If the real yield remains below −0.50% at the next quarterly review — at least 90 calendar days later — trim a further 25%. The trim ladder is bounded: once completed, further crossings do not trigger additional trims unless exit condition (1) fires.
  • If the compound exit trigger fires (real yield persistently below −1.00% for at least 90 days and EUR cash simultaneously offering a better real return): close the full position. Switch proceeds to EUR cash or short-duration nominal German government bonds; the inflation-hedge function is no longer additive versus the alternative.

What's Wobbling

Nothing is materially off course, though two items are worth monitoring.

The cash comparison is the narrower of the two to keep watch on. The linker's 51-basis-point advantage over EUR cash in real terms is comfortable, but it is not wide. If the ECB were to cut its deposit rate materially — or if break-even inflation were to fall further — that gap would narrow. It does not need to reverse for the bond to remain worth holding; but the compound exit trigger for condition (1) has two legs, and the "cash offers better" leg is the more variable of the two.

The second item is the modest nominal Bund yield compression seen in the latest reading: both reference bonds tightened five basis points since the prior run, pulling break-even down three basis points to 1.91%. The move is small and break-even remains within the confirmation range. Sustained further compression would tighten the break-even reading, which would push the linker's implied real return closer to cash — worth watching across the next one or two quarterly reviews.

What Could Still Go Wrong

  • ECB rate cuts compress the cash-yield comparison. If the ECB reduces its deposit facility rate faster than inflation falls, EUR cash real yields decline — narrowing or reversing the linker's current 51-basis-point advantage. This does not directly trigger an exit, but it would eventually close the gap that keeps condition (1) inactive.
  • Sustained Bund yield compression. If long-duration nominal Bund yields continue falling, break-even inflation can drift below 1.50% — into the warning zone. A sustained reading below 1.50% would call for closer scrutiny of whether the linker's inflation-adjustment mechanism is pricing in structurally lower price growth.
  • German fiscal stress. Increased market scepticism about Germany's fiscal trajectory — short of an actual AA− downgrade — could widen sovereign spreads and push Bund yields higher, raising the linker's real yield and pushing its clean price lower. That would improve the valuation entry point rather than threaten the thesis.
  • HICP methodology divergence. Eurostat periodically reviews index construction. A change that caused the index to understate realised inflation — for example by altering the treatment of housing costs — would erode the hedge's effectiveness without formally triggering condition (3), which requires a more abrupt structural change.
  • Currency effect for non-EUR portfolios. The bond is denominated in EUR. EUR depreciation reduces the real value of this position in any other reporting currency; cross-currency risk is not part of the exit conditions.
