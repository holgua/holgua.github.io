Recent Development

The three months ending in March 2026 were a confirmation quarter, not an inflection. Total payment volume — the gross dollar amount flowing across PayPal's network — grew 11% year on year to the equivalent of roughly $450 billion for the period, driven by Braintree processing, PayPal branded checkout, and Venmo. The number of individual payment transactions rose 7% to 6,475 million for the quarter.

The most consequential data point was not in the income statement but in the credit book. Consumer net charge-offs — the share of loans that go permanently unpaid — fell from 4.5% annualised in the first quarter of 2025 to 3.0% in the first quarter of 2026. That is well below the 10% line that would trigger an exit from the position under the rules. The improvement matters because one of the original reasons to hold PayPal was that its credit losses, elevated through 2024, would normalise — and they are doing so.

The buyback engine also delivered: 34 million shares were retired during the quarter at an average of $44.60 per share for a total outlay of $1.5 billion. The share count fell from 920 million to 892 million in a single quarter — a roughly 3% reduction. Compounding that pace across a full year compresses the share count by around 12%, which raises per-share earnings even if total profits stay flat. For the full picture of what conditions must hold for the bet to remain valid, see the Case for the Bet.

About PayPal

PayPal is a digital payments network that sits between buyers and sellers, taking a fee every time money moves through its platform. When a consumer pays for something online using their PayPal wallet, Venmo, or the "Pay Later" instalment option, PayPal earns a fraction of the transaction amount. When a business uses PayPal's Braintree infrastructure to process card payments — including on behalf of large online merchants — PayPal earns again. In total, roughly $1.79 trillion of payment volume moved across the network in the year ending December 2025, across approximately 200 countries and territories. The business was founded in 1998, spun out of eBay in 2015, and has since processed more cumulative digital payment volume than any Western non-bank competitor.

The core insight behind the business is that payments at scale are a two-sided market: merchants accept PayPal because consumers trust it; consumers use it because merchants accept it. PayPal holds money-transmitter licences in all 50 US states plus the District of Columbia, a credit-institution licence in Luxembourg covering the European Union, and a Financial Conduct Authority authorisation in the United Kingdom. These regulatory approvals represent a genuine structural barrier — any company that wanted to replicate PayPal's reach would need to apply for, and receive, equivalent licences in every jurisdiction before it could compete on equal footing. That process has historically taken a decade or more.

The Case for the Bet

The bet is that $5–7 billion per year of cash generated by the business, returned to shareholders through share buybacks at depressed prices, compounds per-share earnings at a double-digit rate even if the overall business grows only modestly. Five conditions must hold:

  1. Branded checkout volume does not lose more than 2 percentage points of market share in a single fiscal year. Branded checkout — when a consumer explicitly chooses to pay with their PayPal wallet rather than entering a card number directly — is the margin-rich segment of the business. The thesis assumes this share may grow slowly or stay flat, but not fall sharply; structural displacement rather than cyclical softness would indicate that Apple Pay, Google Pay, or native browser autofill are permanently claiming the consumer checkout moment.

Latest reading: Branded checkout volume is not separately disclosed as a standalone figure in the three months ending March 2026. Revenue from "PayPal" branded products grew approximately $140 million year on year in the quarter, suggesting the segment is growing, though more slowly than Braintree. Two US securities class actions — the Darcy and Norfolk matters — allege that management withheld data on checkout growth because the underlying trend was worse than disclosed. Both cases are at the pleadings stage with no judicial finding; they are a monitoring flag, not an exit-condition trigger.

  1. No consent order from a regulator materially restricts a core product feature. PayPal operates under active information requests from the US Consumer Financial Protection Bureau and the Federal Trade Commission, and in March 2026 received a new inquiry from the UK Financial Conduct Authority regarding its contractual arrangements with Visa and Mastercard. None of these has produced a consent order or product restriction.

Latest reading: As of March 2026, all active regulatory matters remain at the investigative or pre-enforcement stage. The UK inquiry covers commercial contractual provisions, not a restriction on PayPal's core payment features. The dollar amounts accrued for legal and regulatory risk were assessed as not material as at the end of the quarter. One legacy securities class action — the PPH matter — was dismissed with prejudice in March 2026, removing an overhang.

  1. Consumer credit charge-off rate stays below 10%. PayPal lends money to consumers through its "Pay Later" buy-now-pay-later product and through its consumer credit card. The charge-off rate — the annual share of balances that go permanently unrecovered — is the primary indicator of whether PayPal's consumer lending is being conducted prudently.

Latest reading: The consumer net charge-off rate improved to 3.0% annualised in the three months ending March 2026, down from 4.5% in the same period of 2025. The share of consumer loans classified as current (not in arrears) held steady at 96.3%. Both figures are well within the safety margin — the 10% threshold for this condition is more than three times the current rate.

  1. Management maintains the buyback programme and does not take on material incremental debt above 2.0 times earnings before interest, tax, depreciation and amortisation. The entire per-share compounding thesis rests on the continued deployment of free cash flow into share repurchases at prices below intrinsic value. An acquisition-driven debt increase or an abandonment of the programme would remove the mechanism.

Latest reading: PayPal repurchased 34 million shares for $1.5 billion in the three months ending March 2026, at an average price of $44.60. The remaining board authorisation stands at $12.4 billion. In April 2026, PayPal announced a strategic reorganisation targeting at least $1.5 billion in annualised savings — consistent with continued capital-return discipline rather than a pivot to acquisitions. Net debt remains negative (the company holds more cash than debt), so leverage is not a current concern.

  1. Operating income does not fall below $5.5 billion in any fiscal year, and management does not guide non-GAAP operating income below $5.0 billion for the following year. Operating income (profit after subtracting the cost of running the business but before interest and taxes) is the clearest indicator of whether the margin expansion story is intact. Falling below the stated floor would signal that new CEO Enrique Lores has abandoned the discipline that drove margins from 13.9% in 2022 to 18.3% in the year ending December 2025.

Latest reading: Full-year 2025 operating income of $6,065 million cleared the $5.5 billion floor by approximately 10%. The three months ending March 2026 showed operating income of $1,488 million — down slightly from $1,530 million in the same quarter of 2025, with the decline driven by Braintree's higher transaction-processing costs pulling down the blended margin to 18% from 20%. This is a known structural feature of mixing high-volume, lower-margin Braintree volumes with higher-margin branded checkout revenue, not a new deterioration.

The Share Price

At $45.65, PayPal trades modestly below its fair-value anchor of roughly $48. Fair value here is the price implied by an owner earnings yield (cash generated by the business as a percentage of its market value) equal to the equilibrium rate of 10.5% — the point at which the business is neither cheap nor expensive by the rules governing this holding.

The buy edge sits at $41 and the trim edge at $60. At the current price of $46, the share is above the buy threshold, meaning a new position at today's price carries no margin of safety against fair value — the rules call for waiting for a dip to $41 before adding. Existing holders, however, sit at a price that is inside the hold band and below the fair-value anchor, which is a comfortable place to be.

The market's implied view is at odds with the underlying numbers. The share price reflects roughly 8.4 times last year's earnings — a multiple consistent with a business that is expected to see earnings decline structurally, not compound. The underlying data — return on invested capital of 15% and rising, owner earnings of $5.3 billion in 2025 and growing, an active share-repurchase programme that reduced the share count by roughly 22% between 2022 and 2025 — do not support that narrative.

What's Wobbling

The wobble in the picture is branded checkout. PayPal does not separately report branded checkout volume in its quarterly filing — the number that would confirm or deny whether the consumer checkout business is holding its ground. What is disclosed is that "PayPal" branded product revenue grew approximately $140 million year on year in the first quarter of 2026, a growth rate that is lower than Braintree's, meaning branded checkout is losing relative weight inside the total volume mix even if it is not declining in absolute terms.

The two pending class actions — Darcy and Norfolk — allege that management withheld branded checkout data precisely because the trend was worse than the market understood when forward guidance was set. Courts have not found for the plaintiffs, and the cases are at the earliest procedural stage. But they are a credibility signal: if management has been understating a structural problem in the most closely watched segment, the quality of the thesis confirmation is lower than the headline figures suggest.

None of the five written exit conditions has been crossed, and the wobble is not new — it was already embedded in the thesis when the position was opened. The assessment here is that the risk is monitored, not that it has resolved.

What Could Still Go Wrong

  • Branded checkout displacement becomes structural. If Apple Pay, Google Pay, and AI-agent-routed commerce continue to claim a growing share of the consumer checkout moment, PayPal's branded revenue stagnates while Braintree's lower-margin volumes grow — compressing the overall blended margin toward the exit-condition floor.
  • New CEO Enrique Lores fails to stabilise strategy. Lores took the role in March 2026 from HP, where his background was hardware and personal computing. He has five years of PayPal board knowledge, including the Chair role since 2024, but no fintech operating experience. A third major strategic pivot in five years would impair management credibility and accelerate talent loss.
  • Regulatory enforcement escalates beyond current investigations. Either the Consumer Financial Protection Bureau or the Federal Trade Commission could move from the current investigative stage to an enforcement action that imposes a consent order with operational restrictions — for example, requiring PayPal to release held customer funds faster, which would increase working-capital costs.
  • Consumer credit deteriorates in a macroeconomic downturn. The 3.0% charge-off rate reflects an improving credit environment. A recession that pushes unemployment materially higher would likely reverse this trend; while the 10% exit threshold remains far away, a sustained move toward 6–7% would be a monitoring alert.
  • Agentic commerce routes around the PayPal checkout layer. If AI shopping agents — such as those integrated into Google, OpenAI, or Perplexity — authenticate purchases through native digital wallets rather than PayPal, merchants may experience a secular shift in which checkout-point choice migrates to the AI layer rather than the consumer layer, and PayPal is bypassed even on merchants that currently accept it.

Gaps in the Picture

The most significant gap is that branded checkout volume is not separately disclosed in the quarterly filing. The thesis relies on this metric to confirm that condition (1) — no loss of more than 2 percentage points of checkout market share in a single year — is not being triggered. Without a separately reported figure, this can only be inferred from revenue growth by segment, which is a rougher signal.

The fair-value anchor of $48 used throughout is derived from a cash-yield framework anchored to 2025 annual figures — specifically, the price at which the business's cash earnings equal 10.5% of its market value — not from a discounted cash flow model. A separate scenario model produces a base-case fair value of $141 per share under a different methodology — the two frameworks diverge sharply, and the appropriate anchor depends heavily on which model one trusts. This creates a context warning: the "modestly below fair value" characterisation refers to the cash-yield band used for the position's rules, not to a consensus of valuation methods.

Owner earnings are computed as operating cash flow minus total capital expenditure, net of stock-based compensation. Total capital expenditure is used as a conservative proxy for maintenance capital expenditure; the true maintenance figure is likely lower, meaning owner earnings are likely understated, and the resulting yield is conservatively stated. A breakdown between maintenance and growth capital expenditure is not provided in the filing.

The Rules

The rules governing action on this holding are:

  • If the thesis is confirmed by the latest evidence — all conditions intact, no exit-condition triggers — hold without action and continue compounding.
  • If the thesis is weakening — one or more conditions showing deterioration but no exit trigger yet — do not add. Reassess after two consecutive quarters of confirming data before drawing a conclusion; one quarter of weakness is not sufficient.
  • If the thesis is broken — any one of the five written exit conditions is triggered — exit the full position within 30 days. No averaging down. Document which condition fired.
  • If the share price falls below the buy edge ($41) — confirm the thesis is intact first, then add in tranches over one to two quarters.
  • If the share price rises above the trim edge ($60) — trim 25–33% to harvest the premium, retaining the core position.
  • If the share price rises above the exit edge ($78) — exit the full position. The market is pricing in roughly 11 years of base-case compounding instantly; redeploy proceeds at the next cheap-edge crossing or into a cheaper alternative. No re-entry within 90 days.
