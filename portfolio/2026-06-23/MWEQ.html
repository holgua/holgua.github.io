About the Fund

World Equal Weight is a traded fund (Invesco MSCI World Equal Weight UCITS ETF, Xetra ticker MWEQ, ISIN IE000OEF25S1) that holds roughly 1,500 companies drawn from developed stock markets worldwide and weights each one equally — rather than weighting by market value, as the most familiar global index funds do. The fund rebalances quarterly, mechanically selling recent winners and buying laggards to return every holding to the same weight. This produces a portfolio that is structurally different from a conventional market-cap index: it gives more weight to smaller companies, to non-US markets (Japan, the United Kingdom, continental Europe), and to lower-valued companies relative to their earnings than a standard global fund would. The fund launched in September 2024, is Irish-domiciled, and accumulates income (dividends are reinvested rather than paid out). The share price as of 2026-06-23 was €5.84.

At 0.20% per year (the annual expense ratio — the fee deducted from the fund's assets), the fund sits comfortably within the 0.30% hard ceiling configured for this holding and below the 0.27% early-warning level. Tracking difference and tracking error for the fund are not available from the data sources used here; both figures should be sourced from the Invesco product factsheet.

The Case for the Bet

The bet is structural: an equal-weight developed-market basket will deliver a meaningful earnings premium over the risk-free rate across a full market cycle. Equal-weighting is the mechanism: by giving the same slice of the portfolio to each of roughly 1,500 companies, the fund avoids the condition where ten or fifteen very large technology companies set the tone for the entire holding. The earnings yield of the basket (the ratio of the basket's collective earnings to the share price, expressed as a percentage) is expected to run higher than that of a cap-weighted peer, and that higher yield is what the rules treat as the return premium for accepting the equal-weight structure's trade-offs — notably the approximately 30% annual turnover from quarterly rebalancing, and the modest tracking error from sampling rather than full replication.

Six conditions must keep holding for the bet to work:

  1. The basket earnings yield must remain above the 10-year government bond yield for two consecutive quarters — if it falls below for two straight quarters, the earnings-premium case has failed.
  2. The annual expense ratio (currently 0.20%) must stay below 0.30%; current 0.20% vs. hard ceiling 0.30% (early-warning flag above 0.27%).
  3. Assets under management must stay above €500M; current ~€1.48B vs. floor €500M (early-warning flag below €800M). A collapse to this level would signal fund-closure risk.
  4. The largest single holding must stay below 1.0% of the fund; current largest (Dell Technologies) at 0.27% vs. ceiling 1.0% (early-warning flag above 0.50%). An equal-weight fund should keep every holding near 0.07% at rebalance; drift above 1.0% — about fourteen times the target weight — signals a methodology or rebalancing failure.
  5. The MSCI Equal Weighted Index methodology must remain materially unchanged — no shift to factor-tilted weighting, no removal of the quarterly rebalance, no shrinkage of the universe.
  6. The median return-on-equity of the most-held ten companies (a look-through quality signal) must stay above 12% — a floor set deliberately lower than for cap-weighted peers, because equal-weighting naturally pulls in smaller companies with lower profitability.

The Rules

If the thesis is confirmed — all six conditions hold and the valuation is within the normal band — hold.

If the thesis is weakened — a driver is deteriorating but no exit condition has fired — do not add. Re-validate after the next quarterly rebalance. If the fund's earnings-yield premium over the cap-weighted alternative (a conventional global index fund) compresses below half a percentage point for two consecutive quarters, the diversification benefit no longer justifies the higher annual cost; in that case, consolidate into a lower-cost cap-weight alternative.

If the thesis is broken — an exit condition fires (a vehicle-integrity breach, a value test failure) — exit fully. Move proceeds to a cap-weighted global core holding or to risk-free assets.

If the share price corresponds to a basket earnings yield above 6.25% (the basket is cheap relative to risk-free) — add to the position. That pricing should be rare; when it occurs, the rules treat it as a decisive buying opportunity.

If the share price corresponds to a basket earnings yield below 4.00% (the basket is expensive) — trim 25–33%. Harvest the premium over risk-free and redeploy on the next rebalance.

What's Wobbling

Two drivers are in mixed territory, and valuation is temporarily indeterminate — none of these change the call, but they are the conditions most worth watching.

The earnings-premium driver is the one that matters most. The premium of 0.33% over the 10-year bond yield, while positive, is thin — barely above zero. For the equal-weight bet to pay off over a full market cycle, the basket should consistently deliver a meaningful premium over risk-free. At 33 basis points (0.33 percentage points), that premium is present but narrow, and the data gap on 2026-06-23 means the current reading is unknown. If fresh data shows the premium has turned negative, exit condition 1 would begin its two-quarter persistence count.

Look-through quality is the secondary wobble. A median return-on-equity of 14.14% for the fund's top-ten drift cohort is adequate, but the drift cohort tends to be technology-heavy — and technology companies can have high or volatile profitability. A sustained drop below 12% in the median (which would require the full equal-weight basket's quality profile to deteriorate substantially, not just the drift cohort) would trigger exit condition 6. That is not remotely close today.

The valuation indeterminacy is a data-quality issue, not a thesis signal. The prior reading was within the hold band, the vehicle-integrity checks are all green, and the rules table defaults to hold when valuation cannot be computed but no other exit condition fires.

What Could Break the Fund

  • Earnings-premium collapse. If the basket earnings yield falls below the 10-year bond yield for two consecutive quarters, exit condition 1 fires. At a risk-free rate of 4.509%, the earnings yield would need to stay below 4.509% for six months running. The last valid reading of 4.785% was above this line — but not by much. A modest rise in bond yields or a fall in the basket earnings yield (from a rising share price or compressed margins) could push it through.

  • Fund closure. If Invesco closes the fund — which it has done with sub-scale UCITS products in the past — exit condition 3 fires. The €1.48B current asset base provides substantial cushion, but the fund is only 22 months old; a sustained market drawdown combined with investor redemptions could move assets toward the €500M floor faster than the earnings case would imply.

  • Methodology drift. MSCI could restructure the Equal Weighted index — shifting to factor-tilted weighting, removing the quarterly rebalance, or changing the parent universe. This would invalidate the structural basis of the bet (condition 5). No automated signal monitors this; it requires periodic manual review of MSCI index communications.

  • Equal-weight integrity failure. A sustained top-1 concentration above 1.0% — roughly fourteen times the target weight — would signal that the fund is no longer delivering equal-weight exposure. Currently, Dell Technologies at 0.27% is far inside the 1.0% ceiling, but a quarter where one or two holdings run very strongly before the rebalance date could push closer to the early-warning level.

  • Cost creep. If Invesco raises the annual expense ratio above 0.27% (early-warning) or 0.30% (exit ceiling), the cost advantage of equal-weight exposure over cap-weight peers narrows. At the current 0.20%, this is a distant risk.
