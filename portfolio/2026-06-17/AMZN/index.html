Amazon: The Bet Still Works

The Bottom Line

Amazon's central bet — that its cloud computing division keeps compounding at high speed while the retail and advertising engines expand their profit contribution — is reading well against the latest quarterly numbers. The cloud business grew 28% in the three months ending in March 2026, comfortably above the threshold the bet requires to stay alive. The share price at $250 sits in a neutral zone: above what the valuation framework considers fair value ($176), but well short of the level where the rules call for trimming. The one condition to watch is the capital expenditure surge, which has pushed the company's trailing infrastructure spending to roughly $147 billion over the past year. That figure does not yet trigger any written exit threshold, but it is the highest-priority item to track going into 2027.

About Amazon

Amazon operates three businesses under one roof, each at a different stage of maturity, and each feeding the others.

The oldest and largest is e-commerce and logistics: Amazon sells goods directly to consumers and also provides a marketplace where independent retailers list products and pay Amazon a commission and, often, a fulfilment fee. In the full year ending in December 2025, this North America retail operation generated $426 billion in sales and is now earning a meaningful operating profit margin (roughly 7 cents on every dollar of revenue), after years of being run at close to breakeven to fund growth. The international equivalent — stores in Europe, Japan, and elsewhere — generated $162 billion in revenue and has recently crossed into profit for the first time, adding a third stream of operating income to complement the two that follow.

The second business is Amazon Web Services, the cloud computing and infrastructure platform. Businesses of almost every size rent computing capacity, data storage, and an expanding catalogue of software services from Amazon rather than building their own data centres. This segment is smaller by revenue ($129 billion in FY2025) but dramatically more profitable: it earned $46 billion in operating income in FY2025, roughly 57% of Amazon's total operating profit on just 18% of its revenue. The cloud unit's operating profit margin has held above 35% in recent quarters, which is the reason the rest of the company can afford to run its logistics network at cost and still compound its overall earnings.

The third business is advertising. Amazon's retail platform — with hundreds of millions of active customers searching for things to buy — has become an extraordinarily valuable place for brands to run ads. Advertising services generated $69 billion in revenue in FY2025, growing at 22% year on year, and carries margins well above the retail average.

The Case for the Bet

The core idea is that Amazon is the most diversified compounding engine in large-cap technology: a retail and logistics platform that generates cash and data, which in turn funds two profit pools — cloud computing and advertising — whose unit economics have not yet fully matured. The bet is that both pools can keep growing their profits faster than their revenues for another three to five years, even as the retail core continues to expand its own margins.

For the bet to work, the following conditions must keep holding:

  1. AWS revenue growth stays above 18% year-on-year, with no consecutive two-quarter print below 15%. Below 15% for two consecutive quarters would signal that the artificial-intelligence workload adoption thesis — the reason Amazon is spending heavily on infrastructure — has stalled. See The Conditions Today.

  2. AWS operating profit margin stays above 28%, with no consecutive two-quarter print below that line absent one-off charges. A sustained fall below 28% would signal that the economics of the infrastructure investment cycle have broken down. See The Conditions Today.

  3. Advertising revenue growth stays above single digits for two consecutive years. The advertising business is the third engine; if it stalls, the runway for operating income compounding narrows sharply. See The Conditions Today.

  4. The infrastructure spending surge (roughly $147 billion in the twelve months to March 2026) normalises below $120 billion in both FY2027 and FY2028 — or, if it does not, AWS revenue growth accelerates commensurately. The exit condition is specifically the combination of sustained high spending and absent revenue acceleration, not high spending alone. See The Conditions Today.

  5. No court-ordered structural remedy in the Federal Trade Commission's monopoly case requiring Amazon to separate its businesses. This is a binary, event-driven condition: if a remedy is imposed, the exit rule triggers immediately. See What's Wobbling.

  6. No disclosure of a material weakness in internal controls, and no unexpected change of auditor. Both would be immediate exit signals. See The Conditions Today.

  7. Bezos's insider selling, disclosed via Form 144 filings with the regulator, does not exceed 40 million shares in a single filing. The current cadence — roughly 25 million shares per filing, via a pre-planned selling programme — is within the thesis parameters. A single filing above 40 million would signal an abnormal acceleration. See The Conditions Today.

  8. The company's profit yield (operating cash flow minus capital expenditure, net of share-based compensation, divided by market capitalisation) stays above 3.0%. This is the floor below which the valuation is considered to have fully priced in optimistic capex recovery. The current yield is 4.2%, above the 3.0% floor but below the 6.0% the framework treats as fair value. See The Share Price.

The Rules

Five rules govern what happens at each combination of conditions and share price. They are stated here as fixed — no new judgement enters.

  1. If the thesis is confirmed (all drivers intact or mixed, no exit condition triggered): hold without action. Continue letting the cloud and advertising flywheel compound.

  2. If the thesis is weakening (one or more drivers are deteriorating but no written exit condition has yet been triggered): do not add to any position. Re-run the validation against the next two quarterly filings before making any change. One quarter of data is not enough; the rule requires confirmation across two consecutive quarters before acting.

  3. If the thesis is broken (one or more written exit conditions are triggered): exit the full position within 30 days. No averaging down. The exception: AWS revenue and margin exits require two consecutive quarterly confirmations before action. An imposed regulatory structural remedy or an auditor change triggers an immediate exit with no waiting period.

  4. If the share price falls to or below $140 (the buy threshold), and the thesis is still intact: add to the position in tranches over one to two quarters, but only after confirming that no exit condition has been triggered and that AWS growth is still at or above 18%.

  5. If the share price rises to or above $263 (the trim threshold): trim 25–33% of the position to capture the premium, and re-evaluate the remaining size after the trim. If the share price reaches $421 (the exit threshold), exit the full position. At that price, the market is pricing in roughly 11 years of base-case compounding all at once; the implied forward return falls below what long-run equity ownership normally delivers, and redeploying elsewhere becomes the better option. No re-entry within 90 days.

The Conditions Today

AWS growth (condition 1). In the three months ending in March 2026, Amazon's cloud division reported $37.6 billion in revenue, 28% higher than the same period a year earlier. The $364 billion in contractually committed future cloud revenue (with a weighted-average contract term of 5.5 years) provides a further forward view. This reading is well above the 18% threshold and far above the 15% exit line. Condition 1 is satisfied comfortably.

AWS profit margin (condition 2). The cloud division's operating profit margin in the March 2026 quarter was 37.7%, down roughly 170 basis points (about 1.7 percentage points) from the 39.4% posted in the same quarter a year earlier. The filing attributes the compression to stepped-up infrastructure investment rather than pricing pressure or cost-base deterioration. At 37.7%, the margin remains nearly ten full percentage points above the 28% exit threshold. The direction of travel — slight compression due to planned spending — is worth monitoring but does not change the call today. Condition 2 is satisfied.

Advertising growth (condition 3). Amazon's advertising business generated $17.2 billion in revenue in the March 2026 quarter, up nearly 24% from a year earlier. The filing identifies advertising as a primary driver of improved profit at both the North America and International segments. At nearly 24% growth, this condition is satisfied by a wide margin.

Capital expenditure trajectory (condition 4). Over the twelve months ending in March 2026, Amazon spent roughly $147 billion on infrastructure — almost double the $88 billion it spent in the comparable prior-year period. The exit condition requires consolidated spending above $120 billion in both FY2027 and FY2028 without commensurate acceleration in cloud revenue. With cloud growing at 28% in the latest quarter and a $364 billion backlog in hand, the "without commensurate acceleration" clause is not met today. The forward watch begins in earnest when FY2026 guidance is issued — this is the highest-priority monitoring item going into the next annual reporting cycle.

FTC monopoly case (condition 5). The latest filing references the Federal Trade Commission litigation in the risk factors section and notes that Amazon is defending multiple matters alleging monopolisation. No structural remedy has been imposed; no new trial schedule appears in this quarterly filing. Condition 5 is not triggered.

Controls and auditor integrity (condition 6). The filing discloses no material weakness in internal controls and no change of auditor. EY has been Amazon's auditor since 1996. The Beneish score — a statistical indicator of financial-statement manipulation — sits at −2.663, well inside the clean range. Condition 6 is not triggered.

Bezos insider selling (condition 7). The filing's "Trading Arrangements" section reports "None," meaning no new insider selling plan was established or modified in the quarter. The most recent Form 144 disclosure covers 25 million shares — well below the 40 million threshold. Condition 7 is not triggered.

Owner-earnings yield (condition 8). The current yield — computed as operating cash flow minus total capital expenditure, net of share-based compensation, divided by market capitalisation — stands at 4.2%. This is above the 3.0% exit floor, though well below the 6.0% the framework treats as fair value and the 7.5% it treats as the buy threshold. No exit condition from this driver today.

The Share Price

At $250, the share price sits above the $176 fair-value anchor and well below the $263 trim edge. In the framework's language, the position is in the middle of the neutral band. The profit yield of 4.2% — the annual owner earnings divided by the market capitalisation — is closer to the 4.0% trim threshold than to the 7.5% buy threshold. In other words, the price is drifting toward the expensive side of neutral without yet reaching the level where the trim rule fires.

That neutral reading is consistent with what the valuation work produces. A two-stage discounted cash flow analysis, using conservative assumptions (an 8.9% discount rate, 12% earnings growth in the first five years, 8% in years six through ten, and a 2.5% long-run terminal growth rate), yields an intrinsic value of roughly $227 per share. At $250 the stock trades at a modest premium — approximately 10% above the base-case intrinsic value. There is no margin of safety at today's price for a fresh entry.

For context: an entry at $140 (the buy threshold) would imply a yield of 7.5% plus the assumed 8% growth rate, for a total long-run annual return of about 16%. The same calculation at today's $250 implies about 12% per year. Twelve percent is not unattractive for a wide-moat compounder — but it leaves little buffer against execution disappointment.

What's Wobbling

Capital expenditure surge. The infrastructure spending programme is the dominant forward risk. Amazon doubled its net spending on property and equipment — to roughly $41.5 billion in the March 2026 quarter alone — and the trailing twelve-month total has reached $147 billion. This is not a current exit condition, because AWS revenue growth is accelerating in parallel. But if cloud growth were to slow while spending remained elevated, the capex exit condition would come into view quickly. The quarterly cadence of AWS growth reports is the principal monitoring task for the next four to six quarters.

Tariff exposure on the retail marketplace. The March 2026 filing explicitly warns that China-based sellers account for significant portions of the third-party seller services and advertising revenues. Changes in tariff policy — including the prospect of de minimis exemption changes for small imported parcels — are flagged multiple times as a source of demand and supplier uncertainty. Despite the headwind, North America operating income grew 41% year on year in the quarter. The tariff watch item does not approach any written exit condition (advertising growth would need to fall to single digits for two consecutive years to trigger exit condition 5), but it is a factor that bears monitoring.

The FTC monopoly trial. An unresolved Federal Trade Commission case alleging monopolisation of the online retail market remains active. The filing refers to it in the risk factors but provides no new trial timeline. A court-imposed structural remedy requiring the separation of business units — separating the marketplace from the retail operation, for example — is a written exit condition that triggers immediately. This is a low-probability but high-impact item that sits in the background of every quarterly review.

No share buybacks. Amazon has not repurchased a single share under its $10 billion March 2022 authorisation. Instead, it has issued $37 billion in new dollar bonds and €14.5 billion in euro bonds in March 2026 to fund the AI infrastructure build. The thesis explicitly accepts that Amazon's capital allocation is "acceptable but not excellent," and the zero-buyback record is consistent with that characterisation. The Bezos insider selling programme — 25 million shares in the most recent Form 144 filing — is within the written threshold and proceeds under a pre-planned schedule.

Fair-value drift between methods. A separate scenario analysis using bottom-up earnings estimates across bear, base, and bull cases implies a base-case midpoint of $569 per share — far above the $176 implied by the yield-based method. The divergence, which is flagged in the re-validation, reflects two different frameworks for estimating long-term earnings power: one anchors to today's yield, the other to a three-year forward earnings estimate assuming capex normalises. Neither is definitively correct; the two should be reviewed together before any significant add or trim decision.

What Could Still Go Wrong

  • Cloud growth stalls. If Amazon Web Services' quarterly revenue growth falls below 18% for two consecutive quarters — signalling that the artificial-intelligence workload adoption thesis is not absorbing the infrastructure spend — the investment case weakens significantly. From 28%, a fall to below 18% would be a meaningful deceleration. Microsoft's Azure and Google Cloud are the primary competitors attempting to capture this workload.
  • Capital expenditure stranding. If the infrastructure buildout peaks and cloud revenue growth does not accelerate in tandem, Amazon could be left with depreciating data-centre assets that do not earn their cost of capital. The trailing twelve-month infrastructure spend of $147 billion exceeds the $140 billion in operating cash flow generated in the same period — the company is currently spending more on infrastructure than it takes in from operations on a cash basis.
  • Antitrust structural remedy. A court-ordered separation of Amazon's marketplace from its retail and cloud operations would fundamentally alter the company's financial structure. The retail and cloud businesses cross-subsidise each other in ways that would be disrupted by forced separation. This is a tail risk, not a base case, but it cannot be excluded.
  • Anthropic mark reversal. Amazon holds a large stake in the artificial-intelligence research company Anthropic, which was marked up by $7.7 billion in FY2025 under accounting rules. A downward revision of similar magnitude would flow through reported net income and could affect market perception of earnings quality, even though it is a non-cash item.
  • Retail tariff drag deepening. If tariff policy changes or de minimis exemption changes materially raise the cost of goods sold through the marketplace — eroding the third-party seller economics that underpin both seller services revenue and advertising revenue — the growth trajectory of both segments could slow faster than the current quarterly data suggests.

Gaps in the Picture

Owner earnings definition. The profit yield calculation uses total capital expenditure (operating cash flow minus all infrastructure and equipment spending), not a split between maintenance and growth spending. Amazon does not separately disclose what fraction of its $147 billion trailing spend is required to maintain existing capacity versus expanding it. If a significant fraction is growth investment — which the evidence suggests — the "true" maintenance earnings power is materially higher than the 4.2% yield implies. A more realistic estimate, using 40% of depreciation as maintenance capital expenditure, would put the yield around 4.4–4.5%.

Two fair-value anchors. Two independent calculations produce materially different estimates of Amazon's fair value. The owner-earnings yield framework — anchored to today's yield of 4.2% and a 6.0% target — implies a fair-value share price of $176. A bottom-up scenario analysis using FY2027 earnings estimates and peer-group and historical multiples implies a base-case midpoint of $569. The gap (69%) reflects different assumptions about how quickly earnings normalise: the yield method uses today's depressed cash flow; the scenario method assumes capex peaks and cash flow recovers significantly by FY2027. Both anchors are legitimate; neither is definitive. A future schema update will reconcile the two methods.

Anthropic mark and non-cash income. The reported net income of $77.7 billion in FY2025 includes approximately $15.2 billion of non-cash income related to the Anthropic stake mark-up. Stripping that out, operating net income is roughly $62 billion. The yield calculation uses operating cash flow rather than net income, which sidesteps this issue — but any investor assessing the reported earnings-per-share number should be aware that approximately one-fifth of FY2025 reported profit was non-cash.

Capital expenditure normalisation timeline. The FY2026 guidance implies capital spending will be higher than the FY2025 level. Management has not provided a specific FY2027 target. The exit conditions in the thesis (specifically, conditions 1 and 4) are forward-looking: they trigger only if cloud revenue growth stalls and spending stays elevated into FY2027–28. The current evidence supports the thesis, but the validation point is 12–18 months away, not today.
