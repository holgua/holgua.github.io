About All World

The iShares MSCI World fund tracks the MSCI World Index, a float-adjusted, market-capitalisation-weighted index covering roughly 1,300 large- and mid-cap companies across 23 developed markets. About 70% of the basket is in the United States; the remaining 30% is spread across countries including Japan, the United Kingdom, France, Switzerland, Germany, and Canada. The fund holds no exposure to emerging markets — the "World" in the name refers to the MSCI classification of developed economies, not every listed company on earth. Its role in a portfolio is to provide broad developed-market equity exposure while diluting the concentration risk of an all-US allocation.

The annual cost of running the fund is 0.24% of assets per year, deducted from the net asset value. No tracking-difference or tracking-error figures were available at the time of this refresh; those figures, which measure how closely the fund follows its index after fees, should be verified from the iShares URTH factsheet.

Tax profile. The fund is structured as a Delaware statutory trust. It does not issue the K-1 forms that some investment partnerships require. The proportion of distributions that qualify for lower dividend tax rates, and the fund's capital-gain-distribution history, are not available in the automated pipeline and would need to be verified from the iShares annual report. The in-kind redemption mechanism used by exchange-traded funds often results in zero capital-gain distributions.

  1. Equity risk premium remains positive. The basket's collective earnings yield should exceed the ten-year US Treasury yield by at least two percentage points to compensate for equity risk. What Could Still Go Wrong

The bear case for this fund has two layers: the basket itself could deliver poor returns, or the fund could become an unsuitable wrapper for tracking those returns.

  • A broad developed-market drawdown combined with earnings contraction — the bear scenario assumes both earnings falling roughly 11% from trend and the market de-rating from today's 24× earnings multiple to 14× — would produce a loss of around 20% a year over five years. That is the structural bear: both earnings and the market's willingness to pay for them move against the position simultaneously.
  • The cost rises above 0.35% and BlackRock does not respond with a fee reduction. That fee ceiling is explicit in the exit conditions. At that point the case for holding this particular wrapper rather than a cheaper equivalent disappears.
  • Assets under management fall below $2.0 billion through sustained redemptions. A 75% drawdown from today's $8.06 billion would be required. The consequence is not fund closure necessarily — iShares could merge or restructure the fund — but rather widening bid-ask spreads that make trading in and out expensive.
  • NVIDIA's weight keeps rising toward the 10% ceiling. At 5.17% today, there is 4.83 percentage points of headroom — but if NVIDIA's market value continues to outpace the rest of the index while no reconstitution caps it, the single-name-dominance exit condition approaches.
  • MSCI restructures the World index in a way that undermines the developed-markets mandate. This exit condition is monitored externally; no automated pipeline flags it.

(For this fund, exit fires when the share price reaches the exit edge at $279, when a vehicle-integrity condition breaches, or when the named exit-condition trigger fires — any of three paths.)

The Rules

The pre-committed rules governing this position cover six states:

  • If the thesis is confirmed (equity risk premium positive, all vehicle-integrity guardrails clear, share price within the hold band): hold — the basket is reasonably priced and the vehicle is sound.
  • If the thesis is weakened (equity risk premium negative but exit condition not yet triggered, or look-through quality declining but above the exit threshold): do not add. Re-validate after the next quarter. If the equity risk premium stays negative or the median look-through return on equity keeps falling, swap to a cheaper UCITS-domiciled equivalent or move to cash per the position-sizing rule.
  • If the thesis is broken (vehicle integrity breached or a written exit condition triggered): exit. Move proceeds to risk-free or to a holding that meets the value test.
  • If the share price falls to the buy edge at $134 (earnings yield 6.25%): add to the position. The basket is meaningfully cheap versus the risk-free rate.
  • If the share price rises to the trim edge at $209 (earnings yield 4.0%): trim 25 to 33 per cent of the position. The basket is meaningfully expensive versus the risk-free rate; harvest the premium.
  • If the share price rises to the exit edge at $279 (earnings yield 3.0%): exit. Redeploy proceeds to risk-free or to a holding that meets the value test.

Scenario Definitions

A scenario is a joint assumption about two independent variables: operating trajectory (how the holding's underlying cash flows grow relative to their long-run trend) and exit multiple (what the market pays for those cash flows at the horizon, relative to a fair-value anchor).

Scenario Operating trajectory Exit multiple
Bear Below trend Below fair value
Base At trend At fair value
Bull Above trend At or above fair value

Non-negotiable rule: In the bear case, both dimensions must be adverse simultaneously — a scenario where only the multiple moves is a re-rating scenario, not a bear.

Bear cases are comparable in structure across all holding types (company, ETF, bond), but not in magnitude. A company bear reflects idiosyncratic execution failure; an ETF bear reflects a broad market drawdown. Different mitigations, different diversification implications.
