About the Fund

SP500 is an index fund that tracks the 500 largest listed US companies by the size of their total stock-market value. The fund holds each company in proportion to that value, so the largest companies — led today by Nvidia, Apple, and Alphabet — carry the largest weights. No active manager picks the stocks; the fund simply mirrors its index.

The fund is managed by State Street Investment Management and has operated since January 1993, making it the oldest listed equity index fund in the US. It holds 99.9% of its assets in equities, with a small uninvested cash float of roughly 0.1% that arises because the fund's legal structure — a unit investment trust — cannot reinvest dividends between distribution dates. The annual cost is 0.0945%. Neither one-year tracking difference nor one-year tracking error is available from the current data; those figures can be verified from the State Street SPDR factsheet. The fund does not issue tax partnership forms (no K-1). Capital-gain distribution history is unverified but the structure's in-kind redemption mechanism typically generates none.

Technology stocks account for 38.5% of the basket today, driven by the large market capitalisations of Nvidia, Apple, Microsoft, and Broadcom. No other sector exceeds 12%. This technology concentration is a structural feature of the cap-weighted index rather than a transient tilt.

The Case for the Bet

The bet is that over the holding period the basket's collective earnings, expressed as a yield on today's price, will exceed the risk-free rate by at least two percentage points. The bet is not that the US stock market always rises; it is a specific value-discipline claim about relative yield. Six conditions must keep holding.

Condition 1. The basket earnings yield (what the 500 companies earn collectively relative to their combined market price) must stay above the 10-year US Treasury yield by at least two percentage points — meaning equity holders earn a positive risk premium for taking on the uncertainty of owning businesses rather than lending to the government.

Latest reading: Basket earnings yield 3.72%, 10-year Treasury yield 4.53%, giving an equity risk premium of −0.81 percentage points. The condition is currently not met. Eight consecutive weekly readings have been negative. The written exit rule requires two full quarterly readings before the exit fires; the persistence test at the quarterly level is not yet confirmed.

Condition 2. State Street does not raise the fund's annual expense ratio above 0.15% without a competitive fee response.

Latest reading: Annual cost is 0.0945%, well below the 0.15% ceiling. No change announced. Condition intact.

Condition 3. Total investor money in the fund (a measure of the fund's size and liquidity) does not fall below $100B.

Latest reading: $781.2B — approximately 7.8 times the floor. Condition intact with $681.2B of headroom.

Condition 4. No single holding in the basket exceeds 13% of the total. That threshold marks a regime-change in concentration that would undermine the broad-market mandate.

Latest reading: Nvidia is the largest single holding at 7.5% of the basket. The 13% ceiling is not approached. Condition intact.

Condition 5. The S&P 500 index methodology is not materially restructured in a way that changes the broad-market mandate.

Latest reading: No automated signal is available for index-methodology changes. Monitoring is manual; verify against S&P Dow Jones Indices methodology announcements quarterly. Condition silent — no evidence of breach.

Condition 6. The median return on equity of the ten largest deduped holdings does not collapse below 15%. That threshold marks the point at which the basket's underlying businesses have lost their quality.

Latest reading: Median return on equity (a measure of how much profit the companies generate relative to their own balance-sheet value) is 37.3% across the nine largest deduped holdings — more than twice the 15% exit floor. Two of the top holdings, Nvidia and Apple, show returns above 100% because aggressive share buybacks have shrunk their balance-sheet values, a distortion that inflates the figure mechanically. The median is the more conservative and representative read. Condition comfortably intact.

The Share Price

The basket trades at a price-to-earnings multiple of 26.9 times trailing earnings (in other words, for every dollar the underlying companies earn collectively, the market charges $26.90). The long-run historical average for the S&P 500 sits closer to 16 times. The fair-value anchor used in this framework is 20 times earnings — equivalent to a basket earnings yield of 5.00%.

At today's price of $747.71, the basket earnings yield is 3.72%. That sits below both the 4.00% trim threshold (where the trim rule fires, at a price of $695) and the 5.00% fair-value yield (equivalent to roughly $556 a share). The exit threshold of 3.00% — where the basket is fully priced relative to the risk-free rate — corresponds to a price of $927. The current price sits above the trim line and well above fair value. It has not yet reached the exit boundary.

Standing orders (actionable).

- A standing trim order at $695. The rules call for trimming a quarter to a third of the position when the share price closes at or above this line, to harvest the premium earned above fair value. The current price of $747.71 is already above this line; the trim rule is currently active.
- A standing exit order at $927. The rules call for closing the full position when the share price closes at or above this line. At that price the basket earnings yield falls to 3.00% — at or below the risk-free rate on any reasonable view, removing the equity return case entirely.

Vehicle-integrity breaches (cost, size, concentration — conditions 2 through 4 above) and the named exit-condition trigger (condition 1 — two consecutive quarters of negative equity risk premium) are independent additional paths to exit, separate from the price-based exit order.

What's Wobbling

The equity risk premium has been negative for eight consecutive weekly snapshots. That is the load-bearing wobble — the entire value case for holding the basket rather than a risk-free Treasury rests on this number being positive. At −0.81 percentage points, it is not.

The exit rule requires persistence: two consecutive quarterly readings, not weekly ones. The weekly trend is informational. If Treasury yields fall — not because conditions deteriorate but simply because the rate cycle turns — the premium can recover without any change in the basket's earnings. That is the mitigating path. If yields stay elevated and the next quarterly snapshot (end of September 2026) also shows a negative premium, exit condition 1 fires and the trim rule upgrades to an exit rule.

Meanwhile the trim price band has already been breached. The current price of $747.71 sits $52.69 above the $695 trim edge. The pre-committed trim rule (condition: price above $695) is already active.

What Could Still Go Wrong

  • Earnings collapse. If corporate earnings across the 500 companies fall materially — in a recession, a credit crunch, or a sharp margin compression driven by tariffs or labour costs — the basket's earnings yield stays low even as the share price falls, and the quality signal weakens alongside the valuation.
  • Multiple compression without an earnings offset. At 26.9 times earnings, the basket has embedded significant optimism about future growth. If that optimism reverses, the price can fall steeply before earnings themselves move. The base-case five-year forward return of 1.24% a year already reflects this: it assumes the price-to-earnings multiple compresses from 26.9 to 20 over five years.
  • Persistently elevated risk-free rates. If the 10-year Treasury yield stays above 4% for two or more quarterly periods while the basket earnings yield remains below it, exit condition 1 fires. The current trajectory (eight negative weekly readings, trend widening) makes this the most proximate risk.
  • Technology concentration reversal. Technology stocks represent 38.5% of the basket. A structural repricing of that sector — regulatory, competitive, or macro — would hit the basket more heavily than a broadly diversified exposure.
  • Vehicle-integrity failure. Unlikely given the fund's size, but a State Street operational event, a fee increase above 0.15%, or a single-name position breaking through 13% would each independently trigger an exit regardless of valuation.

(For this fund, exit fires when the share price reaches the exit edge, when a vehicle-integrity condition breaches, or when the named exit-condition trigger fires — any of three paths.)

The Rules

The evaluation reaches one of six states, each with a pre-committed response.

  • If the basket is reasonably priced and the vehicle is sound: hold — no action required.
  • If the equity risk premium stays negative or the look-through quality keeps falling without yet triggering the exit condition: do not add. Re-validate after the next quarterly snapshot. If the condition persists, swap to an alternative broad-market fund or move proceeds to cash.
  • If a vehicle-integrity condition fires or the exit condition triggers: exit the full position. Move proceeds to a risk-free instrument or to a holding that meets the value test.
  • If the share price falls to or below the buy edge ($445, a basket earnings yield of 6.25%): add to the position. The basket is meaningfully cheap relative to the risk-free rate.
  • If the share price rises to or above the trim edge ($695, a basket earnings yield of 4.00%): trim a quarter to a third of the position. The basket is meaningfully expensive relative to the risk-free rate; harvest the premium. This rule is currently active.
  • If the share price rises to or above the exit edge ($927, a basket earnings yield of 3.00%): exit the full position. The basket is fully priced relative to the risk-free rate; redeploy proceeds to a risk-free instrument or to a holding that meets the value test.
